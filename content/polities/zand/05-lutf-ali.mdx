Karim Khan's brothers, sons and nephews fought each other for the vakilate from
the week of his death. Six of them held Shiraz in ten years and most of them were
blinded or killed by the next; the family destroyed itself while the Qajars took
the north, then Isfahan, then everything except Fars.

Lutf Ali Khan, Karim Khan's great-nephew, was the last and by the accounts the
most capable — twenty-one when he took Shiraz in 1789, personally brave, and a
good enough commander that Agha Muhammad Khan took four years to finish him. He
was betrayed by his own chief minister, who closed the gates of Shiraz against
him while he was in the field, and spent his last two years as a fugitive with a
few hundred men, raiding across southern Iran.

He was captured at Bam in 1794, blinded, tortured, sent to Tehran and killed. He
was twenty-five. Agha Muhammad Khan then destroyed Kerman for having sheltered
him.

The Zand dynasty lasted forty-three years and the part of it that anybody
remembers is the twenty-eight in the middle. What it leaves is a bazaar in
Shiraz that people still shop in, a tomb that is a place people go, and a
reputation — which is a strange thing for this site to have to record, because
none of the fields hold it. The extent is null, the influence counts are null,
the succession rule is null. The only quantified thing on the record is a span
of forty-three years, which is shorter than the Mirdasids managed and much
shorter than the Artuqids.

The corpus has no axis for whether a government was any good to live under. That
is a real limit and it is worth naming on the one page where it is the entire
point.
