Agha Muhammad Khan took Shiraz in 1794, killed the last Zand, and then did
something to Karim Khan that is remembered better than most of what either man
did alive.

He had the body dug up, brought to Tehran, and reburied under the steps of his
own palace, so that he would walk over it every day.

## What that is

It is not simply desecration, though it is that. It is a claim about who had
been right.

Karim Khan had governed for thirty years without taking the title of shah,
calling himself the deputy of the subjects, keeping a Safavid infant on a throne
he did not want, and declining to build the kind of terror his neighbours ran
on. Agha Muhammad had watched all of it from inside the household, for sixteen
years, as a hostage.

The reburial is his verdict. A man who ruled without the title ends up under the
staircase of a man who took it, and the point is made daily, with the feet.

## Which of them was right

On the evidence of the following century, the eunuch.

The Qajar dynasty lasted a hundred and thirty years. The Zand state did not
survive Karim Khan by a fortnight in any working sense, because everything about
it — the restraint, the low taxes, the tolerance, the refusal of the title —
depended on one man's judgement and none of it was institutional. His nephews
inherited a wall, a ditch and a set of drums, and spent ten years besieging each
other for them.

Agha Muhammad built nothing pleasant and produced a dynasty. He was murdered by
two of his own servants in 1797, three years after the reburial and one year
after his coronation, having condemned them to death the previous evening and
then carelessly allowed them to serve him overnight.

## And what people go to see

The asymmetry is in what survives above ground.

Shiraz has Karim Khan's citadel, his bazaar — which is still the bazaar, and
still busy — his mosque, his garden, and the tomb of Hafez as he rebuilt it. The
city is one of the pleasantest in Iran to be in, and a great deal of the reason
is a man who ruled for twenty-eight years and refused a crown.

Tehran has the Qajar palaces, and the steps are gone.

<Figure id="karim-khan-tomb" />

Karim Khan's remains were eventually recovered and reburied in Shiraz. He is the
only ruler in this whole sequence — Safavid, Afghan, Afsharid, Zand, Qajar — of
whom Iranians in the following two centuries have said, more or less without
qualification, that things were better when he was in charge.
