Mursili I of Hatti came down the Euphrates from central Anatolia, took Babylon,
ended the dynasty, and went home. He installed no governor and left no garrison.
He was murdered on his return by his brother-in-law, and Hatti spent the
following century tearing itself apart.

The Kassites, who had been present in Babylonia as a minority for generations,
took over the vacated kingship within a few decades and held it for four hundred
years — the longest single dynasty in Mesopotamian history, and not one this
corpus carries.

## An edge that reads wrong

This site records `old-babylonian -> hittite`, type `conquered by`, dated 1595,
and a reader looking at the region's thread will see a line running from Babylon
to Hatti and will naturally read it as a succession. It is not one, and the edge's
own note says so.

The type says only what the vocabulary says: this polity was conquered by that
one. That is exactly true. What is not true, and what no field on this site
asserts, is that Hatti took over. The polity that succeeded this one in Babylonia
is the Kassite dynasty, and it has no page, so there is no edge for the thing that
actually happened and there is an edge for the thing that happened and led
nowhere.

Hard rule 7 is written against threads that assert sequences nobody cited, and
this is the adjacent hazard: an edge each of whose ends is cited, drawn in a
picture that invites a reading neither end supports. The honest handling is not to
suppress the edge — the raid is one of the best-attested events in the second
millennium and it is why this record ends — but to say on the page what the line
does not mean.

The Hittite record's fifth chapter has been amended to match. It was written
saying that Hatti entered this corpus with no edges in either direction, which was
true when it was written and is not now; the amendment is on the page rather than
in the file's history, because a claim about what this site contains, phrased as a
claim about history, is a mistake this project has made before and prefers to
leave visible.

## What survived it

Two things, and they are the two counts on this record.

`descendant_scripts` is 1. Hittite cuneiform is Old Babylonian cuneiform adapted
to write an Indo-European language, and coding rule 3 counts adaptation to a new
language on the record of the polity whose script was adapted. Hittite is the
earliest attested Indo-European language in the world. It is attested because
Anatolian scribes took this dynasty's writing system, probably by way of northern
Syria, and made it write something it had never written. That claim was flagged on
the Hittite record as belonging to a page that did not exist, and this is the page.

`successor_claims` is 0, and the reasoning is a distinction worth stating. Every
subsequent ruler of Mesopotamia wanted to be king of Babylon — the Kassites, the
Assyrians, Cyrus, Alexander, the Seleucids. This region's Neo-Babylonian record
counts two such claims of its own. But those are claims on Babylon, and Babylon
is a city. Coding rule 1 counts a later polity asserting an earlier *polity's*
legitimacy, and nobody in the fifteen hundred years after 1595 claimed to be
continuing the First Dynasty. They claimed a place.

This site has two records for Babylon and two for Assyria and two for Elam, and
in each pair the later one is the same ground and the same gods resuming after an
interruption. There is no edge between them and no count for it, because it is
neither a succession nor a claim.

This paragraph used to end by saying that naming the hole was all the page could
do about it. It is not a hole any more. The Neo-Babylonian record carries
`resumes: old-babylonian`, and three other records carry the same field — but it
is a property of a record and not an edge, and the difference is the whole point.
An edge is an event with a year, and there is no year on which Babylon resumed
being Babylon. An edge is drawn by the region's thread, and putting a
non-succession into the one picture on this site that exists to show successions
is a step toward the single mistake this project is built to avoid.
