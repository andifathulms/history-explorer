Shaka was born out of wedlock to Nandi, a daughter of the eLangeni
clan, and Senzangakhona, chief of the small Zulu clan descended from
Malandela; dismissed in disgrace, mother and son went home to
eLangeni-land to live among a people who never let either of them
forget it. Shaka grew up unpopular and mocked - other boys burned his
porridge-stirrer to a coal and dared him to eat from it "if he were
indeed a chief," forced scalding food into his cupped hands, and
jeered without mercy at his crinkled ears and his other bodily
peculiarities - taunts Bryant records Shaka never forgot and never
forgave. When his own father tried to present him with his first loin
covering, the mark of Zulu manhood, Shaka rejected it with such open
disdain that his return to his mother became unavoidable.

## A famine, and a search for anywhere that would have them

Around 1802 famine struck eLangeni-land hard enough that Nandi could
no longer feed her children, and she and her family took to the road
again, this time to kinsmen at the sources of the Mtungulu river.
Even there Shaka, now about fifteen, found no lasting welcome; both
his father's people and his mother's were pressing for his return,
and a stay with a neighboring chief nearly ended when Senzangakhona,
by one account, tried to bribe that chief into killing his own
unwanted son outright - a request the chief refused. As a last resort
Nandi brought the boy to a great-aunt among the Mthethwa near the
coast, where a headman named Ngomane treated the destitute pair with
simple kindness. Shaka never forgot it, and in his own day of power
raised Ngomane to the highest rank in his kingdom next to himself.

## A duel with a madman on a hill

Under the Mthethwa chief Dingiswayo, Shaka spent six years as a
soldier of the iziCwe regiment and grew into the army's most
celebrated fighter, rejecting the standard practice of throwing an
assegai from a distance in favor of closing to hand-to-hand combat -
reckless against a prepared enemy, devastating against one caught by
surprise. Bryant records one duel that made his name: a raider had
taken to terrorizing a district from a hilltop refuge, driving off
whole herds and defeating every party sent against him. When Shaka's
commander asked in his hearing who would rid them of the man, Shaka
volunteered, met the raider in single combat before the assembled
troop, and killed him - the exploit that first marked him out, in the
army of the chief who had taken in his own destitute family, as a
soldier meant for greater things.
