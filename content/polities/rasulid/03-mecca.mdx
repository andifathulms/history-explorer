---
title: The contest for the holy cities
drafted_from: vallet-2010
phase: peak
---

Yemen is the nearest substantial power to Mecca, and for most of the Rasulid
period the sultans in Ta'izz behaved accordingly: they sent money to the sharifs,
sent grain in bad years, sent the annual pilgrimage caravan, intervened in the
sharifate's own succession quarrels, and on several occasions had the khutba at
Mecca read in their name.

They were competing with Cairo for it, and the competition was conducted in
ceremony and money rather than in armies. The Mamluk sultans regarded the
guardianship of the holy cities as theirs, sent their own caravan and their own
kiswa — the embroidered covering of the Ka'ba, replaced annually, and a public
statement about whose sultan was paying for it — and objected when a Rasulid
kiswa arrived first or when a Rasulid name was preached. There were years when
two coverings arrived and the sharif had to choose, and years when the choice was
made by whichever caravan reached the city earlier.

That sounds like protocol and it was politics. This site's Hafsid page records
the sharif of Mecca's acknowledgement as the one recognition a claimant to the
caliphate could point to; the Ikhshidid and Sulayhid pages record the same thing
from other directions. A ruler whose name was heard in the Friday sermon at Mecca
by pilgrims from Morocco to Sumatra had a claim nothing else could buy, and the
sharifs — who held a small territory, no army worth the name and the most
valuable real estate in the Muslim world — spent nine centuries selling that
recognition to whoever offered most and could reach them.

The Rasulids lost the contest slowly and then completely. Mamluk pressure on the
Hejaz increased through the fourteenth century, and once Cairo controlled
Jeddah's customs it controlled the sharif's income, which settled the question.
By the fifteenth century the holy cities were unambiguously in the Mamluk
sphere, and the sultanate that had been the alternative was losing its own
revenue at the same time and for the same reason.
