title: An axe that broke in the king's hand

By the spring of 1314, after years of methodically retaking castle
after castle from its English garrisons, Robert I had reduced English
control in Scotland to little more than Stirling Castle itself. A
large English army under Edward II marched north that June to relieve
the siege, and the two forces met near the Bannock Burn, close to
Stirling.

## A single combat before the battle

On the first day of fighting, as Robert rode along his own lines on a
small palfrey, an English knight named Henry de Bohun spotted him and
charged alone from the English ranks, lance levelled, hoping to end
the war in a single stroke of chivalric daring. Robert's pony sidestepped
the charge, and as de Bohun's horse carried him past, the king rose in
his stirrups and struck him on the helmet with a battle-axe hard enough
to split his skull to the chin - a blow that broke the axe's own shaft
and nearly unhorsed Robert himself with its force. It was, by every
account, the kind of moment on which the whole future of the war could
easily have turned the other way.

## The battle itself

The main fighting the following day went just as decisively against
the English. Scottish schiltrons - dense formations of spearmen -
withstood repeated cavalry charges on ground chosen to deny the larger
English army room to deploy its full strength, and the English line
broke into rout. Twenty-one English barons and bannerets died in the
fighting, including the Earl of Gloucester, King Edward's own nephew;
forty-two knights were killed and sixty captured, among them the Earl
of Hereford. Edward II himself narrowly escaped the field.

## What one battle actually secured

Bannockburn did not end the war by treaty - that recognition would
not come for another fourteen years, at the Treaty of Edinburgh-
Northampton in 1328. But it broke English military power in Scotland
decisively enough that no comparable invasion was attempted again in
Robert's own lifetime, and it secured, in practice if not yet on
parchment, the independent kingship the wars had been fought to
defend since Wallace's rising seventeen years before.
