Ghur is the mountain country between Ghazna and Herat: high, poor, hard to
enter and harder to hold. It was still substantially pagan in the eleventh
century, four hundred years after the conquest of Iran, because nobody had
found it worth the cost of going in.

Mahmud of Ghazna went in around 1011 and imposed vassalage on the local
Shansabani chiefs. Their conversion, their entry into the political world of the
region, and their standing all date from that, and for a century and a half they
were exactly what such an arrangement produces: a client house supplying troops,
occasionally raiding, occasionally being punished.

The dynasty's beginning is therefore a range of a hundred and thirty years, and
the width of it is not carelessness. The local tradition carries the Shansabani
line back to the ninth century as chiefs of Ghur. The dynasty that appears in
the histories begins with Ghaznavid vassalage around 1011. One of those is a
lineage and the other is a polity, and no source settles which one the Ghurids
ought to be dated from.

The same thinness runs through everything else known about them. Nobody
publishes a figure for how much territory they held or how many people lived in
it, and the sources cannot agree on the name of the last sultan of Firuzkuh.
That is the ordinary condition of a mountain dynasty whose own archive was
destroyed with its capital.
