Ghur is the mountain country between Ghazna and Herat: high, poor, hard to
enter and harder to hold. It was still substantially pagan in the eleventh
century, four hundred years after the conquest of Iran, because nobody had
found it worth the cost of going in.

Mahmud of Ghazna went in around 1011 and imposed vassalage on the local
Shansabani chiefs. Their conversion, their entry into the political world of the
region, and their standing all date from that, and for a century and a half they
were exactly what such an arrangement produces: a client house supplying troops,
occasionally raiding, occasionally being punished.

This site records the start of the Ghurids as a range from 879 to 1011, which is
wider than it looks. The local tradition carries the Shansabani line back to the
ninth century as chiefs of Ghur; the dynasty that appears in the histories
begins with Ghaznavid vassalage. One is a lineage and the other is a polity, and
the sources do not settle which one the Ghurids should be dated from. The bar
covers both.

Almost everything on this page is thinner than its neighbours. There is no
cited peak extent, no cited population, and the identity of the last sultan of
Firuzkuh is a gap because the consulted sources do not agree. That is the
expected condition for a mountain dynasty whose own records did not survive it,
and the panel is left visibly empty rather than filled by inference.
