---
title: Mu'izz al-Din's India
drafted_from: jackson-1999
phase: peak
---

Mahmud of Ghazna had raided India for thirty years and annexed only the Punjab.
Mu'izz al-Din of Ghur, in about twenty, took the Gangetic plain and kept it.

He lost his first major engagement, at Tarain in 1191, against Prithviraja
Chauhan. He returned the following year, won on the same ground, and the
Chauhan kingdom collapsed. Delhi fell in 1193. His generals took Bihar and then
Bengal.

The difference from Mahmud is the point, and it is a difference of intent rather
than of scale. Mahmud's campaigns were extractive and cyclical: raid, remove the
proceeds to Ghazna, repeat. Mu'izz al-Din installed garrisons and governors and
left them there. That is the difference between a religion being carried and a
country being robbed, and it is why the Ghurid campaigns founded a Muslim state
in northern India and Mahmud's, which were larger and more famous, did not.

Then he was assassinated in 1206, near the Indus, with no son.

The Indian conquests were held by his Turkish slave-generals. Qutb al-Din Aibak
kept Delhi and Lahore, Bakhtiyar Khalji had taken Bengal, Yildiz held Ghazna,
Qabacha held Sind. Aibak's line, and then Iltutmish's, became the Delhi
Sultanate, which ruled northern India for three centuries and traced its
authority to a Ghurid appointment.

It is the only claim anybody ever made on the Ghurid inheritance, and it is a
very large one. A dynasty that held an empire for about thirty years produced a
successor state that lasted three hundred, in a country none of its founders had
been born in.
