Ghiyath al-Din died in 1203, Mu'izz al-Din in 1206, and the Ghurid state did not
survive both.

The Indian provinces went to the slave-generals and never came back. What was
left was Ghur and a contested claim to Khurasan, held against the Khwarazmshahs,
who had beaten the Ghurids at Andkhud in 1204 with Kara-Khitai help and were the
strongest power in the east.

Firuzkuh fell to Ala al-Din Muhammad of Khwarazm in 1215. The succession of
short-reigned sultans between 1206 and then is recorded inconsistently, which is
why this site leaves the last ruler as a gap.

Set against its neighbours the Ghurid shape is peculiar. It is the shortest
imperial career in the corpus — roughly thirty years between taking Ghazna and
losing Firuzkuh — and it produced the longest-lasting successor. The rating
panel on this page will read almost empty: three axes with no cited figure and a
longevity bar whose start is a range of a hundred and thirty years.

An empty panel next to that outcome is the clearest argument this site can make
for why the ratings are a secondary feature. The Ghurids are the least
measurable polity here and, on any account that matters, among the most
consequential.
