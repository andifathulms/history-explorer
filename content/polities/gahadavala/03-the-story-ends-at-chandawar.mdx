Govindachandra's grandson Jayachandra - remembered in the popular
Hindi poems and tales of Northern India as Raja Jaichand - ruled from
what Muhammadan historians treated as his capital at Varanasi, and
was reputed, in his own day, the greatest king in India. Popular
tradition credits him with a domain stretching from the borders of
China to Malwa, and from the sea to within ten days' march of Lahore
- claims Smith judges impossible to credit at face value, though they
capture how the kingdom's own reputation had grown since Govindachandra's
restoration. The same popular tradition also remembers Jayachandra as
the father of a daughter carried off by Prithviraj Chauhan of Ajmer, a
story told and retold in Hindi verse for centuries afterward, though
it belongs to legend rather than to anything the inscriptions
themselves record.

## A single battle, a whole kingdom's end

What is not legend is what happened at Chandawar, in the Etawah
district near the Jumna, in 1194. Shihab-ud-din - Muhammad of Ghor -
met Jayachandra's own army there and, in Smith's words, defeated "his
huge host with immense slaughter, in which the Raja was included."
The Ghurid sultan pressed on afterward to Varanasi itself, plundering
the city and carrying its treasure home on fourteen hundred camels.
Smith's own verdict on the battle is blunt: "Thus ends the story of
the independent kingdom of Kanauj" - a paramountcy three dynasties had
fought over for nearly four centuries, closed out in a single
afternoon by an army from beyond India's own northwestern frontier.

## What was left behind

Kanauj itself did not vanish from the map. When the Gaharwar line
died out, local rule passed to chiefs of the Chandela clan from
Mahoba, who governed the city for eight more generations - a
subordinate, tributary afterlife under Muhammadan overlordship rather
than a resumption of the independence Chandawar had ended. The
paramountcy itself, the thing three great kingdoms had spent two
centuries fighting to hold, never returned to any Hindu dynasty in
Northern India again.
