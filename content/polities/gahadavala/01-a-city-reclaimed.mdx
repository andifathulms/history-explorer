---
title: A city reclaimed
drafted_from: smith-1904
phase: formation
---

By the closing decades of the eleventh century Kanauj had spent nearly
a century as a shadow of the city Mihira Bhoja once ruled from - a
diminished court under obscure chiefs, tributary to whatever power
happened to hold the middle Gangetic plain, its old Pratihara dynasty
fading into names nobody thought worth recording. Into that vacuum
came Chandradeva, a chief of the Gaharwar clan, who conquered and
occupied Kanauj a little before 1090 and installed his own line there
- a line later writers would sometimes call "Rathor," though Smith is
careful to note the label is a later misapplication, and that the
family the inscriptions actually name is Gaharwar, or Gahadavala.

Little beyond the bare fact of conquest survives from Chandradeva's
own reign. What his rule did establish was the platform his grandson
would use to make Kanauj a real power again rather than merely a
reclaimed name - proof that the contest for paramountcy over the
Gangetic plain, quiet since the Pratiharas' own final obscurity, still
had one more chapter left before a still greater power arrived from
beyond India's own northwestern frontier.
