George of Poděbrad, the Hussite nobleman the Estates had elected king
in 1458, died in March 1471 still cherished by Bohemians, in Lützow's
own account, second only to Charles IV in their historical affection -
even though his larger ambitions, a leading role in the Empire and a
lasting native dynasty of his own, had both failed by the time dropsy
killed him. The Estates had already recognized a Polish prince,
Vladislav, as his heir, but Matthias Corvinus of Hungary pressed a
rival claim of his own, already holding towns in Moravia and much of
Silesia and Lusatia through years of war against Poděbrad. Rather than
simply honor their earlier promise, the Estates convened at Kutná Hora
to choose freely between the candidates - a decision that mattered
less for its outcome, Lützow suggests, than for what it established:
that the Bohemian crown remained genuinely elective rather than
hereditary by custom. They chose Vladislav, who entered Prague to a
citizens' welcome that August and was handed, by the university's own
doctors, a finely bound Bible to guide his rule.

## A king who reigned from somewhere else

Vladislav's own authority never matched the warmth of his welcome.
Matthias Corvinus kept fighting for years before the two rivals
settled on an uneasy division of the crown's own territories, and once
Matthias died in 1490 and Vladislav added Hungary's own crown to
Bohemia's, he spent most of his remaining reign governing from Buda
rather than Prague. The Bohemian nobility used his prolonged absence
to entrench their own power at the crown's expense, pushing through
regulations - which a reluctant, largely powerless Vladislav simply
ratified rather than contest - that let disputes between the noble
estates and the towns overshadow even the kingdom's long-running
religious divisions for the rest of his reign.

## A ten-year-old inherits two crowns and a losing war

Vladislav died in Hungary in March 1516, leaving both his crowns to a
ten-year-old son, Louis, already crowned King of Bohemia at the age of
three. Louis grew up fighting the same battle his father had mostly
avoided: an Ottoman advance into Hungary that Bohemia's own nobility,
divided by internal feuds between rival noble factions, proved
reluctant to fund or reinforce with any urgency. When Louis's urgent
appeal for troops finally reached Prague, only a fraction of the
promised Bohemian force had actually arrived in Hungary by the time
Louis, badly outnumbered, gave battle at Mohács on 29 August 1526 and
drowned fleeing the rout - a defeat that cost Hungary two centuries of
Ottoman occupation and left Bohemia's own throne vacant for the second
time in fifty-five years, with no native candidate left to fill it.
