Bořivoj's line held Bohemia after Spytihněv's break from Moravia, but
it did not hold it peacefully. When Duke Vratislav I died around 920,
he left two young sons, Wenceslas and Boleslav, under the guardianship
of their mother Drahomira - a proud, ambitious woman from a still-pagan
Slavic tribe who grew jealous of the influence Wenceslas's own
grandmother, Ludmila, held over the boy she had raised in the
Christian faith. Drahomira had Ludmila murdered rather than share
power with her: assassins found the older woman at prayer in the
castle of Tetín and strangled her with her own veil in 921. The
Catholic Church later canonized her as a saint, one of Bohemia's
earliest.

## A prince who reaped his own church's wheat

Wenceslas took up the government not long after, and the record that
survives him describes a strikingly pious ruler - a man who spent long
stretches of the night in prayer and who, by tradition, cut the wheat
and pressed the grapes for the sacramental bread and wine at his own
churches with his own hands. His reign coincided with the rise of
Henry the Fowler's German kingdom, whose armies had already subdued
several neighboring Slavic tribes; when Henry marched on Prague in
928, Wenceslas judged outright resistance hopeless and accepted a
peace bought with an annual tribute of silver and cattle rather than
risk his kingdom on a battle he expected to lose.

## Murdered on the way to early mass

That same caution, paired with a generosity toward the church that
some of his own nobles resented, gave his younger brother Boleslav an
opening. Boleslav invited Wenceslas to a church dedication at Stará
Boleslav, where he himself held residence, and on 28 September 935,
as Wenceslas walked to early morning mass, Boleslav and a band of
fellow conspirators fell on him and killed him after he fought back
alone. The Church canonized Wenceslas as well, and the anniversary of
his death remains, to this day, one of Bohemia's great religious
feasts - a murdered duke whose own cult of sainthood would give the
Přemyslid dynasty a martyr-patron no rival house could claim to share.

## A cruel brother's competent reign

Boleslav, who took the throne as "the Cruel," proved a far more
formidable ruler than the brother he had killed. Henry the Fowler
treated the murder of his own tribute-paying ally as grounds to renew
the war, and Henry's successor Otto I sent two armies into Bohemia in
938; the fighting dragged on with mixed results for over a decade
before Boleslav, unable to withstand Otto's full strength, resumed the
tribute payments his brother had first agreed to. Boleslav turned his
own ambitions eastward instead, joining Otto's coalition against a
renewed Magyar invasion in 955 and personally destroying the
retreating Hungarian army when it tried to force a passage home
through Bohemia - a victory that let him absorb Moravia and push
Bohemian influence deep into the lands beyond the Carpathians. He
sealed an alliance with Poland's own pagan duke, Mieszko I, by giving
him a daughter, Dobrawa, in marriage - a match that led directly to
Mieszko's own conversion to Christianity and, through him, to Poland's.
