Přemysl Ottokar II inherited a hereditary kingdom - Bohemia's kings
had secured that title, and imperial recognition of it, since Ottokar
I's coronation at Mainz alongside the German king in 1198 - and built
it, for a time, into one of the great powers of Europe. Elected Duke
of Austria in 1251 and later ruling Styria and Carinthia as well,
Ottokar commanded, at his height, territory stretching from the Elbe
to the Adriatic, making him by far the most powerful prince within
the German Empire even though he never held its crown himself.

## A king accused of forgetting his own people

Ottokar's expansion came at a cost that would matter more than he
seems to have anticipated. He actively recruited German colonists
into Bohemia's towns, granting them extensive self-government under
German town law, in what Bohemian historians have generally read as
a deliberate strategy to build a merchant and artisan class able to
counterbalance an unruly native nobility. The effect, whatever the
intent, was to make Bohemia's towns overwhelmingly German-speaking
during his reign and to leave Ottokar open to the charge - fair or
not, and even Palacký, no friend to German influence, doubted it -
that he favoured his new German subjects over the Czechs who had
made him king.

## The day of Saint Rufus

That resentment proved fatal when it mattered most. Rudolph of
Habsburg, elected King of the Germans in 1273 partly through
Ottokar's own miscalculation in not contesting a rival he judged too
weak to matter, moved against Bohemia's territorial gains in Austria
and the south. Ottokar was forced to submit and do homage in 1276,
but renewed the war two years later. On 26 August 1278, near
Dürnkrut on the Marchfeld, Rudolph's army met Ottokar's in a
decisive battle - and Bohemian nobles who felt no loyalty to a king
they considered half a German himself, chief among them Milota of
Dedice, deserted or turned against him at the critical moment. When
the battle was plainly lost, Ottokar rode into the thickest of the
enemy ranks rather than flee, and died fighting. The empire he had
spent two decades building did not outlive him by an afternoon: his
seven-year-old son Wenceslas II inherited a kingdom plunged
immediately into factional anarchy, while Moravia passed under
Rudolph's own occupation.
