---
title: A great power among great powers
drafted_from: shaw-2000
phase: peak
---

The New Kingdom is Egypt as an imperial state rather than a civilisation
occupied with itself: Nubia annexed and administered directly, the Levant held
through vassal rulers, and a professional army that campaigned to the Euphrates.

The clearest evidence of what that meant is the Amarna correspondence — a
diplomatic archive of the fourteenth century BC, in Akkadian on clay tablets,
between the Egyptian court and the kings of Babylon, Assyria, Mitanni and Hatti.
They address each other as brothers, negotiate marriages, complain about the
quality of gold shipments, and observe a shared protocol. It is a functioning
international system of great powers, a thousand years before the Achaemenids and
two thousand before anything on this site's Islamic pages, and it is documented
in the participants' own words.

The kingdom declined after Ramesses III into a period of divided authority, and
the site records `fragmentation` with no conquering agent. Egypt continued for
another millennium as a polity worth conquering — the Achaemenids, Alexander, the
Ptolemies and Rome all took it — but not as one of the powers doing the
conquering.
