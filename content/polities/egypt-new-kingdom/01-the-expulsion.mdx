---
title: What being conquered taught them
drafted_from: shaw-2000
phase: formation
---

For about a century before this record begins, the northern half of Egypt was
ruled by foreigners.

The Hyksos — the Egyptian phrase means rulers of foreign lands — were a Levantine
dynasty established in the delta at Avaris, and while they held it the Egyptian
line at Thebes was one power among several, with a Nubian kingdom at Kerma to
its south and the Hyksos to its north. There is a surviving letter from a Hyksos
king to the ruler of Kush proposing a joint attack on Thebes, intercepted in the
desert. Egypt in the Second Intermediate Period was a middle power being
squeezed.

Ahmose expelled them around 1550 and took Avaris, and this record starts there.
What matters for the rest of the page is what the experience produced, because
the New Kingdom is Egypt behaving differently from the Egypt of the previous
fifteen hundred years.

It kept a permanent army. It campaigned outside Egypt continuously rather than
occasionally, and it did not stop at the traditional frontiers: Nubia was
annexed and administered directly under a viceroy — the King's Son of Kush — as
far as the fourth cataract, and the Levant was held through vassal rulers whose
sons were taken to Egypt and educated there.

And the technology came from the people who had held the delta. The horse and
the light chariot, the composite bow, improved bronze-working and body armour
are all Hyksos-period introductions, and the army that conquered the Levant used
all of them.

That is the shape of the whole period, and it is a shape this corpus records
elsewhere in other centuries. A state is conquered, absorbs what conquered it,
and turns outward. The site's Sasanian record has an empire rebuilt after a
steppe confederation killed its king; its Muscovite material is not here yet;
this is the earliest version it carries.
