Hatshepsut reigned as king for about twenty-two years, with the full titulary and
shown in the reliefs in male dress and a false beard. The expansion she is
remembered for was not a campaign. It was a shopping voyage, and she had it
carved on the walls of her funerary temple in more detail than most pharaohs gave
their wars.

## Why she wanted the trees

The temple at Deir el-Bahri was designed in terraces, and she conceived those
terraces as the myrrh-terraces of Punt — the land at the southern end of the Red
Sea that Egyptian tradition held to be the original home of the gods. One of her
inscriptions says that Amun had desired her to *establish for him a Punt in his
house*.

To do that literally, she needed living myrrh trees, and nobody had ever brought
any back. Egyptian expeditions had gone to Punt since the Old Kingdom, but none
had been equipped to carry trees. For as long as anyone could remember, even the
myrrh burned as incense in the temple service had reached Egypt hand to hand
overland, foreign traffic having suffered badly under the Hyksos.

So the authority for the voyage was made divine. Standing before the shrine, the
queen received an oracle from the god himself: that the ways to Punt should be
searched out, that the highways to the myrrh-terraces should be penetrated. *It
is a glorious region of God's-Land,* the god says, *it is indeed my place of
delight.*

## The far side

The fleet carried goods for barter and a great stone statue of the queen, to be
set up in Punt — if it survives there, the remotest monument any Egyptian ruler
ever erected.

They arrived safely. The commander pitched his tent on the shore and was met by
<Figure id="punt-animals" />

Perehu, the chief of Punt, with his conspicuously fat wife and their three
children, whom the Egyptian artists drew exactly as they saw them. It had been so
long since Egyptians had come that way that the reliefs put an astonished
question in the Puntites' mouths:

> Why have ye come hither unto this land, which the people know not? Did ye
> descend upon the roads of heaven, or did ye sail upon the waters, upon the sea
> of God's-Land?

Then trade, and the ships drawn up to the beach with the gang-planks out, loading
until they were down to the marks: fragrant woods, heaps of myrrh resin, fresh
myrrh trees, ebony, ivory, gold, cinnamon wood, incense, eye-paint, baboons,
monkeys, dogs, panther skins — and natives with their children.

own summary is the usual formula and for once may be close to true: *never was
the like of this brought for any king who has been since the beginning.*

## What came up the river

The fleet moored at Thebes and the cargo went through the streets to the palace,
Puntites walking with it, which the Thebans had almost certainly never seen
before.

The queen gave a share straight to Amun: thirty-one living myrrh trees, electrum,
eye-paint, Puntite throw-sticks, ebony, ivory, a live panther caught specially for
her, panther skins, and three thousand three hundred head of small cattle. Piles
of myrrh twice the height of a man were measured out in grain-measures under her
favourite Thutiy, and rings of gold weighed in balances ten feet tall.

This is a different kind of reach from the one the next reign is famous for.
Thutmose III would extend Egypt by seventeen campaigns and a permanent garrison
system. Hatshepsut extended it by sending ships further than anyone living had
sent them, to buy trees, in order that a building in Thebes could be a piece of
the country the gods came from.

Both are the same state at the same strength, doing the only two things a state
that strong can do with the surplus.
