---
title: Expansion resumes, explanation does not
drafted_from: ogot-1992

Once the succession crisis of 1780 to 1810 had run its course and a
settled king again held Munza, Luba's own campaigns of conquest resumed
on two fronts nearly at once - east across country toward the shore of
Lake Tanganyika, and north along the Lualaba river as far as a place
called Buli. Both campaigns were carried forward by military leaders
specifically invested with the kingdom's own sacred fire, a ritual
authority that marked their expeditions as extensions of the throne's own
power rather than independent warlordism.

## A gap the evidence cannot close

Nobody studying this period can say with confidence why the renewed
expansion happened when it did, or why it took the particular direction
it did. Proposed explanations pointing to access to trade routes or to raw
materials do not hold up well against what is actually known about the
kingdom's own economy and geography at the time, and the honest position
is that the inner logic of this second wave of conquest is simply not
understood, rather than that a plausible-sounding explanation exists and
has gone unstated.

## A kingdom still expanding when the trail goes cold

Luba's own history, followed this far, does not end in defeat or
dissolution but mid-motion - a kingdom actively pushing its own borders
outward in two directions at once, thirty years after nearly losing a
major subject territory to its own internal disorder, for reasons the
surviving evidence simply does not preserve. The territorial reach this
second wave of conquest built would prove, within a few decades, easier
to win than to hold.
