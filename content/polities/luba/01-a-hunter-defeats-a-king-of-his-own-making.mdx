---
title: A hunter defeats a king of his own making
drafted_from: ogot-1992

The plains north of the Upemba Depression were, for a long stretch of
their own early history, occupied by a scatter of small chiefdoms rather
than any single kingdom - fishing and farming communities in the river
valleys, linked to each other by trade in raffia, palm oil, fish, copper
and salt rather than by any common government. What tradition remembers
as the origin of the Luba kingdom begins, in fact, somewhere else
entirely: a small state called Kalundwe, founded between the Luembe and
Lubilash rivers by a coalition of three clans, with its own capital at
Cifinda.

## A capital moved twice before it found its place

One of Kalundwe's own kings, a figure remembered as Kongolo - the name
means "rainbow" - moved the capital onto the open plains at Mwibele, near
Lake Boya, in what would become the heartland of the later Luba state.
Other traditions dispute even this much, giving Kongolo a different point
of origin altogether. What the epic narrative agrees on is what happened
next: Kongolo was defeated and killed by a foreigner named Kalala Ilunga -
Ilunga the hunter, arriving from the east - who moved the capital a third
time, to Munza, closer to the region's iron ore deposits and its salt
pans. As Mwine Munza, master of Munza, Kalala Ilunga became the figure
later Luba kingship measured itself against - not necessarily because he
was a real, single historical person, but because the story of a
foreign hunter overturning a native king to found something better suited
what a Luba king was supposed to be.

## A story more reliable about places than about people

Kongolo himself may well not be an authentic historical figure at all,
by the judgment of the scholarship on the period. What can be trusted in
the tradition is not necessarily any one of its named individuals, but the
sequence of places - Cifinda, then Mwibele, then Munza - through which
the kingdom's own center of gravity moved before settling into the plains
north of the Upemba Depression, a location unlike almost every other
chiefdom in the region, which sat instead in a river valley. From that
plains capital, the state that would be called Luba began, sooner or
later, to dominate the smaller kingdoms around it - Kalundwe to the west,
Kikondja to the south - and to take control of the trade routes that ran
north and south through the country it now claimed.
