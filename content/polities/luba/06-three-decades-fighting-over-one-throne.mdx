---
title: Three decades fighting over one throne
drafted_from: ogot-1992
phase: peak
---

The alliance that settled Kadilo's war against the Songye left a lasting
mark on Luba politics that came fully due roughly eighty years later, when
a succession crisis broke out around 1780 and did not resolve until 1810 -
three decades during which the kingdom's central authority was contested
badly enough that its subject peoples took notice, and some of them acted
on it.

## A subject people takes its opening

The Kanyok, subjected to Luba overrule generations earlier when Luba
immigrants had organized a kingdom among them, used the extended crisis at
the center to break free of that overrule entirely. No source suggests
Luba's own court was able to mount a serious effort to bring the Kanyok
back into line while the succession dispute at home remained unresolved -
the crisis had consumed enough of the kingdom's own attention and
resources that a subject people three generations into its own
subordination could simply walk away from it.

## Songye influence at its height

The same three decades marked the high point of Songye cultural and
political influence inside Luba's own court, a trend traceable directly
back to the settlement that had ended Kadilo's war eighty years earlier.
The clearest evidence of how deep this influence now ran was institutional
rather than merely diplomatic: the Bukasandji society, a Songye religious
and political institution, was introduced into Luba affairs during
exactly this period - a foreign institution taking root inside the same
court whose own succession it was actively helping to decide.

## A throne settled, a kingdom diminished

By 1810 the crisis had passed and a settled succession restored central
authority at Munza. What it restored authority over, however, was a
smaller kingdom than the one the crisis had begun with - the Kanyok gone
for good, and a foreign religious institution now embedded at the very
center of Luba political life, a permanent legacy of a war Kadilo had
lost more than a century of consequences earlier.
