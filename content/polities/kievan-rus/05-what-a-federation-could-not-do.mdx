By the time a Mongol army under Batu reached the Dnieper in the winter of
1240, Kyiv had not been the effective centre of Rus' power for seventy
years. It kept a title. It did not keep an army large enough, or allies
close enough, to matter against what was coming.

## A federation, not a fortress

The Rus' principalities Batu's forces met were exactly what a century of
succession wars had made them: separate courts at Kyiv, at Suzdal and
Vladimir in the northeast, at Galicia and Volhynia in the southwest, and
around Novgorod in the north, each with its own army and its own quarrels
with the others, and none of them under any single command that could
have organised a combined defence even if all of them had wanted one.
Mongol columns had already broken Rus' forces once before, at the river
Kalka in 1223, in a battle several princes had ridden to and several
others had ignored.

## December 1240

Kyiv itself, when Batu's army reached it, was governed in the name of an
absent prince, Michael of Chernigov, by a boyar acting as his deputy.
There was no reigning ruler in the city to lead its defence or to
negotiate its surrender. The city fell and was destroyed. What had been,
within living memory of some who died in it, "the mother of Russian
cities" ceased to function as anyone's capital.

## What ended, and what did not

No Rus' prince, in the aftermath, claimed to be founding something new.
Every princely line that continued - in the northeast, in Novgorod, in
the southwest - traced its own legitimacy back to the same house Riurik's
tradition had begun, the same claim every contender in the preceding
century's succession wars had already been making. What changed was that
all of them now held their thrones by a khan's confirmation rather than by
right of the house alone, a condition that would last two and a half
centuries and that none of the princes who lived through 1240 had any way
of knowing would eventually be undone by one of their own descendants,
ruling from a city that had barely mattered while Kyiv still did.
