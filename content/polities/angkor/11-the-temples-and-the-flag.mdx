Angkor was never lost, never rediscovered, and is on the national flag. Each of
those needs saying because the first two are the opposite of what is usually
believed.

## The city that was not abandoned

The story that Henri Mouhot found Angkor Wat swallowed by jungle in 1860 is a
piece of nineteenth-century self-presentation and Mouhot did not claim it. He
said plainly that he was following where others had been.

Angkor Wat was never abandoned. It was maintained as a Theravada monastery
continuously from the sixteenth century at the latest — probably from the
fourteenth — with Buddhist images installed, monks resident, and a pilgrimage
traffic. There are Japanese pilgrim inscriptions in it from the early
seventeenth century, one of them accompanied by a floor plan drawn in Japan.
Portuguese and Spanish missionaries described it in the 1580s and 1590s.

A sixteenth-century Khmer king had substantial restoration work done and had
the unfinished bas-reliefs completed. That is a functioning building, not a
ruin.

The other temples were a different matter. Angkor Thom, the Bayon and the
outlying complexes were largely reclaimed by forest, which is what happens to
brick and laterite in a monsoon climate when nobody is sweeping them.

## What the French did

The French protectorate from 1863 produced the archaeology, the conservation,
and a great deal else.

The École française d'Extrême-Orient cleared, surveyed, mapped and restored on a
scale nobody else could have funded, and the scholarship — Coedès above all —
established the chronology of the inscriptions and made a connected history of
the Khmer empire possible for the first time. Everything anybody knows about
Angkorian kings rests on that work.

It also came with the ordinary apparatus of colonial appropriation: casts and
sculptures removed to Paris, a reconstructed Angkor pavilion at the colonial
expositions, and an argument — advanced explicitly — that the Cambodians of the
nineteenth century could not be the heirs of the people who had built this and
therefore needed a guardian.

André Malraux was arrested in 1924 for taking sculptures off Banteay Srei, which
is a fair summary of the period's range.

## The flag

Cambodia is the only country in the world whose national flag has a building on
it.

Angkor Wat has been on it, in one form or another, since 1863 — through the
protectorate, independence, the Khmer Republic, Democratic Kampuchea, the
Vietnamese-backed republic, and the restored kingdom. Every regime that has
governed Cambodia in a hundred and sixty years, including the one that murdered
a fifth of the population, has put the same twelfth-century temple on its flag.

The Khmer Rouge version was yellow on red. The current one is white on blue and
red. The building does not change.

## What is actually inherited

Not the state, which was gone by the fifteenth century, and not the religion of
the temples, which changed while they were still standing.

The language, which is Khmer and is directly continuous. The Theravada
Buddhism that replaced the temple cult and that Cambodia has practised for six
hundred years. And the fact of the monuments themselves, which have been the
single fixed point in every account Cambodians have given of who they are since
the empire that made them stopped existing.

That is a strange inheritance and a real one. The buildings outlived the state
by six centuries and became the argument for a nation the state would not have
recognised.
