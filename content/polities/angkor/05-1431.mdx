Ayutthaya took Angkor in 1431 and the Khmer court moved south, toward what
became Phnom Penh, and did not come back.

`ended.type` is `conquest` and `ended.by` names a polity with a page on this
site, which is rarer here than it should be — this corpus's African records made
a running observation of how often that field has to be left empty, and nine of
the twelve there are. The edge `angkor → ayutthaya` is this region's only
internal one.

And yet the field is not quite right, which is worth saying on the page rather
than only in a data comment. Ponhea Yat, this record's `last` ruler, reigned for
about thirty years *after* 1431. The dynasty was not extinguished, the country
was not annexed, and Cambodia continued as a kingdom into the twentieth century.
What ended in 1431 was the capital and the six-century programme of building and
irrigation that the capital was.

## What was carried off

The Ayutthayan army took the brahmans.

That is not a figure of speech. Ritual specialists, the court's Sanskritic
apparatus, the consecration rites, the palace vocabulary, the legal formulae —
the working parts of Angkorian kingship went to the Chao Phraya, and Siam
governed with them until the nineteenth century. The Bangkok dynasty was still
using them after that.

It is the only case on this site of a polity acquiring an entire court culture
by capturing the city that had it. The Ayutthaya record carries the same event
as its first turning point, from the other end, and its `legitimation` field is
coded `divine-sanction` for an idiom it took by force from the record that ends
here.

## Why the reach field is empty

`reach_km2` on this record is null.

Angkor at its height held the Cambodian plain, the Khorat plateau, much of the
lower Mekong and a large part of what is now Thailand, and any reasonable figure
would put it among the larger polities on this site. There is no figure here
because the tabulations this site draws reach figures from do not publish one
for it that has been opened and checked, and hard rule 2 forbids producing one.

The correct reading of a null in that column is the one the Mali record's last
chapter gives: fifty-three records here carry a reach figure, the figures come
from tabulations of large empires, and a record without one is not below the
records with one. It is outside the exercise.
