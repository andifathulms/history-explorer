This site's Maritime Asia region says in its own blurb that its polities lived
on controlling straits instead of territory. That is an accurate description of
Srivijaya, Melaka, Aceh and Ternate, and it is the reason Angkor could not go
there.

Angkor is a rice state on an inland plain. It is the largest polity in either
Southeast Asian region on this site, it built at a scale nothing in the islands
approached, and for most of its six centuries the sea route that made Srivijaya
rich was something that happened to other people. Pagan on the Irrawaddy, Dai
Viet on the Red River and Ayutthaya on the Chao Phraya are the same kind of
object. The islands and the peninsula were not one world with a size difference;
they were two economies.

So this record arrives with a new region around it. Mainland Southeast Asia has
five records, one internal edge, and `thread: false` — which is deliberate, and
which the region's comment in `regions.yaml` explains: the project's rule is
that a region and its thread are never added in the same pass, and the rule is
being followed here even though the edge is already written and the build would
accept the flag today.

## What the archaeology changed

Coedès, whose 1968 volume this site uses for the chronology and the
inscriptions, called these polities Indianized states, and the framework is in
the title of his book: temple, script, Sanskrit, kingship and law arriving from
the subcontinent and being received.

Higham's excavation of the pre-Angkorian Mekong is the main reason that reading
no longer stands on its own. There were substantial, stratified, wealthy
settlements on this ground for a thousand years before any Sanskrit inscription,
with their own bronze industry, their own trade and their own hierarchies. What
arrived from India was a vocabulary in which existing power could describe
itself, adopted by people who already had the power.

This site cites both works and takes the older framework as a position rather
than as background, which is a thing worth doing explicitly on the first page of
a new region rather than leaving in a source note.
