Ayutthaya took Angkor in 1431 and the Khmer court moved south, toward what
became Phnom Penh, and did not come back.

And yet "conquest" is not quite the right word, which is worth saying plainly.
Ponhea Yat, the last king to rule from Angkor, reigned for about thirty years
*after* 1431. The dynasty was not extinguished, the country was not annexed,
and Cambodia continued as a kingdom into the twentieth century. What ended in
1431 was the capital, and the six-century programme of building and irrigation
that the capital was.

## What was carried off

The Ayutthayan army took the brahmans.

That is not a figure of speech. Ritual specialists, the court's Sanskritic
apparatus, the consecration rites, the palace vocabulary, the legal formulae —
the working parts of Angkorian kingship went to the Chao Phraya, and Siam
governed with them until the nineteenth century. The Bangkok dynasty was still
using them after that.

It is a very rare thing for a state to acquire an entire court culture by
capturing the city that had it. Ayutthaya afterwards asserted its own kingship
in an idiom it had taken by force from the place it had just destroyed.
