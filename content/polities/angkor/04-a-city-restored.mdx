Rajendravarman came to power around 944 through a claim built on two
lines at once - nephew to Jayavarman IV through his father, and
grandson of Yasovarman I's own sister through his mother - a pedigree
strong enough to override the more direct but still very young heirs
of Jayavarman IV's own house. His first act as king was to reverse
his predecessors' relocation outright: he brought the Devaraja back
to Yasodharapura and rebuilt the palace there, an inscription
comparing the work to Kusha's own restoration of Ayodhya in the
Ramayana - a return the court's own poets framed as putting things
back the way they belonged.

## A raid on Champa's own goddess

Rajendravarman's reign is otherwise known to history mostly through
what his own inscriptions boast of rather than narrate in detail: his
brilliance, one text says, "burned the enemy kingdoms beginning with
Champa." Around 950 he sent an army into Cham territory that reached
the temple of Po Nagar at Nha Trang and carried off its gold cult
statue - a raid remembered on the Cham side of the same conflict as a
genuine desecration, and on the Khmer side as proof of a king who
could reach as far as his predecessors ever had.

## A temple pink stone made famous

Rajendravarman died in 968, and near the very end of his reign a
grandson of Harshavarman I, serving as a court teacher, founded a
temple that would outlast the memory of nearly everyone connected to
it: Banteay Srei, built of a fine-grained pink sandstone that let its
carvers work detail no earlier Khmer monument had attempted.
Rajendravarman's own son, Jayavarman V, inherited the throne as a
small child and did not finish his own education until 974; his long
but thinly documented reign built a new royal residence and held the
kingdom together without the wars or usurpations that had marked
three of the previous four successions - a period, by the standard of
what came immediately before and after it, of simple continuity.
