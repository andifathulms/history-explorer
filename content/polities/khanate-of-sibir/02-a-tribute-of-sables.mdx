The khanate's whole economy can be given in a sentence out of the Russian
archives. In 1555 the khan Yadigar sent Moscow seven hundred sable skins, was
told it was not enough, and the next year sent a thousand sables, a hundred more
for tolls, and sixty-nine in place of squirrel.

## Yasak

The tax was called yasak and it was paid in fur. The khan's Tatars lived on the
open river valleys of the Tobol, the Tura and the Irtysh; the people who trapped
the animals were Ostyaks and Voguls — Khanty and Mansi — in the forest and the
bogs north and west of them, under their own princes, who obeyed the khan and
paid him.

It was a system of tributary chieftainships rather than an administration, and it
had two properties that decided the khanate's history. It produced, from almost
no population, a commodity of enormous value in Europe and Central Asia. And it
could be taken over intact by anyone who beat the khan, because the forest princes
paid whoever was collecting.

## The reach

Howorth reckons the Siberian khan was obeyed by the Voguls, the Ostyaks and the
other Ugrian peoples of the Urals, and the evidence for it is that when Moscow
sent an army against them in 1499 their overlord resented it: Abalak, Mamuq's
brother, marched on Kazan in reply.

That is the pattern of the khanate's outward action for a century. It was
entangled in Kazan, where its princes campaigned repeatedly; it intermarried with
the Nogai murzas across the steppe to the south; and it faced west across the
Urals, where its tributaries touched the Russian fur frontier coming the other
way.

## The roads

There were several. From the Vychegda a route ran by the Vishera to the Kama,
then by the Lozva, the Tavda and the Tobol. From the Vym another went by the
Izhma to the Pechora, and from the Pechora three separate ways over to the Ob —
by the Shchugor and the Sygva, by the Ilych and the Sosva, or by the Usa and the
Sob. The Sygva and Sosva roads were the oldest and most used.

Merchants had been going over them for a long time before any army did. The
khanate sat at the far end of them, which is why it was rich, and why it was
found.
