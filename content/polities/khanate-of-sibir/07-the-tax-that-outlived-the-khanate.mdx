Kuchum's family entered Moscow in a triumphal procession in January 1599. Boris
Godunov, who badly needed a victory, made a feast of it and rewarded the Cossacks
who brought them in.

## What happened to the dynasty

It was absorbed rather than destroyed.

The captives came into Moscow in sledges — the women in pelisses of velvet, satin
and cloth of gold with gold and silver embroidery, the princes in long red robes
trimmed with rare furs, escorted by soldiery dressed in sable, through streets
full of people. They were lodged with nobles and great merchants and given
moderate pensions, and the khan's wives and daughters were allowed to go and live
at Kasimov and Bezhetsk.

Mahmetkul, who had commanded the khanate's armies against Yermak, entered the
Russian army; he fought the Swedes in 1590, and in 1598 he rode out with Tsar
Boris to Serpukhov to face the Crimean Tatars. Abulkhair, Kuchum's eldest son,
was baptised in 1591 and renamed Andrew. Uraz Muhammad, the Kazakh prince taken
at the feast in 1588, was made khan of Kasimov — a Tatar appanage inside Russia,
which is what the Chinggisid dignity had become.

Not all of them submitted. Kuchum's son Ali gathered his brothers and what
Siberian Tatars still regarded them as their lawful rulers, lived in the steppe
without a fixed home and raided the Ishim, the Irtysh and the Tobol as far as the
Yaik and the neighbourhood of Ufa. He opened negotiations in 1600 and would not
come in person; the Russian messengers rode to the Abuga and could not find him.

## What happened to the system

Nothing. The Russians took the yasak.

The forest peoples who had paid the khan a tribute of sable went on paying the
same tribute under the same name to a Russian voivode, and it financed the crossing
of the rest of the continent. Tyumen in 1586 and Tobolsk in 1587 were followed by
Tara in 1594, and then by a chain of stockaded towns that reached the Yenisei
within thirty years, the Lena within fifty and the Pacific within sixty. Each was
built where a river route made it possible to collect furs and defend the
collection, which is precisely the logic the khanate had run on.

The difference was scale. Kuchum had taxed the Ugrians of one river system. The
state that replaced him applied the same instrument from the Urals to the sea of
Okhotsk.

## The name

The khanate's capital was a wooden enclosure with room for one household and its
dependents, and it was abandoned in 1588 and never rebuilt. Its name had already
escaped. The Permians called the place Sibir, the Russians took the word from
them, and it spread outward from a vanished village on the Irtysh to cover five
million square miles and, in most languages on earth, to mean distance and exile.
