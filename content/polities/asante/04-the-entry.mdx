On 22 May 1817 an African Company mission from Cape Coast Castle walked into
Kumasi, and its twenty-six-year-old secretary wrote down what happened with a
precision nobody else managed.

They entered at two in the afternoon, passing under a dead sheep wrapped in red
silk and slung between two poles. Upwards of five thousand people met them, most
of them warriors, with what Bowdich calls awful bursts of martial music,
*discordant only in its mixture* — horns, drums, rattles and gong-gongs going at
once, deliberately, to subdue the visitors by first impression. The smoke of
continuous musket fire closed the view down to the foreground.

They were halted while the captains performed a war dance inside a ring of their
own men, waving English, Dutch and Danish flags together, firing blunderbusses so
close that the flags occasionally caught fire.

## What a captain wore

Bowdich itemised it, and the itemisation is why the passage is worth having.

A war cap with gilded ram's horns projecting in front and the sides extended out
of all proportion with eagle feathers, fastened under the chin with bands of
cowries. A red cloth vest covered in gold and silver amulets and embroidered
cases of every colour that flapped as the man moved, mixed with small brass
bells, horns and tails of animals, shells and knives. Leopards' tails hanging
down the back over a small bow. Loose cotton trousers, and boots of dull red
leather reaching half-way up the thigh, chained to the waist-belt and hung with
bells, horses' tails and strings of amulets. A quiver of poisoned arrows at the
right wrist. A long iron chain held between the teeth with a scrap of Arabic
writing fixed to the end of it.

Bowdich's verdict on the assembled effect is *a figure scarcely human*, which is
the reaction the dress was designed to produce.

## The crowd

The exhibition lasted about half an hour, and then they were let through — the
progress *as gradual as if it had taken place in Cheapside*, which is a young
Englishman reaching for the only comparison he has.

The side streets were crammed. On the left the ground rose, so that rows of heads
went up one above another. The open porches of the houses, *like the fronts of
stages in small theatres*, were full of women and children waiting to see white
men for the first time, and their voices were entirely drowned in the firing.

Then, at the palace, the caboceers passing with their retinues: bands of horns
and flutes trained to play in concert, and enormous umbrellas made to rise and
fall from the jerking of their bearers, which moved enough air to be a relief
under a burning sun.

## And what came next

Bowdich then describes something he would not soften, and it is set down in the
same register as the umbrellas.

While they waited to be summoned, a man was paraded past them for some minutes.
He was being tormented before sacrifice. His hands were tied behind him; a knife
had been passed through his cheeks and his lips noosed on it in a figure of
eight; one ear had been cut off and was carried in front of him and the other
hung by a piece of skin; there were gashes in his back and a knife thrust under
each shoulder blade. He was led by a cord through his nose, by men in tall caps
of shaggy black skin, with drums beating ahead of him.

## Reading both

Bowdich was there to negotiate on behalf of a trading company, and his account
was published to argue that Asante was a serious power worth treating with rather
than a nuisance to be crushed. That is a real bias and it runs the opposite way
from the usual one.

What the book does, and what makes it valuable a century later, is refuse to
choose. The same afternoon contains a court of extraordinary sophistication —
concert bands, engineered umbrellas, a diplomatic reception staged with complete
control — and a man being cut to pieces in the street as part of it.

Both are the same state. An account that keeps only the first is a brochure and
one that keeps only the second is a justification for what Britain did afterwards,
and Bowdich, who had every commercial reason to write the first, wrote both.
