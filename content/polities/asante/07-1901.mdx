Britain and Asante fought five wars between 1824 and 1900.

Asante won the first outright: at Nsamankow in 1824 an Asante army destroyed a
British force and killed the governor of the Gold Coast, Sir Charles MacCarthy,
whose skull was taken to Kumasi and kept as a royal object. It is one of very
few occasions in the nineteenth century on which an African state beat a
European force in a pitched engagement, and the only one on these pages besides
Ethiopia's Adwa seventy-two years later.

The last is the War of the Golden Stool in 1900, and the country was annexed
the following year.

## What happened in between

1874 is the hinge. Wolseley's expedition reached Kumasi, found it evacuated,
looted the palace and burned the city, and imposed an indemnity larger than the
state could pay. The previous chapter is the twenty years that followed — the
secessions, and the argument in council between the party that wanted
accommodation with Britain and the party that wanted the empire reassembled by
force.

Prempeh I was arrested in 1896 and deported to the Seychelles with his mother
and much of the court. He was allowed home in 1924 as a private person and
recognised as head of the Kumasi division in 1926; his successor was recognised
as Asantehene in 1935, and the office exists.

## The stool

In 1900 the governor, Frederick Hodgson, came to Kumasi, demanded the Golden
Stool, and announced that he intended to sit on it.

The country rose. The rising is remembered under the leadership of Yaa
Asantewaa, the queen mother of Ejisu — which is not incidental, since the
office she held was one of the two that controlled the Asante succession. The
British were besieged in the Kumasi fort for months and relieved; the rising
was put down; Yaa Asantewaa was deported to the Seychelles and died there.

The stool was not found. It had been hidden in the forest and stayed hidden for
twenty years. In 1920 African road-builders came across it by accident and
stripped the gold ornaments; they were tried by the Asante and would have been
executed had the colonial administration not commuted it to exile.

It exists. It is not on display.
