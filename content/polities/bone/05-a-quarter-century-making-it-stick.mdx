Sombaopu's fall did not simply hand Bone a kingdom; it created a
partnership that ran South Sulawesi for the following quarter-century.
Arung Palakka took the internal affairs of the peninsula, ruling as
effective overlord of every state on it, while the Dutch company held
the coastal fort - renamed Fort Rotterdam - and controlled what left
by sea.
Gowa itself was not abolished. Its sultans kept their throne,
their court, and their regalia. They simply lost the one thing that
had made those things worth having: anyone who came when the kingdom
called.

## Making sure no rival remained

Arung Palakka spent the rest of his reign consolidating the position
Sombaopu had won him, and he was not gentle about it. He had one of
his own staunchest supporters assassinated on mere suspicion of
standing too well with the Dutch, and he used persuasion, intimidation
and force in whatever combination a given rival required, until no
ruler anywhere in South Sulawesi would contest him. Less than a year
before his death, the assembled armies of the entire peninsula turned
out to demonstrate their unity behind him - the kind of display, the
source notes, that a ruler arranges specifically so his intended heir
can see it happen.

## A succession that held

Arung Palakka died in 1696.
His chosen successor, La Patau, took the
throne unchallenged - the clearest evidence available that the
authority Arung Palakka had spent nearly thirty years building was no
longer personal to him alone. A kingdom that had been forced into a
new religion by conquest in 1611 had, within a single lifetime,
become the power every other state on the peninsula had to answer to.
