---
title: A kingdom run by a baby
drafted_from: macknight-paeni-hadrawi-2020

Bone's third ruler took the throne as an infant. Named Kerrampélua', a
description of his own hair at birth rather than a title he chose, the
child was installed as ArumPone while still unable to exercise any of
the responsibilities the office actually demanded. The chronicle
treats this as a real practical problem rather than a formality to
skim past, describing in some detail the arrangements by which the
kingdom's affairs were managed on the infant ruler's behalf - enough
detail that the account reads less like legend than like a court
working out, in real time, how to keep governing around a throne its
own occupant could not yet fill.

## A regency careful enough to need permission

The seriousness with which Bone treated the young king's own formal
authority shows up in an unlikely place: his father, the second ruler
La Ummasa', is recorded as needing to seek permission for something as
ordinary as travel - a striking detail, given that a mere baby held
the actual throne. Whatever informal power La Ummasa' continued to
exercise in practice, the fiction of the child's own sovereignty was
maintained carefully enough that even a parent's routine movements ran
through it.

## An expanding kingdom, counted in people rather than land

Kerrampélua's own reign, once he grew into it, became the period in
which Bone extended its authority across the surrounding coastal plain
and into the neighboring hills. The chronicle records this expansion
as lists of place names, but the underlying concern was manpower
rather than territory: conquered settlements needed people
transplanted into them to hold what had been won, and at least one
earlier conquest had to be retaken a second time after slipping back
out of Bone's control. Even a kingdom whose own third ruler had begun
life as an infant on the throne grew, within that single long reign,
into a genuine regional power - built one resettled village at a time
rather than by a single decisive campaign.
