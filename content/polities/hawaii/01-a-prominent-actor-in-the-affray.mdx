Kamehameha's own name first enters the written record through one of
the most famous deaths in the Pacific. When Captain Cook's men tried
to bring the aged Hawaii king Kalaniopuu aboard the *Resolution* at
Kealakekua Bay in February 1779, and then opened fire on the canoes
gathering in the bay, the affray that followed ended with Cook himself
killed on the beach. Fornander, weighing the conflicting testimony
available to him, judges that Kalaniopuu was at most a frightened
bystander to his own attempted abduction - and that in the absence of
his heir Kiwalao, the next-ranking chief of the blood royal present
was a young Kamehameha, who by a contemporary trader's own account
had "encouraged the natives to the murder of Captain Cook."

## Three untruths told to an English captain

Kamehameha's own later conduct suggests he had something to hide. Years
afterward, he told a different British captain, Douglas, that
Kalaniopuu had died of poison administered by chiefs who blamed him
for Cook's death - a story Fornander shows to be false on every point:
Kalaniopuu did not die for three more years, died of natural causes,
and no chiefs revolted against him over the affair at all. Fornander's
own reading is that Kamehameha, aware the truth might bring
retribution down on himself, invented the story to draw suspicion away
from his own conduct that day - a young chief's first recorded
brush with the foreign power that would later crown him.

## Bones returned, and a fleet that sailed on unaware

The ships did not linger. On 21 February, what remained of Cook's own
body was returned under a flag of truce and committed to the sea with
military honors; the next day the *Resolution* and *Discovery* left
Kealakekua Bay for the last time, the shore lined with natives waving
what one officer called "every mark of affection and good-will." Sailing
on toward Oahu, the ships found the men of that island already gone to
fight on Molokai - word of Cook's death, and of the young chief who
had played some part in it, had outrun the ships themselves across the
open channel.
