Liliuokalani's own reign, barely two years old, had already been
marked by a running contest with the legislature over how much power
the crown could reclaim from the constitution that bound it. On the
14th of January, 1893, with a new constitution already drafted in
private - one that would strip away the legislature's own checks on
the throne and restrict the vote to Hawaiian subjects alone - she
moved to proclaim it at the palace immediately after that day's
prorogation ceremony, with government troops drawn up and a crowd of
supporters gathered around her.

Her own cabinet refused to sign the document at the decisive moment,
and turned instead to leading citizens for support against her. Faced
with their refusal, the queen partially yielded, telling the assembled
crowd that she would postpone, with deep regret, the constitution's
proclamation for a few days. It was not enough to undo what the
attempt itself had already set moving.

## Yielded to avoid bloodshed

A Committee of Safety, meeting informally among the colony's
residents, moved immediately to organize a provisional government and
recall the volunteer military companies disbanded three years before.
A mass meeting on the 16th ratified the committee's actions; the same
morning, the queen published a declaration that any future changes to
the constitution would come only by the methods the constitution
itself provided - a concession that arrived too late to matter. That
evening, the American warship Boston, newly arrived in port, landed a
force it described as protection for American lives and property.

On the 17th the Committee of Safety completed its provisional
government, took possession of the government building, and
proclaimed the monarchy abolished. Liliuokalani did not resist by
force, and surrendered her authority the same day under a protest she
addressed to the new government itself, trusting that the matter
would be referred to Washington for arbitration.
