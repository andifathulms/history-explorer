The house Kamehameha founded ruled Hawaii without interruption for
more than half a century after him - through Kamehameha II's own brief
reign and death abroad, through Kamehameha III's long tenure, which
gave the kingdom its first written constitution in 1840, and through
the shorter reigns of Kamehameha IV and V. That succession ended
abruptly. Kamehameha V died suddenly of dropsy on the 11th of
December, 1872, not yet forty-three and only nine years into his
reign, without ever naming an heir. With him, the line that had built
the kingdom ended outright.

## An elective throne

The legislature, summoned to choose a successor, elected Prince
William Lunalilo the following January - the highest-ranking surviving
chief by birth, and a popular one, though his own reign proved brief.
When Lunalilo died in turn without naming an heir either, the choice
returned to the legislature a second time, and in February 1874
Colonel David Kalakaua was elected king. What had been, for two
generations, a hereditary throne secured by conquest had become an
office the legislature itself now filled by vote - a change in the
kingdom's own foundations that neither Kamehameha nor his sons had
ever intended, and one that left the monarchy's legitimacy resting on
a legislature's confidence rather than a father's succession from now
on. Kalakaua reigned until his own death in San Francisco on the 20th
of January, 1891, when the throne passed to his sister, Liliuokalani -
the kingdom's first reigning queen, and its last independent monarch.
