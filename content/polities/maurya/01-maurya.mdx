Chandragupta took Magadha around 322 BC in the disorder following Alexander's
withdrawal, and his grandson Ashoka inherited an empire covering most of the
subcontinent. After a campaign against Kalinga that he describes as having killed
and deported hundreds of thousands, Ashoka renounced conquest.

The renunciation is not a later legend. It is inscribed, in Ashoka's own voice,
on rocks and pillars across the empire in Prakrit, Greek and Aramaic, declaring
remorse, announcing a policy of *dhamma* — moral governance, non-violence,
religious tolerance — and instructing officials accordingly. They are the oldest
deciphered inscriptions in India and among the very few places in the ancient
world where a ruler explains himself in the first person and the explanation is
an apology.

Ashoka also sent Buddhist missions outward, to Sri Lanka and the Hellenistic
kingdoms of the west, which is what the religion-carried count on this page
records: propagation beyond the polity's own boundary, described by a source,
which is what the coding rules ask for.

The empire declined after him and the last Maurya was killed by his own
commander-in-chief around 185 BC — `internal usurpation`, with no successor
polity named because what followed was not one.
