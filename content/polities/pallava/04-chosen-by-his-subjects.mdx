When King Paramesvaravarman II died without an heir the main line
could agree on, the Pallava throne did not pass by the usual claim of
birth. It passed, according to the dynasty's own record of the event,
by something closer to a vote.

## A twelve-year-old chosen from a collateral branch

The choice fell on a boy from a collateral line descended from an
earlier Pallava prince, still, by the Vaikuntha Perumal temple's own
inscribed record, only twelve years old at his coronation. The temple
itself - built at Kanchipuram under the new king's own patronage and
originally named Parameshvara-Vinnagaram after him - carries a
sculpted narrative of Pallava dynastic history along its inner
walls, including panels the temple's own inscriptions identify with
the succession crisis itself: the death of the old king, the citizens
of Kanchipuram and the ministers deliberating, and the coronation of
the boy under the throne-name Nandivarman. A later charter, the
Kasakkudi plates issued in Nandivarman's own twenty-second regnal
year, states plainly that he "was chosen by his subjects" - language
distinctive enough that later historians have read it as describing
something like a general election to the throne rather than a
straightforward hereditary succession.

## A king besieged in his own fortress

The choice was contested from the start. Rival Tamil powers backed a
different claimant, Chitramaya, who had his own descent claim through
the old king, and Nandivarman spent much of his reign fighting a long
war against the combined southern powers to hold what the succession
had given him. At one point he was besieged in the fortress town of
Nandipura, near modern Kumbhakonam, by his own enemies, and was
relieved only through the campaigns of his general Udayachandra,
whose exploits fill much of a separate charter, the Udayendiram
plates, issued in his own honor. A Western Chalukya invasion early in
the reign captured Kanchi itself at least once, adding a northern
front to the southern war Nandivarman was already fighting for his
own throne.

## An alliance that helped overthrow an empire

Nandivarman's reign also produced one of the more consequential
alliances in Deccan politics: a marriage tie with the rising
Rashtrakuta chief Dantidurga, himself descended from a Chalukya
princess, whom the Pallava-led southern coalition helped establish
against his own Chalukya overlords after inflicting a crushing defeat
on the Chalukya king Kirtivarman II at the Battle of Venbai - a defeat
severe enough that the Chalukya power never recovered from it, and
that let Dantidurga's Rashtrakuta line replace the Chalukyas as the
dominant power of the western Deccan within a generation. A poet of
the age, Tirumangai Alvar, referred to Dantidurga by his title
Vairamegha and to the honor Nandivarman accorded him - a twelve-
year-old's contested accession, two generations earlier, ending in a
Pallava king helping to topple an empire.
