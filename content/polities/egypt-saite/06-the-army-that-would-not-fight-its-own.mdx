The mercenary establishment that had made this dynasty possible from
Psamtik I's own first campaign eventually broke it. Apries, ruling by the
570s, kept a standing force of Greek soldiers close to the throne in
numbers and favour that native Egyptian troops had come to resent —
resent enough that when a Libyan expedition under Egyptian command failed
badly, the surviving soldiers blamed the king for having sent them
under-supported while keeping his foreign guard back in comfort at home.

## A mutiny that chose its own general

Apries sent a trusted officer, Amasis, to put the mutineers down. Amasis
instead let himself be acclaimed king by the men he had been sent to
suppress — not staging a coup so much as recognising an army already in
open revolt and stepping in front of it. Apries, left with his mercenary
guard and whatever native troops still held to him, marched against his own
former subjects and lost. He held on for several more years as a
diminished rival monarch, apparently with Babylonian backing at one point,
before dying in a final confrontation Amasis is reported to have granted
him a royal burial after.

## What the mutiny actually exposed

The tension the mutiny brought into the open had been present since
Psamtik I first hired Ionian and Carian bronze men against his own Delta
rivals: an army split between soldiers who owed the dynasty their personal
loyalty because it paid and equipped them directly, and soldiers who
belonged to the country the dynasty ruled and increasingly felt like the
lesser-favoured half of its own defence. Amasis did not resolve that split
so much as become its beneficiary — a native commander raised up
specifically because native troops would follow him against a king who
had stopped trusting them with anything that mattered.

He went on to reign forty-four years, the longest and by most accounts the
most stable of the dynasty, presiding over expanded trade with Greek
merchants at Naucratis even as the resentment his own rise had ridden
never fully vanished from how Egyptian and mercenary forces related to one
another for the rest of the century.
