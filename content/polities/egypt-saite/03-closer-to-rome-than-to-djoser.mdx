Once the throne was secure, this dynasty's art, religion and administration
turned backward in a way no earlier period had attempted so deliberately.
Sculptors began copying Old Kingdom statue types feature for feature; tomb
texts revived phrases and formulas out of Pyramid Age funerary literature
that had gone unused for two thousand years; officials adopted archaic
titles their own immediate predecessors had not held. The direction of the
copying is unambiguous and heavily one-sided: this period reaching back
toward the Old Kingdom, not the reverse.

## A gap longer than it looks

The scale of that reach is easy to understate. From this dynasty's own
throne, the Old Kingdom lay roughly two thousand years in the past — a
greater distance in time than separates the present day from the founding
of imperial Rome. Nobody at Sais had a living memory, or a grandparent's
living memory, or any continuous institutional thread back to the world
being copied. What the copying drew on was whatever the monuments
themselves, still standing and still legible, could be made to yield: an
antiquity being reconstructed from ruins rather than handed down.

## Why reach that far back at all

A dynasty founded within living memory of Assyrian vassalage, on the
strength of hired foreign mercenaries and an adoption ceremony rather than
inherited right, had an unusually acute need for legitimacy that recent
history could not supply. The Kushite kings just displaced from Thebes had
made their own claim on Egyptian tradition by taking the full pharaonic
titulary; this dynasty answered by reaching past every intervening period
— past the Kushites, past the fragmented kinglets before them, past the
New and Middle Kingdoms both — to the one era universally agreed to be
Egyptian kingship at its most authentically itself.

Whether the resulting art and religion should be judged as a hollow revival
or a genuine act of cultural recovery is a matter later scholarship has
argued both ways. What is not in dispute is the scale of the ambition: a
dynasty with the shallowest claim to legitimacy of almost any so far in
this valley's recorded history chose to anchor itself in the deepest past
still available to be read.
