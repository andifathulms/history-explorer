Beating the rival Delta princes solved half of Psamtik's problem. The other
half was Thebes, eight hundred kilometres upriver, which had spent the past
century as the seat of the God's Wife of Amun — a chief priestess of enormous
landed wealth and independent standing, currently held by a woman installed
under the Kushite dynasty Assyria had just driven out of Egypt. Whoever held
that office effectively held Upper Egypt's religious loyalty, regardless of
what any Delta king claimed.

## An adoption, not a siege

In his ninth year, Psamtik sent a naval flotilla up the Nile carrying his own
daughter, a young woman named Nitocris, with instructions that she be
adopted as the sitting God's Wife's designated successor. The ceremony that
followed is recorded on a stela commissioned for the occasion: the flotilla
reached Thebes after a voyage of some two weeks, the population turned out
to receive the princess, and the reigning God's Wife — along with her own
designated heir, a woman from the Kushite royal line — formally adopted
Nitocris and transferred their properties and titles to her.

No battle is recorded. The transfer had the consent, or at least the
acquiescence, of the temple's own senior clergy and the city's civil
administration, whose support the ceremony's own stela takes care to
record by name. What made the adoption possible rather than merely
proposed was the fleet that delivered the princess — a demonstration of
force present at every stage without being used at any of them.

## What was actually being settled

Reading the ceremony purely as an act of piety misses what it accomplished.
The Kushite dynasty had held Egypt's south through exactly this office for
generations; installing a Saite princess in the same seat, adopted by the
same clergy who had served Kushite God's Wives before her, transferred
Thebes' loyalty without requiring anyone in Thebes to admit that their own
king had lost anything. Everyone involved could describe the same event as
either a conquest or a continuity, and the fact that both descriptions were
available at once is precisely what let the transfer happen without a war.
Egypt was reunified in name nine years into Psamtik's reign, and almost
none of that reunification was won by force of arms — a naval procession
and a ceremony had done what a Delta war had not needed to.
