title: After the fortresses
drafted_from: daftary-2007

Rukn al-Din Khurshah surrendered to Hulegu in 1256 and was sent east to the
great khan; he was killed on the road. Alamut was taken that December and the
library burned. Möngke ordered the Persian Nizaris killed, and the massacres
that followed are the reason most accounts of this polity stop in 1256.

They should not. Girdkuh held out until 1270. The Syrian castles fell not to
the Mongols but to Baybars, who reduced them between about 1270 and 1273 and
then did with them what the Mamluk sultanate did with everything — kept the
garrisons in service. A son of the last imam is reported to have held Alamut
briefly again in 1275.

What actually survived was not territory. The community went underground in the
Iranian highlands, in Afghanistan, in Sind and Gujarat, and the imamate
continued in concealment — the line was maintained, disputed at points, and its
existence was not public. For long stretches the Nizaris of Iran were taken for
Sufis and the Nizaris of India were known by other names entirely, which is how
a community survives when being identified is fatal.

The line came back into the open in the nineteenth century, when its imam left
Iran after a failed rising, was given the title Aga Khan, and settled
eventually in India — where the largest body of his followers, by then, had
been for centuries. The Isma'ili community that descends from Alamut is now
several million people across some two dozen countries.

Set that beside the two other Isma'ili polities on this site and the pattern is
consistent. The Fatimid state fell in 1171 and its mission outlasted it: the
Tayyibi line reconstituted in Yemen under Arwa on this site's Sulayhid page
went to Gujarat and is still there. The Qarmatian state went and left nothing,
because what it had was a prediction rather than an institution. This one lost
every fortress it held and kept the thing the fortresses had been protecting.

The record on this page ends in 1256 to 1273 because that is when the polity
ended, and a polity is what this site measures. It is worth saying plainly that
the measurement stops well before the subject does.
