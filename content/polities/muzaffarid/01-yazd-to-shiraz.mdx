---
title: An official who kept going
drafted_from: chi-6
phase: formation
---

The Muzaffarids began as Ilkhanid officials. The family held Yazd and its
district as a governorship, and Mubariz al-Din Muhammad inherited that position
as a boy in 1314 — which is why the dynasty's conventional start date sits
twenty-one years inside a state it was still serving.

When the Ilkhanate stopped in 1335 he did what the Jalayir did in Iraq: he kept
what he was holding and took more. Kerman in 1340. Then Fars, which meant
fighting the Injuids — another Ilkhanid family, ruling Shiraz, whose last amir
Abu Ishaq was a cultivated and unserious man remembered mainly because Hafez
wrote about his court with affection afterwards. Mubariz al-Din besieged Shiraz,
took it in 1353, and had Abu Ishaq executed in 1357. Isfahan followed, and in
1358 he took Tabriz from the Jalayirids, briefly, before losing it again.

The parallel with the previous page is close enough to be worth stating as a
pattern rather than a coincidence. Two families of Ilkhanid provincial officers,
neither Chinggisid, neither able to claim the throne, both surviving the
collapse by holding the settled revenue of a region and continuing to run the
administration that had collected it. This site codes both `tribal-levy` and
`land-grant` for the army and `land-tax` and `trade-toll` for the money, from
the same source and the same chapter, because they are two instances of one
thing: the Ilkhanate did not disappear in 1335 so much as decompose into its
provinces, each of which kept working.

The difference is what they did with it, and the difference is one man's
character.
