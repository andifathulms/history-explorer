title: Shah Shuja
drafted_from: chi-6

Shah Shuja ruled from 1358 to 1384 and is the reason the Muzaffarids are more
than a footnote between the Ilkhanate and Timur. He held Fars, Kerman and
Isfahan, took Tabriz from the Jalayirids in 1359 and could not hold it, fought
his own brother Shah Mahmud for twenty years over Isfahan, and made Shiraz the
literary capital of the Persian-speaking world.

He was himself a poet, in Persian and Arabic, and unusually for a royal
versifier the tradition takes him seriously. He read and argued about poetry; the
anecdote literature has him disputing points with Hafez, which is the sort of
story that accretes and is not evidence, but it accreted around him rather than
around somebody else.

The politics were relentless and mostly inconclusive. Brothers, nephews and
cousins held Isfahan, Kerman, Yazd and Shiraz as their own, in the appanage
arrangement their father had set up, and made and broke alliances against
whichever of them was strongest. Shah Shuja spent most of his reign campaigning
against relatives, which is the ordinary condition of a polity coded `appanage`
in this corpus and is why the coding exists.

He died in 1384 and his will is the most consequential document the dynasty
produced: he divided the territory formally among his relatives and — knowing
what was coming out of the east — commended them all to Timur's protection.

This is the arrangement Timur arrived into. A country divided among five or six
princes of one family, each with a city, each with an army, each with a claim on
the others, and all of them nominally the protégés of the man about to destroy
them.
