---
title: Five princes and a will
drafted_from: chi-6
phase: contraction
---

Shah Shuja's death in 1384 turned the appanage arrangement from a nuisance into
the whole of the dynasty's politics. His son Zayn al-Abidin held Shiraz; his
brother's sons held Isfahan and Kerman and Yazd; Shah Yahya and Shah Mansur and
Sultan Ahmad manoeuvred against each other, and the cities changed hands
repeatedly among cousins.

What made it lethal rather than merely undignified was that it did not stop
when Timur arrived in 1387, or when he came back. None of the cousins could
stop manoeuvring, because each of them held a city as his own property and the
man next door was the immediate threat; Timur was a periodic weather event and
a cousin was a permanent one. So the house spent the years between the two
Timurid campaigns doing to each other what he would shortly do to all of them.

Shah Mansur, the most capable of them, was also the most thorough. He took his
own cousin Zayn al-Abidin prisoner when the man came to him for refuge, blinded
him and blinded Shah Yahya's brother as well, took Shiraz in 1391, and had most
of Fars by 1392.

He was also the only one of them who fought.
