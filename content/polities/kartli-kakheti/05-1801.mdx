George XII's request was for something like an incorporation that preserved the
dynasty: Georgia inside the Russian empire, with a Bagrationi still on the throne
under Russian sovereignty. What he was negotiating for is disputed and what he
got is not.

He died in December 1800. Paul I issued a manifesto annexing the kingdom;
Alexander I confirmed it in September 1801 in terms that abolished the monarchy
outright. The Bagrationi were deported to Russia. The kingdom's institutions
were dissolved and replaced with Russian provincial administration, and in 1811
the autocephaly of the Georgian church — held since the fifth century, on this
site's Caucasian Iberia record — was abolished and the church subordinated to
the Russian Synod.

That is the end of Georgian statehood until 1918, and it is the end of this
site's Caucasus region as a place with its own polities. Imereti was annexed in
1810. The eastern Caucasus went to Russia by the treaties this corpus's Qajar
record carries — Gulistan in 1813 and Turkmanchay in 1828 — and Shirvan,
Derbend, Ganja and the country of every polity on the Caucasus pages went with
them.

This record's `ended.by` is empty because the Russian empire is not in this
corpus, and the site does not point a field at an id that does not exist. That
absence is worth naming rather than hiding, because it is the same absence at
the end of four Arabian records and the Qajar one: the corpus records polities
that ended, the states that ended them in the nineteenth century were European
empires, and those are outside its scope.

The Bagrationi held a throne somewhere in Georgia from 888 to 1801 — nine
hundred and thirteen years, which would be the longest dynastic tenure in this
corpus if the span model could carry a family across four polities. It cannot,
and this record is the last of the four.
