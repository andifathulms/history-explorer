---
title: Two of the three
drafted_from: suny-1994
phase: formation

This site's Kingdom of Georgia record ends in 1490 with a royal council formally
dividing the kingdom among three branches of the Bagrationi: Kartli in the
centre, Kakheti in the east, Imereti in the west.

What followed was two and a half centuries of the three kingdoms between the
Ottomans and the Safavids, who partitioned the country between them by treaty in
1555 and again in 1639 — Imereti to Istanbul, Kartli and Kakheti to Isfahan.
Georgian kings converted to Islam to hold their thrones and reconverted when the
opportunity came; Georgian slaves and soldiers turn up throughout this corpus, in
the Safavid ghulam corps that Abbas I built to break the Qizilbash and in the
Mamluk records as one of the frontiers Cairo bought from. Shah Abbas deported
tens of thousands of Kakhetians to Iran in the 1610s and settled Turkic tribes
in their place.

In 1762 Teimuraz II of Kartli died and his son Erekle II, who was already king
of Kakheti, inherited it. Two of the three were one kingdom again for the first
time in two hundred and seventy-two years.

This record therefore carries an edge from the Kingdom of Georgia — `absorbed
remnants of`, 1762 — and it is the second internal edge in this site's Caucasus
region and the one that joins the medieval half to the modern. The other is
Georgian forces taking Ani from the Shaddadids in 1199. Two edges among eight
polities, which is what this ground produced.

What Erekle inherited was a small kingdom in a ruined country, with a nobility
that had spent two centuries becoming more powerful than the crown, a population
reduced by deportation and raiding, and two Muslim empires on either side of it
that had each spent the period treating Georgia as a province they had mislaid.

He was fifty-two, had commanded troops for Nadir Shah in India, and had thirty-
six years left.
