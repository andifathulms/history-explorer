George XII's request was for something like an incorporation that preserved the
dynasty: Georgia inside the Russian empire, with a Bagrationi still on the throne
under Russian sovereignty. What he was negotiating for is disputed and what he
got is not.

He died in December 1800. Paul I issued a manifesto annexing the kingdom;
Alexander I confirmed it in September 1801 in terms that abolished the monarchy
outright. The Bagrationi were deported to Russia. The kingdom's institutions
were dissolved and replaced with Russian provincial administration.

Then in 1811 the autocephaly of the Georgian church was abolished and the church
was subordinated to the Russian Synod. That is the part worth stopping on. The
Georgian church had been self-governing since the fifth century, when Vakhtang
Gorgasali obtained or asserted its independence from Antioch. It had survived
the Sasanians abolishing the monarchy in 580, four centuries of presiding
princes, an Arab emirate in its own capital, the Seljuks, the Mongols, eight
visits from Timur, and two hundred years of partition between the Ottomans and
the Safavids. It did not survive a Christian empire, which is the only kind that
had any interest in it.

The rest went in the same twenty years. Imereti was annexed in 1810. The eastern
Caucasus passed to Russia by the treaties of Gulistan in 1813 and Turkmanchay in
1828, and Shirvan, Derbend, Ganja and the country of every dynasty in the
isthmus went with it.

The Bagrationi had held a throne somewhere in Georgia from 888 to 1801 — nine
hundred and thirteen years, across four kingdoms, ending with a deportation to
Russia. There was no Georgian state again until 1918.
