Ramiro I, who took the throne in 842, faced a threat none of his
predecessors had: a fleet of Norse raiders making its first recorded
landing on the Asturian coast. The visitors did not stay. What drove
them off, in the account that survives, was less the fear of Asturian
arms than the simple poverty of the country itself - a land offering
too little plunder to hold their interest when richer coasts lay
further south.

## Seville learns what Asturias already had

They found those richer coasts soon enough. Fifty-four Norman vessels
swept down on the shores of Lusitania, and after being driven off
around Lisbon they pressed on around the peninsula, raided the African
coast, sacked Cádiz, and finally rowed up the Guadalquivir to occupy
and plunder the undefended suburbs of Seville itself. Only the threat
of an intercepting fleet persuaded them to withdraw. The raid alarmed
Abd al-Rahman II badly enough that he built watchtowers, organized
coastal signal posts and constructed a proper battle fleet - a
response to a single season's raiding that Ramiro's own brief
encounter with the same raiders, two years before Seville ever saw
them, had not provoked from Asturias at all.

## Albelda, taken and held

Ordoño I, who succeeded his father in 850, inherited a kingdom still
feeling its way past that near-miss. His own reign's clearest
success came in 859, when his forces took the fortified town of
Albelda from its Muslim garrison and put the defenders to the sword -
a captured stronghold on the frontier with Pamplona, held rather than
raided and abandoned, in a period when most fighting on either side
still ended in withdrawal rather than lasting conquest.
