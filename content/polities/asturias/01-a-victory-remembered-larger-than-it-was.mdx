title: A victory remembered larger than it was

After the Umayyad army broke the Visigothic kingdom and killed King
Roderic, organized Christian resistance survived, by most accounts,
in only one place: the mountains of Asturias in Spain's far north.
Nobles from the centre and south of the peninsula, a number of
bishops, and the scattered remains of the defeated Visigothic armies
gathered there, and on news of Roderic's death they elected a man
named Pelayo as his successor - the figure later tradition would
remember as the founder of the Spanish monarchy.

## A battle that came after the crown, not before it

Pelayo maintained uneasy relations with the Umayyads for a time,
possibly paying tribute, before open fighting resumed. In a valley
called Covadonga his forces won a victory over an Umayyad column -
traditionally dated to 718, though some accounts place it a few years
later. What later legend turned into a battle of world-historical
scale was, in its own immediate terms, more modest: it secured eastern
Asturias for the holdouts gathered there, nothing more. Pelayo himself
had already been king for several years by this point, which makes
the common claim that Covadonga made him king strictly backward.

## A tiny kingdom among mountains no one else wanted

Outside this one small Asturian foothold, there is no firm evidence of
any other independent Christian state surviving in Spain at this
date, though several probably existed in other mountainous corners of
the north. The war Pelayo's kingdom fought was not yet, in any real
sense, a religious crusade - the Umayyads left the religion and customs
of the conquered population largely alone, and Asturias fought mainly
to recover confiscated estates and restore something like the old
Visigothic order in miniature, quarrels between nobility and crown
included. What the mountains actually offered was terrain the Umayyad
armies had little interest in taking so long as richer country lay to
the south - a fact that shaped Asturias's own survival as much as
anything Pelayo's men did in battle.
