The century after Pelayo's death in 737 produced only two Asturian
kings worth much notice: Alfonso I "the Catholic," who reigned from
739 to 757, and, after a gap of lesser rulers, Alfonso II "the
Chaste." Alfonso I campaigned against Umayyad positions with real
success, but his lasting achievement was not military so much as
demographic - he brought back substantial numbers of Mozarabs,
Christians who had been living under Muslim rule in the temporarily
raided regions to the south, and resettled them in Asturian territory.

## A frontier that moved without a Christian army pushing it

What let Asturias grow in these decades owed less to Asturian
conquest than to Umayyad neglect. The Muslim frontier in the
northwest gradually withdrew - not because Christian armies drove it
back by force, but most likely because internal Umayyad civil wars
drew manpower toward the centre and south of the peninsula, and
because the thin, mountainous northwest held little to reward
occupying it in the first place. By the time this retreat settled into
something like a fixed line, it ran south from the northwest through
Coimbra, Coria, Talavera, Toledo and Guadalajara - a frontier the
Christian side would not be strong enough to push past in any
sustained way for another three centuries.

## People, not garrisons, as the real measure of the gain

The Mozarabs Alfonso I resettled mattered more than any single
battlefield victory, because a kingdom this small could not simply
claim territory - it needed people already living somewhere else to
actually work and hold the land once the raiding parties moved on.
Asturias's own expansion in this period was, in practice, a story of
population transfer as much as conquest: land recovered by force or
abandoned by a retreating frontier, and then filled by settlers with
nowhere else to plant their own future.
