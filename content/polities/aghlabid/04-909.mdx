The Aghlabids were not conquered by a neighbour. They were removed by a
missionary organisation that had spent a decade recruiting in the mountains
behind their own capital.

This site's Fatimid page describes the *da'wa* — the Isma'ili mission,
clandestine, hierarchical, operating inside other people's territory to
persuade a rival caliph's subjects that their ruler was illegitimate. Abu
Abdallah al-Shi'i arrived in Ifriqiya in 893 and went to the Kutama Berbers of
the Lesser Kabylie, who were in Aghlabid territory, were not in Aghlabid
control, and had grievances of the usual kind about taxation and about being
governed from a lowland city by an Arab elite that despised them.

He converted them, and then he did the thing that made it work: he organised
them. The Kutama were reconstituted as a military force with a command
structure and a doctrine, and by 902 they were taking Aghlabid towns.

The Aghlabid response was hampered by the same fault the previous chapter
describes. The emirate's Arab military class — the *jund* — had been in
periodic revolt for a century, the ruling family had spent decades keeping it
occupied in Sicily rather than trusting it at home, and the last emir, Ziyadat
Allah III, had opened his reign by killing most of his own relatives. In 909 he
did not fight a final battle. He collected what he could carry and went east,
and Abu Abdallah entered Raqqada, the palace city outside Kairouan, without
opposition.

Then the mission's own logic asserted itself. Abu Abdallah summoned his master,
who had been in hiding in southern Morocco and had been imprisoned at
Sijilmasa; Abd Allah was released, brought to Ifriqiya, and declared caliph and
mahdi in 909. Two years later he had Abu Abdallah — who had converted the
Kutama, raised the army, taken the province and handed it over — executed.

Two things this leaves for the site. The Aghlabid ending is typed `conquest`
and the agent is the Fatimids, which is accurate and understates the mechanism:
the conquering army was raised inside the conquered polity out of its own
subjects. And the same year removed both this dynasty and the Rustamids at
Tahert, which is why two of this region's pages end on the same date and both
name the same cause.
