---
title: A province, for tribute
drafted_from: abun-nasr-1987

In 800 Harun al-Rashid granted Ifriqiya to Ibrahim ibn al-Aghlab as a hereditary
governorship, in exchange for an annual tribute and the caliph's name in the
Friday sermon. Baghdad stopped paying for the province's garrison and stopped
having to govern it.

That arrangement is the moment the Abbasid empire began converting itself into a
system of clients at the height of its power, and the Aghlabids are the first
case. Everything the Tahirids and the Samanids later did in the east — a
hereditary governorship, a tribute, the caliph's name in the sermon, and no
interference — the Aghlabids did in the west a generation earlier, and with the
caliph's explicit written consent rather than by quiet accumulation.

What they did with it was go to sea. The invasion of Byzantine Sicily began in
827 and took most of a century; Palermo fell in 831, Syracuse in 878. Aghlabid
fleets raided the Italian mainland and sacked the outskirts of Rome in 846. The
Sicily they created was Muslim for two and a half centuries and left an
administrative and agricultural inheritance that the Norman kings who took it
afterwards kept substantially intact.

The dynasty was destroyed in 909 by the Kutama Berber army of the Fatimid
*da'wa*, which had been recruiting in the mountains behind them for a decade.
