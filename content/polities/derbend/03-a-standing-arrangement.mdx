---
title: A polity with no turning points
drafted_from: minorsky-1958
phase: aside
---

This record's `turning_points` list is empty, which is unusual enough on this
site to be worth explaining rather than leaving as an apparent omission.

Coding rule 4.3 covers the case directly and names the polity it was written
for: Srivijaya, whose power was a standing arrangement over a strait rather than
a sequence of decisive days, and whose page is complete with no hinges recorded.
It adds a warning — do not add a thin entry to avoid an empty section, because
an empty list is a true statement and a padded one is false in the direction that
makes the site look more complete than it is.

Derbend is the same shape. Two hundred and eight years of the same three things:
the pass taxed, the city deposing its emir, and the Shirvanshahs and the
highland lordships manoeuvring around both. The chronicle records dozens of
events and none of them altered what the emirate was subsequently able to do,
because what the emirate was able to do was determined by a gap in a mountain
range and did not change.

The revenue field says the same thing more briefly. `trade-toll` alone, which
in this corpus is otherwise carried only by Palmyra — an oasis that taxed a road
across a desert because the ground produced nothing and the position produced
everything. The two polities are eight hundred years and two thousand
kilometres apart and are the same object: a small permanent settlement on a
route that has no alternative, whose whole political history is other people's
need to pass.

There is a difference, and it is instructive. Palmyra had thirteen years in
which it used the position for something else, and this site's Palmyrene record
is largely about them. Derbend never did. It held the gate for two centuries,
taxed it, argued about who was collecting, and produced no campaign, no
conquest and no claim on anybody.

That is a legitimate outcome for a polity and the site should be able to record
it without the page reading as unfinished. A corpus that requires every entry to
have hinges will manufacture them.
