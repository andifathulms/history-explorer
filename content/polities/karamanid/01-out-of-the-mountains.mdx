A dozen Turkmen principalities filled the marches of Anatolia as the Sultanate
of Rum stopped being able to restrain them. This was the largest, it lasted the
longest, and for a century and a half it was the one thing standing between the
Ottomans and the Anatolian plateau.

The Karamanid family were Oghuz Turkmen of the Salur, and they arrived in
Anatolia the way a great many people did in the 1230s: pushed west ahead of the
Mongols. They settled in the
Taurus, the mountain country between the central plateau and the Mediterranean,
and in the middle of the century Karaman Bey took a string of castles there —
Ermenek, Mut, Silifke — mostly at the expense of the Armenian kingdom of
Cilicia below them.

<Figure id="taurus" />

Mountains are the usual answer to how a small polity survives a large one, and
they are the answer here. Konya
and the plateau are open country that any strong power can ride across; the
Taurus is not, and a family that could retreat into it could not be finished by
a single campaign.

<Figure id="aladag" />

That is the whole shape of Karamanid history. They came down onto the plateau
whenever the power there was distracted, took Konya more than once, were driven
back up, and came down again. They did it against the Seljuks under Ilkhanid
supervision, against the Ilkhanids, and then for a hundred and fifty years
against the Ottomans, who needed six separate campaigns and two annexations
before it stopped.
