The state Sargon built this way lasted roughly a hundred and eighty years.

It is conventionally called the first empire, and the qualifier matters: it is
first by a definition — one authority ruling multiple previously independent
polities of different language and identity, through appointed officials rather
than tribute — that was constructed afterwards, partly to make it first. What
is not in doubt is that it was treated as the precedent. Mesopotamian kings for
the next two thousand years described themselves in Sargon's terms, and
Naram-Sin's claim to divinity and to the title king of the four quarters were
reused for centuries.

<Figure id="ak-meluhha" />

What it was in institutional terms is the substance of the record. Sargon
appointed his own men as governors in the cities he took rather than leaving
their rulers in place under tribute; he installed his daughter Enheduanna as
high priestess of the moon god at Ur, which is a political appointment to a
religious office of a kind rulers went on making for the next four thousand
years; and he kept, by his own inscriptions, five thousand four hundred men who
ate before him daily.
