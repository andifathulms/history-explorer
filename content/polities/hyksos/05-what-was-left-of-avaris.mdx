Kamose did not finish the war he opened. His own stelae describe a campaign
that reached Avaris itself — his fleet sailing north, his troops raiding the
harbour, a taunting message sent to Apepi comparing his own advance to the
Hyksos king's own useless wealth sitting undefended — but no source credits
him with taking the city, and he is thought to have died in the fighting
after a reign of no more than three years.

## The brother who finished it

Ahmose, Seqenenre's younger son and Kamose's brother, came to the throne as
a child under his mother Ahhotep's regency and grew into the war his father
had died opening and his brother had died continuing. The fullest surviving
account is not a royal inscription but the tomb autobiography of one of his
soldiers, Ahmose son of Ebana, who names the campaign's stages in the plain
first-person record of a man listing what he personally did in each one:

> Then Avaris was despoiled, and I brought spoil from there: one man, three
> women, a total of four persons. Then his majesty gave them to me as
> slaves.

Avaris fell. The Hyksos kingship that had ruled the Delta for a century did
not survive the city's capture as an independent throne anywhere else in
Egypt. Its rulers, or what remained of their following, retreated across the
frontier into Palestine — and Ahmose did not let the war end at the border.

## The siege that would not end quickly

The same soldier's inscription describes following the retreating enemy to
a fortified town named Sharuhen in southern Palestine and besieging it for
three years before it fell — a siege of that length being itself notable
enough that Ahmose son of Ebana records it as a distinct achievement in his
own career, separate from the fall of Avaris itself.

What Ahmose actually eliminated at Sharuhen — the last organised resistance
of the dynasty that had ruled from Avaris, or simply the nearest fortress the
retreating population could make a stand in — is not stated by the source
that reports it. What is clear is the scale of the response: an Egyptian
king who had spent a childhood as a tributary's heir pursuing the war that
killed his father and brother past his own country's border and keeping an
army in the field for three more years to be sure of finishing it.
