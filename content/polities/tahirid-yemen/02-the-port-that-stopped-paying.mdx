title: The port that stopped paying

Every polity in this region's thread from the Zurayids onwards has stood on the
same fact: that ships from India could not sail straight through to Egypt, so
Aden collected. The Tahirids inherited the collection and inherited it while it
was failing.

Some of the reasons were commercial and predate the Portuguese entirely. Jeddah
under the Mamluks had been drawing traffic north past Aden for decades, and
merchants who could clear their cargo closer to Cairo did. Some of it was
political: the Rasulid administration that had made Aden reliable — the fiscal
registers Vallet works from, the fixed and published dues — did not survive its
own state, and a port whose charges are unpredictable is a port that gets
avoided.

Then in 1498 Vasco da Gama came round the Cape, and within a decade Portuguese
squadrons were in the Arabian Sea trying to close the Red Sea mouth altogether.

This page's `revenue_basis` accordingly carries `trade-toll` alongside
`land-tax`, and the coding should be read as the description of an inheritance
rather than of a working system. The Tahirids' actual money increasingly came
off the highlands they had come from. What Aden gave them was a reason for other
people's fleets to take an interest in Yemen, which is what killed them.
