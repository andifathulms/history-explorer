In 1504 Amir II finished a madrasa at Rada'. It has two domes on a raised
platform, a facade of stucco panelling, and an interior painted in a way that
has no real precedent in Yemeni building, and it is still standing.

This site does not usually give a chapter to a building. It is doing so here for
a reason that is about evidence rather than about architecture. The Tahirids
left no fiscal archive of the kind the Rasulids left, and the chronicle material
for their sixty-three years is thin. What they left was construction —
mosques, madrasas, cisterns and fortifications across the southern highlands —
and Porter's study of the dynasty is built on that as much as on the texts,
because the distribution of a dynasty's buildings is a reasonably honest map of
where its authority and its money actually reached.

That map is worth reading against the one this record does not print. The
`reach_km2` field on this page is null: no work consulted publishes an extent
for the Tahirid state, and hard rule 2 forbids deriving one. But the buildings
are dated and located, and they cluster in the southern highlands and along the
routes to Aden and Zabid, and they are absent from the north where the Zaydi
imams were. That is not a figure and this site will not convert it into one. It
is better than a figure for the question it answers.

The other thing the Amiriyya records is intent. A family that had picked up a
collapsing state in 1454 was, fifty years later, building on the scale of a
dynasty that expected to be there in a century. It had thirteen years.
