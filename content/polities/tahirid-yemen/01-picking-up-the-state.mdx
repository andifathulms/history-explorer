---
title: Picking up the state
drafted_from: bosworth-1996
phase: formation
---

The Rasulid sultanate did not fall to anybody. It came apart over about thirty
years of disputed successions, plague and a collapse in the revenue that had
held it together, until by the middle of the fifteenth century there were
several men calling themselves sultan and none of them governing much.

The Banu Tahir were landowners from Juban, in the highlands south of Sanaa. They
had been men of consequence under the Rasulids without being of the ruling
family, and through the 1440s they were among the parties fighting over what was
left. In 1454 two brothers, Amir and Ali, took what remained of the state and
ruled it jointly.

They did not conquer it. There was a state that had stopped working and a family
standing closest to it when it stopped, and the distinction matters: nobody
besieged Ta'izz and nobody deposed a Rasulid. The revenue failed, the succession
was fought over for thirty years, and at the end of it the men holding the
southern highlands were the men holding the country.

What the Tahirids picked up was smaller than what the Rasulids had had. The
Zaydi imams were back in the northern highlands and were not going to be
removed. Aden's customs, which had built the Rasulid state, were falling as the
Indian Ocean traffic found ways around them. This dynasty governed a reduced
country for sixty-three years, and what it did with them is more interesting
than the reduction.
