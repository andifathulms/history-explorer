---
title: A feast at Po-sŏk

By the mid-920s Kyŏn Hwŏn was fighting a war on two fronts, pressing
Wang-gön's rising Koryŏ in the north while keeping up an offensive
campaign against Silla itself. The Silla war went the better of the
two: in 927 Kyŏn Hwŏn burned and pillaged his way toward Silla's own
capital, and while Silla's envoy was still on the road begging Koryŏ
for help, he slipped inside the city gates at the head of a picked
band of men.

## A capital taken without a battle

The timing could not have been worse for Silla. The king, his son, the
queen and the court were feasting at the Po-sŏk summer house when word
of the attack arrived, and there was no time left to organize a
defense. The king and queen fled the palace without attendants; Kyŏn
Hwŏn's men seized the palace women and looted the treasury before a
relief force of ten thousand Koryŏ troops could arrive — too late to
prevent any of it. The king was run down and forced to take his own
life.

## A kingdom installed, then abandoned to its fate

Rather than annex Silla outright, Kyŏn Hwŏn appointed a relative of the
dead king, Kyŏngsun, to the throne he had just emptied, and withdrew
south with his own army loaded with plunder. It was the furthest Later
Baekje's power over its rival would ever reach: a Silla king killed, a
capital sacked, and a puppet installed by a former Silla soldier who
had spent the previous decade building a kingdom out of his own
rebellion against the throne he had just humbled.

## A victory that cost Kyŏn Hwŏn a friend at Song-do

The raid bought Later Baekje little beyond plunder and reputation.
Wang-gön, who had answered Silla's own call for aid, took the atrocity
as confirmation that no compromise with Kyŏn Hwŏn was possible, and the
two men's letters to each other in its aftermath read less like rival
kings than like enemies who had stopped pretending otherwise. The war
between them, already years old, only hardened from that point on.
