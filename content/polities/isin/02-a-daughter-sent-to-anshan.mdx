Between Ishbi-Erra's own reign and his great-grandson Ishme-Dagan's, two
kings held the throne whose combined years the sources credit without
dwelling on — Shu-Ilishu and, at greater length, Iddin-Dagan, whose own
year-names supply most of what can be said about either reign.

## A marriage recorded because it was worth dating by

Mesopotamian scribes named each year after its most significant event
rather than counting them in sequence, and one of the events chosen to
name a year of Iddin-Dagan's reign was the marriage of his own daughter to
the ruler of Anshan, in the Elamite highlands east of Sumer. A scribe does
not build a whole year's official dating formula around a domestic event
of no consequence; a marriage judged worth naming a year after is a
marriage understood, at the Isin court itself, as a diplomatic achievement.

The alliance follows a pattern this region's kings used repeatedly and
would go on using for the next fifteen hundred years: binding a
neighbouring power that could otherwise threaten a frontier by attaching it
to the ruling family through a daughter's marriage rather than through a
garrison. Elam had supplied the army that ended Ur's own dynasty a
generation earlier; a king of Isin whose own founder had built his kingdom
on Ur's collapse had every reason to want the same power tied to his own
house by something more durable than a treaty a change of ruler could
undo.

## The rite the family kept

Whatever else Iddin-Dagan's reign accomplished, one further year-name
credits him with celebrating the sacred marriage rite in which the king
took the role of the god Dumuzi to the goddess Inanna's own high priestess,
renewing the land's fertility and the dynasty's own favour in the same
ceremony. It is the earliest such celebration this dynasty's own record
credits to a named king, and it did not stop with him: Ishme-Dagan and his
son Libit-Ishtar, a generation and two generations later, are both
independently attested continuing the same rite.

A practice three successive kings maintained across three reigns is not an
individual ruler's private devotion. It is a piece of royal ceremony the
Isin dynasty had adopted as part of what a king of Isin was expected to do,
and Iddin-Dagan's own year-name is the earliest point at which the record
catches the family already doing it.
