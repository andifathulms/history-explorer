Sebastian, the last Aviz king with any real claim to vigour, spent
years dreaming of a crusade in Morocco, and in 1578 he finally sailed
with an army heavy on Spanish, German and Italian mercenaries and
light on cautious advice - only about ten thousand of his roughly
seventeen thousand men were actually Portuguese. He wasted the early
weeks of the campaign hunting near Tangier and encamping idly at
Arzila while his Moroccan opponent, Abd al-Malik, gathered his own
far larger force. Offered the chance to cede the contested town of
Larache without a fight, Sebastian refused to even answer.

## A position with both flanks open

The armies met near the small town of Alcácer Quibir on 4 August
1578. Sebastian had chosen ground that left both his flanks exposed
to a much larger Moroccan cavalry force, and after four hours of
fighting the outnumbered Portuguese army was cut to pieces - more than
nine thousand killed, nearly all the rest taken prisoner. What made the
battle legendary even beyond its scale was that all three principal
rulers involved died within it: Abd al-Malik, already dying, expired
from the exertion of mounting his horse for the first charge and had
his death concealed until the fighting was over; his rival claimant to
the Moroccan throne drowned attempting to flee across a river; and
Sebastian himself vanished so completely, with accounts of his death
contradicting each other in every particular, that a persistent legend
long refused to believe he had died at all and would return one day to
save Portugal.

## A dynasty that outlived its last real heir by two years only

Sebastian had no children, and no full brother left living. The crown
passed to his elderly great-uncle, Cardinal Henry - a celibate priest
in his sixties whose own accession settled nothing about who would
follow him, since the office he had spent his life in made an heir of
his own body impossible. Portugal had lost, in a single afternoon on
a Moroccan battlefield, both its king and any clear answer to who
should come after him.
