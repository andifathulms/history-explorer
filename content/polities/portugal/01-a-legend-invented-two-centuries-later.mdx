Afonso Henriques inherited the county of Portugal from his mother
Teresa, whom he had already fought and defeated to secure it for
himself rather than for her own second husband. In July 1139, while
much of the flower of Muslim Spain's own fighting strength was
occupied fending off the Almohads in Africa, Afonso struck deep into
Muslim territory and crushed a local governor and several of his
subordinate lords at Ourique.

## A story that grew for two hundred years before anyone wrote it down

What later chroniclers made of that victory bears little relation to
what actually happened there. Two centuries after the battle, writers
solemnly claimed that five kings had been defeated, that two hundred
thousand Muslims had been killed, and that Afonso's own soldiers
raised him on their shields afterward and hailed him as king on the
spot. None of this has any support in a contemporary source. The
same later tradition invented a "Cortes of Lamego," supposedly held in
1143 to pass Portugal's founding constitutional laws - a body that
never actually met. Even the famous legend of Christ crucified
appearing to Afonso in his tent the night before the battle, promising
him victory, has no contemporary trace, however deeply it became
embedded in Portuguese memory and however literally the kings of
Portugal would go on wearing the five wounds of Christ on their own
coat of arms.

## What the fabrication was covering for

Strip the legend away and Ourique becomes a real but modest victory:
five provincial governors beaten in a country that already resented
its Almohad overlords, won more through Afonso's own boldness in
advancing so far from home than through any decisive tactical triumph.
The emir he had defeated retook a Portuguese castle within a year. What
actually secured Afonso's independence was not one battle but years of
hard fighting against his own Christian cousin, Alfonso VII of León -
and by 1143, León had recognized what the field at Ourique alone never
could have settled on its own.
