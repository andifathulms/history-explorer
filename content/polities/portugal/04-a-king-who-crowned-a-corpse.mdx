Dinis, who came to the throne in 1279, spent his reign less on
conquest than on building the institutions of a kingdom already
formed - reorganizing royal administration, founding the military
Order of Christ out of the suppressed Templars' own Portuguese estates
and lands, and founding, in 1300, the first Portuguese university at
Lisbon. Student unrest and quarrels with the citizens drove it to
Coimbra within a decade, and it would shuttle between the two cities
for generations before finally settling for good - but the institution
itself outlasted every one of those moves. Dinis was also a poet of
real skill, trained in the troubadour tradition his own father had
favored, and his court became the center of Portuguese literary life
for a generation.

## A prince who loved his wife's own lady-in-waiting

His grandson Pedro's story ended nothing like his. Married in 1336 to
Constance Manuel to cement an alliance with Castile, Pedro fell in
love with Inês de Castro, a Castilian noblewoman who had come to
Portugal in his wife's own household. He treated Constance with every
outward consideration during her life and fathered her children, but
after her death his devotion to Inês became, in his own court's plain
view, an absorbing passion rather than an ordinary prince's fancy. He
refused every foreign match his father proposed and, by most accounts,
went through some form of marriage with Inês in secret.

## Murdered in the streets of Coimbra

King Afonso IV decided to end his son's infatuation by removing its
object. On 7 January 1355, three courtiers acting on the king's own
orders - Álvaro Gonçalves, Pedro Coelho and Diogo Lopes Pacheco -
murdered Inês in the streets of Coimbra. Pedro raised his own vassals
against his father in fury, and though the two reconciled before
Afonso's death, Pedro's first act on taking the throne himself was to
hunt down her killers. Pacheco escaped to England, but Pedro had the
other two extradited from Castile and tortured to death before his own
eyes outside the royal palace at Coimbra. Four years later, on 24
April 1361, he had Inês's body disinterred and carried to the Convent
of Alcobaça, where it was solemnly crowned as queen before its second
burial - a king's last word, whether of grief or of a claim to make
her rightful queen, delivered to a corpse.
