Cardinal Henry's two years on the throne solved nothing about the
succession he had inherited unresolved, and when he died in 1580 the
strongest claimant by blood was Philip II of Spain - Sebastian's own
uncle, and grandson of a Portuguese king through his mother. A rival
Portuguese candidate, António, Prior of Crato, raised what support he
could, but the Portugal of 1580 bore little resemblance to the
Portugal that had elected João of Aviz two centuries before. Wealth,
luxury, the Inquisition and a shrunken free population had worn down
the appetite for another fight against a Spanish claim to the crown,
and the Cortes that met to decide the question listened to the
promises of Philip's own agent rather than to any Portuguese Joao das
Regras arguing for national election as it had in 1385.

## Alcântara

António raised a small force regardless, and the Duke of Alva, leading
twenty thousand Spanish troops into Portugal, crushed it without much
difficulty at Alcântara on 26 August 1580. António fled to France,
and Philip II was proclaimed king of Portugal in his place - a
conquest completed in weeks against resistance that never had a real
chance of holding.

## Promises sworn at Tomar

The following year Philip entered Lisbon and, before a Cortes convened
at Tomar, swore on 15 April 1581 to govern Portugal as a distinct
kingdom rather than a mere province: its own Cortes summoned
regularly, its own offices and church dignities reserved for
Portuguese subjects, its own revenue kept separate from Castile's,
its own courts final in matters of Portuguese justice, and the
lucrative trade of Africa, Persia and India reserved to Portuguese
ships alone. Whether those promises would hold for as long as Philip's
own successors ruled remained to be seen; what was certain, on the day
they were sworn, was that the line running from Afonso Henriques's own
breach with León four hundred and forty years earlier had ended, and
a Spanish king now wore the Portuguese crown as well as his own.
