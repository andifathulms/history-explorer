Le Thanh Tong took Vijaya in 1471. The Cham territory north of Cape Varella
became Vietnamese, and what remained of Champa was a small polity in the far
south that lasted, in diminishing form, until 1832.

Vietnamese historiography calls the long process this belongs to the *Nam
tiến*, the march to the south: five centuries in which the Red River delta's
state expanded down a narrow coast, absorbing Cham principalities one at a time
and eventually reaching the Mekong. 1471 is its decisive act.

## An empty field with a locatable cause

`ended.by` on this record is empty, and it is worth being precise about why,
because it is not the usual reason.

The polity that ended Champa is not obscure, not unnamed, and not outside this
site's scope. It is the Le dynasty of Dai Viet, and this corpus's Vietnamese
record stops in 1400 — seventy-one years short — because that record covers the
Ly and the Tran and says on its own first page that extending a span to cover
four dynasties, an occupation and a partition would produce a record about a
country rather than a polity.

So the field is empty because of a boundary this project drew deliberately, on a
neighbouring page, for a good reason, with this as the cost.

That is a third kind of empty `by`, and this site now has examples of all three
close together. This corpus's African records are empty because the polity on the
other side has no page at all — Portugal, Britain, the Funj. The Pagan record in
this region is empty although the Yuan record exists, because the source it is
drafted from argues the ending was not delivered from outside. And this one is
empty because the right polity is half-present: the name is on this site, the
dynasty is not.

Of the three, only this one has a fix that is a single page of work. The Le
record would close this field, and it would give this region its second internal
edge — which is also what the region needs before its `thread` flag can be
flipped from `false` to `true`.
