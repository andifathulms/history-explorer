By the winter of 1476 the wars Charles had chosen - against the
Swiss, against Lorraine, against half his own neighbors at once - had
worn his army down without breaking his own resolve to fight them.
Besieging Nancy in the depths of that winter, he learned that a
relieving force of Lorrainers under Duke Rene and Swiss mercenaries,
together more than twice the size of his own exhausted command, was
marching to break the siege. Rather than abandon it or wait to be
attacked in his camp, Charles resolved to meet them in the field.

On Sunday, the 5th of January, 1477 - what the chroniclers called the
Vigil of the Kings - he drew his troops up behind a frozen stream
south-east of the town: artillery forward, infantry massed in a
single square in imitation of the Swiss, cavalry thin on both wings.
It was, by every account, the only arrangement that gave so small a
force any chance against so large a one. It was not enough. The
Lorrainers and Swiss, confident after two earlier battles that had
already broken Charles's army without destroying it, closed in
overwhelming numbers and shattered what remained. Charles himself
disappeared in the rout, and for two days no one - not his own
officers, not the enemy - could say with certainty whether he was
dead, in hiding, or fled.

## Identified by her tears

The search that found him was led by men who could recognize him on
sight: his Portuguese physician, a valet, and a laundress who had
prepared his baths. In a ditch outside the town, among a small knot
of men who had fallen fighting together, they found a body stripped
naked, mutilated past easy recognition, the cheek eaten away by
wolves or dogs. The nails were longer than any other man's - Charles
had never had them pared. Two teeth were missing, from a fall years
before. A scar in the neck matched a sword-thrust taken at an earlier
battle. The laundress broke first, throwing herself on the body with
a cry of "My lord of Burgundy!" It was him - the Great Duke, the
destroyer of Liege, the Terror of France, dead in a frozen ditch at
forty-three. The state his father had spent a lifetime assembling by
marriage, purchase and patient negotiation ended, in an afternoon,
by the one method Philip had always been careful to avoid.
