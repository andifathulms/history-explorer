---
title: A crown that died with him
drafted_from: seton-watson-1911
phase: peak
---

The kingship Tomislav had founded a century and a half earlier reached
what Seton-Watson calls its most famous moment under one of the
line of kings who followed him: Zvonimir, the Ban of Slavonia who had
brought his own territory under Croatian suzerainty in exchange for a
promised succession, received his own crown at Split in 1076 directly
from the hands of the Legate of Pope Gregory VII - a second papal
coronation confirming the same choice of allegiance Tomislav had made
generations before. But the achievement proved personal rather than
institutional.

"Zvonimir's greatness died with him," Seton-Watson writes bluntly,
and no comparable king followed to carry the kingdom's own standing
forward. Zvonimir left no son, and the native royal line he had
crowned himself the most celebrated member of simply ran out - leaving
Croatia's own future to be decided not by another coronation, but by
the civil war that followed a dynasty's extinction.
