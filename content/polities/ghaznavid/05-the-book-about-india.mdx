title: The book about India

When Mahmud annexed Khwarazm in 1017 he took its scholars back to Ghazna with
everything else. One of them was al-Biruni, who spent the rest of his life in
Ghaznavid service and produced, some time after 1030, a book about India.

Its title is a statement of method: *Tahqiq ma li'l-Hind* — an investigation of
what is said about India, of the accounts acceptable to reason and those to be
rejected. He learned Sanskrit in order to write it. He read the texts, talked
to Brahmins, worked through the astronomy and the mathematics — which he was
qualified to assess, being one of the best astronomers of the age — and set out
Indian cosmology, philosophy, law, chronology, weights, festivals and caste,
with the sources named and the disagreements between them recorded.

He is also explicit about the difficulty. He says the Hindus differ from Muslims
in everything and that the differences are compounded by the recent wars, which
have made his subjects wary of the people he arrived with; he says that
translating between the two systems is hard because the vocabularies do not
map; and he complains, at length, about the unreliability of what Muslims had
previously written about India. He is not neutral — he thinks his own religion
is true and says so — but he separates the question of what Indians believe
from the question of whether it is correct, and reports the first without
constantly answering the second.

That was produced at the court of the man whose armies had made those wars,
funded by the proceeds. The previous chapter is about a raid whose later career
was enormous; this is a book by a man in the same tents, in the same decades,
doing the opposite thing, and it stayed almost unknown outside a narrow
scholarly circle until a German edition and translation in the 1880s put it
into general circulation.

The Ghaznavid court is generally remembered for two things: for Firdawsi, who
presented the *Shahnama* to Mahmud, was badly rewarded, and said so at length;
and for the plunder. It also produced this, which nothing on this page's rating
panel can register, and which is the single most useful document any polity in
this corpus generated about a society other than its own.
