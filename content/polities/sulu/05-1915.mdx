Spain attacked Jolo in 1578, in the same season it took Brunei, and did not
take it. What followed is the longest continuous resistance to Spanish rule
anywhere in the Philippines — three centuries of it, which the Spanish called
the Moro wars and never finished.

What ended the sultanate's position was not an army. It was steam.

Spanish steamers destroyed the Balangingi settlements in 1848 and deported the
survivors to Luzon. A steam vessel did not depend on the monsoon, which meant
it could reach a raiding base in the season the raiders were at home —
something no sailing fleet in three hundred years had been able to do. The
raiding stopped being viable within a generation, and with it went both the
sultanate's revenue and the labour that produced its export.

## The document

In 1915 the sultan signed the Carpenter Agreement with the United States,
renouncing temporal sovereignty and retaining his authority as head of the
Muslim community of Sulu, which his successors hold.

It is the latest ending of any sultanate in these islands — twelve years after
Aceh's sultan surrendered to the Dutch, and by a very different instrument.
Aceh fought for thirty years and stopped when there was nobody left to fight
alongside. Sulu's sultan signed a paper and kept his religious authority, which
his successors still hold.

## Where the line stopped

Sulu is the northern end of something that began six hundred years earlier and
four thousand kilometres away.

Islam took a throne in Southeast Asia at Pasai on the north Sumatran coast
around 1267. It reached Melaka by a marriage in 1414 and spread outward through
the ports from there — to Java, to Borneo, to Sulawesi, to Maluku, and finally
north through Brunei into the Sulu archipelago and the coasts beyond it toward
Mindanao.

That is where it stopped, because in 1571 the Spanish took Manila bay, having
crossed the Pacific in the other direction. Two expansions that had started on
opposite sides of the world met in these islands and neither one finished.

The line where they stopped is still there. It is the boundary of the Muslim
south of the Philippines, and everything that has happened along it since is
downstream of two fleets arriving from opposite horizons within about a century
of each other.
