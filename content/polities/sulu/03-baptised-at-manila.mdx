In 1750 the sultan of Sulu was baptised a Catholic in Manila cathedral, taking
the name Fernando de Alimudin. Two years later the Spanish arrested him for
treason. He spent about a decade in prison in Manila, was released by the British
when they took the city in 1762, and was restored to his throne at Jolo in 1764.

Azim ud-Din I is this record's `peak` ruler, and putting the peak there rather
than in a century of successful raiding is a judgement this chapter has to
defend.

## What he was doing

He had been deposed once already by his own brother and by datus who objected to
his accommodation with the Spanish. He went to Manila as an exile looking for the
means to get his throne back, and the conversion was the price. Whether he
believed any of it is not something this site can adjudicate; what is on the
record is that he had earlier commissioned a translation of Catholic texts into
Tausug and had corresponded with the Jesuits for years.

The Spanish arrested him because they intercepted a letter, in Arabic, in which
he appeared to be advising the sultan of Maguindanao not to admit missionaries.
Both things were probably true at once, which is what a ruler in his position was
for.

## Why the peak sits here

Because what came out of it, in 1764 and after, is the sultanate at its most
effective: a restored sultan with British recognition, an English East India
Company settlement at Balambangan on Sulu's own terms, and the beginning of the
period this record's second chapter describes — the tripang and pearl trade with
Canton that made Jolo the richest entrepôt in the region for two generations.

The alternative peaks are worse. A raiding economy at its height is not a peak
in any sense this site measures, and the fifteenth-century sultanate is barely
documented.

It is also the single most cross-pressured episode in the archipelago. This corpus
has records that dealt with the Portuguese, the Dutch, the Spanish, the English
and the Ming. This is the only one whose ruler was baptised by one of them,
imprisoned by the same one, freed by another, and put back on his throne by the
second — all inside fourteen years.
