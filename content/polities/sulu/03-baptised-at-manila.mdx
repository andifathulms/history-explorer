In 1750 the sultan of Sulu was baptised a Catholic in Manila cathedral, taking
the name Fernando de Alimudin. Two years later the Spanish arrested him for
treason. He spent about a decade in prison in Manila, was released by the
British when they took the city in 1762, and was restored to his throne at Jolo
in 1764.

Azim ud-Din I is the obvious candidate for the height of the sultanate, and
putting it there rather than in a century of successful raiding takes some
defending.

## What he was doing

He had been deposed once already by his own brother and by datus who objected
to his accommodation with the Spanish. He went to Manila as an exile looking
for the means to get his throne back, and the conversion was the price. Whether
he believed any of it is not answerable; what is on record is that he had
earlier commissioned a translation of Catholic texts into Tausug and had
corresponded with the Jesuits for years.

The Spanish arrested him because they intercepted a letter, in Arabic, in which
he appeared to be advising the sultan of Maguindanao not to admit missionaries.
Both things were probably true at once, which is what a ruler in his position
was for.

## Why the peak sits here

Because of what came out of it. From 1764 the sultanate is at its most
effective: a restored sultan with British recognition, an English East India
Company settlement at Balambangan on Sulu's own terms, and the beginning of the
tripang and pearl trade with Canton that made Jolo the richest entrepôt in the
region for two generations.

The alternatives are worse. A raiding economy at its height is not a peak in
any sense worth the word, and the fifteenth-century sultanate is barely
documented at all.

It is also the most cross-pressured episode anywhere in these seas. Plenty of
rulers here dealt with the Portuguese, the Dutch, the Spanish, the English or
the Ming. This is the only one who was baptised by one European power,
imprisoned by the same one, freed by a second, and restored to his throne by
that second — all inside fourteen years.
