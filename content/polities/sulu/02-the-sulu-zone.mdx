Sulu lived on two things: a toll on what came to Jolo to be traded, and a share
of what was taken at sea.

Champa had run the same combination on the coast of what is now Vietnam, six
thousand kilometres away and eight hundred years earlier — selling what the
hinterland produced and taking what passed when selling was not enough, with
the same fleets and the same crews doing both. Trade and raiding on such a
coast are one skill applied in two directions.

## What was sold

The China trade in the eighteenth century wanted things that could be gathered
rather than grown: tripang, the sea cucumber, dried and shipped to Canton;
pearl shell; birds' nests from limestone caves; wax; shark fin; tortoiseshell.

Sulu sat in the middle of the richest gathering grounds in the world for most
of that list, and British country traders out of India wanted a place to sell
opium and cloth for goods that Canton would take. Jolo became the exchange. The
sultanate taxed it.

## What was taken

The labour that gathered the tripang and dived the pearl shell was largely
captured, and the capturing was done by the Iranun and the Balangingi —
maritime peoples who were clients and allies of the sultanate rather than its
subjects, who kept most of what they took, and whose fleets ranged from Luzon
to the Moluccas and across to the Malay peninsula.

The scale is large. Tens of thousands of people were taken from the coasts of
the Philippines and eastern Indonesia over the century in which the trade was
at its height, and the Spanish coastal watchtowers still standing in the
Visayas were built against it.

It is the same kind of relation Srivijaya, Melaka and Johor had with the *orang
laut* of the straits: sea peoples attached to a polity that could not command
them, serving for a share.

What differs is what the arrangement was for. Those fleets made a strait safe,
and the tolls their patrons collected were a protection charge. These ones made
a region dangerous, on the sultanate's behalf and for a cut of the proceeds.

The same institution, pointed the opposite way.
