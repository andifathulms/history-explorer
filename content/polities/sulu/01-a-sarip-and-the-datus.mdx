The Sulu tradition has a foreign holy man arriving and marrying into the local
ruling house. Sayyid Abu Bakr, a *sarip* — a descendant of the Prophet — reached
Jolo around 1450 by way of Palembang, married the daughter of the ruling datu, and
became the first sultan.

Before him, in the same tradition, comes Karim ul-Makhdum, a preacher who
arrived around 1380 and whose mosque at Tubig-Indangan on Simunul is claimed as
the oldest in the Philippines; and Rajah Baguinda, a Sumatran prince, some
decades later.

## The shape of the story

It is the same story this corpus's Melaka record tells about Parameswara, and the
Ternate and Pasai records tell in their own versions: a stranger of prestigious
descent arrives by sea, marries the local house, and the polity dates itself from
the marriage.

The consistency is not evidence that it happened the same way five times. It is
evidence about what these courts thought a founding was, and the two components
are always the same — descent from somewhere important, and a local woman.
Neither on its own would do.

Note what it implies about the local house: it was there, it had something worth
marrying into, and the chronicle does not pretend otherwise. This record's
`span.start` is 1405, which the tradition gives, and what preceded it is a
polity of datus that the sultanate is grafted onto rather than a blank.

## And the datus stayed

That is the substance rather than the etiquette, and it is why `succession_rule`
here is null.

Sulu was a sultanate over an archipelago of communities each under its own datu,
who held their own followings, their own boats and their own share of what was
taken. The sultan's authority over them was real, negotiated, and variable; the
sultanate's periods of weakness are periods when particular datus were acting on
their own account, and Spanish and later British officials dealing with Sulu
found repeatedly that an agreement with the sultan was not an agreement with the
datus.

This corpus's Brunei record's fourth chapter makes the same observation about the
wazirs and the pengiran, and notes that three Malay records now carry a null in
this field for a structure the vocabulary has no value for. Sulu is the fourth.
