In 1878 the sultan of Sulu signed an agreement with two European merchants over
the territory Sulu claimed on the north-east coast of Borneo. The rights passed
to a chartered company, and from the company to Britain, and from Britain into
the settlement that made that coast part of Malaysia.

The agreement was written in Jawi. The operative word is *padjak*.

Sulu's position, and that of the sultan's heirs since, is that *padjak* means
lease — that the territory was rented for an annual payment. The company's
translation, and the position of the states that inherited its rights, rendered
it as cession. The annual payment was made for well over a century by successive
administrations, which is a fact both readings can accommodate and neither can
settle.

## Why it is on this page

Not for the modern dispute, which is outside this site's period and outside its
competence. It is here because it is the clearest example in this corpus of what
a polity's ending actually consists of.

This record's `ended.type` is `gradual absorption` and its year is 1915, when the
sultan renounced temporal sovereignty by a document. The 1878 agreement is
thirty-seven years earlier and it is the same kind of act: not a battle, not a
siege, not a dynasty replaced, but a signature on a text whose meaning was
contested at the moment it was written.

This corpus keeps finding these. The Attalid record ends by a will. The Venetian
one ends by a vote of its own council. The Holy Roman Empire ends by an
abdication, the Ethiopian one by a committee of officers, this corpus's Mataram
record by a treaty brokered by a trading company, and its Brunei record piecemeal
by three separate grants.

Seven records across five regions whose ending is a document. The `ended.type`
vocabulary offers conquest, fragmentation, dynastic replacement, gradual
absorption, internal usurpation and still contested — six values, all of them
about force or dissolution, and none about signature. Every one of those seven
records is coded with the nearest wrong word.
