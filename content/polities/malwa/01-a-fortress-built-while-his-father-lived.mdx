title: A fortress built while his father lived

Dilawar Khan Ghuri was already governing Malwa for Delhi by 1392,
appointed, most probably, by Firuz Shah Tughluq shortly before that
sultan's death. He never claimed the throne himself, but Timur's 1398
sack of Delhi left him with no real sultan left to answer to, and he
governed the province in every practical sense as an independent prince
for the rest of his life — hospitable enough, in that independence, to
shelter the fugitive Delhi sultan Mahmud Shah Tughluq as an honoured
guest for three years after Timur's invasion drove him from his own
capital.

## A son who disapproved of the guest

His son and heir, Alp Khan, took a harder view of that hospitality than
his father did. He considered the deference shown to a dispossessed
Delhi sultan beneath the dignity of a Malwa that no longer owed Delhi
anything, and while the royal guest remained at Dhar, Alp Khan withdrew
to the fortress city of Mandu and spent his time there perfecting its
defences rather than attending his father's court — a son visibly
preparing for a kingdom his father had not yet agreed to let him rule as
one.

## The throne he could not wait to inherit

He did not wait for nature to resolve the disagreement. In 1406, growing
impatient for the inheritance he believed a Delhi-deferring father was
squandering, Alp Khan poisoned Dilawar Khan and ascended the throne
himself as Hushang Shah — the first ruler of Malwa to claim outright
royal title rather than a governor's office, and the founder, by the act
of his father's murder, of Malwa's status as an unambiguous independent
kingdom rather than a province still nominally answerable elsewhere.
