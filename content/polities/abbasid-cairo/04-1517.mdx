In January 1517 Selim I destroyed the Mamluk army outside Cairo, and among the
assets that passed to the Ottomans was the caliph. Al-Mutawakkil III was taken
to Constantinople with the treasury, the artisans and the relics.

He was not killed, and the line was not continued. He was held, allowed back to
Cairo some years later, and died there in 1543 as a private person with a
pension. The office simply stopped being filled.

What happened to the *claim* is a longer story and this site is careful with
it. The Ottoman position from the eighteenth century onwards was that the
caliphate had been formally transferred at Cairo in 1517 — that al-Mutawakkil
had ceded the office and its insignia to Selim in a ceremony, and that the
Ottoman sultans had been caliphs by right ever since. The account appears in
Ottoman writing of the 1770s, when the empire was negotiating with Russia over
its authority as a religious protector of Muslims outside its borders and had a
concrete use for the argument.

Modern scholarship does not accept the ceremony, and this site does not record
it: the near-contemporary sources for 1517 do not describe a transfer, Selim
did not use the caliphal title in the way the later account requires, and the
story's first appearance is two hundred and fifty years after the event in a
context that explains it. The Ottoman page here says the same thing from the
other end.

What is not disputed is the substance. After 1517 the Ottoman sultan held Mecca
and Medina, and no other claimant held anything comparable — so whatever the
office of caliph now meant, it meant it in Constantinople. The distinction
matters because it is the difference between an institution being transferred
and an institution being obsolete, and this record holds that the second
happened.

That is the third and last of this corpus's endings for that office. It ended at
Baghdad in 1258 when Hulegu killed al-Musta'sim. It ended here in 1517, quietly,
when the man who could perform the ceremony was moved to another city and
nobody appointed a successor. And it ended in 1924 when a parliament in Ankara
voted, which the Ottoman page records. Of the three, this is the only one where
nobody appears to have decided anything: the office was not abolished and was
not transferred. It was carried off with the furniture and not set up again.
