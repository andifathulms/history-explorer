Liao's collapse in the 1120s put every one of its neighbours in the position
of having to decide, quickly and with incomplete information, whether the
power replacing it deserved the loyalty the old one had commanded. Xia had a
century of practice at exactly this kind of calculation, and it made the
choice with a speed that left no room for sentiment about an ally it had
fought as often as it had supported.

## The fugitive emperor turned away

When Yeliu Yanxi, the last Liao emperor, was finally run to ground by Jin
forces after months as a fugitive with a shrinking escort, he had already
tried and failed to find refuge with the Ordos kingdom that his own dynasty
had fought wars against and made treaties with in roughly equal measure.
Parker records the refusal without elaboration: "the Tanguts were afraid to
give him asylum." Xia had read the direction the war was running clearly
enough to know that sheltering a fugitive Liao emperor was a liability with
no plausible upside, and declined to be the last friend of a dynasty that
was, by that point, a corpse still technically breathing.

## A new patron, chosen fast

The refusal was not neutrality. Once Liao's fall was no longer in doubt, Xia
moved to secure standing with its replacement rather than wait to be
conquered or ignored: it "hastened," in Parker's word, "to declare herself a
vassal of the Jin" — language that credits the decision to Xia's own
initiative rather than to any Jin demand. It was the same manoeuvre the
dynasty had run for a century against Song and Liao in turn, adapted now to
a patron with no track record yet established and therefore, if anything,
more reason to value an early and voluntary submission.

## What a century of triangulation had actually bought

The habit that had let Xia survive between two roughly matched rivals for a
hundred years worked exactly as well against a single power with no rival
left to play it against. Jin accepted the vassalage, and Xia's border, army
and internal administration continued much as they had under Song and Liao's
alternating attention — a state that had never depended on the balance
between its neighbours surviving intact, only on being useful enough to
whichever of them currently held the advantage.
