By 1214 Philip Augustus had already spent decades enlarging the
royal demesne at the expense of the Plantagenet kings of England,
stripping King John of Normandy and most of his other continental
lands. That July, marching near Tournai, Philip was resting under an
ash tree beside a chapel when word reached him that his rearguard,
crossing the river Marque at a bridge near the village of Bouvines,
was under attack. A coalition arrayed against him - the Emperor Otto
IV, the Count of Flanders, and English forces in John's pay - had
caught the French army spread out on the march.

## A battle Philip did not choose

Philip turned back to fight rather than retreat. He prayed briefly,
then addressed his knights cheerfully, "as one who went to a wedding
or a feast," and drew up his line with the sacred Oriflamme banner
raised. The imperial centre advanced under a golden eagle mounted on
a dragon standard carried on an Italian-style car; the Count of
Flanders held the allied left. French communal militias from Soissons
opened the fighting, and the Bishop of Beauvais, forbidden by his
office from shedding blood, fought with a mace instead of a sword and
felled the Earl of Salisbury "like an ox." Philip himself was briefly
unhorsed and nearly killed in the press before the knight William des
Barres cut him free. Otto IV's own horse was wounded and bolted,
carrying the emperor from the field; Ferrand, Count of Flanders, and
Renaud of Boulogne were both taken prisoner.

## What one afternoon settled

The victory was decisive in a way medieval battles rarely were. Otto
IV's imperial ambitions never recovered, and within months he had
lost his throne to a Hohenstaufen rival. John fared no better: his
defeat at Bouvines, following his own failed campaign in the west,
discredited him so thoroughly before his own barons that it helped
drive him, the following year, to the negotiations at Runnymede that
produced the Great Charter. For Philip and his kingdom, Bouvines was
the first real French royal victory of its kind - not a defence of
a siege or a raid turned back, but a set battle won outright over a
coalition that had expected to dismember the kingdom he had spent
his reign rebuilding.
