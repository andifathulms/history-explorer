On a single day in October 1307, officers of Philip IV arrested every
Templar in the kingdom of France at once, seizing the order's house
in Paris along with its Grand Master, Jacques de Molay. It was the
kind of coordinated, kingdom-wide action no earlier French king could
have carried out, and it announced a reach of royal power that
reached past the battlefield and into an institution answerable, in
theory, to the pope alone.

## Confessions bought with torture

More than seventy Templars were examined by papal investigators
working separately from Philip's own inquisitors, under conditions
contemporaries described as brutal - torture, threats and promises of
immunity produced confessions to charges that ranged from the merely
implausible to the openly lurid, including worship of an idol with
eyes of glowing carbuncle. Pope Clement V, reluctant from the start,
fled Poitiers in disguise rather than be forced to preside over the
affair in person, and eventually settled at Avignon instead of Rome -
the beginning of a papal residence outside Italy that would stretch
on for decades. The affair dragged toward its end for years. At the
last moment, Jacques de Molay and another of the order's leaders
publicly retracted their forced confessions and declared the order
innocent of every charge. Philip, enraged, had them burned that same
night on an island in the Seine without further trial.

## A reign that ended in scandal, not surrender

Philip's own last years brought no reckoning for the manner of the
Templars' destruction, but they did bring a domestic scandal: the
wives of two of his three sons were convicted of adultery, their
lovers executed, while the crown's demands on the kingdom's finances
and patience wore increasingly thin. An aged Jean de Joinville,
seneschal of Champagne and companion of an earlier crusading king,
lived to see the beginnings of a noble confederation against what its
members called the crown's own growing weight of tyranny. None of it
reversed what 1307 had shown: a French king could break the wealthiest
military order in Christendom and relocate the papacy itself, and
answer to no one for it.
