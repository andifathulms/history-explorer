In 1199 Georgian forces under Tamar's generals, the Zakarid brothers — Armenian
nobles in Georgian service, who governed the Armenian territories the kingdom
took — captured Ani from the last Shaddadid emir.

That is the end of this record, and it is the one internal edge in this site's
Caucasus region: `shaddadid -> georgia`. Everything else here goes outward. The
Bagratid kingdom was taken by Byzantium and passed its inheritance to Cilicia;
Derbend was taken by the Seljuks; the Shirvanshahs were taken by the Safavids.
Five polities in one region and one succession between any two of them, which is
what the ground actually produced and is why the region carried no thread until
now.

The city changed hands afterwards more or less continuously — Georgian,
Mongol, Turkmen, Ottoman — and was finished by earthquakes and by the shifting
of the trade routes rather than by a conquest. What is on the site today is a
ruined city on a closed border in a military zone, with a cathedral, a mosque,
several churches and a great deal of wall.

The dynasty leaves nothing this site can count. The influence fields are all
null: no script, no religion carried, no successor claim. What it has instead is
two chapters' worth of the corpus's own connective tissue — a Kurdish emirate
that governed an Armenian capital under a Turkish sultanate and was ended by a
Georgian queen, and a family that left it in the twelfth century and took Egypt.

That is a fair description of the Caucasus and of why this region is worth
having as a region. Nothing here inherits from anything else here. Everything
here connects to somewhere else.
