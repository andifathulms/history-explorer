Rabbel II died and Trajan made the kingdom into the province of Arabia. That is
very nearly the whole of what can be said about it.

There is no account of a war. No Roman writer describes a campaign, no triumph
was celebrated, and the emperor took no title from it. The provincial coinage
carries the legend *Arabia adquisita* — Arabia acquired — where a conquered
province would say *capta*. What is on the other side is that the Legio III
Cyrenaica moved down from Egypt, a Roman garrison appears at Hegra seven hundred
kilometres south, and a papyrus from the Judaean desert dates by the year the
province was created without saying how.

Bowersock's reading, and the reason this record's `ended.type` is `gradual
absorption` rather than `conquest`, is that the annexation was essentially
administrative: a client kingship of a hundred and sixty-nine years' standing
was wound up when its last king died, by an empire that had been the effective
overlord throughout.

The edge in this site's data says `absorbed remnants of` for the same reason.
`conquered by` would assert a campaign nobody recorded, which is hard rule 2's
prohibition on inventing a figure applied to an event instead of a number.

## Bostra

Trajan moved the centre north. Bostra in the Hawran became the provincial
capital and the legionary base, the Via Nova Traiana was cut from Bostra to the
Red Sea, and the province was administered from grain country rather than from a
gorge.

Petra kept a ceremonial standing, received the title of metropolis, and stopped
being the point of anything. It was still inhabited for centuries — there are
Byzantine churches there, and a burnt archive of sixth-century papyri found in
one of them — and it was no longer a place through which things had to pass.

That is the ordinary fate of the eight `trade-toll` polities this corpus
carries. A chokepoint kingdom is only a kingdom while the route matters and
while nobody larger controls both ends. Rome took both ends.

## What is left

Two things, and the disproportion between them is this record's summary.

The kingdom's political achievement was to survive a hundred and sixty-nine
years inside the Roman system without being absorbed, which is longer than any
comparable client in the east managed, and it ended in an administrative act
that produced no narrative at all. This record has no reach figure, no
population figure, one coded institution that is a tribal levy, and an ending
nobody wrote down.

Its other achievement is that a quarter of the world writes in a script
descended from its bookkeeping hand.

This site measures polities on reach, duration and three influence counts. This
record has no cited figure for reach or for population, which hard rule 3 says is
rendered as a gap and excluded from any weighted total rather than scored as a
zero — so on those axes it is not low, it is absent. Of the three influence
counts, two are zero and one is the writing system of a quarter of the world.

Hard rule 6 says influence is never a single number and must be shown as three
counts, and this page is the clearest demonstration of why. Any weighting that
collapsed the three would divide the only thing about the Nabataeans that
everyone alive still uses by three, and bury it.
