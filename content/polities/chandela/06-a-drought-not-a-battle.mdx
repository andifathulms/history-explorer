The last Chandel king to matter on the wider stage of Northern Indian
history was Paramardi, known popularly as Parmal, whose reign ran
from 1165 to 1203. His defeat in 1182 by Prithviraja Chauhan, who
captured the Chandel town of Mahoba, became one of the most celebrated
episodes in the popular Hindi epic tradition, the Chand-Raisa - a war
still remembered in Upper India long after the kingdoms that fought it
had both disappeared. But it was not that defeat that ended the
Chandel dynasty. Prithviraja's own confederacy fell to Muhammad of
Ghor within a decade of humbling Parmal, and it was the Ghurid
general Qutb al-Din Aibak, not any Rajput rival, who finally closed
out Chandel independence.

## The wall of Alexander, undone by drought

Parmal himself, according to the contemporary Muhammadan account Smith
quotes, resisted Aibak's forces in the field before retreating into
the fortress of Kalanjar, surrendered, and agreed to pay tribute -
"placed the collar of subjection round his neck," in the chronicler's
own phrase - but died before he could make good on any of it. His
minister, Aj Deo, refused to give up as easily as his king had, and
held Kalanjar against the siege until a severe drought dried up every
reservoir inside its walls, leaving the garrison, "in an extreme state
of weakness and distraction," no choice but to march out and abandon
the fort. Kalanjar itself, a stronghold the same chronicler calls
"celebrated throughout the world for being as strong as the wall of
Alexander," fell in the spring of 1203 not to storm or breach but to
thirst - the last major holdout of organized Hindu resistance in
Bundelkhand, and, with Gwalior's own surrender in 1196 and Anhilwara's
capture in 1197, the final piece completing Ghurid control of
Hindustan proper.
