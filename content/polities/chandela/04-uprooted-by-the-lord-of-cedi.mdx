Vidyadhara's own successors could not hold what he had defended.
Neither his son Vijayapala nor his grandson Devavarman left an
inscription recording any success against the kingdom's neighbours,
and the Kalachuri king of Dahala, Lakshmikarna - called by modern
historians the "Hindu Napoleon" for a sphere of influence Dikshit
describes as reaching, on the chroniclers' own telling, from the
borders of Kashmir to the Bay of Bengal - overran the Chandel kingdom
outright. A later court drama, the Prabodhachandrodaya, states flatly
that "the race of the Moon was uprooted by the lord of Cedi, the
destroyer of all the princes"; a Chandel inscription of the next
generation describes Lakshmikarna's armies as having "destroyed many
princes." Dikshit judges it likely that the Chandel king who lost his
throne to Karna, and quite possibly his life defending it, was
Devavarman - whose name later Chandel records omit entirely from
their own dynastic lists, an erasure his family reserved for rulers
they did not care to remember.

## A drama staged at the court of the king who reversed it

It was Devavarman's younger brother, Kirtivarman, who retrieved the
family's fortune. Contemporary and later inscriptions credit him
directly with crushing Lakshmikarna in turn - one calls him "the
pitcher-born [Agastya] in swallowing that ocean Karna" - and an
Ajayagadh inscription hails him as the ruler who created the kingdom
anew. The victory became celebrated enough in his own lifetime that a
court poet, Krishna Misra, wrote it into the Prabodhachandrodaya, a
Sanskrit drama performed, on its own internal evidence, before
Kirtivarman himself; the play credits an ally or feudatory named
Gopala with the actual battlefield success, comparing him in turn to
Varaha raising the earth from the flood and to Narasimha tearing open
a tyrant's breast, while the king's own inscriptions give the credit
to Kirtivarman directly. Whichever man led the army, the Chandel
kingdom that Lakshmikarna had, by the drama's own word, "uprooted" was
standing again by the time Kirtivarman's reign ended around 1100.
