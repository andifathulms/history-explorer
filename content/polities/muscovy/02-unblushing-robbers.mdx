Daniel held Moscow for forty years after receiving it as a boy, and the
chronicle's own verdict on how the earliest Muscovite princes actually
behaved is blunt: they were, in Kliuchevsky's phrase, "robbers of the
most unblushing type." Being junior meant having no old feud to defend
and no rung of seniority worth respecting - and Daniel's whole reign
shows a prince who understood that better than any of his rivals.

## A stratagem against Riazan

Daniel attacked his neighbour Constantine of Riazan without
provocation, defeated him "by a certain cunning" rather than open
battle, took him prisoner, and annexed Kolomna - the town that put the
Moskva river's own mouth under Muscovite control for the first time.
He spent the rest of his reign at odds with his own elder brothers,
Dmitri and Andrei, each of whom held the senior Vladimir throne in
turn: a junior prince fighting his own family for advantage rather
than waiting his place in a line of succession that was never going to
reach him.

That same disregard for the old rules paid off in an unplanned
windfall. Daniel became close friends with Ivan, Dmitri's own son and
ruler of Pereyaslavl - close enough that when Ivan died childless, he
willed his entire appanage to Daniel rather than to his own senior
kinsmen, who by every customary rule of inheritance had the better
claim to it. Moscow absorbed Pereyaslavl without a battle, on the
strength of a friendship its prince had cultivated instead of a
birthright he never had.

## Yuri finishes what his father started

Daniel died in 1303, and his son Yuri wasted no time proving the family
trait ran in the blood. That same year he made an unprovoked attack on
the neighbouring Prince of Mozhaisk, imprisoned him, and annexed his
appanage outright - and then, for good measure, had his own father's
old prisoner Constantine of Riazan murdered and Kolomna's annexation
made permanent rather than provisional. Between father and son,
Moscow's rulers had, within a single generation, secured the entire
course of the Moskva river from source to mouth.

## What the family actually built

None of this reads as statecraft in the grand sense - it reads as
opportunism, seized without apology whenever circumstances allowed it.
But it set the pattern every Muscovite prince after Yuri would follow:
skilful adaptation to whatever the moment offered, rather than loyalty
to tradition or the rota of princely seniority that had governed the
Rurikid house since Kyiv. By the time the fourteenth century's raids
and disruptions began to settle into something more orderly, Moscow's
princes had already stopped needing to be robbers - the territory
Daniel and Yuri had taken by stratagem, murder and a friend's bequest
was the base every later expansion, including the very different
methods Yuri's own brother Ivan Kalita would use, was built on.
