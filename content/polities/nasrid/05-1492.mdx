The marriage of Isabella of Castile and Ferdinand of Aragon in 1469, and their
joint accession, removed the thing Granada had lived on for two and a half
centuries: a divided Christian Spain that could always be played against
itself.

The war began in 1482 with a Castilian raid on Alhama, and in the same months
Muhammad XII — Boabdil in the Castilian sources — rose against his own father.
The emirate spent the following decade fighting Castile and itself
simultaneously, with Ferdinand and Isabella negotiating separately with the
rival claimants and supporting whichever was weaker. Boabdil was captured in
1483 and released on terms that made him a Castilian client, which destroyed
his standing among his own people and was presumably the point.

The war was fought town by town with artillery, and it was expensive and slow.
Málaga held out through the summer of 1487 and was punished for it: the
population was killed or enslaved, and towns afterwards surrendered faster,
which was the intention. Almería and Guadix went in 1489. Granada city was
invested through 1491 and negotiated its surrender in November; Boabdil handed
over the Alhambra on 2 January 1492.

The terms were generous on paper. The Capitulations guaranteed the Muslim
population its religion, its law, its property, its language and its customs.
They were kept for about seven years. After a rising in the Albaicín in 1499
they were declared void, and the choice offered was baptism or exile;
conversion became compulsory in Castile in 1502. The converts and their
descendants — the Moriscos — were then prohibited from Arabic in 1566, rose in
the Alpujarras in 1568, were dispersed across Castile after that rebellion was
crushed, and were expelled from Spain entirely between 1609 and 1614.

Perhaps two hundred thousand people left for North Africa at or soon after the
conquest. The families that stayed lasted another century and then did not.

Two notes on the record. The `ended.by` field is empty, because the polity that
took Granada was Castile-and-Aragon, which is not on this site and is outside
the corpus's scope. And the year is the one every reader already knows for a
different reason: Columbus sailed in August 1492 under a commission granted at
Santa Fe, the camp outside Granada that had been built for the siege, by
monarchs who had spent ten years and their whole treasury taking this emirate
and who signed his contract in the months after it fell. The two events are
usually filed in different histories. They happened in the same place, in the
same year, to the same budget.
