In 628 Muhammad went towards Mecca with his followers in pilgrim dress and
stopped at a place called al-Hudaybiyya, outside the sacred area. Quraysh sent
Suhayl ibn Amr to negotiate. What they produced is the clearest evidence on this
record that the thing at Medina had become a state, and it survives with the
argument still in it.

## The wording

Ali was called to write, and Muhammad dictated the opening: *In the name of God,
most gracious most merciful.*

Suhayl stopped him. *I do not know the Merciful,* he said. *Write as we write:
in the name of God.* The Muslims present objected loudly. Suhayl said that in
that case there would be no agreement about anything. Muhammad told Ali to write
it Suhayl's way.

Then he dictated *this is what the Messenger of God has agreed to*, and Suhayl
stopped him again — and his objection was courteous and completely
uncompromising:

> If I knew that you were the Messenger of God, I would not differ from you and
> I would follow you. I prefer your name. Do you then detest your name and the
> name of your father, Muhammad b. Abdullah?

This time the protest was worse. Usayd ibn Hudayr and Sad ibn Ubada took hold of
the scribe's hand and told him not to write anything but *Muhammad the Messenger
of God*, or the sword would settle it — *why would we permit this insult to our
religion?* Muhammad signalled them to be quiet. A Meccan envoy watching this
turned to his colleague and said he had never seen a people so concerned about
their religion.

And then Muhammad gave way a second time. *I am Muhammad son of Abdullah,* he
said. *Write: in the name of God, this is what was agreed between Muhammad son
of Abdullah and Suhayl.*

He signed as a man with a father, not as a prophet.

## The terms

Ten years of no fighting. Both sides secure, with no bribery and no treachery.
Any Arab tribe that wished could enter Muhammad's agreement or Quraysh's, and
Khuzaa immediately joined his while Bakr joined theirs.

Then the clause that nearly broke it: anyone who came to Muhammad from Mecca
without their guardian's permission was to be sent back, while anyone who went
from Muhammad to Quraysh was not. The asymmetry is total and Muhammad accepted
it. He also accepted that he would turn round and go home that year without
performing the pilgrimage, and could return the following year for three days
only, carrying no weapons but a sheathed sword.

Nine men witnessed it and their names were written on the face of the document.
Suhayl said he would keep it. Muhammad said he would. They argued, a second copy
was made, and each side went away holding one.

## Why it mattered

Read as religion the agreement is a humiliation, and some of his own men read it
that way at the time.

Read as politics it is the moment Quraysh conceded the argument. For six years
they had treated Muhammad as a renegade to be crushed. At Hudaybiyya they sent a
plenipotentiary, negotiated clause by clause, took witnesses, executed the
document in duplicate and bound themselves for ten years. States do that; states
do it with other states. Quraysh had recognised a rival power in Arabia even
while refusing to write down what it called itself.

Within two years Mecca was his.
