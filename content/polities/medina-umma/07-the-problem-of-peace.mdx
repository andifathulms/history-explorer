title: The problem peace created

By 631 Muhammad had a difficulty that success had manufactured, and Watt argues
he could see it coming for some time before it arrived.

Arabia's tribes had raided each other. The *razzia* was not only violence: it was
a redistribution mechanism and a demographic one. A tribe that fell short in a
bad season made the difference up from its neighbour. Raiding also killed people,
which in a country of thin margins was part of how the population stayed within
what the land could carry. And it absorbed the energies of a great many young men
who had nothing else to do.

The Pax Islamica switched that off. Tribes inside the system stopped raiding one
another. The consequences followed immediately: more people, because fewer were
dying, and no way for a tribe in temporary need to make good its deficit. There
was also, as Watt puts it, the psychological problem — an outlet was required for
energies that had previously gone into the razzia.

If the peace was going to last, the standard of living had to be maintained, and
that meant finding a new source of wealth.

## The options

Trade was the obvious candidate and Watt thinks it was considered and rejected.
The Medina–Syria traffic was small; the sources say so little about it that it
cannot have been important. Mecca's commerce, once the city came under his rule,
was not remotely large enough to support the numbers now looking to him. And
Watt adds a reason that is not economic at all: trade carried the risk of
reviving exactly the religious attitude that had been the fault of pagan Mecca.

Booty was the other candidate, and it had been doing this work already. In the
early Medinan years plunder mattered enormously, particularly to the emigrants,
and it is likely that a good many men attached themselves to Muhammad because of
it.

But booty had a structural flaw that got worse as he succeeded. Raiding requires
prosperous non-Muslims within reach, and every year there were fewer. As the
peninsula came inside the peace, the supply of legitimate targets contracted
toward zero. Watt states the conclusion plainly: if the whole of Arabia became
Muslim, raiding would remain possible only on the northern frontier.

## What that means

This is the least mystical explanation available for what happened next, and it
comes from inside Muhammad's own lifetime rather than being read back from the
conquests.

A state had been built that abolished the internal economy of raiding and put
nothing in its place except an army with no one left to fight. The expedition to
Tabuk in 631, the northernmost thing he ever attempted, points where the
pressure pointed. He died the following year with the problem unresolved and the
force intact.

The men who inherited it solved it in the direction the geography left open.
