The Zaydi imamate is conventionally dated from 897 to 1962. That is one thousand
and sixty-five years — longer than Byzantium, longer than the Abbasids, longer
than any other polity in this period anywhere.

The number is real, and it does not mean what it appears to mean.

## An office, not a government

What persisted for a thousand years was the imamate as an institution. The imam
was a qualified Alid who had risen and been answered. When no qualified claimant
rose, or when the man who had risen held only a few districts, or when an
Ayyubid, Rasulid or Ottoman administration governed most of Yemen from the coast,
the office did not cease to exist — but it was not running a state in the sense
the word usually carries.

There were decades in which the imamate amounted to a family in the northern
mountains with a claim. There was also the Qasimid century, when it ruled from
Sa'da to Aden.

Both of those are inside the thousand years, and no single figure can distinguish
them.

## Why the figure is still worth having

The temptation is to fix this by picking a smaller number — counting only the
centuries when an imam governed something substantial, and reporting that
instead. It would be a worse answer.

Yemeni historiography treats the imamate as one institution with interruptions,
and it does so for a reason: continuity of the office is what the claimants
themselves asserted and what their followers acted on. A rising in the fifteenth
century that invoked an imamate founded in 897 is a fact about the fifteenth
century, whatever a modern observer thinks of the intervening gaps. Cutting the
span to the periods of effective government would erase precisely the thing that
made the office worth claiming.

So the honest form is the long number, given with the argument attached. Read it
as the duration of an institution rather than of a state, and set it beside
Byzantium's ten centuries only with that firmly in mind — because Byzantium's
figure measures something else entirely, which is an administration that never
once stopped collecting taxes.
