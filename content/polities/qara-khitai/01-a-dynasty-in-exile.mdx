---
title: A dynasty that walked west
drafted_from: biran-2005
phase: formation
---

The Liao were the Khitan dynasty that ruled the northern Chinese frontier for
two centuries under two administrations at once — one for the steppe, one for
the settled prefectures. In 1125 the Jurchen destroyed it and the Jin took its
place.

Yelu Dashi was a member of the Liao imperial clan who had passed the Chinese
examinations. When the state collapsed he did not surrender. He rode north-west
with a couple of hundred followers, gathered what Khitan garrisons and allied
tribes remained on the steppe frontier, and kept going — across Mongolia, past
the Uyghurs of the Tarim, into the Semirechye. In 1134 he took Balasaghun from
the eastern Karakhanids, whose ruler had invited him in to deal with unruly
Qarluq and Qangli tribes and discovered he had invited in a state.

What he founded there is one of the odder objects in medieval Eurasia. It was a
Chinese dynasty. The rulers took the title *gurkhan*, universal khan, but also
Chinese reign names; the chancery worked in Chinese as well as Persian; the
coinage was struck in Chinese form; the dynastic name they used of themselves
was still Liao. Two thousand miles from the nearest Chinese-speaking
administration, ruling a Muslim, Turkic and Persian-speaking population that
had no reason to be impressed by any of it, they kept the whole apparatus.

It is a state that turns up constantly in other people's histories as the power
in the background. The Karakhanids, the first Turkish Muslim dynasty, spent
their last seventy years collecting taxes for it. The Khwarazmshahs made their
empire by throwing its suzerainty off. It is the thing the Ghurids and the
Chagatai inheritance both keep gesturing at, and it is almost never described in
its own right.
