Yelü Dashi died in 1135, twenty years into the empire he had built by
walking his people three thousand miles west rather than surrender to
the Jin. His own son Yilie was not yet of age, and the Liao shi records
what happened next without treating it as anything unusual: the widowed
empress took the regency, ruled the empire in her own name for seven
years, and handed it to her son only when he was old enough to want it.

## Tabuyan

The empress, named Tabuyan and given the Chinese title Kan tien huang
hou, governed under her own reign-name, Xian Qing, from 1135 until
1142. The Liao shi says nothing about her policies in the detail it
gives Yelü Dashi's own campaigns, which is itself a kind of evidence:
a seven-year regency that produced no crisis worth narrating is a
regency that worked. The empire she handed over was intact, still
collecting tribute from the Khwarazmian shahs and the Kara-Khanid
princes Qatwan had made vassals, with no successor's legitimacy
seriously questioned when Yilie finally took the throne himself in
1142.

## Yilie's own reign

Yilie ruled for thirteen years under the reign-name Shao Xing, long
enough to order a census of his own empire - eighty-four thousand five
hundred families counted across the whole of it, the only such figure
the Chinese sources preserve for the Qara Khitai at any date. He died
in 1155 and was canonised, in the Chinese fashion his dynasty had kept
even three thousand miles from where it was first used, as Renzong.

His own son was still a minor. For the second time in twenty years,
the throne of an empire built by conquest passed not to a man but to a
woman ruling in her own name - this time not a widow, but Yilie's own
younger sister.
