---
title: Unur, and four days in July
drafted_from: el-azhari-1997
phase: peak
---

Mu'in al-Din Unur was not a Burid. He was another Turkish mamluk, atabeg to the
last Burid emirs, and from about 1138 he was the government — which is the same
relation to the Burids that Toghtekin had had to the Seljuks, one turn of the
same wheel later, and the reason this site's `peak` field names a man from
outside the dynasty.

His policy was his predecessors' policy carried out with more skill: keep
Damascus out of Zangid hands by staying on terms with Jerusalem, and out of
Frankish hands by being worth more independent. He held it against Zangi, and
when Zangi was murdered in 1146 he held it against Nur al-Din.

Then in July 1148 the Second Crusade arrived. Louis VII and Conrad III had
brought two royal armies east, achieved nothing in Anatolia, and at a council
at Acre chose their target: not Aleppo, whose ruler was the counter-crusade, but
Damascus, whose ruler was their ally and tributary. They camped in the orchards
west of the city, moved to the eastern side where there was no water, and left
after four days.

It is one of the more complete failures in the period and its consequence was
exact. Unur, besieged by the power he had been paying, called Nur al-Din for
help — and got it, at the price of an understanding. The Damascus–Jerusalem
alignment that had preserved the emirate for forty years could not survive the
Franks having besieged it. Unur died the following year. The city held out six
more, with a Zangid army in the north, a Zangid marriage in the family, and no
one left to balance against.
