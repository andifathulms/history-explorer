---
title: The year the city decided
drafted_from: el-azhari-1997
phase: expansion
---

Taj al-Muluk Buri's vizier, al-Mazdaghani, brought the Nizari Isma'ilis into
Damascus. They were given a house in the city and, in 1129, the frontier
fortress of Banyas — the standard Nizari transaction, a stronghold in exchange
for service, which this site records elsewhere as the whole basis of their
territorial existence.

Damascus did not accept it. Al-Mazdaghani was killed at the citadel, and the
population, led by the ahdath — the armed militia of the city quarters — hunted
the Nizaris through the streets and killed them in numbers the chronicles put in
the thousands. The Nizari commander at Banyas, seeing what was coming, handed
the fortress to the Franks and left.

Three things follow, and they are the reason this is a turning point rather than
an atrocity in a list. The emirate lost a frontier castle to the kingdom of
Jerusalem without a siege. Isma'ili influence in Damascus ended permanently, and
the Syrian Nizaris withdrew to the Jabal Bahra where this site's Nizari page
finds them. And it was demonstrated, in public, that the deciding power in
Damascus was the city itself: an emir's vizier had made a policy, and the
militia and the notables had reversed it by force in an afternoon.

Buri did not forget who had won. The Nizaris did not forget either, and in 1131
they sent two men who stabbed him. He died of the wounds the following year.
