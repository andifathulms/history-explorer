title: Paying the Franks
drafted_from: el-azhari-1997

Damascus spent most of the Burid half-century in some arrangement with the
kingdom of Jerusalem: truces, joint patrols of the Hawran, a shared revenue
agreement over the farmland between them, and for long stretches an annual
tribute paid by the Muslim city to the Christian one.

The modern reflex is to read this as collaboration and the medieval reflex was
to say so loudly, mostly from Aleppo. The Burid case was strategic and is not
hard to state: the threat to Damascus's independence was never really the
kingdom of Jerusalem, which wanted the frontier quiet and the tribute paid. It
was the Turkish power in the north — first the Seljuk sultans, then Zangi of
Mosul and Aleppo, who spent the 1130s and 1140s trying to take Damascus and was
publicly the champion of the jihad while doing it. A city that could not beat
Zangi alone allied with the only other power that also did not want Zangi in
Damascus.

The site's revenue field does not record the tribute, because coding rule 6 is
explicit that money paid out is not a revenue basis, and it would be recorded on
the kingdom of Jerusalem's page if this corpus had one. It is worth noticing
that the absence is a limit: from inside this site's data, the single most
characteristic fact about Burid Damascus is invisible.

We know the period well because of one man. Ibn al-Qalanisi was a Damascene
notable who twice served as ra'is of the city and kept a chronicle from 1056 to
1160 — the only continuous contemporary Arabic account of the First and Second
Crusades written by someone who was there, in the city, in office. Almost
everything on this page passes through him, which is worth stating: the Burids
are legible because a bureaucrat kept notes.
