title: A polity that became the centre
drafted_from: daryaee-2009

Around 205 a man called Papak, whose family held the temple at Istakhr, deposed
the sitting king of Persis and took the throne. He wrote to Artabanus IV, the
Parthian king of kings, asking that his son be confirmed as his successor.
Artabanus refused.

The son was Ardashir. He took Persis anyway, then the neighbouring kingdoms of
Kerman and Elymais and the Gulf coast, refused tribute, and in 224 met Artabanus
in battle at Hormozdgan and killed him. This corpus's Sasanian record begins
there, and its Parthian record ends there with the type `dynastic replacement`.

What this record has to say is about its own `ended` field, which reads `gradual
absorption` and does not fit.

Persis was not conquered: no foreign army took it. It did not fragment. It was
not usurped in 224, because the man who ended it as a separate polity was its own
king. It was not replaced by a different house, because the house continued. What
happened is that a sub-kingdom's ruler became king of kings, and the sub-kingdom
stopped being a distinct thing because it had become the core of an empire — the
province the new dynasty took its name from, whose fire temple was its ancestral
shrine and whose language became the empire's administrative one.

None of the six values in the closed vocabulary describes that. `gradual
absorption` is the nearest and the comment on the field says so, because the
alternative is to type this as conquest and imply somebody arrived.

The corpus does not carry an edge from this record to the Sasanian one either,
and the reason is the same rule that governs the Ayyubid family's origins in
Shaddadid Arran and the Bagratuni's four-century wait between two Armenian
kingdoms. Coding rule 1 excludes dynastic descent on its own: being the family
that a later polity came from is a genealogy, and genealogies are not
successions. The succession edge is Parthian to Sasanian, because that is where
the transfer of authority happened.

What this page is for is the five hundred years before it — which no imperial
narrative covers, which is known almost entirely from silver, and which is where
the family that produced Ardashir was sitting, holding a temple, the whole time.
