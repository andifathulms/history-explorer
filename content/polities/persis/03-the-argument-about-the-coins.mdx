---
title: What a coin can and cannot show
drafted_from: wiesehofer-1996
phase: aside
---

A coin is a very good witness to some questions and a very bad one to others,
and it is worth being precise about which is which, because for five hundred
years of Persis the coins are nearly all there is.

They are excellent for sequence. In Bactria, where no text survives at all,
overstrikes and hoards have yielded twenty-odd Greek kings, their order, their
mints and their approximate dates — a whole dynasty reconstructed from metal.
They are good for audience: the Indo-Greek kings issued bilingual pieces in
Greek and Prakrit on Indian weight standards, and the choice of standard says
plainly enough who was expected to spend them.

The Persis coins are being asked for something harder than either. They are
being asked for an intention. The Aramaic legend instead of Greek; the fire
temple instead of a Greek god; the Persian dress; the winged figure above. Every
one of those is a choice, and the question is what the choice meant to the
people making it — which is not a thing metal can testify to.

Wiesehöfer's discipline is to build the account from Persian material rather
than from the Greek and Roman narratives, and to be careful about what the
material will bear. The Achaemenid-revival reading is attractive precisely
because we know what happened next: a dynasty from this province founded an
empire that presented itself as a restoration. But reasoning backwards from an
outcome is the oldest error in the subject. It is the same move that produced
two centuries of writing about Achaemenid decline, on no better evidence than
the fact that the empire eventually fell — and an empire falling is not proof
that it had been failing.

The same caution applies here in reverse. A province that produced a dynasty is
not thereby a province that had been waiting five hundred years to produce one.
The fire on the coins is certainly a fire; the priestly office is certainly a
priestly office; and whether the men who struck the silver thought of themselves
as keeping an interrupted empire warm is a question they did not answer and we
cannot answer for them.
