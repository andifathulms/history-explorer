---
title: A second kingdom in Sapaudia
drafted_from: wood-1994
phase: formation
---

In 443 Rome settled the Burgundian survivors of the Rhineland
catastrophe in Sapaudia, the Alpine country lying north of Geneva -
territory to be divided between the newcomers and the Gallo-Roman
population already living there, on terms the sources describe only in
outline. Unlike the doomed foothold at Worms, this settlement held. Over
the following decades the Burgundians expanded their own reach down the
Rhône valley, absorbing territory around Lyon and Geneva and, by the
460s and 470s, functioning less as a tolerated enclave than as one of the
military powers Roman authorities in Gaul had to negotiate with directly.

## A family that ran an empire's armies from a Rhône-valley throne

The kingdom's own royal family, later called the Gibichungs, rose with
it. Gundioc, a Burgundian king with a marriage tie to the Roman
kingmaker Ricimer, held the rank of magister militum - the empire's
senior military command - in Gaul during the 460s, based in part around
Lyon and Geneva. His brother Chilperic I held the same office after him,
exercising real authority over the Rhône region even as the western
imperial throne itself changed hands repeatedly under Ricimer's own
influence in Italy. A Burgundian king, by this point, was as likely to be
found securing a Roman general's title as extending his own kingdom by
conquest - a pattern that would define the whole of the next generation's
politics under Gundioc's own son, Gundobad.

## A kingdom built from land division rather than land tax

What made this second kingdom durable, where the first had not been,
was probably as much its terms of settlement as its military standing.
The kingdom's own later law code describes grants of land, dependents
and woodland made to Burgundian settlers in stages stretching back at
least two generations - evidence of an arrangement in which the
Burgundians took a real, permanent stake in the territory they held
rather than merely camping on tolerated Roman ground, the way Guntiarius's
doomed Rhineland settlement may have done.
