Godomar held the Burgundian throne for a decade after Vézeronce, the
reprieve his own victory had won him - but the brothers who had divided
Chlodomer's kingdom rather than let it pass to him alone had not
forgotten the Rhône valley their brother died trying to take. Childebert
and Chlothar, two of Clovis's surviving sons, moved against Godomar in
534, finishing what Chlodomer's own death at Vézeronce had only
postponed.

## A kingdom absorbed rather than destroyed

This second Frankish campaign succeeded where the first, cut short by
Chlodomer's own death, had failed. Godomar was overthrown, and his
kingdom was divided between his Frankish conquerors rather than left
under any Burgundian ruler of its own - the end of an independent
Burgundian crown that had, in one form or another, existed for over a
century since the Sapaudia settlement first gave the survivors of
Guntiarius's destroyed Rhineland kingdom a second chance at one.

## A name that outlasted the throne that carried it

The kingdom's own institutions did not vanish with its last king. Frankish
rulers governing the former Burgundian territory continued to apply the
Liber Constitutionum to their Burgundian subjects for generations, the
same code Gundobad had begun and Sigismund had completed still
governing daily disputes over land and inheritance long after the crown
that issued it was gone. The region itself kept the Burgundian name into
the Merovingian kingdom's own internal geography and long after, a
linguistic afterlife the conquered kingdom's own royal line did not share.
