Sigismund's brother Godomar took the Burgundian throne in the wreckage
Chlodomer's invasion had left behind, and within a year he faced the same
Frankish king again. Chlodomer renewed his attack on Burgundy in 524,
evidently expecting to finish what his first campaign had started.
Godomar met him in the field rather than retreating before him, and at
Vézeronce the two armies fought a battle that ended the Frankish threat
far more decisively than Sigismund's own death a year earlier had ended
the Burgundian kingdom.

## A Frankish king killed in his own moment of victory

Chlodomer was killed in the fighting at Vézeronce. His death threw the
Franks' own plans into disarray far more thoroughly than it settled
anything for Burgundy: rather than completing the conquest Sigismund's
capture had seemed to promise, Chlodomer's own kingdom of Orléans was
carved up among his surviving brothers, and Frankish pressure on Burgundy
eased for a decade in the resulting confusion.

## A reprieve, not a recovery

Godomar's victory kept an independent Burgundian throne alive under his
own rule, but it did not restore the kingdom Gundobad had once built.
Burgundy in the years after Vézeronce was a kingdom governed by the last
of Sigismund's own brothers rather than a settled dynasty with an
uncontested line of succession, still recovering from a crisis - a
murdered heir, a captured and executed king, an invasion survived only
by killing the invader - that had unfolded within the span of two years
rather than being inflicted by any single catastrophic defeat.
