Gundioc's son Gundobad did not begin his career as a Burgundian king at
all. He went to Italy as right-hand man to Ricimer, the Roman general
and kingmaker whose own marriage tie bound him to the Burgundian royal
house, and rose with his patron to the summit of Roman military politics
at exactly the moment the western empire's throne had become Ricimer's
own to dispose of. When Ricimer turned against the emperor he had himself helped install
some five years earlier, it was Gundobad, not Ricimer, who carried out
the killing: in 472 Gundobad killed the emperor Anthemius outright,
ending his reign and his life in the same act.

## An emperor made, then unmade in turn

Ricimer's own death soon afterward left Gundobad his political heir in
Italy. It was Gundobad who arranged the elevation of Glycerius to the
western throne after Ricimer was gone - a Burgundian prince, born to a
kingdom on the Rhône, personally deciding who would wear the purple in
Rome. That authority did not survive a change of allegiance from
Constantinople: when the eastern court's own candidate, Julius Nepos,
displaced Glycerius in 474, Gundobad's own family split over whether to
accept him, and Gundobad himself withdrew from Italy back to Gaul rather
than serve an emperor imposed over his own choice.

## A prince who came home to a throne

Gundobad's return to Burgundian territory in 474 was not a retirement.
His uncle Chilperic I still held real power in the Rhône valley, and
within Gundobad's own generation the kingdom's rule would pass, through
inheritance and internal rivalry among Gundioc's several sons, into
Gundobad's own hands - carrying into his kingship in Gaul the same
instinct for Roman office and Roman legitimacy that had made him,
however briefly, the man who decided which emperor Italy would obey.
