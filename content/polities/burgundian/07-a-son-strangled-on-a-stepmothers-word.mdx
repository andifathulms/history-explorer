title: A son strangled on a stepmother's word

Sigismund had a son, Sigistrix, from an earlier marriage before his own
accession, and a second wife whose influence over the king Gregory of
Tours holds directly responsible for what followed. In 522, stirred up -
so the tradition goes - by that second wife, Sigismund had Sigistrix
strangled. No source used here records what specific grievance or fear
drove the killing, only that it was sudden, and that it was carried out
on the king's own order against his own firstborn son.

## A penance that could not undo the killing

Sigismund's own reaction suggests he recognized immediately what he had
done. Rather than defend the killing as a political necessity, he
treated it as a sin demanding atonement, and set off for his own
foundation at Agaune to do penance there - the same monastery he had
built to sustain perpetual praise of God now serving as the place a king
went to answer for having murdered his own child. Whatever consolation
the penance offered Sigismund personally, it did nothing to steady a
kingdom whose own line of succession he had just thrown into open
question by killing the son who would otherwise have inherited it.

## A crisis a neighbour was watching for

The killing of Sigistrix could not be contained within the Burgundian
court. Word of a kingdom in turmoil - a king doing penance for
infanticide, an heir gone, a dynasty visibly unsettled - reached
neighbouring courts within months, and at least one Frankish king was
already positioned to treat the crisis as an opportunity rather than a
Burgundian family tragedy that was none of his concern.
