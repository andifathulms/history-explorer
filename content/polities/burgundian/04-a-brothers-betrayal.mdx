By the 490s Gundobad shared the Burgundian throne with his own brother
Godegisel, ruling jointly over the country along the Rhône and the
Saône. The two brothers were, by then, already fighting each other, and
Godegisel saw his chance in the rising Frankish king to the north: he
sent Clovis a secret offer of yearly tribute in exchange for help killing
or expelling Gundobad. Clovis agreed, and when Gundobad - unaware of his
own brother's treachery - asked Godegisel to join him against the
advancing Franks, Godegisel marched to the appointed meeting point and
turned on him instead. The combined Frankish and Godegiselian armies
crushed Gundobad's own forces at Dijon, and Gundobad fled south along the
Rhône to Avignon while Godegisel entered Vienne as though he already
ruled the whole kingdom.

## A siege talked away rather than fought

Clovis pursued Gundobad and settled in to besiege him at Avignon,
intending to drag him out and kill him. Gundobad, terrified, turned to
his own adviser Aridius, who proposed a ruse: he would pretend to
defect to Clovis's own service, worm his way into the Frankish king's
confidence, and then talk him out of the siege from the inside. Clovis
took the bait, valued Aridius as a wise and entertaining counsellor, and
was persuaded that ravaging Gundobad's own fields and vineyards gained
him nothing a yearly tribute would not gain more cheaply. Clovis took the
advice, accepted Gundobad's submission as a tributary, and led his army
home.

## A debt collected through an aqueduct

Freed of the Franks, Gundobad soon stopped paying the tribute and turned
his own restored strength on Godegisel, besieging him in turn at Vienne.
When food ran short inside the city, Godegisel expelled the common
people to conserve it for his own household - among them the artisan who
maintained the city's aqueduct, who went straight to Gundobad in fury
and showed his men how to force the aqueduct's own sealed vent with iron
bars and enter the city from below. Gundobad's soldiers came up behind
the defenders on the walls, opened the gates from within, and took
Vienne in a single assault. Godegisel died with the city's own Arian
bishop in the church they had fled to for refuge; Gundobad spared
Godegisel's Frankish garrison but exiled them to the Visigothic court at
Toulouse, executed the Burgundian nobles who had backed his brother, and
took back the whole kingdom - after which, Gregory of Tours records, he
set about establishing milder laws for his own Burgundian subjects so
that they would not oppress the Romans living among them.
