Christianity did not end the fighting that had made Thakombau's own
name, and the war-weariness that had settled Bau's contest with Rewa
in 1855 did not last forever either. In the years after his baptism,
Thakombau's own government - built, by his own account, on the same
matanitu he had once named for his European ministers - waged further
campaigns against tribes still holding out for the old order, forces
trained and led in part by white officers now serving a king whose
own authority had come to rest as much on foreign administration as
on Fijian custom.

By 1874 that reliance had become dependence outright. Sir Hercules
Robinson, Governor of New South Wales, negotiated a Deed of Cession
placing Fiji under the sovereignty of Queen Victoria, and once
Thakombau and the leading chiefs had signed it, the Union Jack was
raised and Fiji became a Crown Colony. Robinson invited the king he
had just dispossessed of sovereignty to visit him in Sydney as a
guest, and sent HMS Dido to carry him there with a large retinue of
his own people.

## A third of the population

The voyage cost Fiji more than the cession itself had. One of
Thakombau's attendants came home from Sydney carrying measles, a
disease the islands had never encountered before, and it spread
through the group with a speed and mortality nothing in Fijian
experience had prepared anyone for. By the early months of 1875 it
had killed roughly a third of the population - among them Ro
Vuthango, a redoubtable old fighting chief in the island's own
interior who had led his own people for decades and now succumbed to
an enemy no army could meet. The epidemic devastated the tribes still
resisting Christian and colonial authority worst of all, and the
resulting unrest, blamed by many on the anger of ancestral gods
neglected under the new order, fed directly into the frontier war the
colony's first governor fought to subdue the interior the following
year. The kingdom Thakombau had spent his whole life building ended,
in the end, not on a battlefield but on a warship's guest deck,
carrying home a sickness that did more damage than any of his own
wars ever had.
