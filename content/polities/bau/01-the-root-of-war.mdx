---
title: The root of war
drafted_from: williams-1858
phase: formation
---

As late as 1800, supremacy in Fiji belonged not to Bau but to Verata,
whose sway ran over part of the main island and several of the
smaller ones. Bau at that date was ruled by Na-Ulivou, who had
already fought off a challenge from thirty rival sons of a chief
named Savou contesting his own succession, and been honoured
afterward with the title Na Vu-ni-valu - the root of war - a title
his successors bore ever since. Aided by white settlers and the
firearms they brought, Na-Ulivou turned that same energy against
Verata itself, stripped it of its dependencies, and left its sovereign
little more than his own town to rule. By the time Na-Ulivou died in
1829, Bau, not Verata, was the power the rest of Fiji had to reckon
with.

His brother Tanoa succeeded him as Vunivalu. The inheritance did not
stay settled for long. Tanoa's reign of twenty-three years was filled,
almost from its start, with rebellious subjects and rebellious sons,
and at its worst a powerful and far-spreading rebellion drove him from
Bau entirely, forcing him into an exile that lasted years while his
enemies held his own chief city. His young son Seru was allowed to
remain on the island, dismissed by the rebels as too young to be worth
killing - a judgment that would prove to be the single worst mistake
of the whole uprising.

Tanoa himself, once restored, showed exactly what kind of ruler the
rebellion had been fought against. Old age eventually forced him to
hand the active duties of his position to a son acting as regent, but
his cruelty did not dim with the years: a cousin who begged his
forgiveness for some offence was instead mutilated and eaten alive
before him, and a son sentenced to death by the club was, when the
first blow failed to kill him, finished at Tanoa's own furious
insistence. This was the man - proud, implacable, and by his own
subjects' testimony still feared even in extreme old age - whose
throne a boy too young to be thought dangerous would one day win back
for him.
