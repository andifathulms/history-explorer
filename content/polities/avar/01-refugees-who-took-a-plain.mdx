The Avars entered the Romans' field of view in 558, arriving as
refugees fleeing the westward expansion of a rival Turkic
federation in central Asia. They moved quickly: within a few
years they had defeated the tribes bordering the Black Sea and
extended their reach west along the Danube, and Emperor Justinian
offered them land to settle - Pannonia Secunda, a former Roman
province then held by a patchwork of Lombards, Gepids and Heruls
rather than by Rome itself.

<Figure id="av-stirrups" />

## The Gepids crushed, the Lombards driven out

The Avars took that land by the same method that had brought them
west in the first place: alliance and force. Joining the Lombards
against the Gepids, who had disputed mastery of Pannonia with them
for years, the Avars helped crush the Gepid kingdom outright in
567. The victory unsettled their own allies as much as their
enemies - the Lombards, now facing an Avar horde as their only
neighbour on a plain the Avars had just helped clear, judged the
prospect bad enough to abandon Pannonia for Italy altogether the
following year. The Avars simply moved into the space the Lombards
left behind, and Pannonia, taken by conquest and vacated by fear,
became the base from which every later Avar campaign - against the
Franks in the west, against Constantinople in the east - would be
launched.
