No chronicle picks up the Avars' story where the 626 siege left off.
Byzantine writers, who had described the failed siege in such close
detail, largely stopped mentioning the Avars afterward, and the
khaganate's own history for the century and a half between that
defeat and its first clashes with Charlemagne's armies has to be
read out of buried coins rather than narrated events. What that
evidence shows is not a single story of decline, but at least two
distinct turns.

## A tribute that stopped, then started again

Byzantine gold coins had been flowing into Avar territory for
decades before 626, almost certainly as the annual tribute the
Chagan had extorted to keep the peace; the siege, fought explicitly
to end that arrangement, marks the point at which the flow of gold
into Avar graves stops abruptly. For twenty-five to thirty years
afterward, next to no Byzantine coin reached Avar territory at all.
Then, from around 650, gold and silver coin begins turning up again
in Avar burials - too much of it to explain by ordinary trade alone,
and a strong sign that some form of renewed relationship with
Constantinople, tribute or gift-giving, had resumed.

## Refugees from a broken Bulgar kingdom

Part of what filled that gap was an infusion of new people. Kuvrat,
ruler of an Onogur Bulgar polity Byzantine sources call Great
Bulgaria, had been Constantinople's own ally against the Avars; when
he died, probably in the 650s or 660s, his sons could not hold his
kingdom together. One of them led his own followers west across the
Danube and into the Avar khaganate itself, reinforcing Avar numbers
just as another of Kuvrat's sons, Asparukh, was leading a separate
group south to found what would become the first Bulgarian kingdom
on Byzantine soil.

## An embassy in 678

The clearest single point of contact in the whole period comes in
678, when an Avar embassy reached Constantinople to congratulate
Emperor Constantine IV on his victory over an Arab siege of his own
capital. Coins struck for Constantine IV between 674 and 681 have
turned up in Avar territory in a pattern that fits neatly with gifts
carried home by that returning embassy - whether or not it was the
first Avar contact with the empire since 626, it stands as proof
that a Chagan was again treating with Constantinople as an equal
worth congratulating, a century and a half before the same khaganate
would meet an enemy it could not outlast.
