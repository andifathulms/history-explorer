For two decades the Avars held Pannonia without pressing Roman
territory directly, but that changed when Sirmium - one of the
great Roman cities of the middle Danube, standing between the Sava
and the river itself - fell to a long Avar siege in 581 or 582.
The Romans bought an end to the war on humiliating terms,
agreeing to deposit eighty thousand gold coins a year with the
Chagan, paid out in silver and embroidered cloth, "as if a prize
for excellence" in the historian's own bitter phrase for a treaty
that rewarded the party that had broken the peace.

## The Chagan's contempt for Roman gifts

The peace did not last two years. Before it broke down, the Chagan
demonstrated exactly how little the arrangement had satisfied him.
Hearing rumours of the enormous animals the Romans kept, he
demanded to see one, and the emperor sent him the finest elephant
in the imperial stables; the Chagan took one look and sent it
straight back, whether from fear or contempt the source does not
say. He then demanded a gold couch to match his new standing, and
when the emperor had one made and sent to him, the Chagan returned
that too, as though it were something cheap. He followed both
rebuffs with a demand for twenty thousand additional gold coins a
year on top of the existing tribute, and when the emperor refused
in anger, the Chagan threw his oaths to the winds and went to war.

## Singidunum, Anchialus, and a threat to the Long Walls

Avar forces took Singidunum by surprise that same summer, catching
its people at the harvest, and went on to destroy Augustae and
Viminacium before besieging Anchialus on the Black Sea coast -
reportedly sparing its hot baths only because the Chagan's own
harem wished to use them. When Roman envoys came to negotiate, one
of them, Comentiolus, answered the Chagan's arrogance with an
open rebuke to his face, accusing him of forgetting the "many
royal gifts and Roman kindness" his ancestors had received. The
Chagan was unmoved: he answered the embassy by threatening to
tear down the Long Walls of Constantinople itself, the outer
defence line barely thirty miles from the capital. The threat was
not yet carried out, but it announced, more plainly than any
treaty violation before it, how far the Avars now believed their
reach could extend.
