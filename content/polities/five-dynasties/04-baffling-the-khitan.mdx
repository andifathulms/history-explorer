The Khitan king who had just finished starving the Later Jin into surrender
held the northern capital in person for a short time afterward, weighing
whether to rule China directly or install a puppet of his own choosing. He
died before he could settle on either course. Guo Wei, left by the dynasty's
founder as chief adviser to his son, had already spent the intervening years
winning several victories over renewed Khitan incursions from Liaodong -
proof that Wei's earlier vassalage had not been the only available answer
to Khitan pressure, only the one its own founder had chosen.

## A throne offered rather than seized

In the vacuum the Khitan king's death left behind, Liu Zhiyuan - Guo Wei's
own patron and a trusted companion of the dynasty that had just fallen - was
placed on the throne by what Boulger calls the public voice rather than by
any formal act of conquest. It was the closest any of the five transitions
came to a genuine consensus choice rather than a naked seizure, though the
underlying mechanism was identical to every transition before it: an army,
and the men who commanded its loyalty, deciding who would rule next.

## Four years, then the same story again

Liu Zhiyuan died within two years of founding the dynasty, and his son Yin
Di turned the security Guo Wei's victories had bought him toward "reckless"
indulgence rather than governance, in Boulger's judgment - a court
squandering exactly the kind of borrowed time none of its four predecessors
had ever been given twice. When Guo Wei returned from his own further
victories against the Khitan to acclamations that made his own ambitions
impossible to keep hidden any longer, Yin Di read the danger correctly and
fled his own capital rather than wait for it. He was recognised and killed
by some of Guo Wei's own soldiers in a village outside the city before the
new reign had even been formally declared. The dynasty Liu Zhiyuan had been
carried to by popular acclaim ended, within four years, exactly the way
every other dynasty in the interregnum had: an army preferred someone else.
