This record's `peak` ruler is Sri Baduga Maharaja, who reigned from about 1482 to
1521 from Pakuan Pajajaran, an inland capital in the hills behind what is now
Bogor.

He is attested by a stone. The Batutulis inscription, set up by his son, records
his consecration, the works he ordered — an artificial lake, roads, a moat — and
his titles. It is a short text and it is the firmest single piece of evidence
this record has for any of its rulers, which is why the `founder` and `last`
fields on this page are null and this one is not.

In Sundanese tradition he is Prabu Siliwangi, and he is not a historical figure
at all. He is the great king of West Java: a founder of the world, a man who
became a tiger, the ancestor whom Sundanese genealogies run to, the subject of a
body of story that has almost nothing to do with the inscription.

## Two people with one name

This site codes the inscription and does not code the legend, and the reason is
coding rule 1 applied to rulers rather than to claims: a name entered with a
reign has to be a name somebody dated.

But the legend is the more consequential of the two, and this record cannot say
so anywhere except here. Siliwangi is why the Sundanese remember having had a
kingdom. He is on the currency of a province, in the name of an army division, in
the name of a university, in the names of streets and hotels and football clubs.
The historical Sri Baduga reigned for thirty-nine years and lost nothing.

## The forty years after him

The record is short and the sequence is fast.

In 1522 his successor signed the treaty with the Portuguese that this record's
next chapter is about. In 1527 the port went. In 1579 the capital went. Fifty-eight
years from the inscription to the end of the last Indic kingdom on Java.

`span.end` reads 1579, and there is no `last` ruler on this record because this
site has not opened a source that fixes a name and a reign to whoever was in
Pakuan when Banten arrived. Six records in this region carry a null in one of the
three ruler slots — Srivijaya, Kediri, Pasai, Patani, Tidore and this one — and
this is one of only two where the null falls on the ruler of a kingdom whose end
has a documented year and a named besieger. The other is Pasai's, and that
record's fourth chapter is about it.
