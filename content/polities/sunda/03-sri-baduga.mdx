Sri Baduga Maharaja reigned from about 1482 to 1521 at Pakuan Pajajaran, an
inland capital in the hills behind what is now Bogor.

He is attested by a stone. The Batutulis inscription, set up by his son,
records his consecration, his titles, and the works he ordered — an artificial
lake, roads, a moat. It is a short text, and it is the firmest single piece of
evidence this kingdom has for any of its rulers.

In Sundanese tradition he is somebody else entirely. He is Prabu Siliwangi: the
great king of West Java, a founder of the world, a man who became a tiger, the
ancestor to whom Sundanese genealogies run, the subject of a body of story that
has almost nothing to do with the inscription.

## Two people with one name

The historical Sri Baduga reigned for thirty-nine years and lost nothing. That
is all anyone can responsibly say about him, and it comes from one carved
stone.

The legendary one is the consequential figure. Siliwangi is why the Sundanese
remember having had a kingdom at all. He is on the currency of a province, in
the name of an army division, in the name of a university, in the names of
streets and hotels and football clubs. A stone gives us thirty-nine years and a
moat; the story gives a people its past.

Both are worth having, and they should not be blended. A tradition that turns a
king into a tiger is not a corrupted historical account, it is a different kind
of thing — and the temptation to reconcile the two, to find the "real"
Siliwangi behind the legend, tends to produce a figure who is neither.

## The forty years after him

The sequence after the inscription is fast.

In 1522 his successor signed the treaty with the Portuguese. In 1527 the port
went. In 1579 the capital went. Fifty-eight years from the stone to the end of
the last Indic kingdom on Java.

Nobody knows who was in Pakuan when Banten arrived. As at Pasai, the last ruler
of a kingdom with a documented end and a named besieger goes unnamed — the
people writing at the time were interested in the harbour, the treaty and the
campaign, and not in the man they were taking the country from.
