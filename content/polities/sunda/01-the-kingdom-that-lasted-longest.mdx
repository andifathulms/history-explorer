Every Indic polity on Java in this corpus ends in the same century. Majapahit is
absorbed by Demak by 1527. The Hindu-Buddhist courts of the eastern salient go
down under Trenggana's campaigns in the 1540s.

Sunda lasted until 1579.

It held the western third of the island — the Sundanese highlands and the coast
on both the Java Sea and the Sunda strait — through the whole of the Islamisation
of the rest of it, and it belongs to no part of the sequence running down the
Brantas. Kediri, Singhasari, second-Kediri, Majapahit, Demak, Pajang, Mataram:
seven hundred years and seven records, each one inside the last before replacing
it, all of them east of the Citarum.
Sunda is the other Java, and the only other record here from it is Banten, the
sultanate that ended this one.

## Where this record starts, and how firmly

669, and the page should say what that is worth.

It is the date the Sundanese chronicle tradition gives, after Tarumanagara, and
it is a traditional date of the kind this site has declined to use elsewhere. The
Ternate record starts in 1486 rather than at the beginning of its king list; the
Tidore record starts in 1521 for the same reason; the Medang record starts at an
inscription in 732.

Sunda is inconsistent with those, and deliberately so, because the alternative
here is worse. Inscriptional attestation of Sunda's kings begins in the eleventh
century and the capital this record names is fifteenth-century, so a documented
start date would put the span at 1030 and cut off three and a half centuries the
sources do describe, just less firmly. The traditional date is on the record with
this paragraph attached rather than a firmer date with nine hundred years of the
kingdom silently dropped.

Both approaches are defensible and this site now uses both. What it does not do
is use either one without saying so.
