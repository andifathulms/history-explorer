Sunda ended in 1579 and the Sundanese did not.

They are the second largest ethnic group in Indonesia, something like forty
million people, with their own language, their own literature, their own
gamelan tuning and their own account of what happened to them. That account is
organised around two things, and neither of them is the fall of the capital.

## Bubat

There is no street named for Gajah Mada in Bandung.

That is the fourteenth century still being argued about. Sundanese and Javanese
accounts of what happened at Bubat in 1357 differ about who was at fault in
ways that map exactly onto which side is telling it, and the tradition against
Sundanese women marrying Javanese men — variously observed, variously denied,
and much discussed — is attributed by the people who observe it to the same
event.

Six and a half centuries is a long time to stay angry about a wedding that did
not happen.

## Siliwangi

The other is Siliwangi: the king who is said not to have died but to have
withdrawn, with his people, into the forest, and to be there still.

A kingdom whose end nobody can date precisely, whose last ruler nobody can
name, and whose people were converted by the polity that destroyed it produced
a story in which the king refused to convert and refused to die, and left.

That is not evidence about 1579. It is evidence about what being conquered by
your neighbours does to what you tell your children afterwards — and it is the
part of Sunda that outlasted the kingdom by four hundred years and forty
million people.
