The Armenian nobility was organised in *nakharar* houses: hereditary lineages
holding defined territories with their own fortresses, their own retainers and
their own cavalry, whose obligations to a king were customary rather than
granted. The Bagratuni were one of them. So were the Artsruni, who ruled
Vaspurakan around Lake Van and took the title of king themselves in 908; so were
the Siwni in the south-east, the Mamikonian who had produced Armenia's great
military families under the Sasanians, and a dozen more.

<Figure id="aghtamar-relief" />

A king in that system does not create his nobility and cannot dismiss it. He is
the senior house, holding a title from outside — which is what the crown from
Baghdad was for — and his authority over the others is a negotiation conducted
annually. His army is not his: it is what the other houses owe him under an
obligation he did not invent and cannot revoke.

The consequence follows directly. A kingdom that is a settlement among houses
divides whenever the settlement is renegotiated, and this one divided repeatedly:
Vaspurakan under the Artsruni from 908, Kars as a separate Bagratid kingdom from
963, Lori-Tashir from 979, Syunik afterwards. By the eleventh century there were
four or five crowned Armenian rulers, all of them related, and none able to
command the others. Both of the empires on either side found that useful, and
neither of them had to engineer it.

<Figure id="sanahin" />

The church is the other permanent institution and it cuts the other way. The
Armenian Apostolic Church had rejected the Council of Chalcedon in the sixth
century, which made Armenians heretics in Constantinople's eyes and made
Byzantine protection something to be feared as much as sought — Byzantine policy
in Armenia consistently included pressure towards Chalcedonian conformity, and
Armenian resistance to it is a constant of the period. It is why a kingdom
squeezed between a caliphate and an empire kept choosing the caliphate: Baghdad
wanted tribute, and Constantinople wanted the church.
