---
title: The kingdom given away
drafted_from: hovannisian-1997

Basil II spent the early eleventh century pushing the Byzantine frontier east,
and he did it by document as much as by campaign.

In 1021 Senekerim Artsruni, king of Vaspurakan, handed his kingdom to the
emperor and accepted estates in Cappadocia in exchange, moving himself and a
reported fourteen thousand of his people west. The reason he gave was Turkmen
raiding, which had begun appearing on the eastern frontier and which nobody yet
understood the scale of.

The following year Hovhannes-Smbat of Ani, under Byzantine military pressure and
with his own brother Ashot IV disputing the throne, undertook to leave the
kingdom to the emperor on his death. He then reigned for another nineteen years.

When he died in 1041 Constantinople produced the document. The Armenians at Ani
crowned Gagik II instead; Byzantium invaded, failed twice, then invited the young
king to Constantinople to negotiate and did not let him leave. Ani was
surrendered in 1045 and Gagik spent the rest of his life on an estate in
Cappadocia, where he was eventually murdered.

That is the ending, and this site records it as `conquest` by Byzantium because
that is what happened, while noting that the instrument was a will.

The strategic judgement behind it deserves to be stated without hindsight and
then with it. Byzantium in the 1020s was the strongest state in the region and
had just finished absorbing Bulgaria; annexing the Armenian kingdoms removed a
zone of quarrelling client-kings and replaced it with imperial administration and
imperial troops. It was coherent policy.

What it also did was dismantle the Armenian noble military system on the frontier
and replace it with a Byzantine provincial establishment that was, twenty years
later, comprehensively unable to do the job. The Seljuks took Ani in 1064, and
Manzikert was in 1071. This site's Anatolia region begins there.
