By 909 Kung-ye's kingdom had reached its widest extent, its standards
planted across the whole of the north and east to within a hundred and
twenty miles of Silla's own capital. That year he called Silla outright
"the Kingdom to be Destroyed" and set Wang-gön, his own most capable
general, to work as military governor of the southwest — fitting out
ships to subdue neighboring islands and pressing a running campaign
against Kyŏn Hwŏn's rival kingdom in the south, a campaign Wang-gön won
with almost total consistency.

## A new name for a new claim

In 911 Kung-ye renamed his kingdom Tă-bong. The change, the source
suggests, reflected a religious absorption that had by then overtaken
him entirely: he proclaimed himself the Buddha Mi-ryŭk, dressed as a
high priest, kept censers burning wherever he went, and made both his
sons formal Buddhists alongside him. He wrote a scripture of his own and
had a monk executed for refusing to accept it as canonical — a ruler no
longer content to hold territory, but determined to hold doctrine as
well.

## A general who kept his distance

As Kung-ye's religious claims hardened into open cruelty and
caprice, military authority slid further into Wang-gön's own hands
almost by default; Wang-gön, for his part, made a habit of requesting
posts as far from court as possible, keenly aware that a rising general
too visible at his king's side invited exactly the jealousy that was
already consuming Kung-ye's judgment. The kingdom that had briefly
looked capable of unifying the peninsula under a single Buddha-king was,
by its own ruler's hand, already turning against itself.
