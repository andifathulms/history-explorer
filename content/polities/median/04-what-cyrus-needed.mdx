---
title: A predecessor worth having
drafted_from: briant-2002
phase: aside
---

The Achaemenids presented Cyrus's overthrow of Astyages not as a conquest but as
a legitimate transfer within a related royal house — Cyrus's own genealogy makes
him Astyages's grandson through his mother, and the Persian and Median elites
appear jointly in Achaemenid iconography and administration thereafter.

That may be true. It is also exactly what a new dynasty needs to be true. A
usurper who is also an heir does not have to explain himself, and the Medes were
too numerous and too militarily useful to be governed as a defeated people.

The wider point is what this does to the record. Almost everything about the
Median empire reaches us through the Achaemenids' account of their own origins
and through Herodotus, who was working from Persian informants a century later.
An empire that needed a predecessor produced the description of one, and modern
scholarship cannot easily separate the Median state from the Achaemenid story
about it.

This site records the edge from Median to Achaemenid as contested for that
reason. The record is kept because a cited figure exists for it, and the identity
line says the existence is disputed. Provenance and truth are different
properties, and this is the page in the corpus where the difference is widest.
