---
title: An empire, possibly
drafted_from: lanfranchi-2003
phase: aside
---

The Medes helped the Babylonians destroy Nineveh in 612 BC, ending Assyria, and
for the next sixty years are described by Herodotus as ruling an empire across
western Iran and eastern Anatolia until Cyrus overthrew Astyages in 550.

Whether that empire existed is genuinely contested. The Median state is known
almost entirely from Herodotus, writing a century later and at a distance, and
from Assyrian references to Median chiefdoms. Archaeological evidence for a
centralised Median administration is thin to absent, and a substantial body of
scholarship holds that the Medes were a tribal confederation that Greek and
later Persian sources retrospectively promoted into an empire because the
Achaemenids needed a predecessor to have succeeded.

The honest position is that a published figure exists for the extent of a state
whose existence is itself argued about. Provenance and truth are different
properties, and this is the case where they come apart furthest: the number can
be traced to a source, and the thing it measures may never have been there.
