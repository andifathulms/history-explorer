---
title: A predecessor worth having
drafted_from: briant-2002
phase: aside
---

The Achaemenids presented Cyrus's overthrow of Astyages not as a conquest but
as a legitimate transfer within a related royal house — Cyrus's own genealogy
makes him Astyages's grandson through his mother, and the Persian and Median
elites appear jointly in Achaemenid iconography and administration thereafter.

That may be true. It is also exactly what a new dynasty needs to be true. A
usurper who is also an heir does not have to explain himself, and the Medes
were too numerous and too militarily useful to be governed as a defeated
people.

The wider point is what this does to the record. Almost everything about the
Median empire reaches us through the Achaemenids' account of their own origins
and through Herodotus, who was working from Persian informants a century later.
An empire that needed a predecessor produced the description of one, and modern
scholarship cannot easily separate the Median state from the Achaemenid story
about it.

So the succession from Media to Persia is itself contested — not the fact that
Cyrus took Ecbatana, which is in a Babylonian chronicle, but the question of
what he took over: an empire, or a coalition of highland chiefs with a good
story attached.
