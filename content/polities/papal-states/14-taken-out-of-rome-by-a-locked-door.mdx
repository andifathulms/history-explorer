Pius VII spent much of his pontificate resisting Napoleon's demands
that the Papal States join his Continental blockade against Britain
and submit to French military occupation. When persuasion failed, the
Pope's Secretary of State, Cardinal Pacca, drafted a bull of
excommunication against "the authors and abettors of the violence and
usurpation" being carried out against the Holy See - not naming
Napoleon directly, but leaving no doubt of its target - which Pius,
after hesitating over its strength, ordered posted at the doors of
Rome's great basilicas after sunset on 10 June 1809. Napoleon's answer
came within weeks.

## An escalade before dawn

On the night of 5 July, cavalry picquets quietly sealed every street
leading to the Quirinal Palace while infantry closed in around it,
and at first light gendarmes, police, and a handful of Roman
collaborators stormed the building by scaling ladders raised against
its walls, their axes breaking down door after door as they advanced
toward the papal apartments. Pacca, who had promised to wake the Pope
at the first sign of danger, found him already dressed in full
episcopal robes by the time the last door gave way. Pius rose from his
chair to stand at the center of the room, a cardinal at each shoulder,
and waited in silence as General Radet entered at the head of the
assault party.

## A refusal answered with an abduction

Radet, visibly shaken, told the Pope he had "a painful and disagreeable
duty" - to convey the Emperor's demand that His Holiness renounce the
temporal sovereignty of Rome and the Papal States, or else be removed
to a destination of Napoleon's choosing. Pius answered him plainly: he
had no power to surrender what was not his own to give away, being
merely the administrator of the Holy See's temporal dominion, not its
owner. Within the hour he and Pacca were hurried down the palace
stairs, through a cortile crowded with troops, and into a closed
carriage whose blinds had already been nailed shut; a gendarme locked
both doors from the outside before the horses moved off. Rather than
driving directly across the city, Radet took a circling route along
the outer walls to avoid any crowd that might have gathered, passing
cavalry patrols with drawn sabres at every junction before the
carriage finally left Rome through the Porta del Popolo, where relay
horses were already waiting.

## A dominion in name only, for five years

Pius spent the years that followed shuttled between successive places
of confinement - Savona on the Ligurian coast, and later Fontainebleau
itself, within sight of Napoleon's own court - while the Papal States
were formally annexed to the French Empire and Rome declared its
second city after Paris. Only Napoleon's own collapse in 1814 freed
him to return, restoring a temporal sovereignty that had existed, for
five years, only as a claim a captive pope refused to renounce even
when he had no territory left to rule.
