Aistulf, king of the Lombards from 749, pursued the same expansion his
predecessors had, and pursued it more aggressively. Pope Stephen II,
finding diplomacy alone no match for Aistulf's own ambitions, made a
decision no earlier pope had risked: he traveled north across the Alps
in person, to Pepin's own court, to ask the debt Zachary's ruling had
created be repaid in the coin the papacy actually needed - military
force.

## A promise made before a single battle was fought

At an assembly of Frankish nobles at Quierzy in 754, Pepin confirmed
what he had apparently already privately undertaken to do: to make war
on Aistulf and restore to the papacy the territories the Lombards had
seized, above all the old Byzantine Exarchate of Ravenna. Illness delayed
Stephen's own return to Rome, but the promise itself, recorded afterward
as a formal deed of gift, stood. What followed was not a single swift
campaign but two.

## A siege that answered a demand

Pepin's first expedition forced Aistulf, besieged in his own capital of
Pavia, to swear under oath to restore Ravenna and the other captured
cities. No sooner had Pepin returned to France than Aistulf broke the
oath and marched on Rome itself, besieging the city in the opening weeks
of 756 and demanding, as the price of mercy, that the pope be handed
over to him. Stephen's own letters to Pepin - preserved in the papal
correspondence and addressed to his "most excellent sons, kings and
patricians of the Romans" - describe a siege pressed hard enough that
the pope warned Pepin he would answer to God himself for how vigorously
he defended the cause he had already sworn to.

## A conqueror who gave away what he had conquered

Pepin's second campaign broke Aistulf for good. This time, rather than
return the recovered territory to its previous owner - the Byzantine
Empire, which had held it before the Lombards seized it, and which still
considered it rightfully its own - Pepin granted the Exarchate of
Ravenna and the Pentapolis outright to the papacy. The gift was Pepin's
to make by right of conquest: the Byzantines had been unable or unwilling
to defend the territory themselves, and it was Frankish soldiers, not
Byzantine ones, who had actually taken it back from the Lombards. What
had been, at Sutri thirty years earlier, the return of a single town now
became the grant of an entire province - the donation that gave a bishop
of Rome the standing of a sovereign ruling continuous territory in his
own right, rather than merely a religious authority governing at another
power's sufferance.
