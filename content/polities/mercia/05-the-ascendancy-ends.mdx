Offa died in 796, the same year his treaty with Charlemagne was
concluded, and the supremacy he had spent nearly forty years building
began coming apart almost at once. Kent revolted again, and
Charlemagne's own hand was visible in the support given to the
Archbishop of Canterbury's renewed complaints against the rival see
Offa had set up at Lichfield. Cenwulf, Offa's successor, met the
challenge with real vigor - crushing the Kentish revolt and then
conciliating the Kentish archbishop by suppressing the rival
archbishopric himself - but the next blow from the Frankish court
proved harder to answer. On the death of the client king Offa had
placed on the West Saxon throne, Ecgberht - who had spent years in
exile at Charlemagne's own court after being driven from Wessex -
returned at once and was welcomed home as king.

Mercian power did not collapse immediately. But the pattern Offa had
built, of dependent thrones filled and vacated at Mercia's own
convenience, was already running in reverse. When Cenwulf himself
died in 821, Mercia was plunged into a civil war that put a man named
Beornwulf, of otherwise unknown family, on the throne. Beornwulf
still commanded real authority in 825 - Essex, Middlesex and Kent
answered to him, and that summer he reached a settlement with the
Archbishop of Canterbury over questions left open since Cenwulf's own
reign.

## Ellendun

It did not last the year. Egbert of Wessex met Beornwulf's army at
Ellendun, near modern Wroughton, in country long disputed between the
two kingdoms, and broke it in what one historian calls one of the
most decisive battles of the whole Anglo-Saxon period. Egbert did not
pause to consolidate. He sent his own son with an army into Kent
within days, driving out the Mercian client ruling there, and by the
year's end Kent, Essex, Surrey and Sussex had all submitted to Wessex
outright. East Anglia, already in revolt against Beornwulf's own
overlordship, turned to Egbert for protection rather than wait to be
forced back into line - and when Beornwulf marched against the East
Angles to do exactly that, they killed him. The kingdom Offa had
built into the dominant power of England answered, within thirty
years of his death, to a king it had once counted among its own
clients.
