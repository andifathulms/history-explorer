The war Offa fought hardest, and to the clearest effect, was against
the Welsh kingdoms on Mercia's own western border. Every Mercian king
before him had thrown men against Wales without lasting result -
Aethelbald himself had led the combined forces of his under-kings,
and even of Wessex, against the Welsh in vain. Offa broke the
pattern. Driving the Welsh back from Hereford and carrying his own
raids into the heart of Powys, he forced its king from his own
capital, which took a new English name from the campaign: Scrobbesbyrig,
the town in the scrub, now Shrewsbury.

Raiding, though, had never held ground for long, and Offa knew it.
Rather than repeat the exercise, he resolved to fix a permanent
frontier: a linear earthwork, still known by his own name, running
some two hundred miles from the mouth of the Wye to the mouth of the
Dee. English settlement was planted in the new ground it enclosed
between the earthwork and the river Severn, which had until then
marked the practical limit of English territory.

## Governing two peoples at once

Offa did not expel the Welsh who remained within reach of his new
border. Following the same pattern the West Saxons would later apply
in their own conquests, he let them stay among their English
conquerors, and issued a code of Mercian law - bearing his own name -
specifically to regulate the dealings between the two populations
now sharing one frontier region. It was, in its way, as durable an
achievement as the earthwork itself: a border kingdom governed by
written rule rather than by the raiding and counter-raiding that had
defined the region for generations before him.
