---
title: The forty-five years when it nearly worked
drafted_from: el-hibri-2021
---

In 1055 Tughril Beg entered Baghdad, expelled the Buyids and restored the
caliph — to exactly the position he had held before. The Great Seljuk sultans
were Sunni and presented themselves as the caliphate's protectors, which changed
the theology of the arrangement and nothing about its arithmetic. The sultan
held the army and the provinces; the caliph held the investiture that made the
sultan's holding lawful, and the arrangement ran, with interruptions, for over a
century. No sourced edge joins the Seljuks to the Abbasids on this site, which
is a statement about what has been read here rather than about the relationship:
the Buyid arrangement it replaced *is* recorded, as a `vassal of` edge running
from the caliphate to the amirs who held it.

What is usually missed is what happened when the protector weakened. Seljuk
power fragmented after 1092 and again after 1157, and each time the caliphs took
back not just prestige but ground — tax, garrisons, an actual administration in
Iraq. By the time al-Nasir li-Din Allah came to the throne in 1180 there was
something real to govern, and he governed it for forty-five years, the longest
reign in the dynasty.

Al-Nasir is the most interesting Abbasid after al-Ma'mun and the least written
about here until now. He rebuilt a territorial state in Iraq, and he tried to
rebuild the caliph's authority over rulers he could not coerce by reorganising
the *futuwwa* — the urban chivalric associations — into an order with himself at
its head, and inviting princes to join it. It was an attempt to make the
caliphate a network of personal allegiance rather than a fiction of delegation.

It very nearly worked, and then it met Khwarazm. Muhammad II repudiated al-Nasir
in 1217 and marched on Baghdad; the campaign broke in the mountains before it
arrived. Within four years the Khwarazmshah's empire had been destroyed by the
Mongols, and the caliphate had thirty-seven years left.

The point for the next chapter is that what ended in 1258 was not a shadow. It
was a functioning state, smaller than it had been and much larger than it had
been in 945.
