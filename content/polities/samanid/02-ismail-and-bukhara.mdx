---
title: Isma'il moves the capital
drafted_from: iranica-samanids
phase: expansion
---

The brothers' arrangement ended the way such arrangements usually do, between
two members of the family. Nasr I ruled from Samarkand; his brother Isma'il
held Bukhara for him. In 888 they fought, and Isma'il won.

Isma'il I is the point at which the Samanids stop being a provincial family and
start being a state, and the clearest evidence of it is the move of the centre
of gravity to Bukhara. Samarkand was the older and grander city. Bukhara was
Isma'il's, and from 892 it was the capital — a working administrative centre
rather than an inherited seat.

In 900 he faced the Saffarids, the other power that had grown out of the
Tahirid collapse, and he beat them at Balkh. Amr ibn al-Layth was captured and
sent to Baghdad. The caliph, who had recently been obliged to recognise Amr as
governor of Khurasan, now transferred the recognition to Isma'il.

This is the moment the thread doubles back on itself. The Saffarids had taken
Khurasan from the Tahirids by force and extracted an unwilling investiture for
it. The Samanids took it from the Saffarids by force and received a willing
one. The caliphal grant did not create either state and could not have
prevented either. It followed the outcome and dressed it.

What Isma'il had by 900 was Transoxiana and Khurasan under one administration,
a capital he had built up himself, and a piece of paper from Baghdad that said
he had been given all of it.
