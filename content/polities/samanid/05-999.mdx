In 999 the Karakhanids took Bukhara. There was very little fighting. The
Samanid army did not concentrate, the city did not hold out, and the last
effective amir was taken.

South of the Oxus, Mahmud of Ghazna — the grandson-in-law of Alptigin's line,
governing Khurasan in Samanid name — did not march north to save his nominal
overlords. He kept Khurasan. The Karakhanids kept Transoxiana. The Oxus, which
had been an internal river of the Samanid state, became the border between two
successors, and stayed one for a long time.

Then there is Isma'il al-Muntasir, which is why this site records the end of
the Samanids as a range rather than a year. A Samanid prince who escaped
Karakhanid custody, he spent from 1000 to 1005 raising forces, retaking cities
briefly, losing them, crossing and recrossing the Oxus, and being defeated by
Karakhanids and Ghaznavids in turn. He was killed in 1005 by Arab tribesmen he
had taken shelter with.

Whether the dynasty ends in 999 or 1005 depends on whether you are dating the
end of the state or the end of the family's attempt to be a state. Sources
differ, so this site keeps both dates and does not choose. The bar for
longevity is drawn as a range for the same reason.

What passed forward was not the territory. It was the administrative Persian,
the chancery practice, the court model and the slave-soldier system — all of it
inherited more or less intact by Ghazna, which is where the thread goes next.
