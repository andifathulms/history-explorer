The fort at Mombasa had changed hands so many times by the early
eighteenth century that its own garrison had stopped meaning loyalty
to any one power for long. Built by the Portuguese, taken by Oman in 1698 after a five-year
siege, briefly retaken by Portugal in 1728 - and lost again within the
year, when the town's own inhabitants massacred the Portuguese
garrison on a fete day and handed the fort back to the Imam - it
passed in 1739 to a clan of Omani settlers, the Mazaru'i, whom the
Imam installed as its custodians.

Custodianship did not stay simple. By the early 1740s the Imam's own
throne in Oman was contested between rival claimants, leaving little
attention to spare for a fort two thousand miles down the coast.
Muhammad bin Osman, the Mazaru'i governor the Imam had placed at
Mombasa, took advantage of the distraction to declare himself
independent. Ahmad bin Sa'id, newly established as Imam after driving
Persian invaders from Oman, answered by sending an agent named Sef bin
Khalif and six accomplices to Mombasa. They came pretending friendship
with the governor. One day they murdered him and seized his brother
Ali as a prisoner, and with the governor dead they took the fort in
the Imam's name.

## Ali's return

Ali did not stay a prisoner. He escaped, gathered help among the
Wanyika of the mainland facing Mombasa, and led an assault that
retook the fort from his brother's killers. In 1746 he made himself
governor in his own right - not, this time, as the Imam's appointed
custodian, but as a man who had taken the fort back with his own
following and saw no reason to hand it to anyone else.

The distinction mattered from the start. That same year Pate, the
rival sultanate up the coast, threw off its own allegiance to Oman;
of all the coast towns, only Zanzibar continued to acknowledge the
Imam's rule and accept an Omani garrison. Mombasa's fort - the single
most fought-over position on the whole East African coast - now
belonged to a family that governed it because they had proved they
could hold it, and to no one else.
