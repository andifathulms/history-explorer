In 1822 Imam Sa'id decided the paper Mombasa had once signed was worth
enforcing. He ordered the Mazaru'i governor to withdraw from Pate;
when they refused, he sent his general Hamad with a fleet that took
Pate, Siu and Faza outright. While the Mazaru'i governor was occupied
defending the Lamu archipelago, Sa'id's own governor at Zanzibar
seized the opportunity to take Pemba Island - the richest of
Mombasa's remaining dependencies - almost without a fight.

Two Mazaru'i expeditions tried to retake it. The first landed on
Pemba only to have a second Zanzibar fleet capture their ships behind
them, leaving them stranded until they capitulated and agreed to give
up any claim to the island in exchange for passage home. The second
expedition accomplished nothing at all. Abdallah, the governor who
had presided over both failures, died in 1823 not long after the
second expedition's return - a death the source attributes chiefly to
his own grief at what had been lost - and his death opened a civil
war within the Mazaru'i family over who would succeed him.

## An offer to become British

Weakened and divided, the Mazaru'i looked outward for protection that
year. When a British hydrographic officer, Captain Vidal, anchored
off Mombasa, the family approached him about becoming a British
dependency rather than an Omani one; Vidal could promise nothing
himself and referred the question to Bombay. No sooner had he left
than an Omani squadron arrived and blockaded the port.

Into that blockade sailed Captain Owen, the senior officer of the
survey Vidal served under, and found Arab warships outside Mombasa
with the British flag already flying over the fort. Owen negotiated a
formal treaty: the Mazaru'i would be confirmed in their old
possessions, and in return would abolish the slave trade, share the
port's customs revenue with Britain, and open the interior to British
access. The flag stood as Mombasa's protection for a time. But London
never ratified what Owen had signed, and in
1826 the flag came down. Mombasa was left facing Sa'id's fleet again
with no outside guarantor at all.
