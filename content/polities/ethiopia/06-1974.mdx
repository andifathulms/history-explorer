In September 1974 a committee of junior officers — the Derg — deposed Haile
Selassie, drove him out of the palace in the back of a Volkswagen, and
abolished the monarchy. He died the following year in circumstances the regime
did not explain.

The Solomonic dynasty had claimed continuous descent for seven hundred and four
years and, in the constitution of 1955, from Solomon.

## Taken from inside

Nobody conquered Ethiopia in 1974. Nothing fragmented — the state continued,
and considerably more centralised than before. No rival dynasty replaced the
dynasty. A committee inside the state simply took the state.

That is a stranger way for a polity to end than it sounds, and it is not
unique. The Attalid kingdom was left to Rome in a will. The Venetian republic
voted itself out of existence with a French army on the mainland. The Holy
Roman Empire was dissolved by its own emperor rather than by anyone who had
beaten him. Four polities, spread across two thousand years, ended by a
document or a decision taken by people already inside them, and in none of the
four is there a battle to point at.

## The last monarchy standing

1974 is very late for this kind of history. Most of the world's dynastic
polities had stopped being dynastic polities somewhere between the eighteenth
century and the First World War.

Ethiopia is the exception because the same dynasty, the same church, the same
script and the same claim were all still in place. The throne Haile Selassie
was driven from rested on a claim of descent from Solomon, set out in a text
compiled in the fourteenth century and written into the constitution of 1955 as
a matter of law.

An institution that had been running since 1270 ended on the radio.

## What the officers took

It is worth being exact about what the Derg actually inherited, because it was
not a medieval kingdom.

Haile Selassie had spent fifty years building a centralised modern state on top
of the old one: a written constitution, a standing army with foreign training, an
air force, a bureaucracy, schools, a national bank, and an international
position — Ethiopia was a founding member of the United Nations and Addis Ababa
was the seat of the Organisation of African Unity. The gult had been abolished
on paper. The provincial lords had been reduced.

Every one of those things was an instrument, and in 1974 the instruments changed
hands intact. A committee of majors and captains took over a functioning modern
state that a monarchy had spent half a century assembling, and used it to abolish
the monarchy.

That is the specific irony of the ending. What killed the Solomonic dynasty was
not the medieval Ethiopia it had ruled but the modern Ethiopia it had built.
