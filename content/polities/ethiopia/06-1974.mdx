In September 1974 a committee of junior officers — the Derg — deposed Haile
Selassie, drove him out of the palace in the back of a Volkswagen, and abolished
the monarchy. He died the following year in circumstances the regime did not
explain.

The Solomonic dynasty had claimed continuous descent for seven hundred and four
years and, in the constitution of 1955, from Solomon.

## `internal usurpation`

This record's `ended.type` is `internal usurpation` and `by` is empty, and both
need the same qualification several endings in this corpus need.

The six values are conquest, fragmentation, dynastic replacement, gradual
absorption, internal usurpation and still contested. Nobody conquered Ethiopia in
1974. Nothing fragmented — the state continued, considerably more centralised
than before. No dynasty replaced the dynasty. What happened is that a committee
inside the state took the state, which is what `internal usurpation` describes
better than the alternatives, and `by` is empty because a military committee is
not a polity this site can name.

This is the third recent record to end by a document or a decision rather than a
battle — the Attalid kingdom left to Rome in a will, the Venetian republic voting
itself out of existence with an army on the mainland, the Holy Roman Empire
dissolved by its own emperor. Four is beginning to look like a category. No
vocabulary change is proposed and this paragraph is where a fifth case will find
the others.

## The latest record on this site

1974 makes this the last-ending polity in the corpus, twelve years past the Zaydi
imamate of Yemen and forty-nine past the Qajars.

That is worth a sentence because it is not a fact about Ethiopia so much as a
fact about the shape of this project. The corpus stops where the sources it works
from stop treating polities as polities, which for most of the world is somewhere
between the eighteenth century and the First World War. Ethiopia is here in 1974
because the same dynasty, the same church, the same script and the same claim
were still in place — an institution that had been running since 1270 and
answering to a text compiled in the fourteenth century.

## What the region gained, and what happened to it

Two edges, and the first thread this corpus's Africa region ever had that was
worth the name.

`aksum -> ethiopia`, `claimed legitimacy of`, 1270, which closed a field on the
Aksum record that had been null with a comment saying nobody had looked. And
`ghana -> mali`, added in the same pass, which joins the first of the three Niger
empires to the second.

With `mali -> songhai` that made three internal edges among five records, and it
also made the region's shape legible for the first time: two entirely separate
sequences, one in the Horn and one on the Niger, with no connection between them
and no possibility of one. Aksum and Mali have nothing to do with each other and
never will.

This paragraph then said that this was an argument for splitting the region and
that the split would cost nothing. Both halves would keep their edges, and the
only edges leaving Africa — `saba -> ethiopia`, `himyar -> aksum` and
`songhai -> saadi` — already crossed a region boundary and were already not drawn
by any thread.

That has now happened. `africa` reached twelve records and was split into three:
West Africa, this record's Nile and the Horn, and the Zimbabwe plateau with the
Swahili coast. It cost zero edges, exactly as predicted. What it did not do was
add anything — the continent still has twelve records in a corpus of nearly two
hundred, and the Mali record's last chapter keeps the list of who is missing.

What this record contributes to its new region is the second half of the only
sequence in it: one polity that lasted from a mountain full of confined princes
to a coup broadcast on the radio, claiming throughout to be the same thing the
Aksumite record beside it was.
