The emperor was deposed in September 1974 and died the following year. His body
was buried under a lavatory floor in the palace, and stayed there until 1992,
when it was dug up. It was reinterred in Holy Trinity Cathedral in Addis Ababa
in the year 2000, twenty-five years after his death, in a ceremony the government
declined to make a state funeral.

<Figure id="e-trinity" />

The claim he had ruled on outlived him in two places, and neither of them is
Ethiopia.

## What the claim was

Budge sets out what the compiler of the *Kebra Nagast* believed, in three
propositions, from the man's own colophons:

> 1. That the lawful kings of Ethiopia were descended from Solomon, King of
> Israel. 2. That the Tabernacle of the Law of God, i.e. the Ark of the Covenant,
> had been brought from Jerusalem to Aksum by Menyelek, Solomon's firstborn son.
> 3. That the God of Israel had transferred His place of abode on earth from
> Jerusalem to Aksum.

Isaac assembled that at Aksum in the early fourteenth century, in the reign of a
dynasty that had taken the throne in 1270 and needed it. The 1955 constitution
reserved the imperial dignity to the line of Haile Selassie, *descendant of
Menelik I, son of Solomon and the queen of Sheba*, which put a fourteenth-century
book into a twentieth-century legal instrument as a title deed.

Nineteen years later the officers who abolished the throne cited, among other
things, that the emperor was the head of an unelected dynasty.

## Where it went

**To Jamaica.** In 1930 Ras Tafari Makonnen was crowned at Addis Ababa with a
list of titles that included Conquering Lion of the Tribe of Judah and Elect of
God, and the coronation was reported around the world. In Jamaica, where a
Christian black population had been reading the psalm about Ethiopia stretching
out her hands to God, a series of preachers concluded that a black king with a
biblical genealogy taking a throne in Africa was the fulfilment of prophecy.

<Figure id="e-coronation" />

The movement that grew out of it took his name — Ras Tafari — and made him a
divine figure. He visited Jamaica in 1966 and something over a hundred thousand
people met the aircraft. He was an Orthodox Christian and does not appear ever to
have accepted the identification, which made no difference whatever, because
nothing in the doctrine required him to.

A claim assembled in a highland church in the fourteenth century, to legitimise a
usurping dynasty, became a religion in the Caribbean six hundred years later,
among people whose ancestors had been shipped there from a different part of
Africa entirely.

**And to a family in exile.** The dynasty's descendants live abroad. The Crown
Council maintains the succession. Nobody has restored anything, and there is no
serious movement to.

## What actually survived

Not the dynasty and not the claim, but the two things underneath both of them.

The church is still there. It has been Miaphysite since Frumentius in the fourth
century, took its head from Alexandria until 1959, and is now autocephalous — the
same institution, in the same liturgical language, with the same canon, running
continuously for sixteen hundred years, which almost nothing else on earth can
say.

And so is the writing. Ge'ez is written today in the script Aksum built out of a
South Arabian alphabet by adding vowels, used for Amharic and Tigrinya, by tens of
millions of people. It is the only indigenous African writing system in
widespread daily use, and it has been running without interruption since before
the conversion.

The kings claimed Solomon. What they actually inherited, and passed on, was an
alphabet and a church — and both of them are older than the claim, and have now
outlasted it.
