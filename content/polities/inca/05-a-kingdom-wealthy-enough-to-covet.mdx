Pachacuti's own reign, and that of his son and successor Topa Inca
Yupanqui, turned a highland lordship that had barely survived one
invasion into a state expanding on every frontier at once. Much of that
expansion came, as it had at Cuzco itself, through campaigns that began
as something smaller than their own eventual consequences - an
unauthorized raid by one of Pachacuti's own generals into central Peru
committed the empire to holding territory five hundred miles from its
nearest secure outposts, and rather than retreat from the obligation,
Pachacuti chose to conquer the intervening land outright to make good on
it.

## The richest kingdom on the continent, taken whole

That same overextension eventually brought the empire into direct war
with Chimor, the wealthy kingdom of the north coast that rivalled Cuzco
in organization if not in territory. An army under Pachacuti's son Topa
Capac, with the young heir Topa Inca serving alongside him, broke
Chimor's own resistance in 1470 and carried its king back to Cuzco in
honoured exile, along with plunder richer than any campaign before it
had produced. Pachacuti used part of that wealth to cast golden statues
of the Creator, the Sun and the legendary ancestress Mama Ocllo, and to
gild the walls of Cuzco's own Temple of the Sun.

## An empire administered rather than merely conquered

What distinguished the Inca expansion from a simple run of conquests was
the infrastructure built to hold what had been taken. Roads radiating
out from Cuzco linked the new provinces to the capital; a system of
compulsory labour service, the mit'a, moved workers and soldiers alike
according to the state's own needs rather than local custom; and
administrators kept the accounts of an empire that had no writing system
on quipus - knotted cords whose colours and knot patterns, read by
trained specialists called quipucamayocs, recorded numbers with enough
precision to run the business of a continent-spanning state and,
according to some of the same specialists, to serve as reminders for
historical and ceremonial knowledge besides.
