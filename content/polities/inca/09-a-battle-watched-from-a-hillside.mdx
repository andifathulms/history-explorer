Huascar's defeat at Ambato did not end the war. He raised a second,
larger army under a new general, Huanca Auqui, reportedly more than
eighty thousand strong, and sent it north in forced marches to reach
the Cañari country before Atahualpa's own forces could do further harm
there. Atahualpa met the challenge in kind, assembling a comparable
force at Tumebamba and declaring he would not stop until he reached
Cuzco itself.

## A commander who fought from a safe distance

The two armies collided in the province of the Paltas, near Caxabamba.
Atahualpa himself, by every account, was not present on the field,
watching the battle instead from an adjacent hill while his own
captains commanded in his name. Despite Huascar's army including many
experienced Orejón officers and Huanca Auqui performing his own duty
loyally, Atahualpa's forces won decisively; more than thirty-five
thousand men were killed across both armies, and the victors pursued
the fleeing survivors for miles, killing, capturing and plundering the
abandoned camp as they went.

## A triumph interrupted by news from the coast

Atahualpa received word of the victory with open joy, declaring that
the gods themselves were fighting on his side. He did not press on to
Cuzco in person to finish the war, however - word had already reached
him that a small company of foreign strangers had landed on the coast
days before. Whatever he made of that news at the time, it was enough
to make him pause at the very moment his own civil war stood closer to
a final resolution than it had ever been.
