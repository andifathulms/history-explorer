Francisco Pizarro's company reached Cajamarca in November 1532, where
Atahualpa - fresh from his own victory over Huascar's army - waited
with a large retinue in the town's central square, apparently
unconcerned by a foreign force he outnumbered many times over. Pizarro
sent Friar Vicente de Valverde ahead, a cross in one hand and a Bible
in the other, to address the Inca directly through an interpreter and
urge him, on God's behalf, to go and speak with the Spanish commander.

## An insult that answered an insult

Atahualpa asked to examine the book the friar carried. Handed it
closed, he could not work out how to open it, and when Valverde reached
out to help him, Atahualpa struck the friar's arm in anger rather than
accept the assistance. He opened the book himself, showed no interest
in its pages, and threw it several paces away - answering Valverde's
appeal with open scorn, accusing the Spaniards of mistreating his own
chiefs and stealing cloth from his storehouses along the road. Valverde
returned to Pizarro and reported that the Inca had thrown the Scriptures
to the ground.

## An ambush sprung in the space of a cry

That was the signal Pizarro had been waiting for. He put on a cotton
jacket, took up his sword and dagger, and led a handful of men directly
into the crowd of Atahualpa's own retinue, seizing the Inca by the arm
himself and crying out "Santiago!" Guns fired, trumpets sounded, and
Spanish cavalry and infantry who had been hidden around the square
charged into the open space. Terrified Indians fleeing the horses broke
down part of a surrounding wall in the crush; the horsemen rode down and
killed those they overtook, while the infantry cut down most of those
who remained. Pizarro himself would not release his grip on Atahualpa,
even as Spanish soldiers killed the litter-bearers holding the Inca
aloft until the litter itself began to fall - receiving a minor wound to
his own hand in the effort to keep his prisoner alive rather than let
him be killed in the general slaughter. Not a single armed hand was
raised against a Spaniard the entire time.
