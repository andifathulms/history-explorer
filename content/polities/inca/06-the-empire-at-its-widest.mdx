---
title: The empire at its widest
drafted_from: cieza-1883

Under Huayna Capac, Pachacuti's own grandson, the empire reached a
territorial extent no Inca ruler before or after him would match: a
dominion running from the river Ancasmayu in the north to the river
Maule in the south, a span of more than twelve hundred leagues along
the spine of the Andes. Huayna Capac spent much of his own reign in the
far north, at Quito, extending Inca authority into what is now Ecuador
and consolidating conquests his father had begun.

## A fortune brought to a provincial capital

The scale of Huayna Capac's own wealth, concentrated at his northern
base rather than at Cuzco itself, struck contemporaries as extraordinary
even by Inca standards. More than five hundred loads of gold and a
thousand of silver were said to have been brought to Quito on his own
orders, along with quantities of precious stones and fine cloth. He was
feared throughout his dominions as a stern administrator of justice - a
reputation his own subjects, by every account, respected rather than
merely endured.

## A ruler whose own success left the succession untested

Huayna Capac's long residence at Quito, so far from the traditional
seat of Inca government at Cuzco, was itself a quiet sign of how far the
empire had already outgrown the compact highland kingdom his
great-grandfather Pachacuti had built it from. It also meant that when
his own death eventually came, it would fall not on the empire's
traditional heartland but on a frontier capital far from the court
structures and settled expectations that might otherwise have managed
his succession more smoothly.
