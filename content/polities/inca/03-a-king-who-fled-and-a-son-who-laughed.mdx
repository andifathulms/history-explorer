title: A king who fled and a son who laughed

For generations after its founding, Cuzco remained one regional power
among several in the southern Andean highlands, its kings ruling a
lordship rather than an empire. That balance held until the Chanka
people, having already conquered the neighbouring Quichuas and occupied
much of the province of Andahuaylas, decided that a rich and famous city
like Cuzco was worth adding to their own conquests. Their commander,
Hastu Huaraca, led an army out of Andahuaylas toward the Inca capital,
laying waste to the settlements along the way.

## A ruler who chose flight over defence

When word reached Cuzco that the Chanka were coming and that neither the
aged king Viracocha Inca nor his own heir, Inca Urco, had made any
preparation to meet them, the city's leading nobles were thrown into
open panic. Viracocha Inca left the city entirely, taking his household
to the valley of Yucay rather than face the threat directly. His son and
designated successor, Inca Urco, treated the danger as a joke, laughing
off warnings that should have alarmed him as much as anyone.

## A city that looked past its own king for a defender

With the throne offering no leadership, the frightened nobility turned
instead to another of Viracocha's sons, Inca Yupanqui, and asked him to
take charge of the city's defence. He answered pointedly: when his
father had once wished to give him the royal fringe, the nobles
themselves had refused and insisted his "cowardly brother" take it
instead; he had never sought the throne through usurpation, but now
that Inca Urco had shown himself unfit, it fell to the city to do what
the moment required. The Orejones agreed to settle the question of
who would actually rule only once the war itself was won - and Inca
Yupanqui, given command of a city his own father and brother had
already abandoned to its fate, set about organizing its defence.
