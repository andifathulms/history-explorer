---
title: A brother sealed in a cave
drafted_from: cieza-1883

The six siblings who emerged from Pacarec Tampu did not go directly to
Cuzco. They founded a settlement at Pacarec Tampu itself and gathered
great stores of gold there before moving on - and it was during this
early stage of the story that one of the brothers, Ayar Cachi, showed a
strength his siblings found less impressive than alarming: with stones
hurled from his sling, he was said to split hills apart and throw their
fragments up to the clouds.

## A trap dressed as an errand

Rather than share credit for such power, the other brothers deceived
Ayar Cachi with gentle, flattering words, asking him to return to the
cave where their treasure was kept and fetch a golden vase they claimed
to have forgotten. He agreed without suspicion. The moment he was
inside, his brothers sealed the cave's mouth with stones so thoroughly
that no trace of the opening remained, and the ground shook violently
enough, the story goes, that whole hillsides collapsed into the
valleys below. Ayar Cachi was worshipped afterward as a sacred being,
Huanacauri, the brother whose death - engineered by his own
kin - the Incas would trace their descent from without ever quite
admitting how it had happened.

## A city named for a killing

The surviving siblings pressed on toward the valley that would become
Cuzco, then inhabited by other peoples entirely. One version of the
story credits a sister, sent ahead to find the best site for a
settlement, with killing a local inhabitant, cutting out his lungs, and
walking into the valley with them still in her mouth, bloody, to
terrify the population into flight. Whatever the literal truth of any
of this, the founding brothers made themselves masters of the valley
without serious resistance, named their new settlement Cuzco, and
began the work of building an empire from a city whose own origin
story, by the admission of the men who told it, had already cost one
of its founders his life.
