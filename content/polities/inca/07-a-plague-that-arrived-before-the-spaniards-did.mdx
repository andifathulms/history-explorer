At the height of his own power, Huayna Capac was struck by a contagious
disease sweeping through the empire's provinces - a pestilence that
killed, by the chroniclers' own count, more than two hundred thousand
people across the land. Every sacrifice offered at the temples and
huacas on his behalf failed to save him. As his condition worsened, he
called his captains and relations to his side and addressed them on
several matters before he died.

## A dying prophecy about the ship

Among the things Huayna Capac reportedly said in his final days was a
forecast that the strangers who had already been sighted offshore in a
ship would return in force and conquer the land. Cieza himself doubted
the story, suspecting either invention after the fact or, if the king
really did say it, that some darker power had spoken through him -
since no ordinary man, he reasoned, could have known the Spaniards were
already arranging their own return as conquerors.

## An inheritance nobody could agree how he had left it

What Huayna Capac actually intended for his own succession, the sources
cannot settle. Some say he divided the empire between two sons, giving
the northern dominion around Quito to Atahualpa, the son who had always
accompanied him on campaign, and the rest to Huascar, his son by his own
sister and the wife he had most wanted as his heir. Others insist he
made no such division at all, and simply confirmed Huascar as sole heir
to the whole empire, to the satisfaction of the people of Cuzco. Whichever
account was true, the ambiguity itself was enough: within a few years of
Huayna Capac's death, his two sons were at open war over an inheritance
neither could agree the other was entitled to.
