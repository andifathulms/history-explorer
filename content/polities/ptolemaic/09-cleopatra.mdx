Cleopatra VII was the seventh of her name and, according to Plutarch, the first
ruler of the dynasty who learned Egyptian.
Three hundred years of ruling the country in Greek had not required it.

Her policy was the only one available and she pursued it further than her father
had: attach the kingdom to whichever Roman was going to win, and get from him
what could not be got any other way. With Julius Caesar she got the throne back
from her brother and, at Rome's expense, kept Cyprus. With Mark Antony she got
territory no Ptolemy had held since Panion — parts of Phoenicia, Coele-Syria,
Cilicia, Cyrenaica, Crete — handed to her and her children at the Donations of
Alexandria in 34, which is the largest Ptolemaic empire in a hundred and seventy
years and existed entirely by Roman grant.

Then Actium, in 31, and Octavian at Alexandria the following August. Antony
killed himself; Cleopatra, after a delay in which she seems to have tried and
failed to get terms for her children, killed herself too. Caesarion, her son by
Caesar and the last Ptolemy, was hunted down and executed, on the reported
advice that it was bad to have too many Caesars.
