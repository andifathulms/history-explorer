Ptolemy II held a festival at Alexandria and had somebody write down what went
past. The account survives because a compiler of dinner-table curiosities copied
it out five centuries later, and it is the most complete inventory of a Hellenistic
state's claims about itself that exists.

The animals first, because they are the argument. Twenty-four chariots drawn by
four elephants each. Sixty drawn by pairs of goats, twelve by antelopes, seven by
oryxes, fifteen by buffaloes, eight by pairs of ostriches, seven by gnus, four by
pairs of zebras and four more by teams of four zebras — each with a boy in
charioteer's dress and a broad hat, and a smaller boy beside him with a little
shield.

Then the cargo. Camels carrying three hundred *minae* weight of frankincense,
three hundred of myrrh, two hundred of saffron, and two hundred more of cassia,
cinnamon, iris and other spices. Ethiopians bearing six hundred elephant tusks,
two thousand faggots of ebony, sixty gold and silver bowls and a quantity of gold
dust. Two huntsmen with gold-pointed spears and two thousand four hundred hounds
— Indian, Hyrcanian, Molossian. A hundred and fifty men carrying trees with birds
and animals of every country hung from them, and cages of parrots, peacocks,
guinea fowl and pheasants. Two thousand bulls of one colour with gilded horns and
gold frontlets and breastplates.

Then the metal. A golden statue of Alexander on a chariot drawn by real
elephants, with Victory and Athena beside him. Thrones of ivory and gold, one of
them Ptolemy Soter's, carrying a crown made out of ten thousand gold coins.
Three hundred and fifty golden censers. A gilded thunderbolt forty cubits long.
Eagles twenty cubits high. Three thousand two hundred golden crowns, and one
mystic crown of gold studded with stones, eighty cubits high, set at the door of
the temple.

Then the army: fifty-seven thousand six hundred infantry and twenty-three
thousand two hundred cavalry, with more armour laid out for inspection than
anybody could count — though, the source notes, Kallixeinos did make a catalogue
of it.

Read the list as a balance sheet and every item is a route. The frankincense and
myrrh are the Arabian incense road, tapped at the Red Sea end instead of the
Gaza end. The elephants are the Ptolemaic hunting stations down the African
coast, built for exactly this animal. The tusks, ebony and gold dust are Nubia.
The zebras, gnus and oryxes are country nobody else in the Mediterranean could
reach. The Indian dogs and the parrots are the sea route east. And the golden
Alexander on the elephant chariot is the claim the whole dynasty rested on,
carried past the crowd in the same parade as the spices.

The one number that is not a boast is the accounting. The crowns came to two
thousand two hundred and thirty-nine talents and fifty minae, and the treasury
clerks had it counted before the spectacle was over.

That is the point at which the display stops being a display. A state that can
put eighty thousand soldiers and the produce of three continents through one
street, and have the audit finished the same afternoon, is telling its subjects
and everybody else's ambassadors the same thing — and the thing it is telling
them is not about elephants.
