The old account of Ptolemaic Egypt was a Greek administration imposed on a
passive Egyptian population, extracting grain through a bureaucracy of
unprecedented reach — a command economy with a foreign ruling caste. Manning's
argument, and the reason this page's institutions are coded from him, is that
this describes the paperwork rather than the state.

What he calls bargained incorporation is the observation that the Ptolemies
governed through Egyptian society rather than over it, because they had no
instrument capable of anything else. The people who could actually collect a
tax, register a field, settle a dispute or produce a harvest were Egyptian
priests, village scribes and local notables, and every one of them could
withhold cooperation. So the kings negotiated: with the temples, which kept
their land, their revenues and their courts; with the priesthoods, in synods
whose decrees the kings then had carved on stelae in three scripts; with
soldiers, by settling them on land they came to treat as their own.

The Rosetta Stone is the document of that arrangement and is almost never read
as one. It is a decree of the priests of Egypt assembled at Memphis in 196 BC,
listing what the boy-king Ptolemy V had granted them — remitted taxes, released
prisoners, temple revenues confirmed — and specifying in return the honours the
priests would pay him in every temple in the country. It is a settlement between
a weakened crown and the institution that could legitimate it, published in
hieroglyphic, demotic and Greek because all three audiences had to be able to
read it. The stone that gave the modern world Egyptian is a receipt for a
political bargain.

This has consequences for how this site codes the polity. `divine-sanction` and
`descent` are both real and both public, but they were public in different
languages to different constituencies, and the field cannot say that. A reader
comparing this row to the Abbasids' `caliphal-investiture` is comparing a single
claim with a double one. The chapter is where that has to live.
