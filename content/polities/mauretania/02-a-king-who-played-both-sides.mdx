Alliance with Jugurtha did not stop Bocchus from testing the other
side. As the Roman general Metellus pressed the war, he sent envoys
urging Bocchus to abandon a doomed son-in-law before his own kingdom
shared Jugurtha's ruin - warning him that a war easy to begin was hard
to end, and that only the victor, not the man who started it, got to
choose when it stopped. Bocchus answered warmly, professing he wanted
peace but pitied Jugurtha's misfortune, and would treat if Jugurtha
were offered the same terms. Messages went back and forth for months
without a decision either way.

## A third of a kingdom, offered twice

When the Roman consul Marius pushed Jugurtha out of Capsa and a string
of other strongholds, Jugurtha sent for Bocchus again, this time with
a concrete bribe attached: a third of Numidia itself, once the war was
won or settled on terms that left Bocchus's own lands untouched.
Bribery had worked on Bocchus's own courtiers before it worked on the
king, but it worked. Bocchus led his army into Numidia and joined
Jugurtha in a night attack on Marius's own column, catching the Romans
before they could form ranks or receive orders - a chaotic, close-run
fight that felt, by the Roman survivors' own account, more like a
brawl with bandits than a battle between armies.

## A ruin he chose not to share

The fighting that followed wore Bocchus down rather than Marius.
Months of further reverses convinced him that Jugurtha's cause, and
not Rome's, was the one about to fail - and when Jugurtha himself
proposed one final scheme, a peace conference at which Bocchus would
seize the Roman quaestor Sulla and hand him over as a hostage, Bocchus
turned the plan back on the man who had suggested it. An ambush laid
for Sulla caught Jugurtha instead, and Bocchus delivered his own
son-in-law to Rome in chains rather than let a losing war cost him his
own throne.
