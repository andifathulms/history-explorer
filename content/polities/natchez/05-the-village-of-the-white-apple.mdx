The commandant who replaced the one Tattooed Serpent had once negotiated
with arrived at Fort Rosalie with grand ambitions and no memory of the
lesson 1723 should have taught. Sieur de Chopart, newly installed and
determined to build the finest settlement in the colony, decided that
nothing but the village of White Apple itself - a square league of prime
land two leagues from the fort - would satisfy his plans, and summoned
its Sun to inform him the village would have to move. The Sun answered
that his ancestors had lived there for as many years as there were hairs
in his own long braid, and that they intended to keep living there.
Chopart, in a rage, threatened him with consequences if he did not
comply within days.

## A delay bought with a promise of tribute

The Sun's council of elders, rather than refuse outright, proposed a
delay: let the village stay through harvest, and each household would
pay Chopart a monthly tribute of corn and a fowl. Chopart, whose greed
the source that records this exchange calls plain to see, accepted at
once - not realizing, as the Natchez fully intended, that he had just
given them the time they needed to prepare a very different answer. In
council, the elders were unanimous: the Natchez had been steadily
reduced to something like servitude by a growing French presence they no
longer had the strength to resist by half-measures, and the only real
solution was the coordinated destruction of the French in the province
entire.

## A plan carried nation to nation

Deputies carried the plan to allied nations along the Mississippi, each
receiving a bundle of marked canes to count down the days until a single
coordinated strike; the Chickasaw agreed to spare the more assimilated
Tonica and Ouma, judged too close to the French to be trusted with the
secret. Inside the Natchez nation itself, the scheme was kept from every
woman, princesses included, until the young Grand Sun's own mother - a
sharp, forceful woman called the Tattooed Arm, who suspected something
from the unusual secrecy of the council meetings - pressed her son until
he admitted enough for her to guess the rest. She could not talk him out
of it, but she tried repeatedly to warn the French: through a soldier she
swore to secrecy, and later through her own urgent visit to the fort's
under-lieutenant. Both warnings were dismissed, one soldier even clapped
in irons for repeating it. Chopart himself spent the night before the
appointed day at a party in the Natchez villages, returned to the fort
drunk at dawn, and when pressed one final time to take precautions,
sent his interpreter to ask the Grand Sun directly whether an attack was
planned - a question the young ruler answered calmly enough to satisfy
him completely.

## The morning of Saint Andrew's Eve

The attack came exactly as planned. Warriors entered the fort and the
surrounding houses under cover of delivering the promised corn tribute,
armed as though for a hunt; a single volley of gunfire was the signal for
warriors already inside French homes across the settlement to strike at
once. Chopart, fleeing into his own garden, was run down and killed by a
man of low rank sent for the purpose - no warrior, the source notes,
would lower himself to the task. Of roughly seven hundred French at the
post, only a handful escaped to carry word of the massacre back to New
Orleans. It was the worst single loss of life the French colony of
Louisiana would ever suffer at native hands, and the nation that
inflicted it had, in the same act, made its own survival impossible to
negotiate.
