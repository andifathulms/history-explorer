For years after the French built Fort Rosalie among them, the Natchez
supplied a struggling colony with food it could not yet grow for itself,
trading corn, game and labor for guns, powder, brandy and cloth the
Natchez had never possessed before. It was a genuinely useful
partnership, undone in the winter of 1723 by a debt as small as it was
badly handled: a young soldier who had extended credit to an old warrior
of the village of White Apple for a basket of corn grew impatient when
the corn was not yet dry enough to shake out, threatened to cudgel the
old man over it, and when the warrior challenged him to settle the
matter like men, called out the guard instead. A soldier fired; the
warrior died of his wound the following night.

## A village's answer, and a war chief's peace

White Apple's response was immediate: two Frenchmen killed in reprisal,
followed by a coordinated attack on the nearby settlements. The fighting
lasted only three or four days before Tattooed Serpent, the Great Sun's
own brother and the nation's leading war chief, brokered a peace on
behalf of the French commandant he personally trusted. A Natchez peace
delegation carried the calumet first to a French colonist they judged
more reasonable than their own commandant, and only then, at his
insistence, to the fort itself - a small but telling sign of how little
standing the post's actual commander had earned among the people whose
cooperation his settlement depended on. Trade resumed as before, and for
a few more years the partnership that had built Fort Rosalie held.

It would not last. The same village, the same crop, and much the same
grievance would return six years later, with consequences the Natchez
would not survive.
