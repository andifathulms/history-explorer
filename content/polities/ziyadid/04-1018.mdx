title: The house ends without falling
drafted_from: bosworth-1996

The Ziyadid line failed in 1018 and nothing happened.

There was no siege of Zabid, no battle, no conquering army from the highlands or
from Egypt. The last of the family died with the treasury, the army and the
administration already in the hands of the slaves who had been running them for
a generation, and those slaves went on running them. For four more years the
state had a Ziyadid name attached to it in some form and a household in actual
possession of it, which is an arrangement Umara's account treats as unremarkable
because by then it had been the arrangement for decades.

In 1022 Najah, one of that household, ended the pretence — removed the last
figurehead, took the state in his own name and obtained a diploma from the
Abbasid caliph confirming him in it. The edge this record carries to the Najahid
page is `slave-general of`, dated 1022 rather than 1018, because 1022 is when
the succession was declared and 1018 is when it had already happened.

The four-year gap between this page's `end` and the Najahid page's `start` is
therefore not a defect in the data. It is the interval in which a state had no
dynasty and carried on regardless, and it is the most exact thing this record
can say about what the Ziyadids had become. The `ended` type is `dynastic
replacement`, and the vocabulary has no entry for a house that is not overthrown
but simply outlived by its own servants, which is what this was.
