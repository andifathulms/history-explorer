Erdeni Bek's own submission to Beijing had settled the valley's foreign
policy. What settled its internal shape - the string of subject towns
and forts that would let the next generation call Kokand the largest of
the three khanates - was the work of a bek whose own father Erdeni had
had murdered.

## The child who inherited

Erdeni died in 1770, and Fergana's beks ran through two placeholders in
quick succession - Suliman, then Shah Rukh, who lasted three months -
before the local notables settled on Narbuta, grandson of the dynasty's
own founder Abdul Karim. Narbuta's own father had been killed by Erdeni
Bek in a land grab against Isfara years earlier; the boy had been
spared, his father's heirs scattered, and when Erdeni's own line ran
out it was the survivor of that murder the Khokandians chose to
succeed him.

## Tribute both ways

Narbuta spent his reign methodically taking what Erdeni had only
raided: Andijan, Namangan, Ush and the towns around them, brought
under Kokand's own administration rather than left as tributaries. He
struck no coin and had no khutbeh said in his own name - he governed,
formally, as a subject of the Manghit amirs at Bukhara - but he kept up
his own direct tribute relationship with the Qing court, sending envoys
to Beijing every year or two and receiving gold in return. An Afghan
ambassador who watched one such Chinese audience in the 1790s described
the ceremony that answered it: a hand issuing from a gilded kiosk in
the imperial palace, and a voice asking, in Turkic, whether "my son
Narbuta" was in good health, while the Khokandi envoy prostrated
himself and answered that his master wished only to satisfy His
Majesty's will.

The same envoy left the fullest surviving description of what Narbuta's
own court actually looked like: a palace walled in glazed tile, fifty
or sixty guards carrying petitions to and from the gate rather than
letting anyone approach the bek directly, a Friday procession of ten
thousand soldiers to the mosque where Narbuta heard disputes and
referred the hard ones to his own muftis. His last years were spent in
open war for Khojend against a rival Uzbek clan holding Uratepa, and
in 1799 he moved against the Kazakh-backed khoja ruling Tashkent - a
war his own sons would finish for him.

## What the survivor of a murder built

Narbuta died having never called himself khan and never needed to.
Fergana was consolidated town by town under his own administration, a
tribute relationship with China that outlasted him by decades, and
three sons - Alim, Omar and Shah Rukh - who inherited a state solid
enough to fight over rather than one that still needed defending. It
was the platform his eldest son Alim would use to do openly what
Narbuta had only prepared: take the title of khan, strike his own coin,
and turn Fergana's largest bek-ship into the expansionist state the
next generation of chroniclers actually called a khanate.
