The kings of Pontus claimed descent from Darius I on one side and from Alexander
and Seleucus on the other, and they said so publicly and continuously for two
hundred years.

The Persian claim is the older and probably the real one: the dynasty descends
from a family of Persian nobles who held Kios in Mysia under the Achaemenids and
who came out of the collapse of Lysimachus's kingdom in the 280s with a
principality in northern Anatolia. The name Mithridates is Iranian, the dynastic
cult was Iranian, and the fire-worship and the Persian aristocracy of the
interior were still there when Strabo, who was born at Amaseia, described it two
centuries later.

<Figure id="persepolis" />

The Macedonian claim is the useful one. A Persian dynasty ruling Greek cities on
the Black Sea in a world of Hellenistic monarchies needed to be a Hellenistic
monarchy, and by Mithridates VI's time the coins are Greek, the court is Greek,
the propaganda is Greek, and the king is on the coinage with the flowing hair of
a Hellenistic ruler and the aegis of Alexander.

<Figure id="mithridates-coin" />

## The count on the Achaemenid record

Three later dynasties claimed the Achaemenids publicly, and this is the earliest
of them by a century and a half; the other two are the Parthians and the
Sasanians.

Being descended from a house is one thing and claiming it is another, and the
Mithridatids did the second as loudly as anyone ever has. The descent is on the
coinage. Mithridates VI used the Achaemenid royal title, king of kings. And when
he went to war with Rome, the argument he put to the Greeks of Asia was that he
was liberating them from tax farmers, in the tradition of both his lineages at
once — the Persian kings who had ruled Asia and the Macedonian one who had
taken it from them.

## Sinope

The kingdom spent its first century as an inland Anatolian principality of no
particular consequence, and then in 183 Pharnaces I took Sinope — the largest
Greek city on the southern Black Sea, a colony of Miletus with a harbour on
both sides of an isthmus — and moved the royal seat there from Amaseia.

That is the hinge of everything after. An inland kingdom of Anatolian valleys became a maritime power with a
fleet and access to a closed sea; the grain of the Crimea, the fish of the
Bosporus and the traffic of the whole Black Sea littoral came within reach; and
the dynasty committed to presenting itself in Greek to a Greek coastal
population. From 183 the kingdom lived on its valleys and on the sea in roughly
equal measure.

<Figure id="sinop" />

Mithridates VI inherited that position, took the Bosporan kingdom in the Crimea,
made the Black Sea effectively a Pontic lake, and then used it to fight Rome for
twenty-five years.
