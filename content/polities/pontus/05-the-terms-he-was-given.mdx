The cities of Asia had opened their gates to Mithridates because he was not
Rome. Within three years he was doing to them what Rome had done, and the
clearest case is Chios.

<Figure id="chios" />

A letter arrived from the king. Appian gives it in full and it reads like a
schedule of grievances from a landlord. You still favour the Romans. Many of
your citizens are still living among them. You are enjoying the profits of Roman
property and not accounting to me for it. Your trireme ran into my ship at the
battle off Rhodes — I chose to blame the pilots, hoping you would observe the
rules of safety and remain my submissive subjects. Now you have secretly sent
your leading men to Sulla and never denied doing it publicly, as people not
cooperating with him ought to have done.

Then the sentence. My friends think that those who conspire against my
government, and intend to conspire against my person, deserve death. *I will let
you off with a fine of 2000 talents.*

## The theatre

The Chians wanted to send envoys. The king's officer would not permit it. They
had already been disarmed, the children of their leading families had already
been handed over as hostages, and a barbarian army was in the city.

So they groaned aloud, and collected the temple ornaments and the women's
jewellery until they had made up the 2000 talents.

The officer then accused them of giving him short weight, and summoned the whole
population to the theatre. He posted troops with drawn swords around the theatre
and along the streets leading down to the sea. He brought the Chians out one at a
time, put them into ships — men separate from women and children, all handled
with contempt — and shipped the entire citizen body to Pontus.

<Figure id="priene-theatre" />

## What it cost him

That is the moment the war changed, and it did not change because of a battle.

Mithridates' whole position in Asia rested on being the alternative to Roman tax
farmers. A province that had killed eighty thousand Romans in one day had
committed itself completely and had nowhere else to go — which is enormous
leverage, and he spent it. After Chios, and after Ephesus and the other cities
found out what had happened at Chios, the cities began going back to Rome, and
the king who had been let in started having to besiege the places that had
welcomed him.

## The peace

The end of the first war was correspondingly cheap and correspondingly final.

Sulla had to get to Italy, where his enemies held Rome. So he made terms:
Mithridates gave up everything he had taken and went back to his paternal
kingdom of Pontus as his sole possession, and remained king of it.

<Figure id="sulla" />

Nobody was tried for the massacre. The man who ordered it kept his throne and
his title by treaty. What he had lost was the thing the treaty could not restore
— any prospect that a Greek city in Asia would ever open its gates to him again.
He spent the remaining twenty-two years of the war being pushed north and east,
and at every stage the question was whether he could find anyone to take him in.
