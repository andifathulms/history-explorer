Mithridates I Ktistes died around 266, in his eighties, and left the
kingdom to a son, Ariobarzanes, about whom almost nothing survives
beyond the fact that he struggled to control the Galatian mercenary
bands his father had relied on and left no coinage behind him.
Ariobarzanes died around 250 and passed the throne to his own son -
another Mithridates - while the boy was still a minor.

## A kingdom harried by its own mercenaries

The regency years were harsh. With no adult king to hold them, the
Galatian war-bands that had served the dynasty as hired muscle turned
on the Pontic countryside instead, harrying it so thoroughly that
famine threatened the population outright. Heraclea Pontica, a Greek
city that had kept friendly relations with the Mithridatid house since
its founding, sent what relief it could - and was attacked by the same
Galatian bands in consequence, the price of loyalty to a client dynasty
too weak to protect its own friends.

## A battle that decided who ruled the Seleucid world

Mithridates II came of age into exactly the kind of crisis his family
specialized in exploiting. When the Seleucid brothers Seleucus II and
Antiochus Hierax went to war over the throne in the late 230s,
Mithridates married Seleucus's own sister - reportedly receiving
Greater Phrygia as her dowry - and then threw his support behind her
younger brother Antiochus instead, leading a large Galatian army into
the field on Hierax's behalf. At the Battle of Ancyra the combined
Pontic-Galatian force shattered Seleucus's army outright; twenty
thousand were said to have died, and word spread through the
victorious camp that Seleucus himself had been killed. He had not -
he escaped in the disguise of a common cavalry trooper's armor-bearer
and made it safely across the Taurus into Cilicia, while his own
concubine Mysta, dressed down as a serving-maid to avoid recognition,
was sold into slavery at the Rhodian market before the friendly
Rhodian state identified and ransomed her back to him.

## What one battle bought a minor dynasty

Ancyra broke Seleucus's authority across most of Asia Minor for years
and left Antiochus Hierax the dominant figure in the region - a
position Hierax could not actually hold without the same Galatian
muscle that had won it for him, and one that collapsed into fresh
anarchy within a few years regardless. For Mithridates II, though, the
alliance had already paid for itself: a Seleucid marriage, a claim on
Phrygian territory, and proof that a kingdom too small to defend its
own harvests from mercenary bands could still tip the balance of power
between two much larger states, simply by choosing which Galatian
horde to point at whom.
