By the reign of Sigismund II Augustus, the last Jagiellonian to hold the
Polish throne, the union with Lithuania first sealed by his ancestor's
marriage nearly two centuries earlier had produced one of the largest
combined territories in Europe, and one of its most productive cultural
moments - Copernicus among them, working in a kingdom that had by then
absorbed the Teutonic Order's own former lands as an ordinary fief
rather than an armed threat on its border.

## An old enemy that stopped being one

The military order that had bled both Poland and Lithuania for three
centuries finally dissolved as a political problem rather than a
military one. Its last Grand Master, pressed to submit to his rightful
overlord and stripped of his authority, retired abroad, and Poland
converted the former Teutonic territory into an ordinary ducal fief -
Regal Prussia remaining directly Polish, the rest held as a vassal
duchy. The frontier that had cost Poland and Lithuania their heaviest
losses for generations had, by Sigismund's reign, simply stopped being a
frontier at all.

## Livonia, and a war neither side could finish

The order's Livonian branch, further north, met the same fate on the
same terms. Facing invasion from Moscow it could not withstand alone,
the Livonian Order submitted to Poland, and its last grand master
received the duchy of Courland as a Polish fief in exchange. The war
against Moscow that followed did not go easily: early in the fighting,
the Russians invaded Lithuania itself, and it was at that moment of
crisis that Poland's own nobility made their price for further help
explicit.

## Reinforcements withheld until the union was real

The Polish nobles refused to march to their fellow subjects' defence
except on one condition: that the long-deferred formal union between
Poland and Lithuania finally be completed rather than merely implied by
two centuries of shared kings. The crown granted the condition, and in
1569 a joint diet at Lublin concluded the arrangement definitively -
Lithuania and Poland placed under the same laws and the same government,
with a combined assembly convening not in either kingdom's own historic
capital but at Warsaw, a town in Mazovia chosen precisely because it
belonged fully to neither. The kingdom Mieszko had begun with a
courtship and Bolesław had crowned with an emperor's favour ended its
separate existence not on a battlefield of its own but in the terms it
set for someone else's.
