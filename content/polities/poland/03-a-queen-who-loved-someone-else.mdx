Casimir the Great died in 1370 with no son to succeed him, and the
throne passed to his sister's son, Louis, already reigning as king of
Hungary. It was the first time the Polish nobility bound an incoming
king's accession with formal written conditions rather than simply
receiving him on inherited right - a bond called the Pacta Conventa,
which would govern every royal accession afterward.

## A king who never really governed

Louis had every reason to disappoint the arrangement. He lived in
Hungary throughout his reign, filled Polish offices with his own
Hungarian appointees regardless of his subjects' objections, and left
the kingdom's neighbours to make frequent incursions while its internal
affairs went unattended. His death in 1382, leaving no son of his own,
brought the Piast line - already reduced, by this account, to Louis's
own inherited claim through his mother - to its actual end.

## A fifteen-year-old and her cousin's broken heart

The Poles called Louis's younger daughter, Jadwiga, to the throne in
1384, and the two years between his death and her accession were spent
fending off a rival claimant who had married her elder sister. The
Polish nobility, having secured the succession they wanted, then set
about controlling who Jadwiga herself would marry. Her own preference,
her cousin William of Austria, was rejected outright: an Austrian match
would have subordinated Poland to the same encroaching Habsburg
influence the nobility wanted to avoid. When Jadwiga tried to receive
William in secret regardless, the nobles surrounded the palace, sent him
home, and kept her under guard until she agreed to accept the candidate
they had already chosen for her instead.

## A pagan duke's price for a crown

That candidate was Jogaila, grand duke of a pagan Lithuania whose
territory, by this account, already reached from Poland's own eastern
border past the Dnieper to the north. His offer was calculated to be
irresistible: permanent union of his vast duchy with Poland, and his
own conversion along with all his Lithuanian subjects to Christianity.
Jogaila was baptised under the name Władysław and crowned king of
Poland in 1386, and Jadwiga's own broken engagement became the price of
an alliance that would outlast every participant in the arrangement by
nearly two centuries.
