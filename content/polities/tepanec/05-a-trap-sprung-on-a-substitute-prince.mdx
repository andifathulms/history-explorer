title: A trap sprung on a substitute prince
drafted_from: bancroft-1875

Once his secret preparations for renewed war were complete, Tezozomoc
announced he was ready at last to swear the allegiance he had promised
- pleading old age and poor health as reasons Ixtlilxochitl and his son
Nezahualcoyotl should come to him instead, unarmed, so that his own
still-fearful Tepanecs could witness the ceremony without alarm. The
emperor agreed at first, though he no longer trusted the offer, and at
the last moment sent a substitute, Prince Tecuiltecatl, in his own place
to make excuses. Tezozomoc had the substitute flayed alive on arrival.

## A capital taken while its emperor watched

With his trap sprung on the wrong man, Tezozomoc ordered his own army to
march on Texcoco immediately, entering Acolhua territory the day after
the murder and surrounding the capital the day after that. Only a few
lords answered Ixtlilxochitl's call for help, and after the small
garrison held out as long as it could, the emperor slipped out of the
city by night with Nezahualcoyotl and a handful of companions, taking
refuge in a nearby forest. Texcoco itself fell soon after, betrayed from
within by a ward chief who opened its gates to the besiegers, and what
followed was plunder and slaughter that scattered Ixtlilxochitl's
remaining partisans as far as Tlaxcala.

## A father's last words to a hunted son

Cornered at last by a mixed force of enemies, and with no hope of relief
or supply, Ixtlilxochitl chose to make his own final stand rather than
keep running. He told his son to hide in the branches of a nearby tree,
turned alone to face the approaching soldiers, and died fighting. His
last words to Nezahualcoyotl, by the account most sources agree on,
urged the boy to flee to friends in Tlaxcala, to deal leniently with his
own enemies as his father had - whatever that mercy had cost him - and
left him, he said, no inheritance but a bow and an arrow. Tezozomoc's
own long campaign of patience, betrayal and feigned submission had, at
last, made him master of the whole valley.
