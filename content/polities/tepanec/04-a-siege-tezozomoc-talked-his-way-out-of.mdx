---
title: A siege Tezozomoc talked his way out of
drafted_from: bancroft-1875
phase: peak
---

Tezozomoc's own bid for supremacy over the whole valley did not begin
from a position of strength. The Chichimec emperor Ixtlilxochitl,
exasperated by years of Tepanec provocation, eventually raised an army
large enough to march directly against him, defeating Tezozomoc's
forces in successive battles and driving his allies back within the
fortifications defending Azcapotzalco itself. For months the Tepanec
capital was besieged, its defenders slowly worn down rather than
stormed outright.

## A surrender that was never meant to be honoured

Rather than face the siege's outcome, Tezozomoc sent an embassy throwing
himself on the emperor's mercy - pleading their own distant kinship,
pledging his allies' submission, and promising to travel personally to
Texcoco to swear the allegiance he had so long withheld. Ixtlilxochitl,
weary of war, accepted the offer against the advice of his own lords,
lifted the siege, and disbanded his army. Tezozomoc had no intention of
keeping a single one of his promises.

## An emperor abandoned by the allies who had won his war

The consequences of that clemency were immediate and lasting.
Ixtlilxochitl's own allies, who had fought expecting to divide Tepanec
territory among themselves once victory was secured, found themselves
denied any share of spoils from a war that had, on paper, been won and
then simply given away. Discontent spread quickly, and Tezozomoc,
still without the least intention of honouring his own submission,
fanned it further - quietly promising the same disaffected lords new
territory carved from Texcoco's own domain, and independence from
imperial authority altogether, if they would abandon Ixtlilxochitl in
his turn. A siege Tezozomoc had lost outright was, within a year or two,
turning into the undoing of the very emperor who had won it.
