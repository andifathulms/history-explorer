title: A palace dedication turned assassination

Maxtla had been passed over for the imperial succession, but he had not
been left with nothing: his father had made him king of Coyoacan, a
province he had governed for years already. He had no intention of
settling for it. Tayauh, the brother their father had actually named
heir, was weak and indecisive, with real friends nowhere outside the
kings of Tenochtitlan and Tlatelolco, who likely favoured him only out
of dislike for Maxtla rather than genuine loyalty. Maxtla, by contrast,
held high military rank and the army's own confidence - and only days
after Tezozomoc's funeral, he had himself proclaimed emperor of the
Chichimecs outright, offering his brother his own former lordship of
Coyoacan in exchange for the throne.

## A poisoned gift accepted, and then reversed

Tayauh, humiliated, went to live in Tenochtitlan instead of accepting
the trade, and the Mexica king Chimalpopoca openly blamed him for
surrendering his own claim so easily. On Chimalpopoca's advice, a plot
was arranged months later: a new palace would be built for Tayauh at
Azcapotzalco itself, Maxtla would be invited to its dedication, and
during the ceremony he would be strangled with a wreath of flowers while
being shown through the rooms.

## The trap that killed its own designer

The plot did not survive contact with Maxtla's own vigilance. A page
overheard the conspiracy and reported it, and Maxtla, rather than
avoiding the dedication, attended it and helped with the palace's own
construction, then had Tayauh stabbed to death in the middle of the
festivities meant to kill him instead. What had been engineered as
Tayauh's revenge for a stolen throne became, in the end, the final act
that removed the one rival with any legitimate claim to challenge
Maxtla's own seizure of it.
