The Chinese community at Banten was large, old and structurally important, and no
field on this record can say so.

They were the pepper buyers, financing growers on advance and consolidating small
lots for export. They were the engineers and the labour for Ageng's canal. They
farmed revenues, ran the mint at various points, and provided the shipping that
took Banten's pepper north to China — which is where most of it went, not to
Europe.

The same is true of every port on this region's pages. Chinese communities were
at Melaka before 1511, at Makassar, at Ayutthaya one region over, at Batavia in
numbers, at Brunei since before the sultanate. This corpus's Melaka record
describes four *syahbandars*, harbourmasters for the different merchant
communities, and one of the four was for the Chinese.

## Why there is nowhere to put it

Look at what the schema offers.

`revenue_basis` says where a state's income came from. `military_basis` says how
it raised soldiers. `scripts_and_languages` records the language of
administration. `core_region` is a sentence of prose. None of them has room for a
resident foreign merchant community that financed the crop, moved the export,
lent to the crown, and in several ports was a quarter or more of the town.

This corpus's Yuan record has the same complaint about the *ortogh* — merchant
partnerships financed by Mongol princes, farming taxes and running the state's
long-distance trade — and concludes that the seven values in `revenue_basis` are
categories of extraction, and that a category of finance has no place in them.

Here it is worse, because the community is not an arm of the state at all. It is
a population inside the polity with its own institutions, its own law in practice,
and its own connections to a market outside — and the polity's economy does not
work without it.

## And what happened to them

Nothing good, mostly, and not on this record.

The Batavia massacre of 1740, in which the Dutch company killed something like
ten thousand Chinese residents of its own capital, is fifty-six years after this
record ends and belongs to a polity this site does not carry. The relevance here
is that the survivors fled east and their arrival triggered a war that pulled the
company deeper into Java — which is on this corpus's Mataram record, in the
sequence of succession wars its third chapter describes.

So the one place a Chinese community appears in the causal chain this site can
draw is as the victims of a massacre, on a page about somebody else's succession.
