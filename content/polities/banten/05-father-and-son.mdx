Sultan Haji was Ageng Tirtayasa's son and designated heir. In 1682 he took the
capital with Dutch company troops, besieged his father in the interior, and
captured him. In 1684 he signed the treaty that gave the company the pepper
monopoly and excluded every other European from Banten.

He is this record's `last` ruler. The man who ended this polity is on its own
list of rulers, which is true of very few records on this site.

`ended.type` is `gradual absorption` and `ended.by` is empty. The company has no
page here — that is the ordinary reason, and thirteen other records in this
region have an empty `by` for a version of it. What is unusual is that even if the company had a page, naming it
would be only half of what happened. This ending was arranged inside the family.

## Two endings from inside

Set it beside this corpus's Johor record, which ends in 1699 when the sultan's
own nobles killed him on the way to the mosque and the bendahara took the throne.
Both are polities ended by the people closest to the ruler; both have an empty
`ended.by` for that reason rather than for want of a record; and both were
followed by a long period in which the name continued and the thing did not.

The difference is what the two endings cost. At Johor what went was the orang
laut, who had served the dynasty rather than the office and would not serve the
usurpers — an institution that had survived Srivijaya and Melaka and did not
survive a killing.

Here what went was the position. Ageng's Banten was the last open port in the
western archipelago; Makassar, its eastern counterpart, had been destroyed for
the same policy fifteen years earlier. After 1684 there was nowhere in the
islands to buy pepper or spices outside the company's system, which had been the
company's object since it arrived and which it finally achieved by helping a son
against his father.

The sultanate continued as an office until Raffles abolished it in 1813. This
record stops at 1684, on the same test this site applies to the two courts that
came out of Mataram: what ended is a polity's disposal of its own external
relations, and a title is not that.
