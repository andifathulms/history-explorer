In 1527 a Demak expedition took Banten and then took Sunda Kelapa, the port at
the mouth of the Ciliwung that this corpus's Sunda record had promised to the
Portuguese five years earlier. It was renamed Jayakarta.

The Dutch company took it in 1619 and called it Batavia, and ran everything it
did in Asia from there for three hundred and twenty-two years. The name it has
now is the one given in 1527 by the force that arrived to stop a Hindu kingdom
selling the harbour to Europeans.

Banten is what that expedition turned into. The edge `demak → banten` is typed
`seceded from`, which is the only use of that type anywhere in the archipelago:
Hasanuddin, whose father had led the campaign, was ruling Banten as a sultanate
of its own by 1552, while Demak itself was falling apart in the succession war
this corpus's Demak record's last chapter describes.

## Pepper, again

`revenue_basis` here reads `trade-toll` and `monopoly`, which is character for
character the coding on this corpus's Aceh record at the other end of Sumatra.

Both lived on pepper. Banten taxed it at the port and held a crown monopoly over
the growing districts of Lampung, across the Sunda strait in southern Sumatra;
Aceh did the same on the coasts it controlled at the northern tip.

And both monopolies failed in the same way, which is worth stating because the
fields cannot. A crown monopoly on a bulk crop depends on being the only buyer,
and a European company with ships and credit could buy upcountry, from the
growers, without calling at the capital at all. Once that was possible the
monopoly was a claim rather than a mechanism. The Aceh record's second chapter
makes the point; this record is the second instance of it, three thousand
kilometres away and about the same commodity.
