Sultan Haji was Ageng Tirtayasa's son and designated heir. In 1682 he took the
capital with Dutch company troops, besieged his father in the interior, and
captured him. In 1684 he signed the treaty that gave the company the pepper
monopoly and excluded every other European from Banten.

Sultan Haji is the sultanate's last ruler. The man who ended the polity is also
the last name on its own list of kings, which is a rarer thing than it sounds.

It is tempting to file this as a Dutch conquest, and the company certainly
supplied the troops and collected the monopoly. But no company took Banten. A
son took it from his father, with borrowed soldiers, and then paid for them
with the thing the sultanate had lived on.

## Ended from inside

Johor went the same way fifteen years later, when the sultan's own nobles
killed him on the road to the mosque and the chief minister took the throne.

Both polities were ended by the people closest to the ruler rather than by
anyone outside. Both were followed by a long stretch in which the name
continued and the thing did not: a sultan at Banten under company terms, a
bendahara dynasty at Johor with no fleet. Being destroyed by a stranger at
least leaves a date. Being handed away by your heir leaves an office that goes
on being occupied.

The difference is what the two endings cost. At Johor what went was the orang
laut, who had served the dynasty rather than the office and would not serve the
usurpers — an institution that had survived Srivijaya and Melaka and did not
survive a killing.

Here what went was the position. Ageng's Banten was the last open port in the
western archipelago; Makassar, its eastern counterpart, had been destroyed for
the same policy fifteen years earlier. After 1684 there was nowhere in the
islands to buy pepper or spices outside the company's system, which had been
the company's object since it arrived and which it finally achieved by helping
a son against his father.

The sultanate continued as an office until Raffles abolished it in 1813. But a
polity is a thing that disposes of its own external relations, and after 1684
Banten did not: the treaty settled who it could trade with, and with whom it
could not. What survived for another hundred and twenty-nine years was a title.
