Mali has no conqueror and no date anybody would defend closely. What it has is a
contraction lasting two and a half centuries.

## What went, in order

Timbuktu in 1433, to Tuareg out of the desert. It was the first substantial loss,
and it went to a power with no state behind it — which is the part worth
noticing. The empire's frontier at the desert edge had stopped being defensible
before any rival empire existed to test it.

Then Gao. Songhai had been a Malian province in the fourteenth century; by the
1460s it was taking the Niger bend for itself.

Then the Atlantic. Portuguese ships reached the Senegal in the 1440s and the Gold
Coast in the 1470s, and gold that had gone north across the Sahara for six
centuries began going west to the sea instead. Mali understood the problem
directly: an embassy went to Portugal in 1534 asking for help against Songhai —
the earliest recorded diplomatic contact between a West African empire and a
European state — and got nothing.

Niani was sacked by Songhai in 1545 and briefly held. Morocco destroyed Songhai
in 1591, and Mali outlived its destroyer by decades without being able to take
any advantage of it. The Bamana of Segu finished the empire in the seventeenth
century.

## How large it had been

At its height in 1380 Mali covered 1,100,000 square kilometres, on Taagepera's
tabulation.

For scale: larger than the Holy Roman Empire at its widest. About four-fifths of
the Neo-Assyrian empire at its peak. About four-fifths of the Byzantine empire at
*its* peak — which was in 555, eight hundred years earlier, by which point
Byzantium had almost nothing left. Nothing in Latin Europe came close.

That comparison is worth making because the general-history version of this
empire is a pilgrimage and a gold price, and it leaves out the fact that Mansa
Musa was travelling as the ruler of one of the largest states on earth.

One caution about figures of this kind, since this is as good a place as any to
give it. Estimates of territorial extent survive mainly for empires large enough
to have been catalogued by the people who compile such tabulations, which means a
kingdom with no figure attached is not thereby a small one. It is a kingdom
nobody has measured. Most of the polities on these pages are in that position,
and the silence is about the state of the scholarship rather than about the size
of the state.
