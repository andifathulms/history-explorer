Almost every polity on this site used gold coinage, and a great deal of that
gold came from West Africa, up the trans-Saharan routes through Sijilmasa and
Awdaghust. The Almoravids on this site were a Saharan movement sitting on the
northern end of those routes. Mali held the southern end.

Sundiata Keita assembled it in the 1230s out of the Malinke chieftaincies of the
upper Niger, on the ruins of Ghana. It controlled the goldfields of Bambuk and
Bure, the Niger as a transport artery, and the caravan termini at Timbuktu and
Gao. Mansa Musa's pilgrimage to Mecca in 1324 — with an entourage and quantity of
gold that reportedly disturbed the Cairo market for years afterwards — is the
event that put a West African ruler on European maps, and the Catalan Atlas of
1375 draws him holding a gold nugget.

This page has no reach figure. Taagepera does not tabulate Mali, and the empire's
authority was strongest along the river and the trade routes and progressively
weaker away from them, which is not well described by an area in any case.

Islam here is counted as carried. It arrived with the merchants, was adopted by
the ruling class, and was then propagated onward — Timbuktu and Djenne became
centres of Islamic learning with endowed institutions drawing scholars from the
whole Maghreb, which is what the coding rules ask for and what mere adoption
would not supply.
