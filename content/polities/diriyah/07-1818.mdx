Diriyah was not garrisoned or annexed. It was demolished — the walls, the
towers, the palaces and the houses systematically pulled down and the date
palms cut, on orders intended to ensure that nothing could be rebuilt there and
that the movement would have no centre to return to.

<Figure id="turaif-collapsed" />

Abdullah ibn Saud was sent to Cairo, then to Istanbul, where he was displayed
and beheaded. Members of the Al Saud and the Al al-Shaykh were deported to
Egypt. The emirate ended completely, and by the standards of the period the
suppression was thorough.

<Figure id="abdullah-coste" />

It did not take. Six years later a cousin of the executed imam, Turki ibn
Abdullah, was in Riyadh reconstituting the state — the Egyptian garrisons could
not hold the interior any more than anybody else had been able to, and the
doctrine had not been deported with the families that professed it. That is the
next record.

Two things belong at the end.

The first is who exactly did this. The army was Egyptian, raised by a governor
in the process of making himself independent, who within a decade would be at
war with the sultan who had commissioned him. The war was nonetheless fought on
Ottoman authority, in the Ottoman sultan's name, to recover a title that was
his — so the destruction of the emirate is properly the sultan's act and was
carried out by a man who would shortly be his enemy. It is a case where the
honest
answer is a sentence and the field takes an id.

The second is what the emirate demonstrated, which nobody at the time drew the
right conclusion from. Central Arabia had been left alone for a thousand years
because it was poor, empty and expensive to reach, and everyone had assumed it
was therefore politically inert. In sixty years an oasis of a few hundred
households, with a doctrine and no money, took the eastern coast, sacked a
shrine city in Iraq and held Mecca. Ibrahim Pasha's campaign proved that the
interior could be conquered by a state that really wanted to; the century after
it proved that no state did.
