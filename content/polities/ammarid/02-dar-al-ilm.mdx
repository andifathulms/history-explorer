The thing the Banu Ammar are remembered for is a library.

The Dar al-Ilm at Tripoli was founded by the family in the later eleventh
century on the Fatimid model: an endowed institution with copyists on the
payroll, open to scholars, collecting across the disciplines rather than only
in law and hadith. A dynasty of judges spent its surplus on the one kind of
institution judges understand, and it worked — the city became a place scholars
in the eastern Mediterranean had reason to travel to.

## The numbers nobody can use

Every later account gives a figure for the number of volumes, and the figures
are enormous and wildly inconsistent with one another.

They are not competing measurements. They are a literary convention about
destroyed libraries, of the kind that also attaches to Alexandria and to
Cordoba, and the sensible thing is to notice that the tradition insists the
collection was vast and to decline to say how vast. Picking one of the numbers,
or splitting the difference, would produce a fact that nobody ever recorded.

## How it ended, on three accounts

What is not in doubt is that the library existed, that it was among the notable
collections of the Islamic Mediterranean, and that it did not survive 1109.

How it was lost is contested in the medieval sources and remains so. One line
of report has the Franks burning it deliberately after the city fell. Another
has it destroyed in the general sack, which was thorough. A third has it
already broken up and dispersed during the seven years of blockade, when a
besieged city was selling whatever could be sold.

The three accounts are not reconcilable and the question is probably not
answerable. What they agree on is the date.

## What a family of lawyers left

The Banu Ammar founded no religion, adapted no script, and no later state
claimed to continue them. By the ordinary measures of influence they registered
nothing at all.

They also endowed the most important research collection on that coast, in a
city they had taken over by simply continuing to do their jobs after the
government above them stopped functioning, and it was the most consequential
thing they did. It is worth being clear that these two statements are about the
same people, and that the second is the one a reader should keep.
