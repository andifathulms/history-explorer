title: Nothing to hand over

What happened after the murder is the clearest demonstration in the corpus of
what Nadir's state had actually been. There was no succession war, because there
was nothing coherent to fight over. The Afghan contingent rode east under Ahmad
Khan Abdali with as much of the treasury as they could carry, and founded the
Durrani state at Kandahar that modern Afghanistan dates itself from. The
Caucasus provinces went their own way. Every regional commander with troops
became the government of wherever he was standing.

Nadir's nephew Adil Shah took the throne, killed all of Nadir's family he could
find except Shahrukh — a grandson who was also, through his mother, a grandson
of the Safavid Sultan Husayn, which is the reason he was spared — and was
himself deposed and blinded within a year. Shahrukh was proclaimed at Mashhad in
1748, blinded in 1750 by a rival, and then restored, and held Khurasan as a
client of whoever was strongest for another forty-six years.

That is the whole Afsharid afterlife: a blind man in Mashhad, tolerated by the
Zands and then by the Qajars because he was harmless and because his Safavid
blood made him useful to nobody. In 1796 Agha Muhammad Khan Qajar came to
Mashhad, tortured him to find where Nadir's treasure was buried, and killed him.

This site's span field for the polity therefore closes as a range, 1747 to 1796,
and the two dates measure different things: when the empire stopped, and when
the family did. It is the same shape as the Jalayirid record two pages back and
the Artuqid record in another region, and it is the ordinary way these houses
end.

The comparison the corpus invites is with the Timurids. Both were built by a man
of no legitimate descent who held an enormous following by continuous victory;
both took Delhi; both empires came apart on the founder's death. The difference
is what was left over. The Timurid century produced a court style three later
empires worked from. Nadir left a stripped treasury, a depopulated countryside, a
blinded son, and the man who founded Afghanistan.
