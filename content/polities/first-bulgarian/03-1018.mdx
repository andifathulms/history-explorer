Basil II spent the years from 976 to 1018 destroying Bulgaria, and this corpus's
Byzantine record's eighth chapter — the one filed under contraction while
describing the empire's most successful period — calls it a campaign of attrition
rather than of battles, conducted almost annually for four decades.

The Bulgarian state at that point was not the one Simeon had ruled. It had lost
its eastern half to a Byzantine campaign in 971 and been reconstituted in
Macedonia under Samuel, with its centre at Ohrid rather than Preslav; Fine treats
the continuity as real and the standard Bulgarian view treats it as unbroken,
and this record's `capitals` field carries the move.

## Kleidion

In 1014 Basil trapped a Bulgarian army in a mountain pass at Kleidion and
destroyed it. According to the Byzantine account he then blinded the prisoners —
some fourteen thousand of them — leaving one man in a hundred with a single eye
to lead the rest home, and Samuel died two days after they arrived.

This record marks the turning point `contested: true` and the reason is worth
stating precisely, because it is not squeamishness.

The figure is unverifiable and comes from a source written a century later. The
epithet Basil is known by — *Boulgaroktonos*, the Bulgar-slayer — is not
contemporary either; it first appears in the twelfth century and was then
enthusiastically adopted by nineteenth-century Greek nationalism and, from the
other direction, by Bulgarian. What is not in doubt is that a battle was fought,
that the Bulgarian army was destroyed, and that the state surrendered four years
later.

Coding rule 4.4 says `contested` marks a dispute about whether an event was a
hinge rather than about its date or details. Here the hinge is not in doubt and
the flag is being used slightly differently — for an event whose *substance* is
partly a later construction — and this paragraph is the site saying so rather
than hiding it in a boolean.

## A hundred and sixty-seven years

Bulgaria was annexed. Basil administered it moderately by the standards of the
period — taxes in kind rather than coin, the local aristocracy given Byzantine
titles — and the patriarchate was reduced to an archbishopric at Ohrid under
imperial appointment.

Then in 1185 two brothers revolted over a tax and there was a Bulgarian state
again.

This site records that with the `resumes` field on the Second Bulgarian record,
and not with an edge. The two records are one object seen twice across an
interruption, which is the field's whole definition, and it is the first use of
it outside the Ancient Near East — where it was built for the two Babylons, the
two Assyrias, the two Elams and the two Egypts.

The Balkans region this record sits in therefore has two polities and no internal
edge at all, and carries `thread: false`. That is the ordinary state and the
workflow rule requires it: a region enters with the flag off and it is flipped
only when real sourced edges join two of its members. There are none here and
there may never be.
