Boris was baptised in 864, after several years of negotiating with Rome and
Constantinople simultaneously and taking whichever offered the better terms. The
better terms were an archbishop of his own and, within a generation, a church
using a language his subjects spoke.

That second part is what made the conversion decisive. A Bulgar khan adopting
Greek Christianity would have made his state a Byzantine dependency in
everything but name, because the church would have been Greek and its clergy
answerable to Constantinople. A Bulgarian church in Slavonic was something else,
and Boris got it by taking in the pupils of Cyril and Methodius when the
Moravian mission this site's Byzantine record's seventh chapter describes was
driven out by Frankish clergy.

## Preslav

In 893 a council moved the capital to Preslav, made Slavonic the language of
church and state, and expelled the remaining Greek clergy. The literary school
that followed produced translations of the Greek fathers, original Slavonic
compositions, and the script.

Cyrillic was developed there, from Greek uncial letters, by Cyril's pupils —
Clement and Naum at Ohrid, and the Preslav school. It is written today by
something over two hundred and fifty million people.

And this record's `descendant_scripts` count is zero.

That is coding rule 3 working exactly as written and it is the most
counter-intuitive result on this site. The rule counts a script derived from or
newly adapted from the polity's writing system, on the record of the polity whose
script was adapted. The parent of Cyrillic is Greek uncial. Greek is Byzantium's
script, so the Byzantine record carries the count — where its own aside calls it
the clearest single case in the corpus of what the axis is for.

This corpus has now produced that result four times. The Hittite claim lands on a
Babylonian page. The Middle Elamite one lands on a Mesopotamian page. The
Urartian one lands on the Assyrian record Urartu was built to resist. This is the
starkest, because the workshop and the parent script are in different polities
and nobody disputes which was which.

What this record does carry is `religions_carried: 1`, and coding rule 2 says
explicitly that transmission rather than invention is the measure. The clergy,
the books, the liturgy and the alphabet went out of Preslav and Ohrid to Serbia
and to the Rus, and the Rus conversion of about 988 was supplied from here.

## Simeon

Boris's son had been educated at Constantinople, and the Byzantines called him
half-Greek. He spent thirty years at war with the empire, beat it repeatedly, and
wanted one thing it would not give: to be crowned emperor in the capital.

He took the title *tsar* — the Slavonic form of caesar — and styled himself
emperor of the Bulgarians and the Romans, which is a claim on the Byzantine
imperial title and not a translation of a barbarian one. This site's Byzantine
record now counts three successor claims and Simeon is the earliest of them, six
centuries before Mehmed II took the same title with the city.

He never got the city. He besieged it twice, could not take walls his army had no
way of assaulting, and died in 927 with the war unwon.

The peace his son made is this record's fourth turning point and it gave Bulgaria
what the war had been about without the throne: recognition of the tsar's title,
an autocephalous patriarchate, and a Byzantine princess. It is the high point of
the state and it is also the moment it stopped growing.
