Basil II, having spent forty years on the conquest, governed the result with a
light hand. Forbes's judgement is that he treated the territory more as a
protectorate than a possession: taxes levied in kind rather than coin, the
existing arrangements left largely alone, the nobility conciliated.

After his death it stopped being like that.

## The see at Ohrid

The Bulgarian patriarchate, established at Ohrid in 972, was reduced to an
archbishopric. In 1025 the see was given to a Greek, who lost no time in
eliminating the Bulgarian element from positions of importance throughout the
diocese.

That is the sentence to sit with, because the institution survived and its
purpose was inverted. The church Boris had spent four years negotiating for and
Simeon had extracted from a defeated empire — the thing that made Bulgarians a
people rather than two populations sharing a country — went on existing for the
next seven and a half centuries as a Greek-run instrument for making them
something else.

The nobility went the other way. Many were transplanted to Constantinople, where,
Forbes says, their opposition was numbed by the bestowal of honours. It is the
oldest method there is and it is remarkably cheap: a court title, an estate, a
place in a ceremonial that was the most impressive in the world, and a Bulgarian
boyar's grandson was a Byzantine.

## What the eleventh century did to the ground

The Balkans in these years were crossed by everyone.

Pechenegs and Cumans came over the Danube repeatedly, invited by Greeks against
Bulgars and by Bulgars against Greeks, and the pattern was always the same: the
barbarians stayed longer and did more damage than had been bargained for, and
left some of their number behind as settlers nobody had asked for. Emperors
planted colonies of Armenians and Vlachs. The Normans arrived in 1081. The
crusaders came through in 1096 and behaved so badly that the population of the
peninsula was permanently disinclined to sympathise with the cause.

The ethnographic map of the Balkans that everyone would later fight about was
substantially made in the century after Bulgaria stopped existing, by an empire
moving populations around a province it had won and could not garrison.

## The heresy takes the job

And the Bogomils grew.

Forbes's account of why is the useful one: under the weight of the Greek
administration and the turmoil of the invasions, the heresy became a refuge for
patriotism and an outlet for its expression. There was no Bulgarian state to be
loyal to and no Bulgarian church to belong to, and there was a doctrine holding
that all visible authority belonged to the devil, preached in Slavonic, by people
who owned nothing.

Alexios Komnenos persecuted it bitterly, and the persecution did what
persecutions of this kind reliably do. It spread west from its centre at
Philippopolis into Serbia, and from Serbia into Bosnia, where the local church
was accused of it for the next two centuries — and further, across the Adriatic
and eventually into Languedoc.

So the two things this state built that outlasted it both went on working with
the state gone. The church it had fought Constantinople for spent seven hundred
years as Constantinople's instrument. The heresy it had spent a century
persecuting became the vehicle for the loyalty the church could no longer carry,
and travelled a thousand miles doing it.

## 1185

The interruption lasted a hundred and sixty-seven years, which is long enough
for nobody alive to remember the thing that ended.

Then two brothers from the Tarnovo region fell out with the imperial government
over a tax, and there was a Bulgarian state again — with the title of tsar
resumed, a patriarch at a new capital, and the bones of the first empire's saints
carried to Tarnovo to make the point that this was not a new country.
