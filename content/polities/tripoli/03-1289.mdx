title: What Qalawun did to the city

After Antioch fell in 1268 the northern Frankish position was one county, held
by the man who had been prince of Antioch, with Latakia as an outlying remnant
until 1287 and the Mamluk state to the south deciding when to finish it.

The county spent those years quarrelling with itself. Bohemond VII fought a war
against the Templars and against the commune of Tripoli, a body of townsmen and
Italian merchants who at one point ran the city in defiance of him. When he died
in 1287 without children the succession went to his sister Lucia, who was in
Apulia, and the commune preferred a republic under Genoese protection. The
dispute was still live when the Mamluk army arrived.

Qalawun besieged Tripoli in March 1289 and took it after a month. What happened
then is the reason this record's third turning point is worded as it is. The
city was not occupied. It was destroyed — the population killed or enslaved, the
buildings pulled down, and the peninsula on which the old city stood abandoned.
A new Tripoli was built inland at the foot of Raymond's castle, and that is
where the city is today. Removing a port from the map is a deliberate policy and
the Mamluks applied it along that coast: a harbour the Franks could not use was
a harbour no crusade could land in.

The edge from this page runs to the Mamluk record, and it is the middle one of
three. Antioch 1268, Tripoli 1289, Acre 1291. The Frankish states of the Levant
had been there for a hundred and ninety-one years when the sequence began and
twenty-three years later there were none, and every one of the three edges is
typed the same way and points at the same polity.

This record ends the northern half of that story two years before the southern
half. The last count of Tripoli died a hundred kilometres away in Cyprus, and
what remained of the four states — the titles, the law books, the surviving
families, the crown of Jerusalem itself — went to the island and stayed there.
Cyprus is not in this corpus. When it is, it will take an edge from the
Jerusalem page and not from this one, because what continued was a kingdom and a
claim, and the county of Tripoli made no claim to anything.
