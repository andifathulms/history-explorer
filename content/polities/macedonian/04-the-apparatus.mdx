---
title: The empire he kept
drafted_from: ab-bosworth-1988
phase: aside
---

Alexander conquered the Achaemenid empire and then, to a degree that startled
his own officers, governed it as the Achaemenid empire.

He kept the satrapies and in several cases kept the satraps — Mazaeus, who had
commanded a wing against him at Gaugamela, was left as governor of Babylon. He
kept the taxation and the treasury system. He kept the Aramaic chancery. He
buried Darius in the royal tombs and hunted the satrap who had killed him, which
is not the behaviour of a conqueror towards a defeated enemy and is exactly the
behaviour of a successor towards a murdered predecessor.

He also adopted the court. Persian dress in a modified form, Persian ushers and
chamberlains, and — the flashpoint — *proskynesis*, the prostration or bow the
Persian court required before the king, which Greeks understood as an act owed
only to gods and Macedonians understood as a humiliation. The attempt to impose
it on his Macedonian officers in 327 failed and was dropped.

The Susa marriages and the enrolment of thirty thousand trained Iranian infantry
in 324 are the same programme carried further, and this record's turning point
for that year is about the reaction: the Macedonian army mutinied at Opis over
the discharge of veterans and, underneath it, over being replaced.

Two things follow for this corpus.

The first is that this is the earliest instance on the site of a pattern it
records a dozen times afterwards — the conqueror who keeps the apparatus. The
Arabs kept the Sasanian and Byzantine administrations; the Mongols kept the
Chinese and Persian ones; the Seljuks and the Ak Koyunlu and the Timurids hired
the Persian bureaucracy the moment they had a settled country. Alexander did it
first and did it inside two years.

The second is that the resistance came from his own side. The people who
objected to governing Persia as Persians were the Macedonians who had conquered
it, and the objection was not principled but proprietary. That, too, recurs: the
Qizilbash on this site's Safavid page objected to the ghulams for the same
reason, and the Mamluk households objected to the awlad al-nas.
