Sunassura's own subordination to Mittani did not last. The most
important surviving document from his reign is a treaty concluded not
with a Mittanian king but with the Hittite king Tudhaliya I, whose own
reign is dated to roughly the 1430s through the 1390s. The treaty is
detailed enough to fix Kizzuwatna's own borders precisely, paragraph
by paragraph, in a way no earlier document had - the kind of careful
specificity a stronger power uses when formalizing exactly how much
autonomy a weaker one will be permitted to keep.

## An ending with no battle in it

Nothing in the sources describes a Hittite conquest of Kizzuwatna, no
siege, no defeated army, no captured king. The kingdom's own
independent political history simply stops being visible in the
record at the point the Sunassura treaty was concluded, and what comes
after, in later Hittite documents, is Kizzuwatna administered as part
of the Hittite state rather than negotiated with as a separate one.
Whatever process completed that transition - and there is reason to
think a Kizzuwatnean-born queen at the Hittite court may have played
some part in it, though the evidence is too thin to say for certain
how large a part - it was a change accomplished through treaty,
marriage and administrative absorption rather than through the kind
of war that leaves an ending anyone thought worth recording as one.
