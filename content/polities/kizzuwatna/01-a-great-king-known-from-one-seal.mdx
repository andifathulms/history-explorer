title: A great king known from one seal

Cilicia's history before the sixteenth century is largely a blank the
sources cannot fill - contact between the region and the expanding
Hittite Old Kingdom appears to have been slighter and less regular
than historians once assumed, and there is little sign that the plain
between the Taurus and Amanus mountains was ever brought under stable
outside control before Kizzuwatna itself emerged as a kingdom in its
own right. The first ruler of that kingdom known by name survives
through the barest kind of evidence: a clay seal impression, found at
the site of Tarsus, reading only "Ispudahsu, Great King, son of
Pariyawatri."

## A title that had to be recognized to mean anything

Calling oneself Great King on a private seal is one thing; being
treated as one by a neighboring power is another, and Ispudahsu
achieved both. He concluded a formal treaty with the Hittite king
Telipinu, whose own reign is dated to roughly 1525 to 1500 - the
earliest surviving document to treat a Cilician ruler as a legitimate
diplomatic partner rather than a target for campaign or tribute. Where
earlier Hittite kings may have passed through the Cilician gates on
their way to Syria without leaving any lasting mark on the region
itself, Telipinu's own generation was dealing with someone who
expected, and received, a negotiated settlement instead.

## A kingdom that starts in the written record already formed

What the seal and the treaty do not explain is how Ispudahsu came to
hold the title at all - there is no account of a founding conquest, no
predecessor named, no origin story preserved anywhere in the
surviving Hittite or Cilician archives. Kizzuwatna simply appears in
the historical record already functioning as a state substantial
enough for its neighbor to treat with, which is itself the clearest
evidence available that whatever process produced it had already run
its course by the time anyone thought to write it down.
