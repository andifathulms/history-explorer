---
title: The khanate that stayed on the steppe
drafted_from: biran-1997
phase: formation
---

Chagatai's inheritance was Transoxiana and the Tarim — the old Karakhanid and
Kara Khitai lands, containing Bukhara and Samarkand, two of the wealthiest cities
in the Islamic world, and a very large amount of pasture.

The khanate spent a century unable to decide which of those it was. Its rulers
were pulled between the settled Muslim towns that generated revenue and the
nomadic Mongol military that generated power, and every attempt to move toward
the towns — Kebek minting a settled coinage and building a residence near
Qarshi in the 1320s, Tarmashirin converting to Islam in the 1330s — produced a
revolt from the steppe. Tarmashirin was overthrown for it.

The khanate split in the 1340s into a western half, Transoxiana, that became
Muslim and settled, and an eastern half, Moghulistan, that stayed nomadic. This
is the case that shows what the Ilkhanate and the Golden Horde avoided by
converting decisively: the Chagatai state fractured along exactly the seam that
every steppe polity governing farmland has to bridge, and this site's Liao page
describes the alternative solution — two administrations, deliberately kept
apart.

Out of the western half, and out of a minor Turkicised Mongol clan within it,
came Timur.
