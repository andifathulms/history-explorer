---
title: What the Mongols left in place
drafted_from: suny-1994
phase: contraction
---

The Khwarazmian Jalal al-Din, driven west by the Mongols, sacked Tbilisi in 1226
— this site's Khwarazmian record ends five years later — and the Mongols arrived
properly in 1236. By 1243 the kingdom had submitted: tribute, a census, a levy
of troops for Mongol campaigns, and a resident overseer.

What is interesting is what was not taken. The dynasty stayed. The church stayed,
with its property and its jurisdiction. Georgian law and Georgian nobles went on
governing Georgian districts. The Mongols wanted soldiers, silver and quiet, and
they had discovered — as this site's Ilkhanate record describes across a much
larger territory — that a client dynasty collecting for them was cheaper than a
province.

The cost was the ability to decide anything. When the succession was disputed in
the 1240s the Mongols settled it by recognising both claimants, and Georgia
spent seventy years as two kingdoms with two kings, both called David, both
confirmed from outside. Georgian contingents fought in Mongol armies at
Baghdad in 1258 and at Ayn Jalut in 1260, on this site's Ilkhanate and Mamluk
pages, on somebody else's business.

George V, called the Brilliant, put it back together between 1299 and 1346 —
stopped the tribute as Ilkhanid authority collapsed, recovered the western
half, restored the law and the roads, and briefly made the kingdom what it had
been. He is the last Georgian ruler in this record who was not primarily
managing a decline.

Then Timur came, eight times between 1386 and 1403, and this site's Timurid
record describes the method: take the submission and the plunder, install or
confirm, leave, and return when the submission lapses. Georgia submitted and
lapsed repeatedly, and each cycle removed more of the country's population and
capital than the last. The kings kept the title through all of it.
