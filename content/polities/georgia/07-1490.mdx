The fifteenth century finished it, and not by invasion. The Timurid campaigns
and then the Turkmen ones had ruined the countryside and destroyed the crown's
revenue; the great noble houses had recovered the autonomy David IV had taken
from them, because a crown that cannot pay a standing army has to ask them for
one; and Constantinople fell in 1453, which removed the Christian empire Georgia
had oriented itself towards for six centuries and left it surrounded.

The last kings of a united Georgia spent their reigns fighting their own
princes. In 1490 a royal council formally acknowledged what had already
happened and divided the kingdom among three branches of the Bagrationi:
Kartli in the centre, Kakheti in the east, Imereti in the west, with several
princedoms effectively independent besides.

Nobody conquered anything in 1490. The country came apart, and the three pieces
were held by three branches of the same family.

What followed took three centuries. The kingdoms lived between the Ottomans and
the Safavids, who partitioned the country between themselves by treaty in 1555
and again in 1639 — Imereti to Istanbul, Kartli and Kakheti to Isfahan. Georgian
kings converted to Islam to keep their thrones and reconverted when they could.
Georgians were bought and sold as soldiers across the whole Islamic world:
Abbas I built the ghulam corps that broke the Qizilbash substantially out of
Georgians and Armenians, and Cairo was buying on the same frontier. A country
that could not defend itself was, among other things, a source of supply.

Kartli and Kakheti were united in 1762 under Erekle II, who signed a treaty of
protection with Russia in 1783, was sacked by Agha Muhammad Khan of Persia in
1795, and whose kingdom Russia annexed outright in 1801.

One number is worth ending on. The Bagrationi held a throne somewhere in Georgia
from 888, when Adarnase took the title in the south-western marches, to 1801,
when the last of them was deposed by Russia. That is nine hundred and thirteen
years.

It is not the tenure of a state — the state broke in 1490 and had been remade
several times before that. It is the tenure of a family, holding whichever
Georgian throne was available at the time, which is a different and in some ways
more impressive kind of durability. There were three kingdoms after 1490 and all
three of them were Bagrationi.
