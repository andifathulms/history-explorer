---
title: What a Christian kingdom looked like in these fields
drafted_from: suny-1994
phase: aside
---

This corpus is heavily Islamic and Iranian, and its vocabularies were built on
that material. The coding rules say so explicitly and warn about it: when a
polity outside the centre of gravity does not fit, the answer is null or a
vocabulary change argued on the merits, never the nearest familiar label.

Georgia is a useful test because it fits almost perfectly, and the places it does
not are informative.

`client-levy` and `standing-professional` describe the Georgian army with no
strain at all — and `client-levy` is coded on very little else here, because a
hereditary landed nobility owing military service is the standard European and
Caucasian arrangement and is not the standard Islamic one. The corpus has it on
this record and on the Armenian one next door, which is the point: two Christian
kingdoms of the isthmus arrived at the same institution and everything around
them did not.

`primogeniture` with a co-ruler device is likewise unstrained, and the nearest
comparison the corpus offers is Ptolemaic Egypt fourteen centuries earlier.
`descent` covers a Davidic claim as easily as it covers a Sasanian or a
Ghassanid one, all three of them constructed.

What the fields cannot hold is the church, and the omission is systematic rather
than particular to this page. The Georgian church was autocephalous, held vast
property, ran the law in large areas of life, maintained foundations on Athos and
in Jerusalem and Bulgaria, and supplied the kingdom's ideology, its literacy and
a good part of its administration. There is no institution field for it. The
`religions_carried` count measures propagation outward and reads null here
correctly, because Georgia converted its own highlanders and nobody else.

That is not an argument for a new field. A `state church` value would need a rule
applicable to sixty polities across Buddhism, Zoroastrianism, four Christianities
and every branch of Islam, and this site's coding rules are explicit that a
vocabulary which expands to fit each awkward case ends up describing nothing.
It is an argument for noticing which pages the chapters are doing the most work
on, and this is one of them.
