The fifteenth century finished it, and not by invasion. The Timurid campaigns
and then the Turkmen ones had ruined the countryside and destroyed the crown's
revenue; the great noble houses had recovered the autonomy David IV had taken
from them, because a crown that cannot pay a standing army has to ask them for
one; and Constantinople fell in 1453, which removed the Christian empire Georgia
had oriented itself towards for six centuries and left it surrounded.

The last kings of a united Georgia spent their reigns fighting their own
princes. In 1490 a royal council formally acknowledged what had already
happened and divided the kingdom among three branches of the Bagrationi:
Kartli in the centre, Kakheti in the east, Imereti in the west, with several
princedoms effectively independent besides.

This record types that `fragmentation` with an empty `by`, which is exactly
right: nobody conquered anything, and the successors were the same family.

What followed is outside this span and belongs in a paragraph. The three
kingdoms spent the next three centuries between the Ottomans and the Safavids,
who partitioned the country between them by treaty in 1555 and again in 1639 —
Imereti to Istanbul, Kartli and Kakheti to Isfahan. Georgian kings converted to
Islam to keep their thrones and reconverted when they could; Georgian slaves and
soldiers turn up throughout this corpus, on the Safavid page as the ghulam corps
Abbas I built to break the Qizilbash and on the Mamluk page as one of the
frontiers Cairo bought from. Kartli and Kakheti were united in 1762 under
Erekle II, who signed a treaty of protection with Russia in 1783, was sacked by
Agha Muhammad Khan in 1795 — which this site's Qajar record describes from the
other side — and whose kingdom Russia annexed outright in 1801.

The Bagrationi held a throne somewhere in Georgia from 888 to 1801, which is
nine hundred and thirteen years and would be the longest dynastic tenure in this
corpus if the span model could express it. It cannot, because what persisted was
a family across four polities and this record covers one of them — the same
limitation the Zaydi and Omani records state about an office, arriving here about
a house.
