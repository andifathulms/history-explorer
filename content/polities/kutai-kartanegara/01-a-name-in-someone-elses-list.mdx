Kutai Kartanegara began in 1300 at Jaitan Layar, a settlement near the
mouth of the Mahakam river later known as Kutai Lama, founded by a
ruler remembered as Aji Batara Agung Dewa Sakti. What outside evidence
survives from this earliest period does not come from the kingdom's
own records at all. A Javanese court poem composed in 1365, the
Nagarakretagama, lists the territories the Majapahit general Gajah
Mada had brought under his own empire's influence, and among them
appears a name - Tanjung Kute - now identified with this same kingdom.

## A reputation built on travel

The founder's own reputation, according to the kingdom's later
tradition, spread through contact rather than conquest: frequent
visits to the Majapahit court made Kutai known to outsiders in a way
its own isolated position on the Mahakam would not otherwise have
allowed. A small river kingdom on the coast of Borneo became visible
to the wider archipelago precisely because its own founder kept
appearing at the court of the region's dominant power.

## A kingdom named by scholars after the fact

The name Kutai itself carries a complication worth noting early: it
was not necessarily the kingdom's own name for itself, and modern
scholarship has questioned whether it originally applied to Aji Batara
Agung Dewa Sakti's own line at all, or was borrowed by later
researchers from the region where much older inscriptions - belonging
to an entirely separate, far more ancient kingdom - happened to be
found. That separate kingdom's own fate would become central to this
one's story two and a half centuries later.
