Kutai Kartanegara's later seventeenth-century succession ran smoothly
enough by the standards of the age: Pangeran Dipati Agung governed
from 1635 to 1650 in the years right after the conquest of Martadipura,
succeeded by Dipati Maja Kusuma for thirty-six years, and then, in an
arrangement unusual for the period, by a woman - Aji Ragi, who ruled
under the title Ratu Agung from 1686 to 1700 before her own brother
succeeded her.

## A capital abandoned under pressure

What forced real change was not a succession dispute but an outside
threat that had nothing to do with dynastic politics. Raiders known as
the Lanun Solok targeted the kingdom's own wealth and its women in
repeated attacks on Kutai Lama, the coastal capital the dynasty had
held since its founding four centuries earlier. The losses were severe
enough, year after year, that around 1700 the reigning ruler moved the
kingdom's own seat away from the coast entirely, relocating upriver to
Pemarang in the Jembayan district - a retreat from piracy rather than
from any rival kingdom's army.

## A title that changed one more time

The move brought a measure of peace, and the dynasty's own succession
continued afterward without further crisis: Pangeran Anum Panji
Mendapa governed from 1730 until, aging, he arranged for his own
son-in-law, Aji Muhammad Idris, to take the throne in 1732 - the first
ruler of this line to be formally styled Sultan rather than by any of
the earlier titles the dynasty had used. A kingdom that had begun as
an obscure river settlement had, by that point, absorbed an ancient
rival's own name and prestige, survived a pirate crisis that cost it
its original capital, and settled into a new royal title altogether.
