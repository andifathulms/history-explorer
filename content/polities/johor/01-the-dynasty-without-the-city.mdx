This corpus's Melaka record has carried a single successor claim since it was
written, and the item was a name rather than an id, because the polity it named
had no page: *Sultanate of Johor*. The comment beside it ended by observing that
coding rule 1 does not require a claimant to have a record on this site.

It does now, so the item is an id and the edge `melaka → johor` is drawn.

Alauddin Riayat Shah II was the second son of Mahmud Shah, the sultan who lost
Melaka to Albuquerque in 1511. His father had withdrawn up the peninsula and died
on Bintan; in 1528 Alauddin established himself on the Johor river with the
Melakan regalia, the Melakan court offices — bendahara, laksamana, temenggung,
in the same order — and the claim that this was the same sultanate in a different
place.

And with a chronicle. The *Sejarah Melayu*, the *Malay Annals*, which this
corpus's Melaka record's first and last chapters are drafted from, was composed
here, at this court, a century after the city fell, to make exactly that
argument. It is the successor's account of the thing it says it is continuing,
and this site's source note on it compares it to the Timbuktu chronicles on the
Songhai record: elegy written by the survivors.

## What `descent` is doing on this record

The `legitimation` field here carries `descent` and nothing else, which is
unusual — most records on this site pair it with something.

There is nothing to pair it with. Johor had no caliphal grant, no Ming seal, no
Ottoman guns, no distinctive religious authority of the kind this corpus's Aceh
record built out of the ulama and the pilgrimage. What it had was a line, and
the line was the argument. The whole seventeenth-century politics of the strait —
who could command the orang laut, who could summon the Malay lords, whose
daughter was worth marrying — ran on where a claimant stood relative to Melaka's
dynasty.

Which is why the last chapter of this record is what it is.
