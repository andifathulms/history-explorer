This record's `succession_rule` reads `nomination`, and it is the most confident
succession coding of the five records in this region. The other four are all
null.

The Tran practice was this: the reigning emperor abdicated in favour of his
designated heir while still alive and in good health, took the title of senior
emperor — *thượng hoàng* — and governed alongside the man he had just installed.
The heir was on the throne, publicly, with the previous holder standing next to
him, years before there was anything to fight over.

It is the answer to a problem this site's records run into constantly. Look at
what the corpus's null succession fields are usually null *for*: the Singhasari
record, whose chronicle is four generations of revenge killings; Ayutthaya, which
changed dynasty five times in four hundred years with most transitions violent;
Aceh, where the merchants installed four queens in a row to keep the throne
weak; Angkor, where the throne was repeatedly taken by force. A designated heir
is normal. A designated heir who is already reigning, with the designator alive
to enforce it, is not.

## Why it is codeable

Coding rule 7 asks for the rule rather than the outcome, and a hundred and nine
of this site's hundred and ninety-five records are null in this field — most of
them because what the sources describe is a pattern of results rather than a
stated principle.

The test this record passes is that the practice was deliberately adopted at the
start of the dynasty, applied across most of it as policy rather than as
circumstance, and then abandoned — after which the throne was usurped inside two
reigns and the dynasty ended. The rule's presence and its absence both produced
what the rule predicts, which is about as much as a coded institution on this
site is ever able to show.

`nomination` is not a perfect name for it. The value in this vocabulary means
the incumbent designates the successor, which is the substance here; what it
does not capture is the abdication, the co-reign, and the fact that the
designation was executed rather than merely announced. This site's vocabulary
has been extended twice on corpus-wide audits — `investiture` and `matrilineal` —
and neither audit was run off a single record. This is a single record. The
value stays as it is and the comment carries the difference.
