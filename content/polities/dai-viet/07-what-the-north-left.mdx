The Ly and the Tran established the thing that Vietnamese history afterwards is
about: a state that ran on Chinese institutions and defined itself by not being
China.

## The vocabulary

Everything formal in the Vietnamese state was Chinese in origin and Vietnamese
in use.

The examinations, on the Chinese classics, in classical Chinese, run from 1075
and continue — with interruptions — until 1919, which makes Vietnam the last
country on earth to abolish the Confucian civil service examination, fourteen
years after China did.

The law codes are modelled on Tang and Ming originals. The six ministries, the
censorate, the ranks, the court dress, the calendar, the dynastic histories
written in the Chinese manner about the dynasty just ended — all of it.

And the writing. Literary Chinese was the language of administration and
scholarship for nine hundred years. Alongside it, from the Tran period, *chu
nom*: Chinese characters used and combined to write Vietnamese, a script of some
tens of thousands of graphs that only a person already literate in Chinese could
read, and the vehicle for the vernacular poetry that Vietnamese readers actually
care about.

## And the argument

The point of all that machinery was to be a separate country using it.

Vietnamese rulers took the title *hoang de* — emperor, the same word as the
Chinese one — at home, and sent tribute to the Chinese court as *vuong*, king,
abroad. Two titles, two audiences, one man. It is the most precise institutional
statement of the relationship anybody devised, and it lasted until the French.

The Mongol wars are the other half. Three invasions in thirty years, three
defeats, and Tran Hung Dao's proclamation to his officers before the second — a
text every Vietnamese schoolchild still learns — is an argument that they should
prefer death to serving the invaders. The tactics were withdrawal, scorched
earth, and the stakes driven into the bed of the Bach Dang river in 1288, which
impaled the Mongol fleet on a falling tide.

That last was a deliberate repetition. The same trick had been used in the same
river against the Chinese in 938 and again in 981. Doing it a third time, three
hundred years later, against a different enemy, is a state telling itself a
story about its own continuity.

## What came next

The twenty-year Ming occupation from 1407 was the most serious attempt ever made
to absorb Vietnam back into China, and it was systematic: the administration
reorganised as a Chinese province, the examinations imposed, and — according to
the Vietnamese accounts — books collected and sent north or destroyed.

Le Loi's ten-year insurgency drove them out by 1427. The proclamation of victory
written for him by Nguyen Trai opens by stating that Dai Viet has its own
civilisation, its own mountains and rivers, and its own customs, distinct from
the north's — which is the whole argument of the preceding four centuries in
one sentence, made necessary by the fact that the institutions being defended
had all been imported.

## The march south

The other inheritance is the direction.

The Ly and Tran had taken Cham provinces piece by piece for three hundred years.
The Le finished the job in 1471. The Nguyen carried it into the Mekong delta over
the following two centuries, taking Khmer territory the way the earlier dynasties
had taken Cham.

Vietnam is the shape it is — a thousand-mile ribbon down the coast, with a delta
at each end — because of a process that began under the Ly and the Tran and did
not stop until there was nothing left to move into.
