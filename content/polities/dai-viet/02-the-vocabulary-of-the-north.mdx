The Red River delta had been administered from China, on and off, for something
over a thousand years, ending in the tenth century. What it built afterwards was
a state that described itself in the northern empire's own imperial vocabulary.

The ruler was an emperor at home, in the same characters the court in the north
used for itself, with the same mandate, the same ritual, the same calendar, and
his own claim to be the centre of a civilised world with barbarians around its
edges — among whom he counted the Chams and the Khmer. The bureaucracy sat
examinations on the Confucian classics. The records were kept in literary
Chinese.

<Figure id="dv-literature2" />

And abroad, to the northern court, the same ruler was the King of Annam, holding
that title by investiture from the emperor, sending tribute missions on schedule.

## A rank accepted abroad and contradicted at home

The discomfort in this arrangement was real and the Ly and Tran courts lived
with it deliberately rather than by oversight. Melaka's own investiture from
the Ming, decades later, cost that sultanate nothing to accept — a new state
with everything to gain from the recognition and no rival claim of its own to
protect. Dai Viet's rulers had no such easy bargain. They called themselves
emperor at home, in the same register the northern court used for itself, over
a state that had fought its way out of that same empire's direct rule only a
century earlier — and they went on accepting investiture as King of Annam
continuously for three centuries, including in years when the two courts were
openly at war. The contradiction was not resolved. It was managed, deliberately
and for generations, as the ordinary way of dealing with a much larger
neighbour a Vietnamese army could beat in the field but not permanently ignore.

## Chu Nom

The Vietnamese language also got its own writing system in this period: chữ
Nôm, assembled out of Chinese characters, some borrowed for their sound, some
for their sense, and many invented by combining two characters into one. It let
Vietnamese be written down for the first time on its own terms rather than in
literary Chinese, and later poets and chroniclers used it for work meant to
be read as Vietnamese rather than translated into it.
