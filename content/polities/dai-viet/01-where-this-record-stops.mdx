---
title: Where this record stops
drafted_from: taylor-2013
phase: formation
---

Ly Thanh Tong proclaimed the name Dai Viet in 1054. It was still the state's
name in 1804, when Gia Long changed it.

This record does not run for those seven hundred and fifty years. It covers the
Ly and the Tran, 1054 to 1400, and stops there.

The reason is what a polity is on this site. Between 1400 and 1804 the Red River
delta had a usurping dynasty, a twenty-year Chinese occupation, the Le, a
century of Mac contest, a two-century partition between two families holding
opposite ends of the country while both nominally served a Le emperor with no
power, and finally the Nguyen. Putting all of that inside one span because the
name was continuous would produce a record about a country, and every field on
it — the succession rule, the revenue base, the ending — would have to be a
weighted average of things that contradict each other.

The Ly and the Tran are one order. A capital at Thang Long, a Buddhist court, an
examination system growing alongside an aristocracy, a defined relationship with
the empire to the north, and an expansion southward against Champa: it starts,
it runs three and a half centuries, and it is ended by a usurpation with a date.

That is the object this record carries, and the rest of the name's history is on
the list of what this site has not written. So is the Le dynasty specifically —
which is not merely an omission but a hole with a shape, because the Le are the
polity that ended Champa in 1471, and that record's `ended.by` is empty as a
result.
