The last attempt at it was John IV's, in the 1450s, and it is the strangest
alliance any of them attempted. He married his daughter Theodora to Uzun Hasan, the
ruler of the Akkoyunlu — the Turkmen confederation of eastern Anatolia and
western Iran — in the hope that the Akkoyunlu army would come west if the
Ottomans came east. Then Constantinople fell in 1453, and Trebizond became the
last state anywhere still ruled by a Roman emperor.

It lasted eight more years. In the summer of 1461 Mehmed II brought an army
overland and a fleet along the coast, the city held out for about a month, and
David surrendered on 15 August. He and most of his family were executed in
Constantinople two years later. Uzun Hasan did not come.

The Roman empire is conventionally dated to 1453, which is where the institution
that mattered stopped, and that is the right date. The literal end is here: on a
strip of coast at the other end of Anatolia, eight years later, under a dynasty
that had given up calling itself Roman a hundred and eighty years before it
stopped existing, in a city that surrendered after a month because nobody came.

Fifteen hundred and eight years after Augustus, the last man anywhere entitled
to be addressed as Roman emperor handed over his keys in August, was taken to
Constantinople, and was executed there two years afterwards with most of his
sons.
