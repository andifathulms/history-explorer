title: What left before the city fell

Basilios Bessarion was born at Trebizond around 1403, when the empire had about
sixty years left. He became a monk, then a bishop, went to the Council of
Ferrara-Florence in 1438 as part of the Byzantine delegation seeking union with
Rome in exchange for military help against the Ottomans, argued for the union,
and stayed in Italy when it collapsed. He was made a cardinal, was twice a
serious candidate for pope, and spent thirty years doing one thing.

He collected Greek manuscripts. He bought them, had them copied, and pursued
them across Greece and Italy, on the explicit reasoning — which he set out
himself — that the Greek-speaking world was being conquered and its books would
go with it if somebody did not move them. In 1468, fifteen years after
Constantinople fell and seven after Trebizond did, he gave the collection to
Venice. About a thousand codices, some six
hundred of them Greek, the largest such collection assembled by one person. It
is the founding core of the Biblioteca Marciana and it is still there.

Two things are worth drawing out.

The first is that it worked. Medieval libraries mostly end one way: Alamut
burned, the Fatimid palace collection was sold off and scattered during the
famine, the Ghurid records went at Firuzkuh, the Samanid library that Ibn Sina
had used burned. The usual story is of somebody realising afterwards what had
been in the building. Bessarion saw it coming and moved the material while there
was still time to move it, which is rare enough to be worth saying twice.

The second is where he was from. A man born in a Greek city on the Black Sea,
in an empire that had given up calling itself Roman a hundred and twenty years
before he was born and would be extinguished eight years after Constantinople,
moved the surviving literature of that civilisation to Venice, where it was
printed. A substantial part of what Europe learned to call the classics passed
through the hands of a Trapezuntine.

Trebizond is usually a curiosity: a strip of coast, an improbably long survival,
a footnote to 1453. This is the thing that came out of it, and it is sitting in
Venice.
