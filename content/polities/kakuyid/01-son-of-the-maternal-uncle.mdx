title: Son of the maternal uncle

The dynasty is named for a relationship through a woman, which is rare enough
in this corpus to be the first thing worth saying about it.

*Kakuya* is Daylami for the son of a maternal uncle. Rustam Dushmanziyar was
uncle to Sayyida Shirin, the Buyid regent who governed the Jibal from Rayy for
her son Majd al-Dawla for more than thirty years and is one of the few women
this period's political narrative cannot avoid. His son Muhammad received
Isfahan from her in about 1008 on the strength of that connection, took the
title Ala al-Dawla in the Buyid manner, and then did what governors in this
region do: he stopped being one.

By the 1020s he held Isfahan and Hamadan in his own right, minted, and was
negotiating with the Buyids as a power rather than as a relative. This site's
Iranian Intermezzo pages have now recorded the same movement from a Tahirid
governorship, a Samanid guard command, a Saffarid ayyar band, a Ghaznavid slave
captaincy, a Seljuk pasture grant and a Khwarazmian washing-bowl appointment.
The Kakuyid version is the smallest and the most domestic: a nephew given a
city by his cousin's mother.

The position was never strong. Isfahan sat between the Buyids of Fars, the
Buyids of Iraq, and — from the 1020s — Mahmud of Ghazna, who took Rayy in 1029
and ended Buyid rule in the Jibal, and whose son Mas'ud took Isfahan itself for
a period in the 1030s. Ala al-Dawla lost the city more than once and got it
back each time, which is the actual skill the sources credit him with: an
ability to survive being conquered.

What he did with the intervals is the next chapter.
