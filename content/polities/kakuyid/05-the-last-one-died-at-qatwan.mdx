Isfahan fell in 1051 and became the Seljuk capital. Nizam al-Mulk would
administer an empire from the city the Kakuyids had lost.

The family was not destroyed. It was moved sideways. Yazd and Abarkuh were
granted to them as fiefs, and they held those for another ninety years as
Seljuk governors, married into the Seljuk house, and built — mosques, canals,
fortifications, and at Yazd a set of tomb towers of the kind Qabus had built at
Gurgan. So it is not a conquest with a date on it: the polity stopped being
independent in 1051 and the family stopped mattering in 1141, and there is no
single date on which it was destroyed.

Garshasp II, the last of them, was killed in 1141 at the battle of the Qatwan
steppe.

That is the battle of Qatwan: Sanjar's army
destroyed by a Buddhist steppe empire outside Samarkand, Transoxiana passing
under non-Muslim suzerainty, and news of it travelling west to become the
Prester John expectation. A Daylamite prince from Isfahan, holding Yazd from a
Turkish sultan, died in Transoxiana fighting Khitans from north China, and his
dynasty ended there.

So the dynasty has two ending dates ninety years apart, and they answer
different questions. The
Kakuyid state — the thing that held Isfahan, defied the Buyids, survived
Mahmud, and paid Ibn Sina — ended in 1051. The Kakuyid family ended in 1141, on
somebody else's battlefield, in somebody else's war.
