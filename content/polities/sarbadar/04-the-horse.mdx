title: The saddled horse

The Sarbadars were Shi'i, at least in the dervish half, and the form their
Shi'ism took is the most striking thing recorded about them. They are said to
have kept a horse saddled and bridled at Sabzavar, brought out at appointed
times, for the Twelfth Imam to ride when he returned — which was expected not as
a doctrine about the end of time but as an event that might occur that year.

The reports come from writers who did not approve, and this site prints the
practice as a report rather than as a fact about the state's liturgy. What is
better established is that the coins carry Shi'i formulae, that the dervish
faction's authority was the hidden imam's rather than any caliph's, and that
this is why the record's `legitimation` field reads `divine-sanction` — the
vocabulary's value for a right to rule that comes from above rather than from a
predecessor, a lineage or an army.

The other episode belongs to the last ruler and points forwards. Ali Mu'ayyad,
by then trying to hold a Shi'i state together against a Sunni majority
population and his own factions, wrote to Muhammad ibn Makki al-Amili — the
Twelver jurist from Jabal Amil in what is now southern Lebanon, known to the
tradition as al-Shahid al-Awwal, the First Martyr — and asked him to come to
Khurasan and give the state a body of law. He did not come. He wrote a book
instead, *al-Lum'a al-Dimashqiyya*, the Damascene Glitter, which became one of
the standard texts of Twelver jurisprudence and is still taught.

Read that against this site's Safavid page. In 1501 a dynasty declared Twelver
Shi'ism the religion of Iran and discovered it had no clergy to enforce it with,
and imported jurists from Jabal Amil to build one. A hundred and twenty years
earlier a small state in Khurasan had run into the same problem on a smaller
scale and written to the same place. The Safavid programme was unprecedented in
scale and was not, on this evidence, unprecedented in kind.

Al-Shahid al-Awwal was executed in Damascus in 1384, on charges arising from his
Shi'ism, three years after the Sarbadar state had ended.
