---
title: A fugitive founds a city
drafted_from: abun-nasr-1987
phase: formation
---

Idris ibn Abd Allah was a great-great-grandson of Ali who survived the massacre
of an Alid rising at Fakhkh near Mecca in 786 and fled west, as far west as it
was possible to go. Berber tribes of the Awraba around Volubilis accepted him as
imam in 788, which gave them a leader with unimpeachable descent and no local
ties, and gave him a following.

He was poisoned within three years, on Abbasid initiative according to the
sources. His son Idris II founded Fez, which drew Arab settlers from Ifriqiya and
Andalusi refugees from Cordoba and became the first substantial Islamic city of
the far Maghreb.

The Idrisids matter less for what they ruled — the dynasty fragmented among
Idris II's many sons almost immediately — than for what they demonstrated. Within
forty years of the Abbasid revolution, a descendant of Ali could establish an
independent state on the edge of the Islamic world and be accepted as its
legitimate ruler on grounds the caliphate could not contest without conceding
the argument. The far Maghreb never returned to Abbasid authority.
