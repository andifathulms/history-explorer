The last Idrisid principality was removed in 974, and by then there had not been
an Idrisid state for a hundred and forty years.

What ended in 974 was the last of the fragments — a family holding a town or a
stretch of the Rif, taken by whichever Berber client of whichever caliphate was
being paid that year. There is no battle worth the name, no last ruler anybody
remembers, and no account written by anybody on the Idrisid side.

## An ending by other people's war

The Idrisids were not conquered. They were used up.

For most of the tenth century the far Maghreb was the ground on which the
Fatimids in Ifriqiya and the Umayyads in Córdoba fought a war neither of them
could win, because neither could occupy Morocco and both could afford to pay
Berbers to. Miknasa, Zanata and Sanhaja chiefs took subsidies, changed sides when
the subsidies changed, and fought each other with two caliphates' money.

The Idrisid principalities were not participants in that war so much as terrain.
They were taken and restored and taken again as the payments shifted; a family
with an impeccable pedigree and no army is a useful thing to install and a cheap
thing to remove.

By 974 there was nothing left to install.

## What was actually lost

Not much, which is the honest assessment, and it is worth separating from what
survived.

There was no Idrisid administration to dismantle, no fiscal system, no army, no
body of law issued in the dynasty's name, and no territory it had held
continuously for a century. Politically the dynasty was a hundred and seventy
years of ambition mostly unrealised, undone by a single division among sons and
finished off by other people's proxies.

What survived was the city and the claim, and neither of them needed the dynasty
to be in power. Fez went on and got larger. Sharifian descent went on being the
most valuable political credential in Morocco, and went on being so for the next
thousand years, in the hands of families who were not Idrisids and did not need
to be.
