title: Descent, used as a claim
drafted_from: abun-nasr-1987

The Idrisids are the first polity in this corpus to make Alid descent the working
basis of a state, and they demonstrate what the site's coding rules are trying to
separate.

Being descended from Ali is a genealogical fact. It is not, under the rules here,
a successor claim — descent counts only when it is publicly invoked as the basis
of a right to rule and a source records the invocation. The Idrisids invoked it
constantly: it was the entire argument for why Berber tribes with no prior
attachment to the family should accept an outsider as imam, and it is why the
Abbasids regarded a fugitive in Morocco as worth poisoning.

The dynasty itself fragmented almost immediately. Idris II divided his territory
among his sons and the Idrisid lands became a set of small principalities,
absorbed piecemeal in the tenth century as the Fatimids and the Cordoban Umayyads
fought their proxy war across the Maghreb — which is why this site records the
ending as `gradual absorption` with two agents rather than a conquest.

The claim outlived the state. Sharifian descent remained the strongest
legitimating credential in Moroccan politics for a thousand years afterwards, and
the dynasties that later ruled from Fez and Marrakesh asserted it. That is a real
influence and this site does not count it, because those claims fall outside the
period and the rules exclude modern and near-modern invocations. The count is 0
where the history is not.
