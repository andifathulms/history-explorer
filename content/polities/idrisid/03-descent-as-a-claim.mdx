The Idrisids were the first dynasty anywhere to make descent from Ali the working
basis of a state, and the distinction between having such a descent and using it
is the whole of what they were.

Being descended from Ali is a genealogical fact and by itself confers nothing. It
becomes a political instrument only when somebody invokes it publicly as the
ground of a right to rule and people accept the invocation. The Idrisids invoked
it constantly: it was the entire argument for why Berber tribes with no prior
attachment to the family should accept an outsider as imam, and it is why the
Abbasids regarded a fugitive in Morocco as worth poisoning.

<Figure id="dirham" />

The dynasty itself fragmented almost immediately. Idris II divided his territory
among his sons and the Idrisid lands became a set of small principalities,
absorbed piecemeal in the tenth century as the Fatimids and the Cordoban Umayyads
fought their proxy war across the Maghreb.

The claim outlived the state by a thousand years.
