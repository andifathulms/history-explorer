Llywelyn ap Gruffudd had spent years trying to build, in Wales, a
position comparable to what the king of Scots held in the north - a
native ruler acknowledged as overlord of his own country rather than
simply one baron among many. Edward I would not accept the
arrangement, and a war that began in 1282 ended that December when
Llywelyn, hurrying to rejoin his own scattered forces, was killed by a
Shropshire veteran who did not recognize him until papers on the body
revealed his rank.

## A war that outlived its prince

Llywelyn's death simplified the war without ending it. His brother
David tried to hold out as Prince of Wales from the mountains of
Snowdonia, retreating from stronghold to stronghold as Edward's
forces, reinforced by hired chivalry from Gascony and Ponthieu, tracked
him down. Betrayed in June 1283 by men of his own tongue, David was
tried at Shrewsbury that autumn and executed as a traitor - the last
native Prince of Wales to hold the title before the English crown
claimed it for itself.

## Castles, shires, and a birth at Caernarfon

Edward spent the following months settling the government of his
conquest rather than simply garrisoning it. Fortresses ringed
Snowdonia, each with a new town attached, English traders and settlers
drawn in by privilege, and the castle's own constable serving as the
town's mayor by design. The Statute of Wales, issued at Rhuddlan in
1284, extended English shire government formally over the conquered
land, organizing Gwynedd into the counties of Anglesey, Carnarvon and
Merioneth. At Caernarfon itself, the new capital of Gwynedd, a son was
born to Edward that April - a boy who, seventeen years later, would
become the first English Prince of Wales, the title itself now a
permanent fixture of the English crown rather than a native one it had
just extinguished.
