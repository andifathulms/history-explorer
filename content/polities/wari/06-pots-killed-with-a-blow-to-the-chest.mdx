---
title: Pots killed with a blow to the chest
drafted_from: tung-2012
phase: peak
---

Wari's own expansion carried a distinctive religious practice with it
wherever its influence reached: the ritual offering of large quantities
of finely made, brightly decorated ceramics. A pit would be dug, and
elaborate polychrome pots - some depicting supernatural beings or human
figures - were deliberately smashed and thrown in, the blow aimed first
at the head or chest of whatever figure the vessel portrayed, as though
the pot itself were the thing being killed rather than merely broken.

## Offerings that travelled with sacrifice

At Conchopata, these ceramic-killing deposits appear alongside sacrificed
camelids and human trophy heads, tying the practice directly to the
same violent expansion visible elsewhere in the archaeological record.
Similar offering deposits turn up at Nawinpukio near the capital and, far
from the Wari heartland, on the south coast at Maymi in the Pisco Valley
and Pacheco in the Nasca Valley - concrete evidence that this specific
religious practice, and not merely generic Wari-style objects, had
spread well beyond the Ayacucho basin.

## A destroyed site that still changed a valley

Pacheco's own offering deposit was investigated in the 1920s, before the
surrounding site was destroyed by modern agriculture, but even that
partial record shows the deposit's impact: Nasca settlement patterns in
the area were completely disrupted around the time Pacheco was
established, and new burial customs appear alongside it - evidence,
however read, of intense interaction between Wari and the existing
Nasca population, and quite possibly of Wari authority being
established there directly rather than merely traded for.
