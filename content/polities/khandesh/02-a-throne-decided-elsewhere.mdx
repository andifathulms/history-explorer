Malik Ahmad died on 29 April 1399, and his small state did what small
states wedged between larger ones often do: it split. His two sons
divided the territory between them, Nasir taking the east — where he
founded Burhanpur in 1400 and captured the hill fortress of Asir from a
Hindu chieftain — and Hasan the west, ruling from their father's old seat
at Thalner. In 1417 Nasir ended the arrangement by force, capturing
Thalner and imprisoning his own brother before Hasan's appeal to Gujarat
for help could be answered; Hasan's descendants found permanent refuge at
the Gujarati court instead, intermarrying for generations with the royal
house that had failed to save him in time.

## Consolidation under Jharkhandi Sultan

The kingdom's real high point came later, under Adil Khan II, who took
the throne in 1457 and ruled for over four decades. He consolidated
Khandesh's hold over its own core territory, suppressed the Koli and
Bhil raiders who had made travel through the region dangerous, and
extended his reach as far as Jharkhand — modern Chota Nagpur — earning
himself the epithet Jharkhandi Sultan for a campaign well beyond
Khandesh's ordinary borders. He was proud enough of the achievement to
briefly stop paying the tribute Khandesh had owed Gujarat since an
earlier invasion, until a show of force from Mahmud Begada in 1499 or
1500 reminded him where the actual balance of power lay.

## A succession no one inside Khandesh could settle

Adil Khan II died without an heir in September 1501, and what followed
exposed exactly how little independence his own consolidation had really
won. Two rival branches of the Faruqi family, one backed by Gujarat and
the other by Ahmadnagar, spent seven years fighting for the throne
through proxies and invading armies rather than through any process
Khandesh's own institutions could resolve. It ended only when Mahmud
Begada marched in person to install his own grandson on the throne as
Adil Khan III in 1508 — a kingdom whose peak of internal strength had
been real, but whose eventual succession was decided, in the end, by
which neighbour's army arrived at Thalner first.
