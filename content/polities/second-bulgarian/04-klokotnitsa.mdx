In March 1230, at Klokotnitsa on the Maritsa, Ivan Asen II destroyed the army of
Epirus and captured Theodore Komnenos Doukas, who had crowned himself emperor at
Thessalonica and was, at that moment, the man most likely to retake
Constantinople.

Theodore was blinded, reportedly for conspiring afterwards. Bulgaria absorbed
Macedonia, Albania and most of Thrace, and for about a decade held everything
between the Danube and the Aegean except the capital and the Peloponnese.

## Who it decided the Roman empire for

Three Greek governments claimed to be the Roman empire after 1204: Nicaea in
Anatolia, Epirus in the western Balkans, and Trebizond on the Black Sea. In the
1220s Epirus was the strongest of them. Theodore had taken Thessalonica from the
Latins in 1224, had himself crowned emperor in it, and was moving towards
Constantinople.

Klokotnitsa removed Epirus from that competition in an afternoon. Nicaea, the
weaker claimant, took Constantinople thirty-one years later against no serious
Greek rival — and it was in a position to because a Bulgarian tsar had destroyed
the serious Greek rival for reasons of his own that had nothing to do with
Nicaea.

The restoration of the Byzantine empire in 1261 was made possible, indirectly and
unintentionally, by a Balkan ruler whose lifelong policy was to prevent one.

## What Ivan Asen II did with it

He styled himself *emperor of the Bulgarians and the Greeks*, which is exactly
the claim Simeon had made three hundred years earlier and had died without
making good. He had the victory carved on a column in the church
of the Forty Martyrs at Tarnovo, where the inscription still is, and it says in
so many words that all the land from Adrianople to Durazzo was his and that the
Franks held only Constantinople.

He minted. He married a daughter to the Latin emperor's heir and was nearly made
regent of the Latin empire, which would have put a Bulgarian tsar in charge of the
crusader state his father had beaten. He negotiated with Nicaea, restored the
Bulgarian patriarchate on Orthodox terms in 1235 with Nicaean and eastern
recognition, and besieged Constantinople in alliance with Nicaea and then broke
off the alliance because he preferred a weak Latin emperor to a strong Greek one.

That last decision is the interesting one and it looks like the right call. A
restored Byzantine empire was the only power that could threaten Bulgaria's
position, and Ivan Asen spent his last years preventing one.

## One reign

He died in 1241. The Mongols raided the Balkans the following year, Bulgaria
began paying tribute to the Golden Horde, his son was a child, and the state
never held Macedonia again.

The whole of it — the Aegean coast, the Albanian ports, the empire of the
Bulgarians and the Greeks — lasted about eleven years and depended entirely on
one man being alive. What survives of it is a column in a church at Tarnovo with
the boundaries cut into the stone, listing land the state held for a decade and
never saw again.
