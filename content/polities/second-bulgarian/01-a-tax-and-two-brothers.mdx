In 1185 the emperor Isaac II Angelos levied a special tax across the Balkans to
pay for his wedding. Two brothers from the Tarnovo region, Theodore and Asen,
went to him to ask for a grant of land and were refused; Asen was slapped in the
face. They went home and raised a revolt.

It failed. They crossed the Danube, came back with Cuman cavalry, and by 1189
had a state with a capital, a crown and a patriarch.

## What they said they were restoring

Not a new kingdom. The brothers took the title *tsar*, were crowned at Tarnovo,
and had the relics of the First Bulgarian Empire's saints — Ivan of Rila among
them — moved to the new capital, which is the medieval way of saying that this
is the same country.

The claim was not that a new dynasty had succeeded an old one. It was that the
country had resumed. And there is no date on which that happened: 1185 is a
revolt over a tax, not a restoration ceremony, and the men who started it were
asking for an estate rather than for an empire.

Whether the claim was true is a fair question and it is not one the sources can
settle. The Asens were probably not descended from the First Empire's dynasty and
almost certainly said they were. What is certain is that after a hundred and
sixty-seven years of Byzantine administration there was a Bulgarian ruler with a
Bulgarian title in a Bulgarian capital, with the old empire's saints in his
cathedral — and that everybody involved, including Constantinople, understood
what was being asserted.

## The Cumans

The brothers' decisive asset was hired cavalry from across the Danube.

The Cumans were a Turkic steppe people holding the plain north of the Black Sea,
and for the first thirty years of the Second Empire they are the arm that wins
its battles — horse archers who could beat Byzantine and, shortly, Frankish
heavy cavalry by feigning flight and turning. They were paid, they married into
the Asen family, and they eventually settled inside the state, which is roughly
what the Bulgars had done to the Slavs five centuries earlier and is a fair
description of how states in this region got made.

Beside them the revolt inherited an instrument from the government it had
displaced: the *pronoia*, a grant of the revenue of an estate in return for
service. Byzantium had developed it, the Greek government in exile at Nicaea was
arriving at the same thing independently in the same decades, and Bulgaria simply
kept using what the imperial administration had left in place.

## What it took off Byzantium

The Balkans north of the Balkan range, permanently, twenty years before the
Fourth Crusade took Constantinople.

The empire's collapse is usually dated to 1204, when the crusaders took the city,
or to the civil wars of the 1340s. This went before either of them, and it went
for the price of a wedding tax and a slap.
