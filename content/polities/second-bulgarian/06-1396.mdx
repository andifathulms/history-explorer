In 1330 the Serbs destroyed a Bulgarian army at Velbazhd and the tsar was killed
in the field. Serbia under Stefan Dušan became the dominant Balkan power for the
following thirty years, and this corpus's Byzantine record has him taking
Macedonia, Thessaly and Epirus while the empire fought its own civil wars.

Bulgaria spent those decades as a junior partner, and then did the one thing that
made the ending certain: Ivan Alexander divided the state between two sons, one
at Tarnovo and one at Vidin, and they did not cooperate.

## The Ottomans

This corpus's Ottoman record's second chapter is called the Balkans first, and
this is what it walked into.

The Ottomans crossed into Europe permanently in 1354 — ferried, as the Byzantine
record's ninth chapter says, by one side in a Byzantine civil war. Sofia fell in
1382, Tarnovo in 1393 after a three-month siege, and Vidin in 1396 after the
crusade assembled to save it was destroyed at Nicopolis.

The Bulgarian patriarchate was abolished and the church put under Constantinople,
which had lost the argument about it five centuries earlier and now won it under
Ottoman administration. There was no Bulgarian state again until 1878.

## What the two records add up to

This corpus now carries Bulgaria twice, in a region that has two polities, no
internal edge and `thread: false`.

That is the honest shape and the workflow rule required it: a region enters with
the flag off and it is flipped only when real sourced edges join two of its
members. The two Bulgarian records are joined by `resumes`, which is not an edge
and is not drawn. Both of them point outward — the First at Byzantium in 1018,
this one at the Ottomans in 1396 — and both of those edges cross out of the
region.

A region with no thread is not an incomplete region. Hard rule 7 says so and this
corpus has spent a good deal of effort making sure the site renders it as one
plain sentence rather than as a gap. What the two records here have instead of a
thread is a field that says they are the same country, which is a stronger claim
than any edge would have been.

## What the fifth chapter adds

One more thing left Tarnovo before the Ottomans took it, and it went in the same
direction the alphabet had gone four hundred years earlier. Patriarch Euthymius's
school revised the Slavonic texts against the Greek and standardised the
orthography; when the city fell its members went to Serbia, Wallachia and Russia,
and two of them became metropolitans of Kiev. The historiography calls it the
second South Slavic influence, and the first is on the other Bulgarian record.

Both times the export happened as the state was being destroyed.

## What is missing

Serbia, which is named on three records in this corpus and beat this one at
Velbazhd and was the Balkan power for a generation. And the Rus, named on two,
including this site's Khazar record, which says in its own text that Rus is not
on the site and that the record therefore names it in prose rather than in a
field that would not resolve.

Both belong in this region and neither is here yet. When Serbia arrives there
will be an internal edge — `second-bulgarian -> serbia` is not one, since Serbia
did not succeed Bulgaria, but the Serbian and Bulgarian records both end against
the Ottomans and both point outward, so the thread may stay false for a while
longer.

That is worth stating rather than implying. A region whose members were
contemporaries and rivals rather than successors will not develop a thread by
being filled in, and this site's whole framing rule is that succession is a
property some polities have rather than the spine everything hangs on.
