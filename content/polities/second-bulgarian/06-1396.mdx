In 1330 the Serbs destroyed a Bulgarian army at Velbazhd and the tsar Michael
Shishman was killed in the field. Serbia under Stefan Dušan became the dominant
Balkan power for the following thirty years and took Macedonia, Thessaly and
Epirus while the Byzantine empire fought its own civil wars. Bulgaria spent those
decades as the junior partner in somebody else's ascendancy.

Then it did the thing that made the ending certain. Ivan Alexander divided the
state between two sons, one at Tarnovo and one at Vidin, and they did not
cooperate — not against each other, particularly, and not with each other at all.

## What walked into that

The Ottomans crossed into Europe permanently in 1354, ferried across the
Dardanelles by one side in a Byzantine civil war that had hired them.

What they found in the Balkans was a peninsula in which nobody could combine.
Serbia's empire had dissolved into principalities after 1371. Byzantium was
bankrupt and fighting itself. Bulgaria was two states with one dynasty. The
Ottoman advance is often described as irresistible and it was not: it was
methodical, it took forty years, and at every stage it faced opponents who could
not be brought to act together for a season.

Sofia fell in 1382. Tarnovo fell in 1393 after a three-month siege. Vidin held on
until 1396, and fell when the crusade assembled in western Europe to relieve it
was destroyed at Nicopolis on the Danube — a defeat so complete that it ended
serious western intervention in the Balkans for a generation.

## The church

The Bulgarian patriarchate was abolished and the Bulgarian church placed under
the patriarch of Constantinople.

That is worth pausing on, because it is the same argument being settled a fourth
time. Boris had extracted an archbishopric from Constantinople in 870 by playing
Rome against it. Simeon had extracted a patriarchate in 927 by thirty years of
war. Kalojan had got a primate out of Innocent III in 1204 and Ivan Asen II had
got the patriarchate restored on Orthodox terms in 1235.

Five centuries of Bulgarian rulers had fought Constantinople for control of their
own church and had won every round. Constantinople won the last one in 1393
without fighting at all, because a Muslim conqueror preferred to deal with one
Christian hierarchy rather than several, and the one he chose was the Greek.

## Two hundred and eleven years

There was no Bulgarian state again until 1878.

The second Bulgarian empire lasted about as long as the first had, and it is the
lesser of the two by most measures — smaller at its widest, shorter at its peak,
and without a single durable institution to set beside an alphabet. It had one
great reign of eleven years, one legendary battle it won against the crusaders,
one peasant who briefly wore the crown, and a school of editors working in the
last twenty years before the walls came down.

Then five hundred years of somebody else's empire, during which the country's own
name survived mainly as the name of a province.
