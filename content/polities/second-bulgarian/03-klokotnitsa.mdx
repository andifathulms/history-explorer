In March 1230, at Klokotnitsa on the Maritsa, Ivan Asen II destroyed the army of
Epirus and captured Theodore Komnenos Doukas, who had crowned himself emperor at
Thessalonica and was, at that moment, the man most likely to retake
Constantinople.

Theodore was blinded, reportedly for conspiring afterwards. Bulgaria absorbed
Macedonia, Albania and most of Thrace, and for about a decade held everything
between the Danube and the Aegean except the capital and the Peloponnese.

## What the victory did to somebody else's page

There were three Greek governments claiming to be the Roman empire after 1204 —
Nicaea, Epirus and Trebizond, of which this corpus carries the first and the
third. Epirus was the strongest of the three in the 1220s: Theodore had taken
Thessalonica from the Latins in 1224 and was moving on Constantinople.

Klokotnitsa removed it. This corpus's Nicaean record's account of its rivals
stops mentioning Epirus after 1230 and does not explain why, because when that
record was written the reason had no page. The reason is that a Bulgarian tsar
destroyed it in an afternoon, and Nicaea — which had been the weaker claimant —
took Constantinople thirty-one years later against no serious Greek competition.

Two records in this corpus turn on that battle and neither of them is the one
that fought it.

## What Ivan Asen II did with it

He styled himself *emperor of the Bulgarians and the Greeks*, which is the same
manoeuvre Simeon made three hundred years earlier and which this site codes as
`titulature` on both records. He had the victory carved on a column in the church
of the Forty Martyrs at Tarnovo, where the inscription still is, and it says in
so many words that all the land from Adrianople to Durazzo was his and that the
Franks held only Constantinople.

He minted. He married a daughter to the Latin emperor's heir and was nearly made
regent of the Latin empire, which would have put a Bulgarian tsar in charge of the
crusader state his father had beaten. He negotiated with Nicaea, restored the
Bulgarian patriarchate on Orthodox terms in 1235 with Nicaean and eastern
recognition, and besieged Constantinople in alliance with Nicaea and then broke
off the alliance because he preferred a weak Latin emperor to a strong Greek one.

That last decision is the interesting one and it looks like the right call. A
restored Byzantine empire was the only power that could threaten Bulgaria's
position, and Ivan Asen spent his last years preventing one.

## One reign

He died in 1241. The Mongols raided the Balkans the following year and Bulgaria
began paying tribute to the Golden Horde — this corpus carries that record — his
son was a child, and the state never held Macedonia again.

This record's `reach_km2` is null and its peak is a decade. What it has instead
of a number is a column in a church with an inscription on it, and the fact that
two other records on this site are shaped by a battle their own pages do not
explain.
