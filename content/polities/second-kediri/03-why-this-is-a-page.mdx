The obvious objection to this record is that twelve months is not a polity, and
that Jayakatwang's restoration should be a chapter of the Kediri record rather
than a page of its own. It is a fair objection and it was raised. This chapter is
the answer, so that a reader can disagree with it on the evidence.

## What the merge would cost

Merging means one Kediri record spanning 1042 to 1293.

That span has a seventy-year hole in it, from Ganter in 1222 to Jayakatwang's
revolt in 1292, during which the kingdom did not exist and its territory belonged
to this corpus's Singhasari record.

The problem is not that the hole would be undocumented. A chapter can say
anything. The problem is that this site draws spans on a time axis — on the
region's thread, on the timeline, on the corpus-spans chart on every polity page
— and a span is drawn as a bar. A single Kediri record would be rendered as one
continuous bar 251 years long, next to a Singhasari bar occupying seventy of
those years, and nothing in the drawing could show that the first bar is hollow in
the middle.

This site has a hard rule about that class of error. Rule 9 says never join two
cited figures with a line, because a line from 909 to 969 asserts an extent in 940
that nobody published — "the same invention hard rule 2 forbids, committed in
pixels instead of YAML, where it is harder to notice and impossible to cite".

A continuous bar across a seventy-year interruption is the same move. It asserts
a kingdom in 1250 that no source claims.

## And what the graph would do

The second cost is in the succession picture.

The edges here run `kediri → singhasari` (Ganter, 1222) and `singhasari →
second-kediri` (the revolt, 1292). Merged, those become an arrow from Kediri to
Singhasari and an arrow from Singhasari back to Kediri — a two-node loop in a
graph whose entire meaning is that one thing came after another.

The alternative is to drop one of them, which loses either the battle that ended
the first kingdom or the revolt that ended Singhasari. Both are the best-attested
events on their respective records.

## What the split costs instead

It costs a thin page. Four coded fields on this record are null, its `founder`,
`peak` and `last` are the same man, and two of its three chapters are about the
schema rather than about Java.

That is a real cost and it is the lesser one. The site's own instructions say a
polity with no edges is complete and must never be treated as second-class; a
polity with a short span is the same case. This corpus's Alodia record is thin
where it counts and exists because a neighbouring page named it as an absence,
and this corpus's Pajang record covers eighteen years and closed a field that had
been empty for two passes.

The `resumes` field exists precisely so that an interruption can be stated rather
than smoothed over, and its test — set out in the project's own rules — is
whether the sources treat the two as periods of one continuous object. They do:
the historiography calls this the second kingdom of Kediri, using the same word
for the first.

Twelve months is the shortest thing that test has ever been applied to on this
site. It is not the wrong test.
