Kublai Khan had sent envoys to Java demanding submission, and Kertanegara — in
the account that survives — had the last of them branded on the face and sent
back. The Yuan expedition dispatched to punish him arrived in 1293.

Kertanegara had been dead for a year. The man on the throne was Jayakatwang,
who had killed him, and who had no quarrel with anybody in China.

Raden Wijaya, Kertanegara's son-in-law, met the Mongols, presented himself as
Singhasari's rightful heir, and offered to guide them to the usurper. They took
the offer. Kediri was destroyed. Raden Wijaya then turned on the expedition,
drove it back to its ships, and founded Majapahit out of the same campaign in
the same year.

It is a rare way for a kingdom to die: destroyed by an expedition sent against
somebody else, in a war it had no part in starting.

## Nothing else survives

A kingdom that lasted twelve months leaves almost no record of its own.

Jayakatwang raised revenue and troops the way Singhasari had raised them the
year before, because there was no time to do anything else and no source
describes him doing otherwise. There is no reform, no monument, no law, no
inscription programme. What there is, is one revolt, one year, and one
catastrophe arriving from a direction nobody in Java had chosen.

It is a complete history and it is almost empty, and those are not in tension.
Some polities simply do not last long enough to be anything.
