Kublai Khan had sent envoys to Java demanding submission, and Kertanegara — in
the account that survives — had the last of them branded on the face and sent
back. The Yuan expedition dispatched to punish him arrived in 1293.

Kertanegara had been dead for a year. The man on the throne was Jayakatwang, who
had killed him, and who had no quarrel with anybody in China.

Raden Wijaya, Kertanegara's son-in-law, met the Mongols, presented himself as
Singhasari's rightful heir, and offered to guide them to the usurper. They took
the offer. Kediri was destroyed. Raden Wijaya then turned on the expedition,
drove it back to its ships, and founded Majapahit out of the same campaign in the
same year.

It is the only case on this site of a polity being destroyed by an expedition
sent against somebody else.

## Four null institutions

Every coded field on this record is null except `legitimation`, and on a
twelve-month polity that is a description rather than a gap.

This site has a hundred and thirteen records null in `succession_rule` and
forty-two null in `military_basis`, out of a hundred and ninety-nine, and on most
of them the null means nobody has opened a source that says. Here it means there is nothing to say: Jayakatwang raised
revenue and troops the way Singhasari had raised them the year before, because
there was no time to do anything else, and no source describes him doing
otherwise.

A record can be complete and almost empty. This one is, and this corpus's Alodia
record — thin where it counts, and there because a neighbouring page named it as
an absence — is the precedent.

## What it closes

Singhasari's `ended.by` now reads `[second-kediri]`.

That record's fifth chapter used to explain that the field was empty because
Kediri was not a polity in this corpus, and that what the field could not say was
that the usurper was also an external power that had spent seventy years as a
subordinate. It can say it now, and that chapter has been rewritten to say what
changed.
