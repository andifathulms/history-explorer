Jayakatwang was lord of Kediri and a vassal of Singhasari — the kingdom that
had destroyed Kediri at Ganter seventy years earlier and taken its place.

In 1292, with Kertanegara's army across the Java Sea on the Sumatran expedition
and the capital thinly held, he revolted, marched on it, and killed the king.

The sources describe what followed as a restoration: the second kingdom of
Kediri, using the same name the historiography gives the first.

## Seventy years of waiting

It is worth stopping on what that means. Kediri had been the dominant power in
eastern Java for a hundred and eighty years before Ganter. After 1222 its lords
were subordinates in somebody else's kingdom, and stayed subordinates for three
generations.

Then the overlord's army left the island, and the lord of Kediri took the
throne back.

Whether Jayakatwang thought of himself as restoring his ancestors' kingdom or
simply as seizing an opportunity is not recoverable. What is recoverable is
that the sources — and the people naming the kingdom afterwards — treated it as
the same Kediri coming back, which tells you the first one had not been
forgotten in the seventy years it was gone.

## And then a fleet arrived

He held it for about a year.

What ended it had nothing to do with Java's own politics. Kublai Khan had sent
envoys to Kertanegara demanding submission, and Kertanegara had sent the last
one back disfigured. The expedition dispatched to punish him was already at sea
when Jayakatwang killed him, and it arrived in 1293 to find a different man on
a throne it had no quarrel with.

The next chapter is what that fleet did.
