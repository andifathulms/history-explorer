This is the shortest record on this site. It covers one year.

Jayakatwang was lord of Kediri and a vassal of Singhasari, the polity that had
destroyed the kingdom of Kediri at Ganter seventy years earlier. In 1292, while
Kertanegara's army was across the Java Sea on the Sumatran expedition, he
revolted, marched on the capital, and killed him. The sources describe what
followed as a restoration of Kediri — the second kingdom of that name — and that
is the whole basis for this being a record rather than a paragraph on somebody
else's page.

## Why `resumes` and not an edge

This site has a field, `resumes`, for two records that are one polity seen twice
across an interruption. It is deliberately not an edge: an edge is an event with
a year, and this relation has no year; and edges are drawn by a region's
succession thread, which is a picture of one thing coming after another rather
than of one thing coming back.

There were five pairs before this one and they are all long: the two Babylons,
the two Assyrias, the two Elams, the two Egypts, the two Bulgarias. Second
periods of centuries, separated by centuries.

This pair is seventy years apart and the second half is twelve months, which
makes it look like an abuse of the field. The test the project sets is not
duration. It is whether the sources treat the two as periods of one continuous
object, and here they do it in the name: what Jayakatwang held is called the
second kingdom of Kediri, by the same historiography that calls the first one the
first.

The alternatives were both worse. A single record spanning 1042 to 1293 would put
a seventy-year hole inside one span with nothing on the page able to show it.
Leaving this year out entirely would leave this corpus's Singhasari record with
an empty `ended.by` — which it had, because the polity that ended it was not
here.

So: two records, one `resumes`, no edge between them, and the succession graph
runs Kediri → Singhasari → this → Majapahit without a loop in it.
