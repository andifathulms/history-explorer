By 1598 Hideyoshi's commanders in Korea held three fortified coastal
stations and little more, worn down by six years of a war none of them
could finish. When the aging general Kato, commanding one of the
stations, judged the conflict no longer worth pursuing, he tried
repeatedly to bribe or slip past the Korean and Chinese fleets blockading
his own withdrawal — offers of silver and swords that Yi Sun-sin
refused outright each time they were made.

## A prayer before the last fight

Aware of enemies at his own court who wanted him gone regardless of his
record, and sensing that this engagement might be his last, Yi is said
to have prayed before the battle: "To-day I am to die. Give me but one
more victory over these Japanese and I shall die content." He massed
his fleet before dawn against the Japanese ships attempting to slip out
past the blockade, drove them into a burning, chaotic retreat, and
personally led the pursuit of the fleeing vessels standing exposed in
the prow of his own ship.

## A wound hidden until the fighting stopped

A stray bullet struck Yi in the midst of the battle's final hour. His
last order was not for aid but for silence: "Do not let the rest know
that I am dead, for it will spoil the fight." His nephew carried the
engagement to its conclusion without revealing the admiral's death, and
only when a fellow commander boarded Yi's ship afterward and found him
already gone did the news break — "there is no soldier now left in
Korea," his ally is said to have lamented over the body.

## An empire's collapse settles what the sea could not

Word soon followed from Japan itself that Hideyoshi had died, and with
his death the political will sustaining the invasion evaporated
entirely; the remaining Japanese forces were recalled and active
fighting in the peninsula ended. Joseon had survived the war, but at a
cost that would take generations to repair, and it had done so on the
strength of a navy built almost entirely around the ingenuity and
sacrifice of one commander who did not live to see the peace he had
made possible.
