---
title: A confession imposed by decree
drafted_from: newman-2006
phase: formation
---

Isma'il was fourteen when he took Tabriz in 1501 at the head of the Qizilbash —
Turkmen tribal followers of a Sufi order his family had led for two centuries,
who regarded him as a figure of divine authority. He then declared Twelver
Shi'ism the official religion of a country that was overwhelmingly Sunni, and
enforced it.

Nothing else on this site is quite like this. Every other conversion here is
gradual, follows conquest by generations, or is a ruling-class affair that leaves
the population alone — the Fatimids held Egypt for two centuries without making
it Isma'ili. The Safavids changed the confession of a large settled population
inside roughly a century, by importing Arab Shi'i scholars from Lebanon and
Bahrain to build a clergy that did not previously exist there, and by
suppressing the alternative.

The consequence is the modern map. Iran is a Shi'i state in a Sunni region
because of a decision taken in 1501, and the Ottoman-Safavid wars that followed
were the first sustained confessional conflict within Islam between two great
powers.

Abbas I completed the state. He broke the Qizilbash monopoly on military power
with a standing army of Georgian and Armenian slave-soldiers — the same
institution, four hundred years after the Samanids and Ghaznavids on this site
used it — moved the capital to Isfahan and rebuilt it, and ran the silk trade as
a crown monopoly. The dynasty fell in 1722 to an Afghan invasion and was formally
ended by Nadir Shah in 1736.
