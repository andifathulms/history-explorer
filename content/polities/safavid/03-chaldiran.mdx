title: Chaldiran

Between 1501 and 1510 Isma'il took Iran. He beat the remnants of the Ak Koyunlu,
took Iraq-i Ajam, Fars and Kerman, then Baghdad, then in 1510 marched east and
killed Muhammad Shaybani Khan of the Uzbeks near Merv, taking Khurasan and
Herat. He was twenty-three, and the Qizilbash who had done it believed he could
not be defeated because of what he was.

In August 1514, on the plain of Chaldiran north-east of Lake Van, Selim I met
him with an Ottoman army that had artillery chained in a line and janissaries
with matchlocks behind it. The Qizilbash charged the guns. The army was
destroyed, Tabriz was occupied, and Isma'il's own wife was captured.

The frontier that battle set has moved very little in five hundred years, which
is the fact usually reported. The consequence inside Iran is the larger one and
it is Babayan's subject. A man whose followers believed he was divine had been
beaten in a field by a man with cannon. Isma'il, by the accounts, never
personally led an army again and spent the remaining decade of his life
hunting, drinking and out of public sight; the Qizilbash amirs governed and
fought each other, and when he died in 1524 his ten-year-old son inherited a
decade of civil war among them.

What the state did afterwards was quietly change what it was. The messianic
claim was not renounced — it could not be, it was the reason the tribes had come
— but it stopped being the working basis of government, and the Arab Shi'i
jurists the dynasty had been importing since 1501 were given the space to build
something more durable and considerably less exciting in its place. The
progression from a millenarian movement to an orthodox clerical establishment
took about a century, and Chaldiran is where it starts.

It is also the second of the four gunpowder battles this corpus now dates
against each other — Otlukbeli 1473, Chaldiran 1514, Marj Dabiq 1516, Ridaniya
1517. In forty-four years the same Ottoman combination beat a Turkmen
confederation, a Persian empire and the Mamluk sultanate, and each of the losers
understood exactly what had happened and could not buy or build the answer.
