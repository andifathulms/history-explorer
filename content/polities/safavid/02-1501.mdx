---
title: A confession imposed by decree
drafted_from: newman-2006
phase: formation
---

Isma'il was fourteen when he took Tabriz in 1501 at the head of the Qizilbash. He
then declared Twelver Shi'ism the official religion of a country that was
overwhelmingly Sunni, had the first three caliphs cursed publicly from the
pulpits, and enforced it — by execution where necessary, and there is no reason
to soften that.

Nothing else on this site is quite like it. Every other conversion in the corpus
is gradual, follows conquest by generations, or is a ruling-class affair that
leaves the population alone: the Fatimids held Egypt for two centuries and did
not make it Isma'ili, and this site's Fatimid page says so. The Safavids changed
the confession of a large settled population within roughly a century.

They did it with an apparatus that did not exist when they started. There was no
Twelver clerical establishment in Iran to enforce a Twelver orthodoxy, so the
dynasty imported one — jurists from Jabal Amil, Bahrain and Iraq, given
stipends, endowments and offices — and put it in charge of law and education.
That is the subject of a later chapter on this page, because the institution
they built outlasted them and acquired its own doctrine of where authority sits.

Two consequences run out of 1501 and neither has stopped. The first is the map:
Iran is a Shi'i state in a Sunni region because of a decision taken by a
fourteen-year-old, and the confessional boundary in Iraq and eastern Anatolia
was drawn by the wars that followed it. The second is that those wars — Ottoman
against Safavid, on and off for two centuries — were the first sustained
confessional conflict between two great Muslim powers, and both sides obtained
rulings that the other's subjects could lawfully be killed.

This site records the decree as a turning point of type `conversion`, which the
closed vocabulary defines as a change in the confessional order of a state. Every
other entry under that type in the corpus is a ruler changing confession and the
state following him. This is the one where the state set out to change the
population.
