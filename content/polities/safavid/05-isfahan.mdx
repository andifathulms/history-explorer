Abbas moved the capital to Isfahan in 1598 and rebuilt it. Tabriz had been
indefensible since Chaldiran and Qazvin was still too far north; Isfahan sits in
the middle of the plateau, out of reach of both frontiers, on a river, in
irrigated country that could feed a court.

What he built there is one of the most complete surviving pieces of imperial
urban planning anywhere. A new maidan five hundred metres long, arcaded on all
four sides: the royal mosque at the south end, the entrance to the old bazaar at
the north, the palace gate on one flank and a private mosque on the other, with
polo played in the middle. Behind the palace gate a garden avenue, the Chahar
Bagh, running a mile and a half south to the river and across it on a bridge
that is also a dam. The forms are Timurid — the four-part garden, the four-iwan
court, the double dome — and the scale is not.

It was also a commercial instrument, and the two purposes were designed
together. Abbas ran the silk export as a crown monopoly, made the state the sole
buyer, and moved the Armenian merchant community of Julfa in Azerbaijan
wholesale to a new suburb across the river — New Julfa, which got its own
churches, its own municipal autonomy and a network reaching Venice, Amsterdam,
Aleppo and Manila. He dealt directly with the English and Dutch East India
companies to route silk by sea around Ottoman territory rather than through it,
which was a policy against the Ottomans conducted with a ledger.

The maidan is the argument in built form. A mosque, a palace, a bazaar and a
customs revenue arranged around one rectangle, with the shah able to watch all
four from a balcony.

The bill came later. Matthee's account of the following century turns partly on
this: the crown lands that paid for the ghulam corps and the court could not be
reconverted when the silk revenue fell, and a capital built for display and
commerce in the middle of a dry plateau was a difficult place to defend and an
impossible one to provision under siege. In 1722 that is exactly what happened
to it.
