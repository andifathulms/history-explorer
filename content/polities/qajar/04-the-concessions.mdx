title: Selling what was left

The Qajar state's characteristic financial act was to grant a foreigner an
exclusive right in exchange for cash now and a share later. This site's
`revenue_basis` field cannot record it, and the gap is worth stating precisely,
because the vocabulary was built for pre-modern states and this is where it
stops reaching.

`monopoly` is wrong: the state did not operate these monopolies, it sold them.
`tribute` is wrong: nobody was subordinate to anybody, at least formally. `trade-
toll` covers the customs and not the concession. What was actually happening was
the alienation of future revenue by a government that needed money in the
current year, which is a modern fiscal instrument and has no value in a
vocabulary designed around land tax and plunder.

The list is long and a few entries carry it. In 1872 the Reuter concession gave a
British subject the exclusive right to build railways and tramways, exploit all
mineral resources, run the customs, and found a national bank — Lord Curzon
called it the most complete surrender of the entire industrial resources of a
kingdom into foreign hands that had ever been dreamed of. Russian pressure and
domestic outrage killed it. In 1889 the Imperial Bank of Persia got the note
issue. In 1890 a British company got the monopoly on the purchase, sale and
export of all Iranian tobacco, which is the subject of the next chapter. In 1901
William Knox D'Arcy got a sixty-year concession over oil across most of the
country for twenty thousand pounds and a sixteen per cent royalty.

From 1898 the customs were administered by Belgian officials, on the reasoning
that a neutral third party was preferable to either of the two powers. The state
borrowed from Russia to pay for a royal tour, and from Britain to pay Russia.

By 1907 the two powers formalised what they had been doing informally: the
Anglo-Russian Convention divided Iran into a Russian sphere in the north, a
British sphere in the south-east and a neutral zone between, without consulting
the Iranian government and while it was in the middle of a constitutional
revolution.
