---
title: A law the shah did not make
drafted_from: amanat-2017
phase: contraction
---

In December 1905, after the governor of Tehran had sugar merchants beaten on the
soles of their feet for the price of sugar, the bazaar closed and a large number
of clerics and merchants took sanctuary — first at a shrine, then, in the summer
of 1906, some twelve thousand of them in the garden of the British legation.
They asked for a house of justice. By the time the movement finished asking, they
wanted a constitution and an elected assembly.

They got both. Muzaffar al-Din Shah signed the fundamental law in December 1906
and died five days later. The first majles met, drafted a supplementary law
modelled partly on the Belgian constitution with a clause giving a committee of
senior clerics a veto over legislation contrary to Islamic law, and began trying
to audit the government's finances.

His successor Muhammad Ali Shah bombarded it. In June 1908 the Persian Cossack
Brigade, under its Russian commander, shelled the majles building and the
constitutionalists were killed, imprisoned or driven out. Resistance held out at
Tabriz through a siege, and in 1909 constitutionalist forces from Gilan and
Bakhtiari tribesmen from the south converged on Tehran, took it, deposed the shah
and put his eleven-year-old son on the throne.

The constitution survived. What did not survive was the possibility of an
independent Iranian government in this period. The 1907 convention had already
divided the country into spheres; in 1911 Russia sent an ultimatum demanding the
dismissal of the American treasurer-general the majles had hired to reform the
finances, and when the assembly refused, Russian troops occupied the north and
the assembly was closed by the cabinet. During the First World War, in which
Iran was neutral, Russian, British and Ottoman armies fought across it, and the
resulting disruption and famine killed a large share of the population.

The Constitutional Revolution is the hinge on this page all the same. It made
law into something a body other than the monarch could make, and it is the
reference point for every Iranian political argument of the following century,
including the one in 1979 that ended the monarchy altogether.
