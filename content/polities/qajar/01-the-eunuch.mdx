---
title: The hostage takes the country
drafted_from: amanat-2017
phase: formation
---

Agha Muhammad Khan left Shiraz within days of Karim Khan's death in 1779, having
spent sixteen years there as a hostage, and spent the following fifteen taking
Iran. He had been castrated as a child in the aftermath of Nadir's murder, which
by every convention of the period disqualified him from ruling and had been the
reason he was thought safe to keep alive.

He took the Caspian provinces first, because the Qajar tribe was there, then
Tehran — a small town he made his capital in 1786, chosen because it was close to
the Qajar pastures and defensible, and which has been the capital ever since.
Then Isfahan, then Fars. The last Zand was hunted for four years, captured,
blinded and killed in 1794; Kerman, which had sheltered him, was destroyed and
its population blinded in numbers the sources put at twenty thousand. In 1795 he
invaded Georgia — a Safavid dependency that had put itself under Russian
protection — and sacked Tbilisi. In 1796 he was crowned at Tehran with Nadir's
jewels, went to Mashhad, and tortured the blind Shahrukh to death to find where
the rest of Nadir's treasure was.

In 1797, in camp at Shusha, two of his own servants whom he had condemned to
death the previous evening and carelessly left on duty overnight killed him.

The reign is eighteen years of almost unrelieved cruelty and it did one thing
that outlasted it: it reassembled Iran. The country Agha Muhammad Khan left to
his nephew was, for the first time since 1722, a single state with one government
and a capital, which is why this record's founder is a man nobody has ever
admired.
