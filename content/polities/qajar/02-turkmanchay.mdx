title: The century's terms are set

Fath Ali Shah ruled from 1797 to 1834 over a court of legendary display — the
long beard, the jewelled robes, the enormous number of sons — and a state whose
central problem had just changed shape. Iran's neighbours were no longer the
Ottomans and the Uzbeks. They were Russia, expanding steadily south through the
Caucasus, and Britain, increasingly concerned about the approaches to India.

The first Russian war ran from 1804 to 1813 and ended at Gulistan with the loss
of Georgia, Daghestan and most of what is now Azerbaijan. The second ran from
1826 to 1828 and ended at Turkmanchay with the loss of Erevan and Nakhchivan, an
indemnity the treasury could not pay, a commercial treaty fixing tariffs, and
extraterritorial jurisdiction for Russian subjects — the capitulations, which
meant that a foreigner in Iran was not subject to Iranian law.

The frontier drawn at Turkmanchay has not moved since. But the more consequential
clauses are the other ones, and Amanat's account puts the emphasis there. A
state that has been beaten twice and cannot pay an indemnity begins granting
things instead of money: legal privileges, tariff ceilings, and eventually
concessions. Everything in the following century that looks like a sale of the
country to foreigners begins as a way of settling a debt from these wars.

The one serious attempt to prevent it was made by the crown prince. Abbas Mirza,
governor of Azerbaijan and commander in both wars, understood exactly what he had
lost to and built a *nizam-i jadid* — a new-model army with European drill,
uniforms, artillery and British and French advisers — and sent the first Iranian
students to Europe. He was, by the general verdict, the ablest Qajar. He ran out
of money for it repeatedly, because the revenue to support a standing army did
not exist, and he died in 1833 before his father.

The corpus has heard this before. It is the argument the Otlukbeli and Chaldiran
chapters make about the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries, and the Afsharid page
about the eighteenth, arriving intact in the nineteenth.
