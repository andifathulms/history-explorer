---
title: Four hundred years of a distant sovereign
drafted_from: peters-1994

When Selim I took Cairo in 1517 the sharif Barakat did not wait to be
approached. He sent his son to Egypt with the keys of the Ka'ba and his
submission, before any Ottoman force came near the Hejaz, and was confirmed in
his position. This site's Mamluk page records the transfer from the Egyptian
side; from here it is the single most successful decision the sharifate ever
made.

What it bought was the arrangement the family did best. The Ottomans took the
title — Servant of the Two Noble Sanctuaries, which the sultans used in
preference to almost everything else and which the Ottoman page records as the
part of the 1517 inheritance they actually put at the centre of their
self-description. The sharifs kept Mecca. Istanbul sent the surra, the grain,
the annual caravan and a governor at Jeddah who collected the customs and shared
them; the sharif governed the city and the Hejaz tribes, and the sovereign was
two thousand kilometres away and disinclined to campaign in the desert.

It was among the most autonomous provinces the Ottoman empire had, and it was
autonomous because the alternative was expensive. An Ottoman army in the Hejaz
would have had to be supplied from Egypt across the Red Sea in perpetuity, in
order to govern a place whose entire product was prestige that a compliant
sharif already delivered.

Istanbul's real instrument was the appointment. Sharifs were deposed and
installed from the capital with great frequency — the office changed hands
dozens of times over four centuries, usually between cousins of the same family,
usually with a faction in Mecca and a patron in the Porte behind each — and this
site's `succession_rule` field is null because of exactly that. The office was
hereditary in a family and the holder was chosen by a foreign sovereign, and the
vocabulary has no value for the combination.

The system worked for both parties for the better part of three hundred years,
which is longer than most things in this corpus work for anybody. Then somebody
arrived who did not want the sermon.
