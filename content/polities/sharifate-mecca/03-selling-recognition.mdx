This record's `revenue_basis` reads `trade-toll` and `tribute`, and it is the
purest case in the corpus of a state financed by other states.

The Hejaz grows almost nothing. What the sharifate had was Jeddah, the port
where the Red Sea traffic for Mecca landed, and the sharifs took a share of its
customs — which is why the Mamluk seizure of that revenue in the fifteenth
century mattered so much, and why this site's Rasulid page treats Cairo's
capture of the Jeddah customs as the moment the Yemeni alternative closed.

The rest was transfers. The *surra*, an annual purse sent from Cairo and later
from Istanbul for the upkeep of the holy cities and the stipends of their
inhabitants. Endowments established for Mecca and Medina by rulers and rich
individuals from Morocco to Bengal, the revenues of which flowed to the Hejaz in
perpetuity. Grain shipments from Egypt, without which the city could not be fed.
And direct gifts from any sovereign who wanted something.

What they wanted was the sermon. The khutba at Mecca names a sovereign, and it
is heard by pilgrims from every part of the Muslim world in the one place they
all come to — which makes it the single most valuable piece of publicity in
Islam, and the sharifs were the people who decided whose name was in it.

This site has been circling that fact from four directions without a page to put
it on. The Hafsid record has the sharif's acknowledgement as the one recognition
a Tunisian claimant to the caliphate could point to. The Ikhshidid page measures
what such recognition was worth. The Sulayhid page has an Isma'ili client of
Cairo controlling the appointment of the amir of Mecca, which is the high-water
mark of the Fatimid claim. The Rasulid page has Ta'izz and Cairo competing over
whose kiswa arrived first. The Mamluk page has the guardianship passing to Selim
in 1517.

All of those are transactions with this polity, seen from the other side of the
table. A small family in a barren valley, with no army and no crops, spent nine
centuries auctioning the only thing it had, and was paid by whoever won.
