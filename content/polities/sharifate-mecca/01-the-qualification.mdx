---
title: A qualification nobody could confer
drafted_from: peters-1994
phase: formation
---

Mecca has no agriculture, no industry, no strategic position and very little
water. It has the Ka'ba, and once a year it has several tens of thousands of
people who have travelled a very long way to be there. Everything about the
polity on this page follows from that combination.

From the tenth century the city was held by *ashraf* — descendants of the
Prophet through his grandson Hasan — and from about 968 the office of amir or
sharif of Mecca was hereditary in a Hasanid family, holding under the
suzerainty of the Fatimids, who had just taken Egypt and who wanted the Friday
sermon at Mecca read in their caliph's name.

The genealogy is the whole institution. A governor can be dismissed and replaced
with another governor; a sharif could not, because the qualification was descent
from the Prophet and no sultan could confer it on somebody who lacked it. An
overlord who wanted a different sharif had to choose a different Hasanid, and
after 1201 that meant a different member of one family — which is why the
Ottomans deposed sharifs constantly and never abolished the office.

This site codes the legitimation `descent` and it is one of the few pages where
that value carries the entire weight. Elsewhere in the corpus descent is a claim
made alongside conquest or investiture, and frequently a constructed one: the
Shirvanshahs became Sasanians, the Rasulids became Ghassanids, the Safavids
became descendants of the seventh imam. Here it is the office itself, it was
genealogically documented and publicly contested, and nothing else was on offer.

The other half of the arrangement is that the sharifs never had force worth
mentioning. Their army, coded `tribal-levy` on this record, was their own
extended kin and allied Bedouin, and it could raid a caravan and could not hold
a city against anyone serious. In nine and a half centuries the sharifate lost
Mecca to an outside power repeatedly and got it back nearly every time, because
the outside power always needed a Hasanid to run it.
