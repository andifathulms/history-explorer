In 1262 a Mongol army crossed the frozen Terek to attack another Mongol army, and
the Golden Horde went to war with its own cousins in Iran on the side of the
Muslim power that had beaten them both.

## Berke

Batu's brother Berke was a Muslim, and had been one for a long time. Rubruck,
passing through the Kipchak in 1253, was told that he would not allow anyone at his
camp to eat pork. He was the first Chinggisid ruler of a major khanate to convert,
and he did it while his cousin Hülegü was on his way to Baghdad.

The quarrel had three causes and Howorth lists them in order. Hülegü had executed
princes of the Jochid line serving in his army. The great khan had patronised him
pointedly over Berke's head. And Berke claimed Arran and Azerbaijan — the winter
pasture south of Derbend that the Horde's armies had been using — which Möngke had
assigned to Hülegü.

The sack of Baghdad and the killing of the caliph, by a Buddhist cousin, gave the
grievance a shape.

## The scattering

When the tension became obvious, the Golden Horde contingents serving in Hülegü's
army understood what was coming and ran.

One body got back to the Kipchak through the Derbend pass. A second, under two
generals, went east across Khurasan with Hülegü's troops behind them and seized
Ghazni. A third — two hundred men — rode into Syria and threw themselves on the
mercy of the sultan of Egypt.

Baybars ordered them well treated and supplied with barley and grain, robes of
honour and sugar. He went out of Cairo in person to meet them, quartered them
outside the city, gave them horses, persuaded them to accept Islam, made their
chiefs emirs and enrolled the rest among the Mamluks.

Then he questioned them about their country, and sent an embassy to Berke.

## Who Baybars was

The answer to that question is the most important fact about the Golden Horde's
economy, and it takes one paragraph of Howorth to state.

The Mamluks were a corps of slave soldiers, young Turks bought chiefly out of the
Kipchak steppe, founded by Saladin and given pre-eminence for their loyalty. When
the Mongols overran the Kipchak, a great number of young Turkish captives were sold
and the corps was enormously enlarged. Its chiefs had by then become sultans of
Egypt.

Baybars was a Kipchak Turk of the Borlu tribe. He had been sold by the Mongols to
the Venetians, resold at Sivas to an Ayyubid sultan, put in the guard, and given the
title Bunduqdari. He advised the killing of Hülegü's envoy in July 1260, defeated
Hülegü's general Kitbuqa at Ain Jalut in September and recovered Syria, murdered his
own sultan on the way home when refused the government of Aleppo, and made himself
sultan. Under his patronage a surviving Abbasid was set up as caliph in Cairo.

So: the Mongol conquest of the Kipchak enslaved the population of the Kipchak; the
slaves were shipped to Egypt through the Black Sea ports; they became the army of
Egypt; that army stopped the Mongol advance at Ain Jalut; and its commander then
allied with the khan of the people who had enslaved him against the Mongols of Iran.

The letter Baybars sent to Berke assured him of his friendship, urged him to fight
Hülegü, listed his own troops of Turks, Kurds and Arabs, enumerated the Muslim and
Frankish princes who were his vassals, and told him about the fugitives who had
arrived saying that he was their master. He sent with it a certified genealogy of
the caliph he was about to install.

## The crossing

Berke sent thirty thousand men under Nogai. The Persian historian Wassaf described
the advance, and Howorth quotes him:

> In the winter of 662, when the almighty goldsmith covered the river of Derbend
> with plates of silver, and the furrier of the winter had clad the hills and heaths
> with ermine. The river being frozen hard as stone to the depth of a spear's
> length, an army of Mongols went forth at the command of Berke Oghul, filthy as
> ghuls and devils of the wilderness, and as numerous as the rain drops, their waves
> rolled over the frozen river with the speed of the wind and of fire. The rattling
> of their waggons and horses' hoofs was like thunder and lightning.

That is a court historian of the Ilkhans describing his master's cousins as devils.
Two branches of one family, sixty years after Chinggis, at war over a province and
a religion.

## What it settled

The Caucasus war ran, on and off, for sixty years and neither side won it. What it
settled was the Horde's orientation.

It made the Golden Horde a Muslim power with a permanent alliance with Cairo, a
permanent enemy in Tabriz, and a commercial interest in keeping the Black Sea slave
route open — which is why the Genoese were allowed to sit at Caffa, and why the
Horde's foreign policy for two centuries was conducted with the Mediterranean rather
than with Mongolia.

It also meant that Hülegü's Ilkhanate, hemmed in by Berke to the north and Baybars
to the south, never expanded again.
