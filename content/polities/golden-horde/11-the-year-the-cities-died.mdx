The Golden Horde was not destroyed by Moscow. It was destroyed by another
Chinggisid claimant's patron, in two campaigns, and what he destroyed was not its
army but its towns.

## The quarrel

Toqtamish had been put on the throne of the Horde with Timur's help, reunited the
two halves of it, and in 1382 burned Moscow and restored the tribute that Kulikovo
had been supposed to end. Then he turned on the man who had made him, and invaded
Transoxiana and Azerbaijan, and while Timur was away in Persia his people destroyed
one of Timur's own cities.

Timur beat him in the field in 1391. Toqtamish came back. Timur beat him again in
1395 on the Terek, and this time went on into the Horde's country in winter and did
what he had done in Persia and India.

## Astrakhan

The Volga washed the walls of the town, and the inhabitants had a winter custom:
they piled masses of ice around it and poured water over them until they had a
rampart of ice with a gate cut through it.

The governor came out submissively when Timur approached, and was arrested and sent
towards Sarai. Timur entered, ordered the inhabitants, the cattle and the property
brought out, and destroyed the place. The governor, on orders, was forced under the
ice of the Volga by his escort.

## Sarai

From Astrakhan he went on to Sarai, the residence of the khans of the Kipchak.

> There also the inhabitants were driven out like sheep, and the town destroyed.

Howorth gives the motive, from Timur's own court historian: revenge for the
destruction of a Chagatai capital that the people of the Kipchak had levelled while
Timur was campaigning in Persia.

The campaign nearly destroyed the army that carried it out. The winter and the
hardness of the march killed most of the horses; a pound of millet sold for seventy
dinars, a cow's head for a hundred, a sheep's head for two hundred and fifty. Timur
ordered the spoil of Astrakhan and Sarai divided among the troops so that every man
could be remounted, and went home by Derbend.

## Why it was fatal

Because the Horde was not only a horde.

Sarai, New Sarai, Saraychiq, Azaq, Bulghar, Astrakhan and the Crimean towns were
real cities with mints — Howorth works from their coins — and they were where the
Horde's wealth was realised. The tribute of Rus' came down the rivers to them. The
caravan trade from Khwarazm and China arrived at them. The Genoese and Venetians
bought slaves and bought grain at their ports and paid customs on it. The khan's
authority over his own princes was underwritten by his ability to distribute what
came through them.

A nomad army that loses a battle rides away and re-forms; that had happened to this
one before. A nomad state that loses its cities loses its treasury, its mint, its
customs and its reason for anybody to obey the khan, and there is no riding away
from that.

After 1395 the Horde never again had a ruler who could command all of it. The
Crimea broke away in the 1440s, Kazan in the 1430s, Astrakhan in the 1460s, and the
rump was finished by the Crimeans in 1502 — and a Russian tributary that had been
sending silver to Sarai for a century and a half stopped sending it, and started
taking the pieces one at a time.
