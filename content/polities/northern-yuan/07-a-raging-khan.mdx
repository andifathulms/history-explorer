Ligdan was born in 1592 and succeeded his grandfather in 1604 with the title
Khutughtu Khan, which he owed to his zeal for Lamaism. He had the Kanjur translated
into Mongolian, raised a great statue of Shakyamuni in 1617, and built temples and
monasteries — all of them, the chronicle says, finished in one summer.

He also decided to be what his title said he was, and that destroyed the dynasty.

## The position he inherited

Mongolia had been divided among the sons of Dayan Khan and had gone on dividing.
The senior family's authority, especially north of the Gobi, had become very
nominal. The tribes had been emancipating themselves from it for a century, and the
great khan of the Chakhar was in practice one prince among a dozen with a better
title than the rest.

Wanting the position his ancestors had filled was not unreasonable. How he went
about it was.

## The method

Sanang Setsen, who was an Ordos prince and no impartial judge, reproaches him for
having scattered the six tümens, and quotes a proverb at him: *a raging khan
disturbs the state, as a raging elephant overthrows his keepers.*

Every time he asserted himself a tribe left. He raided Liaodong, out of jealousy of
the rising Manchu power. When the Khorchin allied with the Manchus he marched
against them and ravaged their lands; they called the Manchus in and the Chakhar
were beaten. In 1626 the chiefs of the Naiman and the Aokhan, who were his own
vassals, abandoned him and submitted to the Manchus; the Barin and Jarud fled to the
Khorchin; the Üzemchin and Abaga escaped over the desert to the Khalkha.

In 1627 the Ordos, Tümed, Khorchin, Abaga and several Khalkha clans made a league
against him and fought his forty thousand men at Ju Tseng in the Tümed country and
beat them. On the way home the confederates ran into another three thousand Chakhar
going to ask the Ming emperor for rewards, and dispersed them too.

In 1628 he beat the Kharachin. He subdued the Tümed. He attacked Datong and
Xuanhua and demanded an annual tribute from the Chinese court. He drove Sanang
Setsen himself into the desert.

Every victory cost him another tribe.

## The end

The Manchus marched against him and he fled. He went towards Tangut and died on the
way, in the steppe of Shira Talas. Sanang Setsen dates it 1634.

His widow, who was herself of Manchu royal descent, took their son Erke Khongkhor
into the Ordos country to a place called Toli. The Manchu emperor received them
kindly. He married his own daughter to the young khan. He took Ligdan's second wife
into his own harem. He treated Erke Khongkhor and his brother as his own sons.

## What was handed over

The line of Khubilai did not die out and was not destroyed. It was married in.

And it was after his successes in Mongolia, and very largely with the help of the
Mongols who had joined his banners, that the Manchu emperor marched victoriously
from one end of China to the other. The house that left Dadu in 1368 supplied the
cavalry for the conquest that replaced the dynasty which had expelled it.
