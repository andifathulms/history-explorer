Most conquest dynasties in China end with a body. This one ended with a departure,
and that is the whole reason there is anything else to write.

## The retreat

Toghon Temür left Dadu in 1368 and went north, and kept going. He was at Shangdu,
then at Yingchang beyond the Wall, and when the Ming general Li Wenzhong marched
on Yingchang he learned on the road that the emperor was already dead. He was
fifty-one and had reigned thirty-five years.

Li Wenzhong took the city anyway. The heir, Ayushiridara, got away towards
Karakorum; his own son was captured, along with the late emperor's harem and
several princes and grandees, and sent to China. At Xingzhou thirty-seven thousand
Mongols submitted, and at Honglo Khan sixteen thousand more.

## What the Ming emperor said

The court congratulated the Hongwu emperor on the destruction of the Yuan. His
reply is the most interesting document of the whole transition. He had not taken
up arms against the Yuan dynasty, he said, but merely to put down the rebels who
were desolating the empire, and if the Yuan emperors had behaved decently he would
not have displaced them.

His grandees demanded that the captured prince Maidiribala be sacrificed in the
hall of the imperial ancestors. The emperor refused. There were precedents, he
said, but he would not follow them: the princes of the Yuan had been masters of
China for nearly a hundred years, and his own ancestors had been their subjects,
and he could not bring himself to do such a thing.

He had the young man change out of Mongol dress into Chinese, made him a prince of
the third rank with a title meaning *he who respects politeness*, and gave him and
the princesses a palace and attendants.

That is a dynasty being displaced rather than exterminated, on the explicit
grounds that it had held the Mandate — and it is why the men who rode north could
go on being the Yuan.

## The Yuan in the north

At Karakorum, Ayushiridara was joined by the general Köke Temür and proclaimed
emperor. Sanang Setsen, the Mongol chronicler Howorth follows for the steppe side,
calls him Biliktu Khagan, *the wise*, and says he was born in 1338 and mounted the
throne in 1371.

Nothing about the titles changed. The reign names went on being issued. The dynasty
went on being the Great Yuan. What changed was that it now governed pasture instead
of provinces, and that the men who had been its army were now its constituents.

## The unfinished war

Liaodong held out for the Mongols into 1371, and when its governor surrendered he
was assassinated by officers loyal to the fallen dynasty. Kao Jianu still held the
mountain forts there. Naghachu had a strong force in the Khingan range and
threatened the province for sixteen more years before his whole horde came in.

Ming armies went north repeatedly. In 1372 one reached Karakorum and was beaten so
badly that the offensive stopped for a decade. The frontier settled into raids and
markets and embassies, which is where it stayed, with interruptions, for the next
two hundred and fifty years.

The Yuan had not fallen. It had been evicted, and it knew the difference even if
nobody else did.
