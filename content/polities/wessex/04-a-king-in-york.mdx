Æthelstan came to Mercia's throne with an advantage his father had
lacked: he had been raised in the household of Æthelred and
Æthelflæd, was already trusted by the Mercian aristocracy, and was
recognized as king there independently of his election in Wessex. That
double authority gave him a freedom of action in the north that Edward
had never had. When Sihtric, the Norse king of York, died in 927
leaving the throne to his young son Olaf, Æthelstan answered the
succession dispute that followed with an invasion of Northumbria
itself.

## Eamont

The campaign was short. Olaf and his uncle Guthfrith were driven out
of Northumbria, Æthelstan took York, tore down the Danish
fortifications built inside its walls, and distributed what he found
in its treasury. On 12 July 927, at Eamont near Penrith, the kings of
Scotland and Strathclyde and the English lord of Bamburgh all became
his men, each undertaking to suppress the pagan practices still
current among the Norse settlers of the north. A West Saxon king now
ruled, in person, the territory a contemporary chronicler was already
starting to call England - north and south of the Humber alike, for
the first time under one crown.

## Wales and Cornwall brought to the same table

Within four years Æthelstan's reach extended further still. He met
the Welsh princes at Hereford and secured a yearly tribute, along with
an agreement fixing the Wye as the boundary between English and Welsh
territory near that city - a line that still separated Herefordshire
and Gloucestershire from Welsh Erging generations later. Several of
the Welsh kings, Hywel Dda of Dyfed most conspicuously, became regular
guests at Æthelstan's court, adopting English coinage and English
habits of government. From Hereford, Æthelstan marched directly
against the Britons of Cornwall, refortified Exeter, expelled its
British inhabitants, and fixed the Tamar as Cornwall's own boundary
with England. A kingdom that had been fighting for its survival a
half-century earlier was now the power every neighbouring ruler in
Britain answered to.
