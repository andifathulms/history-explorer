Cnut's victory over Edmund Ironside at Ashingdon in 1016 was followed,
not by an immediate conquest, but by a compact: the two kings met on
an island in the Severn near Deerhurst and divided England, Edmund
taking Wessex and Cnut everything north of the Thames. It was a
settlement built to fail, imposing divided loyalty on any nobleman who
held land in both halves - but it never had the chance to fail on its
own terms. Edmund died on 30 November, within weeks of the agreement,
and the West Saxons accepted Cnut as king of the whole country he had
so recently only half held.

## A Danish king who ruled as an English one

Cnut's nineteen-year reign left the machinery of English government
largely as he found it, run through earls and the existing framework
of shires and hundreds rather than replaced by a Danish administration
imported wholesale. When he died in 1035 the kingdom he had built
did not survive him intact: rival claims from his sons by different
wives split England for several years, with Harold Harefoot recognized
first as regent and then as king while Harthacnut, delayed by troubles
in Denmark, waited. Harthacnut finally succeeded to the whole kingdom
in 1040, ruled barely two years, and died suddenly at a wedding feast
on 8 June 1042 - "as he stood at his drink," in the account that
survives. With him the male line of Cnut's Danish house ended.

## The native line comes home

The succession that followed was decided quickly and, for once, without
a battle. Edward, son of Æthelred II by his Norman marriage and
Harthacnut's own guest and adopted heir since 1041, was raised to the
throne by popular acclamation in London before Harthacnut was even
buried, and crowned at Winchester on Easter Day 1043. It was a return,
after twenty-six years of Danish kingship, to the house that had ruled
Wessex since Egbert - but the new king had spent most of his life at
the Norman court his father's marriage had opened to him, and the ties
he had formed there would outlast his own reign in ways nobody
crowning him that Easter could have expected.
