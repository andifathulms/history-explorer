In about 1158 Shutruk-Nahhunte invaded Babylonia. His son Kutir-Nahhunte finished
the job three years later, ending the Kassite dynasty that had ruled Babylon for
something over four centuries. What came home to Susa is the reason this record
exists in this corpus.

The statue of Marduk, taken from Esagila. The victory stele of Naram-Sin of
Akkad — the one showing the king climbing a mountain over his enemies, wearing
the horned crown of a god — carried off from wherever it had stood for a
thousand years, and re-inscribed by Shutruk-Nahhunte, in Elamite, down the side,
recording that he had taken it. And the diorite stele of Hammurabi's laws.

## Two of this site's records were written around that plunder

This corpus's Akkadian record is about a state most of whose physical remains are
gone. Its capital has never been located. What survives is the Naram-Sin stele
and a handful of other objects, and the stele survives because this polity took
it to Susa and a French expedition dug it out in the 1890s.

This corpus's Old Babylonian record has a chapter called "The stele", which opens
by saying that the most famous object of the Mesopotamian world was found at
Susa, and observes that it reached the modern world because of a polity the
corpus did not then carry, taking it from the polity that chapter is about, and
being excavated by a French expedition in Iran.

This is that polity. Both of Mesopotamia's most reproduced monuments are in the
Louvre because an Elamite king put them on a boat.

## The odd shape of an influence count

Now consider what this site's coded fields make of that.

`descendant_scripts` on this record is 0, because Elamite cuneiform is
Mesopotamian cuneiform adapted, and rule 3 puts an adaptation on the record of
the polity whose script was adapted. `religions_carried` is 0. `successor_claims`
is 0 — nobody claimed this kingdom's legitimacy; the Anshan claim that Cyrus
makes belongs to the Neo-Elamite record three centuries later.

So on the influence axis this record scores nothing at all, and the reason is
that the axis measures transmission and this polity's most consequential act on
the record of the ancient world was *removal*. It concentrated the surviving
monumental evidence of two other civilisations in one city, where it stayed until
archaeologists arrived.

That is not influence in any sense coding rules 1 to 3 recognise, and it should
not be forced into one — inventing a fourth count to catch it is the temptation
this project has considered and rejected before. It belongs in a chapter. But it
is worth stating plainly that a page can score zero on every influence axis this
site has and still be a substantial part of the reason the other pages have
objects to point at.

## Why it stopped

Nothing about the Shutrukid conquest was durable. Elam held parts of Babylonia
for a few decades, and around 1110 Nebuchadnezzar I of Babylon — of the Second
Dynasty of Isin, and five hundred years earlier than the Nebuchadnezzar this
corpus carries — came the other way, beat the Elamites and took Marduk back. The
recovery was a founding event for Babylonian religious literature; it is
plausibly the occasion for which Marduk's promotion to head of the pantheon was
composed.

For this record it is the ending, and the `ended.by` field is empty because the
Second Dynasty of Isin has no page here. Elam then goes quiet for three
centuries.
