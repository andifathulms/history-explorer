Five centuries after the Elamite kings emptied Babylonia into Susa, an Assyrian
king came and emptied Susa.

Ashurbanipal took the city in 646 BC and wrote an account of what he did there.
It is unusually specific, because the point of the inscription is the
completeness of the thing.

## The treasuries

He entered the palaces, he says, and dwelt there amidst rejoicing, and opened
the treasure-houses in which the silver and gold and property of the kings of
Elam had been heaped up — *into which no foe other than myself had ever brought
his hand*.

Then the list of what came out, and the list is the point. Along with Elam's
own wealth he recovered the silver and gold of Sumer and Akkad and the whole of
Babylonia, which the former kings of Elam had carried off *in some seven raids*
and brought to Elam.

That is the plunder of Mesopotamia, going home. The steles and the statues and
the temple treasure that Shutruk-Nahhunte and his son had shipped east in the
twelfth century were still sitting in Susa when the Assyrians arrived, and
Ashurbanipal took what he found back.

## The obliteration

Then the part that is not about property.

He destroyed the ziggurat of enamelled brick and broke down its bronze
pinnacles. He carried off Inshushinak, the oracle god who dwells in seclusion,
*the work of whose divinity no one has ever seen*, and then twelve more gods
and goddesses by name, with their priests and their furniture. Thirty-two royal
statues in gold, silver, bronze and marble went to Assyria, along with the
guardian colossi from the temple gates.

His soldiers entered the sacred groves that no stranger had ever penetrated,
saw what was in them, and set them on fire. The tombs of the earlier and later
kings of Elam were opened, devastated and exposed to the sun; the bones were
carried off to Assyria, so that their shades might be denied rest and cut off
from food offerings and libations of water.

Over twenty-five days' journey of Elamite country he scattered salt and thorn.
The noise of people and the tread of cattle he banished from the fields, and
left wild asses and gazelles to lie down there as if at home.

## What was still in the ground

The claim is total and the claim is not quite true, which is the last thing
worth saying about it.

The victory stele of Naram-Sin of Akkad and the diorite stele of Hammurabi's
laws had been in Susa for five hundred years when Ashurbanipal arrived, and
they were still in Susa two and a half thousand years later, when a French
expedition dug them out in the 1890s and 1901.

An Assyrian king who told the world he had taken everything back, and a modern
excavation that found the two most famous objects in Mesopotamian history
exactly where the Elamites had left them. Somebody's account is incomplete, and
the plunder outlasted both the people who took it and the people who came to
take it home.
