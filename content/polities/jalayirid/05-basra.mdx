The house did not end in 1410. What was left of it held Basra and the lower
Euphrates for another twenty-two years, under a succession of claimants who
appear in the record mainly when somebody attacks them, until Husayn II was
killed in 1432 and the Kara Koyunlu took the last of it.

This site's span field therefore closes as a range, 1410 to 1432, and both dates
are defensible: the first is when the Jalayirids stopped being a power, the
second is when they stopped existing. It is the same shape as the Artuqid
record, which runs to 1409 on a rock at Mardin long after the dynasty had
stopped mattering, and it is the ordinary fate of these houses — not extinction
but a long reduction to whatever could not conveniently be besieged.

One correction belongs here, because adding this page exposed it. The Kara
Koyunlu chapter on Qara Yusuf, written before this record existed, said that he
"took Baghdad from the Jalayirids and finished them." He took Baghdad, and he
killed Sultan Ahmad in 1410, and the Jalayirids were not finished for another
twenty-two years. That chapter has been corrected in the same commit that
created this page, which is the argument for adding the polities a corpus is
already leaning on: prose about a state nobody has had to write a record for
drifts, because there is nothing to check it against.

What the Jalayirids leave is the transmission. The Ilkhanid administrative
apparatus reached the Turkmen confederations and then the Safavids through them
rather than directly, and Persian painting reached Herat the same way. Ninety-
seven years, two capitals, no successor claim on this site's record, and a
manuscript in the British Library that changed what the rest of the tradition
looked like.
