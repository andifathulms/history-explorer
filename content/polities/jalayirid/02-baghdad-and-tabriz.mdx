The Jalayirids ended up holding the two cities that mattered in the west, and
the way they got them explains what kind of state it was.

## The amir who did not claim the throne

Hasan Buzurg — Big Hasan, to distinguish him from a rival of the same name —
was a Jalayir amir of the Ilkhanid establishment, married into the Chinggisid
house, who came out of the scramble after 1335 holding Baghdad and Arab Iraq.

He did not claim to be the ruler of anything. He raised a Chinggisid puppet,
issued in that man's name, and governed. So did his rivals; for about twenty
years the wars of post-Ilkhanid Iran were fought between amirs each of whom had
a Chinggisid of his own, because the one thing everybody agreed on was that the
throne belonged to a lineage none of them was in.

That arrangement is the reason the Jalayirids started with an advantage. Baghdad
still had the Ilkhanid divan in it — the registers, the assignment system, and
the Persian secretarial families who had been running the province for eighty
years. A man who took Baghdad and did not disturb the office got a functioning
government with the paperwork included.

## Tabriz

Tabriz was the other half and it was harder, because Tabriz was the prize
everybody wanted: the Ilkhanid capital, the terminus of the trade from the
Black Sea, and the seat from which Azerbaijan was governed.

It changed hands repeatedly through the 1340s and 1350s among the Chobanids,
the Jalayirids and eventually the Golden Horde, whose khan Jani Beg took it in
1357. Hasan Buzurg died the same year. It was his son Shaykh Uways who took
Tabriz back in 1359 and held it, and from that point the Jalayirid state is a
two-capital arrangement — Baghdad in the winter, Tabriz in the summer, Arab Iraq
and Azerbaijan under one administration for the first time since the Ilkhanate.

## What that made them

By the 1360s the Jalayirids were the largest of the successor states, and the
only one that had inherited both a capital and a chancery rather than building
either.

It is worth being clear that this was not a conquest empire. They took two
cities and the machinery that came with them, dropped the Chinggisid fiction
when nobody was left who cared, and spent the rest of their existence defending
what they had against the Muzaffarids in the south, the Kara Koyunlu on the
Anatolian flank, and eventually Timur.

Everything they are remembered for was made in those two cities in the fifty
years they held both.
