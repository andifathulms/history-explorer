---
title: A sultan who kept his exits open
drafted_from: chi-6
phase: contraction
---

Uways died in 1374 and his sons fought over the inheritance. There was no
partition and no rule of any kind — only brothers contesting the whole of it,
which is not a succession system but the absence of one.
Sultan Ahmad came out of that in 1382, having had his brother killed, and ruled
for twenty-eight years, of which a substantial proportion was spent outside his
own territory.

Timur came west in 1393 and took Baghdad; Ahmad fled to the Mamluks in Cairo,
and came back when Timur left. That was not an unusual outcome and it was not
the disaster it looks like. A ruler in this region could be beaten out of his
territory without being destroyed, because Timur took submissions and plunder
and went home, and Qara Yusuf of the Kara Koyunlu was doing the same thing in
the same years, often in the same places and sometimes in the same refuge.

What the arrangement required was somewhere to go, and a great deal of Ahmad's
reign consists of maintaining relations with people who might have to shelter
him. He was a poet, a musician and a painter, and the courts he cultivated were
the ones where that counted. It is not obvious how much of this was policy and
how much was temperament, and by 1400 the distinction had stopped mattering,
because he needed all of it.
