---
title: Sultan Ahmad's twenty years of running
drafted_from: chi-6
phase: contraction
---

Uways died in 1374 and his sons fought over the inheritance. There was no
partition and no rule of any kind — only brothers contesting the whole of it,
which is not a succession system but the absence of one.
Sultan Ahmad came out of that in 1382, having had his brother killed, and ruled
for twenty-eight years, of which a substantial proportion was spent outside his
own territory.

Timur came west in 1393 and took Baghdad; Ahmad fled to the Mamluks in Cairo. He
came back when Timur left. Timur came back in 1401 and destroyed the city —
this is the sack with the towers of skulls — and Ahmad fled again, this time to
the Ottoman sultan Bayezid, who was destroyed by Timur at Ankara the following
year partly for sheltering him.

Qara Yusuf of the Kara Koyunlu was doing the same thing in the same years and
often in the same places, and the two of them sheltered together. A ruler in
this region could be beaten out of his territory without being destroyed,
because Timur took submissions and plunder and went home. When Timur died in
1405 both men came back.

Ahmad retook Baghdad and then, in alliance with Qara Yusuf, Tabriz. Then the two
of them quarrelled over which of them Tabriz belonged to. In 1410 Ahmad marched
against his ally and was defeated and killed.

It is a fitting end for the dynasty in a way that the chronicles do not need to
point out. Three generations of Jalayirids had governed Iraq and Azerbaijan
using the Ilkhanate's own administration; the man who killed the last effective
one led a pastoral confederation that had been the Jalayirids' hired cavalry in
the 1370s.
