Rome's empire is on this site because so much of the site's later content is
about people claiming it.

Charlemagne's coronation in 800 was an attempt to have the western half again.
Mehmed II took Constantinople in 1453 and the title Kayser-i Rum with it. Those
are the two successor claims counted on this page, and they span six hundred and
fifty years and two religions.

There used to be three. The Byzantine Empire was in the list, and this paragraph
argued against its own data in the next sentence, because the Byzantines on this
site are not a successor to Rome — they are Rome, the eastern half continuing
without a break in institutions, law or self-description, calling itself
*Basileia Rhomaion* to the end. Coding rule 1 counts a later polity asserting an
earlier polity's legitimacy, and a polity does not claim itself.

The site's own edges had it right the whole time. `roman -> byzantine` is
`partitioned from`; `roman -> carolingian` and `roman -> ottoman` are `claimed
legitimacy of`. The rulebook says the count and the edges are kept separately on
purpose and that a mismatch is a bug worth finding, and this is what one looks
like when it is found: two claims, two edges, and a chapter that had been
contradicting the field above it since it was written.

The empire itself is worth stating plainly. At Trajan's death it held about five
million square kilometres and perhaps a fifth of the world's population, ran on
a professional standing army and a body of civil law that is the ancestor of most
legal systems in use today, and maintained a Mediterranean-wide economy that did
not recover its scale in the west for a thousand years after it stopped.

The site's record ends it in 395 with `fragmentation`, at the final
administrative division into eastern and western halves. That is a choice about
where to cut a continuous thing: the west was deposed in 476, the east continued
to 1453, and neither date is the end of the same object.
