The Sena dynasty's own origins lay far from Bengal. Smith records its
founder, a chief named Samantasena, as having come from the Deccan -
one more instance of the migration that had carried warrior families
across the subcontinent for centuries. Around the middle of the
eleventh century Samantasena, or by some accounts his son
Hemantasena, established a small principality at Kasipuri, identified
with the modern town of Kasiari; neither man, by Smith's own account,
achieved anything approaching real power, and the family's early
history reads as little more than a minor Bengal chieftaincy among
many under nominal Pala overlordship.

## A grandson who took a kingdom

The real founding of the Sena dynasty as a historical power belongs
to Samantasena's grandson, Vijayasena, who raised himself to
independent sovereignty in the late eleventh or early twelfth century
and wrested a large part of Bengal outright from the declining Pala
kingdom. Vijayasena reigned some forty years, fought successful wars
against other regional powers besides the Palas, and maintained
friendly relations with Choraganga of Kalinga, whose own extraordinary
seventy-one-year reign extended Kalinga's conquests into the far north
of Orissa around the same period the Kaivarta rebellion was tearing
the Pala kingdom apart from within. It was Vijayasena's own conquests,
not his grandfather's modest principality, that gave the Senas the
territory and standing his own son would inherit and reshape.
