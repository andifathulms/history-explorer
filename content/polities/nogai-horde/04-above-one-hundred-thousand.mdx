In 1558 an English merchant on his way to Bukhara spent part of the summer at
Astrakhan and watched the Nogai die. He is the only eyewitness of it, and he is
not a sympathetic one.

## What he saw

Anthony Jenkinson was the Muscovy Company's agent. He recorded that the Nogai
were *all destroyed in the yeere 1558, at my being at Astracan, through ciuill
warres among them, accompanyed with famine, pestilence, and such plagues, in such
sort, that in the sayd yeere there was consumed of the people, in one sort and
another, aboue one hundred thousand.* The like plague, he says, was never seen in
those parts. A country of great pasture remained unpeopled — *to the great
contentation of the Russes, who haue had cruell warres a long time together.*

The survivors came into Astrakhan in great numbers to surrender to their enemies
and beg relief, and were ill entertained. They died of hunger all over the island,
lying in heaps, unburied and like beasts, *very pittifull to beholde.* Many were
sold by the Russians and the rest were driven off. Jenkinson notes, with the
detachment of a man pricing a commodity, that he could have bought as many Tatar
children as he wanted from their own parents — a boy or a girl for a loaf of bread
worth sixpence in England — but that his party had more need of victuals.

## What was destroyed

He also wrote down how they had lived, in the past tense, because by the time he
wrote it was past.

They were divided into companies called hordes, each under a ruler called a murza
whom they obeyed as a king. They had no town and no house, but lived in the open
field, each murza with his people, wives, children and cattle about him, moving
when the pasture was consumed. When they moved they carried houses like tents set
on wagons drawn by camels, and in them their wives, children and all their riches,
*which is very little*. Every man had at least four or five wives besides
concubines. They had no use of money at all, but bartered cattle for clothing and
necessaries. They delighted in no art or science except war, at which they were
expert, but were for the most part herdsmen, with great store of cattle, which was
all their riches. They ate much flesh, especially horse, and drank mare's milk,
and were often drunk on it.

That is a complete description of a mobile pastoral economy with no reserve. It
explains the death toll. A horde that is fighting itself cannot move its herds to
the right pasture at the right time, and a people whose entire capital is livestock
has nothing to fall back on when the herds go.

## The civil war

The war was Ismail's. He had killed his brother Yusuf in 1555 and spent the years
after it fighting Yusuf's sons, the sons of Sheikh Mamai, and anyone who would not
accept him; Moscow's armies were on the Volga throughout, taking Astrakhan in 1554
and annexing it in 1556, and the murzas were choosing sides in that quarrel as
well as their own.

A hundred thousand dead is Jenkinson's figure and he was counting nothing. What
is certain is that the power which had killed a Crimean khan in his own camp
thirty-five years earlier was, after 1558, never able to threaten anybody again.
