The most consequential rule in the post-Mongol steppe was that only a descendant
of Chinggis Khan could be a khan. It decided who could found a state, who could
only serve one — and it produced, in the Manghit, a power that was stronger than
most khanates and could never become one.

## Edigü

Idiku, or Edigü, was a beg of the Manghit clan who entered the service of Timur
while Urus Khan and Toqtamish were fighting over the throne of the Kipchak. He
went with Timur on the great campaign that broke Toqtamish, was rewarded with a
jewelled girdle and a caparisoned horse, and asked leave to go home and gather up
his own scattered clans. Timur gave it, along with a diploma exempting his people
from capitation tax.

He spent the next twenty years making and unmaking khans of the Golden Horde. He
put Timur Kutlugh on the throne, then Shadibek, then Puladbeg when Shadibek was
deposed in 1407, then Chekre — and treated the khan at Sarai, Von Hammer says,
rather as an equal than as a master. He invaded Russia. He fought Vytautas of
Lithuania for a generation. He was finally killed in a battle with a son of
Toqtamish; one account has him wounded and hiding in the reeds, where he was found.

He could not be khan, and did not try. What he left instead was a name: the
hordes his descendants ruled came to be called after an earlier Jochid prince,
Nogai, and the murzas of all of them traced themselves to Edigü.

## The horde on the Yaik

When he fell, the greater part of his people went east of the Volga and camped on
the Yaik and the Emba. Two of his sons went to Turan; one of them, Nur ad-Din,
became the founder of the power of the Great Nogai. His son was Waqqas; Waqqas's
sons were Musa, Yamgurchi, Hasan and a fourth killed in the Uzbek wars.

These men were subjects of Abu'l-Khayr while he lived. When he died they were
among the leaders of the revolt against his house, and in 1481, allied with Ibak
of Tyumen, they rode into the camp of Ahmad Khan of the Golden Horde — Yamgurchi's
own brother-in-law — and killed him.

That is the horde announcing itself: not by taking a throne but by removing the
man who sat on the senior one.

## What they were for

Everything afterwards follows from the same position. The Nogai murzas had
cavalry, pasture between three river systems and no dynastic claim, and their
neighbours all had claims and needed cavalry.

So they married into Kazan and fought its wars; they dominated Astrakhan, and one
of them boasted to Moscow that the children of Timur Kutlugh had submitted to
him; they made and broke khans in the Crimea; they were the refuge and the
executioners of the khans of Siberia. Ivan III let Musa marry his daughter to the
khan of Kazan, and refused to let the khan's sister marry Yamgurchi's son, which
is a precise measure of how the arrangement was understood in Moscow.

The Manghit were not a state with a dynasty. They were the thing dynasties had to
come to terms with.
