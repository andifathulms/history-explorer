The horde was finished off from two directions by peoples who had not existed as
powers when it was founded.

## The Yaik

In 1580 Ivan IV sent an army under Ivan Murashkin against the Cossack bands who
had been plundering the Volga since the taking of Astrakhan — robbing merchants
and even the tsar's own envoys. Murashkin dispersed them, and they scattered in
three directions. One body went north under Yermak and destroyed the khanate of
Siberia. One went to the Terek. The third marched east along the Caspian shore to
the mouth of the Yaik, where they heard from prisoners about a famous Nogai
capital upriver.

They took Saraychiq, burned it, laid violent hands on the inhabitants and dragged
the corpses out of their tombs. The details survive in the correspondence between
the tsars and the murza Urus the following year.

Then they built a town of their own in the middle of the horde's country and
called it Uralsk, and became, Karamzin says, the terror of the Nogai — of Urus
above all, who never stopped complaining to Moscow about their brigandage. The
tsar replied that they were vagabonds and fugitives beyond his control. Urus's
answer deserves to be remembered:

> A town of such importance, could it exist without your countenance? Some
> prisoners whom we have taken say they are subjects of the Tzar.

## The oath

What the horde had left was arbitration. In November 1604 the boyar Simeon
Godunov came to Astrakhan with full powers to settle the quarrels of the Nogai
chiefs. They assembled, declared Ishterek the senior prince, and the Russian
commissary put him on oath.

He and all the descendants of Ismail were to fight Russia's enemies to the death;
to grant nobody the title of prince or regent without the tsar's confirmation; to
stop the internal broils of the tribe; and to have no intercourse with the shah,
the sultan, the khan of the Crimea, the rulers of Bukhara and Khiva, the Kazakhs,
the shamkhal of the Kumyks or the Circassians. He was to camp where he was told —
near the Caspian, on the Terek, the Kuma, and the Volga by Tsaritsyn — and to
subdue the people of Kasai in Little Nogai, so that from the Black Sea to beyond
the Caspian there should be one horde under one man answerable to Moscow.

He kept it long enough to harry the Azov Nogai into selling their children at
Astrakhan, and broke it in 1613 by ravaging the Ukraine and riding to the
neighbourhood of Moscow, because he disapproved of the election of Michael
Romanov.

## The Kalmyks

Then the Torghuts came. They crossed into the Tara district in 1606, were beaten
back by degrees, and after a catastrophe at the hands of the Mongols around 1620
and pressure from the Kazakhs behind them they poured westward in numbers and
settled on the Chumysh, the Ob and the Tobol.

They kept coming. Kalmyk chiefs began demanding the tribute the Bashkirs had paid
the Nogai. In 1633 Kho-Urluk and his son marched against the Nogai chief Kanai
with the help of a rebel murza and pressed him so hard that he appealed to Moscow;
orders went to the commanders of Tobolsk, Tyumen and Tura to punish the Kalmyks,
and those towns were much too far away to do anything at all.

In 1643 Kho-Urluk moved down to the neighbourhood of Astrakhan and tried to detach
the Nogai princes from their Russian allegiance; the Russians marched out and
killed him with some of his sons and grandsons. It made no difference. Not long
after, his sons Yeldeng and Loosang crossed the Yaik into the Volga steppe and
subdued the Nogai tribes of Kitai-Kipchak, Mailebash and Yedisan, and the Turkmen
of the Red Camel Horde south of the Emba.

The greater part of the Volga Nogai became the Kalmyks' subjects and followed
their fortunes. Howorth's summary of the whole process is four words long: the
Kalmyks swept over the steppes of the Yaik, broke it to pieces, and scattered it
hither and thither.
