For roughly a hundred years, from the killing of Ahmad Khan to the burning of
Saraychiq, no ruler on the western steppe could act without reference to the
Manghit murzas. Two episodes show the range of what that meant.

## 1523: the conqueror at dinner

Mehmed Giray of the Crimea marched on Astrakhan in alliance with Sheikh Mamai and
took it, and then quartered his army inside the walls.

His cousin Aguish put the case to Mamai in one sentence that is the entire
political theory of the horde: *What are we doing, idiots that we are, assisting a
neighbouring and ambitious power whose aim it is to subdue us one after another?*

Mamai listened. He advised the khan, out of concern for his soldiers' discipline,
to camp outside the town in the Tatar manner; the khan agreed. Aguish came up
with his own army. The two of them surrounded the tent while Mehmed Giray was at
dinner, killed him and his son Bahadur and several of his grandees, fell on the
camp, and chased the survivors to the Don, where many drowned. They then entered
the Crimea, wasted it, and destroyed the twelve thousand men its chiefs raised to
stop them.

The strongest ruler in the west, removed in a fortnight by allies, at no
territorial gain to themselves. The Nogai wanted no delta and no peninsula. They
wanted no neighbour strong enough to want theirs.

## 1555: Ismail kills Yusuf

The other episode is the same logic turned inward, and it destroyed the horde.

By the 1550s the murzas had divided over the largest question on the steppe.
Yusuf, the senior prince, listened to Süleyman and to the khan of the Crimea, who
urged him to rally to the threatened cause of Islam; he allied with Astrakhan,
married his nephew into its royal house, and imprisoned a Russian envoy. His
brother Ismail took the other side, offered Ivan IV his alliance, and became the
patron of Dervish Ali, a claimant to the Astrakhan throne.

In December 1553 Ismail's envoys arranged at Moscow that a Russian army would
come down the Volga with him to punish Astrakhan and Yusuf both. It did, in 1554,
and put Dervish Ali on the throne.

In 1555 Ismail murdered his brother and wrote to the tsar from Saraychiq:

> Your enemy no longer lives; my nephews and sons have unanimously put the reins
> of power in my hands. I rule over all the clans.

He did not. Yusuf's five sons crossed the Volga and beat him and killed his
cousin; the sons of Sheikh Mamai turned on him too; at one point he was so hard
pressed that he asked permission to take refuge in Kazan or Astrakhan — in Russian
towns, from his own people. He killed another cousin, patched up a peace with
Yusuf's family, and survived.

## What it cost

Ismail asked Moscow to build a fort on the Volga delta and another on the Irgiz,
to hold down murzas who would not submit to him. He asked the tsar to call him
brother, and the tsar declined as beneath his dignity. He invited the Russians
into the steppe as his guarantors and made his own succession a Russian interest.

He is in every sense the peak of the horde's power — no Nogai chief before him
ruled all the clans, even briefly — and he reached it by handing the decision to
somebody else.
