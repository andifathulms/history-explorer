Pergamon's library was the second of the Hellenistic world and the only serious
rival to Alexandria's, which is to say it was the second of two. The figure
usually repeated for it is two hundred thousand rolls, from Plutarch, who says
Antony gave the whole collection to Cleopatra — this corpus's Ptolemaic record
being on the same regional shelf, four hundred kilometres and one dynasty away.

The two libraries are the clearest case in this region of what Hellenistic
monarchy was for. Neither the Ptolemies nor the Attalids were Greek in any sense
their subjects would have recognised as central; both spent enormous sums
establishing that they were the patrons of Greek learning; and the competition
between them over books, scholars and editions of Homer is a proxy war about
legitimacy conducted in a reading room.

## Parchment

The famous story is Pliny's. The Ptolemies, alarmed at the rival collection,
embargoed the export of papyrus; Pergamon, unable to get writing material, was
forced to develop treated animal skin; and the material took its name from the
city — *charta pergamena*, and so parchment, pergamena, pergamino, Pergament.

The story is almost certainly not true in the form Pliny tells it. Skins had been
written on in the Near East for centuries before the Attalids existed, and this
corpus's Achaemenid record has Aramaic chancery documents on leather. What
Pergamon plausibly did was improve the preparation enough to make a superior
product, at a moment when a rival's supply was constrained.

## Why it is not an influence count

This record's `descendant_scripts` field reads 0, and the parchment story is the
reason the field needs a note rather than passing without comment.

Coding rule 3 counts writing *systems* derived or newly adapted from the
polity's own. Parchment is a writing material. Even on the most generous reading
of Pliny, the Attalids did not produce a script; they produced a surface, and the
scripts written on it were the ones already in use.

That distinction sounds pedantic and is not. The rule exists so that the axis
measures something specific and comparable across a hundred and sixty records,
and a rule that admitted "made an important contribution to writing" would admit
almost anything. The Nabataean record in this same region carries 1 on this axis
and the item is the Arabic alphabet — a chain of letterforms that can be followed
inscription by inscription. That is what the count is for.

So the parchment goes in a chapter, which is where this site puts the things its
vocabulary cannot hold, and the count stays honest at zero.

## What the library did instead

It produced Crates of Mallos and a school of Homeric scholarship that argued with
Alexandria for a century; it produced the practice of comparing manuscripts and
noting variants; and its holdings, wherever they physically ended up, went into
the stream that later became the classical canon.

None of that registers anywhere on this page either. This corpus measures reach,
duration and three counts, and a small Anatolian kingdom that spent its Roman
subsidy on scholars scores nothing on any of them.
