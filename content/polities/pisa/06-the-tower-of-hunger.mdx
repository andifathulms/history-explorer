Ugolino della Gherardesca's rehabilitation after Meloria did not last.
Ruling jointly with his nephew Nino Visconti, he drove the city's chief
ghibelline families into exile, ceded castles to Florence and Viareggio to
Lucca in a bid to buy off Pisa's guelph enemies, and made himself, in the
process, nearly as hated as the defeat he had been blamed for. When
Visconti went to Sardinia and returned to find himself barred from power,
civil war broke out between the two men, and Archbishop Ruggieri degli
Ubaldini seized the opening: he lured Ugolino out of the city, summoned
the ghibelline leaders in his absence, and had himself proclaimed Podestà
and Captain of the People in Ugolino's place.

## A palace stormed, a family imprisoned

Ugolino tried to fight his way back with a small force under an ally, Tieri
da Bientina, but after a day and a night of street fighting the palace
fell and Ugolino, two of his sons and two of his grandsons were taken
prisoner; a bastard son and another grandson had already been killed in
the assault. The Archbishop had the five survivors shut into a tower on
the Piazza degli Anziani, and once the new government was installed the
keys of the prison door were thrown into the Arno. No priest was allowed
near the tower to hear a confession; within days all five had starved to
death. Contemporaries across Italy condemned the cruelty - not, most said,
for Ugolino's own sake, whose earlier treacheries seemed to many to have
earned him some reckoning, but for the two grandsons and youngest son who
had done nothing to deserve it. The tower has been known ever since as
the Tower of Hunger, and Dante would later place Ugolino in the depths of
his own Inferno for it.

## A government that had run out of legitimate hands

What Meloria had begun, the Tower of Hunger confirmed: Pisa's own
governing class, having lost the fleet that had made the city's wealth and
prestige possible, spent the years after 1284 devouring itself in exactly
the factional violence a strong maritime republic had once been able to
suppress. No single family or office would command the same unquestioned
authority again before the Republic's own end.
