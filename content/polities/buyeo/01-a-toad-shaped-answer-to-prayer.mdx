King Hă-bu-ru of Buyeo had one sorrow no victory or treaty could
resolve: no son to inherit his throne. Riding alone through the forest
one day, tradition says, he dismounted by a rushing stream and prayed
to the Great Spirit for an heir. When he turned to remount, he found
his own horse standing before a boulder with tears running down its
face. Overturning the stone, he discovered beneath it a child the
color of gold, shaped like a toad.

## A kingdom that moved with its own heir

Hă-bu-ru raised the foundling as his son, naming him Geumwa - "Golden
Toad" - and not long afterward the kingdom itself relocated eastward,
settling near the White Head Mountain under the new name East Buyeo.
Whether the move and the succession were connected, or simply
remembered together because they happened close in time, the
tradition does not say; what it makes clear is that Geumwa grew up
expecting to rule a kingdom that had already reshaped itself around
his own arrival.

## A bride already marked by a god

When Geumwa reached manhood he found, weeping by a shoreline, a young
woman named Yu-hwa who claimed to be the daughter of the Sea King,
driven from her own family's home after being seduced by a wandering
spirit. Geumwa took her into his household as a wife, but confined her
to a chamber lit by only a single narrow aperture - a precaution that
did nothing to prevent what happened next. A ray of sunlight found its
way to her regardless, and by it, tradition says, she conceived.

## An heir who arrived already unwelcome

What Yu-hwa eventually produced was not a child but an egg the size of
five measures of grain - an object so unnatural that Geumwa, in anger,
threw it to be destroyed by his own animals. Pigs and dogs would not
touch it; cattle and horses warmed it with their breath instead, and a
stork descended to shelter it beneath her own wings. Geumwa relented
and allowed Yu-hwa to keep it, and in time it broke open to reveal a
boy whose skill with a bow, evident by the age of seven, would earn
him a name of his own - and, before long, more enemies at court than
any child should have to face.
