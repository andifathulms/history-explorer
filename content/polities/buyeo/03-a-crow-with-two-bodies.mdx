title: A crow with two bodies

Dae So, king of Buyeo a generation after Chu-mong's flight, came into
possession of an unusual omen: a red crow with two bodies but a single
head. His own soothsayers read it as a sign that two countries would
be joined under one ruler, and Dae So, pleased with the interpretation,
concluded it meant Buyeo would conquer Goguryeo - the kingdom his own
family's runaway prince had founded to the south. He sent the crow to
Goguryeo's king as a formal declaration of the coming war.

## An omen read two ways

Goguryeo's king answered with an interpretation of his own: red, he
noted, was the color of the south, and the omen therefore pointed to
Goguryeo's victory over Buyeo rather than the reverse. Rather than wait
to be proven right or wrong by events, he took the initiative himself,
sending a powerful army north before Dae So could act on his own
confident reading of the crow.

## A night attack that mired its own army

Dae So attempted to catch the invading force by surprise, launching a
night attack as Goguryeo's troops camped before an extensive marsh
near his own capital. The attempt went badly: his own soldiers became
mired in the swamp they had chosen to cross, and a giant warrior
fighting on Goguryeo's side waded into the mud and returned carrying
Dae So's own severed head. With their king dead, the Buyeo army
surrendered outright.

## A kingdom that ended without entirely disappearing

Not every survivor accepted the outcome. Dae So's own brother escaped
with a hundred followers and settled near the Yalu River, founding a
smaller holding Goguryeo chose to overlook rather than pursue further.
The main Buyeo kingdom - the one Chu-mong had fled a generation
earlier, and the one whose own throne had once been decided by a
golden foundling and a sunlit egg - ended in a single defeat before the
capital its own founding dynasty had built.
