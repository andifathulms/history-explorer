The peace that ended the war with Hannibal had barely been signed
before Massinissa began justifying the position Rome had handed him.
He encroached first on the Emporia, the richest Phoenician territory
in Africa, land Carthage had held for three centuries; when the
Carthaginians appealed to Rome under the terms of their own treaty,
Scipio himself came to judge the dispute and decided nothing at all,
leaving Massinissa in possession of his plunder. Fresh encroachments
followed along the Bagradas river on the other side of Carthaginian
territory, each provoking a new Roman commission, and each commission
ending exactly the way the last one had.

## A worm that finally turned

Decades of this wore even Carthaginian patience down. When the
Carthaginians at last took up arms to defend what remained of their
own land, they were decisively beaten, and their own failure to ask
Rome's leave before defending themselves handed the Senate the exact
pretext it had been waiting for. Massinissa lived to see the outcome
he had spent fifty years provoking: the beginning of a third war
between Rome and Carthage, one Rome had no intention this time of
leaving a treaty behind to end.

## Outlived by the war he started

Massinissa did not live to see how it finished. He died in 149 BC, the
same year old Cato the Censor also died, after the destruction of
Carthage had been resolved on but before the city itself had fallen -
spared, in the one mercy left to him, the sight of the outcome he had
worked his whole reign to bring about. He left his kingdom intact and
unmatched in the region to his son Micipsa, its borders swollen by
half a century of raids Rome had never once ruled against.
