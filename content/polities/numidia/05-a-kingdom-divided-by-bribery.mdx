News of Hiempsal's murder split Numidia in two: the larger faction
followed Adherbal, the more warlike joined Jugurtha, who armed what
forces he could and set about winning the kingdom's cities by force or
consent as each case required. Adherbal, trusting his numbers, met him
in open battle and lost, fleeing first into Roman territory and then to
Rome itself to lay his brother's murder before the Senate in person.

## A speech the money nearly buried

"Senators," Adherbal told them, "my father Micipsa charged me on his
deathbed to account only the administration of the kingdom as my own,
the real authority as belonging to you" - and asked why the grandson of
Massinissa, a king only yesterday, should have to beg for what Rome had
granted his family two generations before. Jugurtha answered the
charge with silver. His envoys reached Rome first, loaded the king's
old friends with gifts, and bought enough new ones among the Senate to
turn a body inclined against him into one debating how loudly to
defend him. A decree ordered the kingdom split between the two rivals
by a commission of ten, headed by Lucius Opimius - a man Jugurtha had
counted among his enemies, until gifts and promises taught him to
prefer his own advantage to his reputation. The commissioners gave
Jugurtha the richer, more populous half of Numidia and left Adherbal
the coastline: harbors and public buildings that looked valuable
without being it.

## A siege the commission could not lift

Possession of half a kingdom only sharpened Jugurtha's appetite for
the rest. Within a short time he was raiding Adherbal's own territory
outright, certain now that, as friends in the Roman camp at Numantia
had once told him, everything at Rome could be bought. He besieged
Adherbal at Cirta itself, and when a fresh Roman commission arrived to
order the siege lifted, met with them just long enough to avoid open
defiance of the Senate before resuming the attack the moment they
left.

## A massacre that finally forced Rome's hand

Inside Cirta, the Italian traders who had helped defend the walls
persuaded Adherbal that Rome's own greatness would protect him even in
surrender, and to give himself and the city up on that single
condition. Jugurtha's first act on taking the city was to have
Adherbal tortured to death; his second was an indiscriminate massacre
of every adult Numidian and every trader his soldiers could catch. It
was the deaths of Roman citizens, more than the murder of a king Rome
had already failed twice to protect, that finally moved a bribed
Senate to declare the war it had spent years finding reasons to avoid.
