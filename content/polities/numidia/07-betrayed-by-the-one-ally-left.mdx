Years of war wore down every advantage Jugurtha had once bought
himself. Gaius Marius, elected consul and given command in Numidia,
pressed the fighting harder than his predecessors had, and Jugurtha's
last real hope narrowed to his own father-in-law, Bocchus, king of the
Mauritanians next door - a man who, until this war, had scarcely known
the Roman people by more than their name. Bocchus wavered between the
two sides for months, at one point even sending a column to meet
Marius's own quaestor Sulla with a show of horsemen that briefly
convinced the Romans they were riding into an ambush, before insisting,
with tears, that he had meant no treachery at all.

## A plot Jugurtha suggested himself

Weary of a war that had already dragged past what any treaty could
easily settle, Jugurtha proposed to Bocchus, through his own envoy, a
plan to end it at a stroke: arrange a peace conference between all
parties, then seize Sulla and hand him over as a hostage valuable
enough to force Rome's hand. Bocchus deliberated for days, promised to
carry it out, and set a time and place for the conference - treating
Sulla's own envoy and Jugurtha's with equal courtesy and identical
promises, so that both men left him certain of victory.

## A king who changed his mind once, in the dark

On the night before the meeting, Bocchus is said to have summoned his
friends, then dismissed them again, pacing alone for hours with his
face betraying every turn of a decision he would not speak aloud.
Before dawn he sent for Sulla instead, and turned Jugurtha's own
scheme back against its author. When day came, Jugurtha approached the
agreed hillock with most of his intimates, unarmed as the conference
required. The signal was given, and his own ambush plan fell on him
from every side: his companions were cut down where they stood, and
Jugurtha himself was delivered in chains to Sulla, who marched him to
Marius.

## A death Sallust never bothered to describe

Sallust's own account ends here, with Jugurtha in bonds and the war
declared over - he never troubled to record what became of either the
king or his kingdom afterward. Other sources fill the gap: a few days
after Marius's triumph in Rome, Jugurtha was thrown into the Tullianum,
Rome's underground prison, and left there to be strangled or to die of
cold and hunger. His kingdom passed to his half-brother Gauda, its
western territory between Caesarea and the river Muluccha cut away and
handed to Bocchus as the price of his own betrayal.
