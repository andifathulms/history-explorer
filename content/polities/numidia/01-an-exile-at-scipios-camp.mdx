Massinissa had spent years fighting for Carthage in Spain, doing the
Carthaginians good service even while, sensing which way fortune was
turning, he opened a secret understanding with the Roman general
Publius Scipio. Syphax, ruler of the neighboring Massaesylian
Numidians and a far more powerful chief than Massinissa's own family
had ever been, was bound to Carthage by a similar treaty - one the
Carthaginians knew he would break the moment it suited him.

## Two generals, one African court

Scipio and his Carthaginian counterpart Hasdrubal, unknown to each
other, had both abandoned their armies in Spain and crossed to Africa
on the same errand: to win Syphax's friendship in person. They met at
his court without open hostility, each pursuing his own object.
Scipio's charm won Syphax's promise of alliance easily enough - until
Hasdrubal's own daughter, Sophonisba, arrived. Her beauty made a
deeper impression on Syphax than any Roman envoy had, and on the
promise of marrying her he threw over his new friendship with Rome
and renewed his old one with Carthage.

## Driven out for a woman he never touched

Syphax's change of heart cost Massinissa everything. His new
father-in-law drove him out of his own hereditary kingdom outright,
and when Massinissa finally reached Scipio's camp near the Fair
Promontory, it was not as an ally bringing useful strength but as an
outlaw at the head of a handful of horsemen - aid that might, Scipio
feared, cost the Romans more than it was worth. It was a bitter
disappointment after the alliance he had been counting on, and it
seemed to augur badly for the whole African campaign about to begin.
