Rome's declaration of war changed less than Jugurtha had feared. The
consul Bestia, sent to prosecute it, took the king's bribes almost as
readily as the Senate had, and the officers he left in Numidia
followed his example without shame - some returning captured elephants
for a price, others selling back deserters, still others simply
plundering friendly territory. The corruption reached so far that a
tribune named Gaius Memmius, no friend to the nobility that had
profited from it, forced the Senate to summon Jugurtha to Rome under a
public guarantee of safety, hoping his own testimony would expose the
senators who had taken his money.

## A king silenced by his own bribe

Jugurtha came to Rome looking anything but royal, and immediately
bought a tribune of his own, Gaius Baebius, to shield him from the
crowd's fury. When Memmius brought him before a public assembly and
ordered him to name his accomplices, the mob shouted for him to be put
in chains - and Baebius simply commanded the king to stay silent,
facing down the crowd's rage with nothing but the shamelessness the
bribe had bought him. It worked. The assembly broke up mocked and
humiliated, and Jugurtha's confidence grew with every hour he remained
unpunished.

## A murder committed in the Senate's own city

A Numidian named Massiva, grandson of Massinissa through a different
line, was in Rome at the time, and a consul eager to conduct the war
persuaded him to petition the Senate for Numidia's throne on the
strength of his own royal blood - reasoning that Jugurtha, already
loaded with guilt and public hatred, could never withstand a rival
claim. Jugurtha answered the threat the only way he knew: he ordered
his most trusted attendant, Bomilcar, to have Massiva killed, secretly
if possible. The assassin was caught in the act. Bomilcar was put on
trial only through the personal insistence of the consul, since strict
law offered no standing against a man traveling under Rome's own
safe-conduct - and Jugurtha, seeing the case turn against him at last,
smuggled Bomilcar out of the city before a verdict could fall, and left
Italy himself soon after under Senate order.

## The line he could not resist

Passing out through the city gates, Jugurtha is said to have looked
back at Rome in silence more than once before speaking the words that
outlived every treaty he ever bought: "A city for sale, soon to fall if
once it find a buyer." The war he had spent so much silver avoiding
resumed in earnest behind him.
