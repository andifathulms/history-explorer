---
title: Three attempts, and the sermon at Mecca
drafted_from: petry-1998
phase: peak
---

The Ikhshidid state existed for thirty-four years and spent all of them doing
one thing, which was stopping the Fatimids.

The Isma'ili caliphate had taken Ifriqiya in 909 — this site's Fatimid and
Aghlabid pages — and Egypt was the obvious next object: the richest province in
the Islamic world, adjacent, and governed by a succession of weak Abbasid
appointees. Fatimid armies came east in 914 and again in 919, before the
Ikhshidids existed, and were beaten off with difficulty by the caliphate's own
forces. Muhammad ibn Tughj was granted the province in 935 substantially
because he was the officer who looked most likely to hold it, and a third
Fatimid attempt came in 936 and failed against him.

What that bought was thirty years and the argument that went with them.

The Ikhshidids also held the Hijaz, and this site has now recorded from several
directions how much that was worth. The Hafsid page has the sharif of Mecca
briefly acknowledging a caliph in Tunis in 1259 and the whole page turning on
it. The Fatimid page has holding the Hijaz as the thing that made Cairo's claim
more than territorial. The Ottoman page has Selim acquiring the guardianship in
1517 and a largely Christian empire becoming the first power of the Muslim
world.

For thirty years the Friday sermon at Mecca and Medina was pronounced in the
name of the Abbasid caliph, because an Ikhshidid governor in Egypt kept the road
open and paid for the grain that fed the holy cities — while a rival caliph in
Ifriqiya, who regarded that caliph as a usurper, could not get to them.

That is a strange thing for a small provincial dynasty to have been doing. The
Ikhshidids were not defending a doctrine; they were Sunni servants of a
caliphate that was by this date a Buyid pensioner, as this site's Abbasid and
Buyid pages describe, and they were personally as autonomous as the Tulunids
had been. What they were defending was an arrangement in which they were the
useful party, and it held exactly as long as they could feed Mecca and beat an
army in the delta.
