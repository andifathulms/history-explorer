Nectanebo I had been on the throne barely five years when Persia mounted
the most serious attempt yet to take the restored kingship back: an
invasion force of some two hundred thousand men and five hundred ships,
under the joint command of the Persian satrap Pharnabazus and the Athenian
mercenary general Iphicrates, hired specifically for the expertise no
Persian officer could supply against Egyptian defences.

## Around the wall, not through it

Pelusium, guarding the easternmost and most heavily fortified approach to
Egypt, held. Rather than force it, the invading army ferried its troops
further along the coast and forced a landing at the mouth of the Mendesian
branch of the Nile instead, taking the fortress there and opening a route
toward Memphis that Egypt's frontier defences had not been built to cover.

## An argument, and then a flood

The campaign stalled at exactly the point it should have succeeded.
Pharnabazus and Iphicrates disagreed over whether to press on toward
Memphis immediately, while Nectanebo was still scrambling reinforcements
into the capital, or wait for the rest of the invasion force to come up in
support — and while the two commanders argued, the season turned. The Nile
flood arrived, as it did every year, and turned the Delta the army was
standing in into open water and unusable ground. An invasion that had
broken through Egypt's strongest fortress withdrew without ever fighting
the decisive battle it had come for, beaten by a river on a schedule no
general's competence or incompetence had any control over.

## What the win actually proved

Nectanebo did not defeat this invasion by force of arms in any battle
worth naming. He survived it by holding Memphis long enough for the
country's own geography to do what his army alone might not have managed.
It is the same defence, in substance, that had protected Egypt from land
invasion for most of its history — an approach that turns to swamp on a
predictable annual cycle — succeeding one more time against an army large
and well-led enough that nothing else about the campaign suggests it
should have failed.
