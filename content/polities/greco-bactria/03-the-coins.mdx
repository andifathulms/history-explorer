---
title: A polity known from its money
drafted_from: mairs-2014
phase: aside
---

Almost every polity on this site is known from writing about it. This one is
known from its coinage, and the difference is worth a chapter because it changes
what the record can say.

The Greco-Bactrian coins are, by common consent, among the finest produced
anywhere in antiquity. The portraits are individual to the point of being
unflattering — Euthydemus as a heavy-jawed old man, Antimachus in a flat hat
with an expression of visible amusement, Eucratides in a plumed helmet — and
they are executed at a level of die-cutting that classical numismatists rank
with the best Sicilian work. Eucratides struck a twenty-stater gold piece of
about a hundred and seventy grams, the largest gold coin surviving from the
ancient world, which was found in the nineteenth century and is now lost again.

From that material a dynasty has been reconstructed: perhaps twenty kings, their
approximate order, their approximate dates, their titles, the gods they claimed,
the mints they controlled and roughly when they lost them. It is a remarkable
piece of scholarship and it is inference from objects.

This record's fields are shaped by that and say so. `succession_rule` is null
because the sequence itself is the argument. `legitimation` is coded from the
epithets on the reverse — Soter, Nikator, Theos, Aniketos — and from Zeus and
Herakles and Apollo, which is a thin basis for a claim about public
self-description and is the only basis there is. `reach_km2` is null and
`peak_population` is null, and no source consulted publishes either.

The site's Saba' record makes a related point from the opposite direction — a
kingdom known from tens of thousands of its own inscriptions and from no
narrative at all. Between the two of them they mark out what this corpus can and
cannot do with a polity that did not write history about itself, which is most
of the ancient world.

There is one more thing the coins show, and it is the beginning of the next
page. South of the Hindu Kush the same dynasty's issues change: the weight
standard becomes Indian, the shape becomes square, and the reverse legend is in
Prakrit written in Kharoshthi alongside the Greek. A currency is a public
document, and a bilingual one is a statement about who is expected to use it.
