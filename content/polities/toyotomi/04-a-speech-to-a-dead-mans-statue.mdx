Conquering Korea, and China beyond it, had been Hideyoshi's ambition
since boyhood. Serving under Nobunaga, he had once asked for a year's
revenue from Kyushu and a supply of weapons, offering to provide his
own ships and provisions and subdue Korea himself, then use a
conquered Korean army to take China and unite all three countries as
one. Nobunaga laughed at the proposal. Hideyoshi did not forget it.

## A conversation with a statue

While travelling through the Kantō region, Hideyoshi visited Kamakura
and stood before an effigy of Yoritomo preserved in a shrine there.
Rubbing and patting its back, he addressed the four-century-dead
founder of Japan's first shogunate directly: "You are my friend. You
took all the power under Heaven. You and I, only, have been able to do
this - but you were of a famous family, and not like me, sprung from
peasants. I intend, at last, to conquer all the earth, and even China.
What think you of that?" His own stated philosophy was blunter still:
the earth is the earth's earth, a doctrine that left him little
patience for anyone else's claim to land he wanted for himself.

## Demands dressed as diplomacy

With Ashikaga power long collapsed, Korea had stopped sending tribute
to Japan generations earlier, and Chinese emigrants brought Hideyoshi
word of disorganisation inside China itself that only sharpened his
appetite. He sent two embassies demanding Korea resume paying tribute,
the second one succeeding, and warned China's own emperor, through
intermediary tribute-bearers from the Ryūkyūs, that refusal to receive
his demands would bring invasion.

## An ambition the country could not actually sustain

The invasion Hideyoshi finally launched drained Japan's own resources
for years, devastated Korean towns and countryside without provocation
the Koreans themselves had given, and achieved none of the continental
conquest he had once described to a dead general's statue as his
destiny alongside Yoritomo's own. Chinese reinforcements eventually
forced the Japanese army into retreat, and only Hideyoshi's own death
in 1598 finally ended a war his country had never wanted and could not
sustain for his own ambition's sake.
