In 1586, Hideyoshi attained the rank of Kuambaku - imperial regent, the
highest civil office a subject of Japan could hold. It was an office
that, for centuries, no one outside the Fujiwara family had ever filled.
His enemies, unable to challenge the appointment itself, mocked it
instead, calling him Saru Kuanja - crowned monkey - reviving, in
contempt, the same nickname that had followed him since a childhood no
one had expected to end anywhere near the imperial court.

## A name granted rather than inherited

Because Hideyoshi held no family name that carried any weight at court,
the emperor granted him one by patent: Toyotomi, the name by which he
and his descendants would be known in history. Toyotomi Hideyoshi -
soldier, general, regent - now governed a country whose highest
civilian rank he held entirely on the strength of ability no inherited
title could have supplied.

## Retirement that changed nothing but the title

In 1591 Hideyoshi resigned the regency to his own son and took instead
the title of taikō - a style properly meaning any regent's father, but
applied so consistently to Hideyoshi alone that "the taikō," used
without further qualification, meant him and no one else in Japanese or
foreign accounts of the period. The resignation changed his formal rank
without loosening his actual grip on the country in the slightest;
Hideyoshi remained, in substance, the same ruler under a different
name, exactly as adaptable to circumstance as every earlier alias he
had answered to.

## A soldier's banner grown into an empire's

Hideyoshi's own battle standard told the story of his rise in a single
image: a single gourd at his first campaign, with another gourd added
after every subsequent victory, until the cluster became an imposing
sheaf carried aloft, in gilded replica, at the head of his marching
columns. Wherever that banner moved, contemporaries said, victory
followed it - a fitting emblem for a man who had built, gourd by gourd
and name by name, an authority no inherited claim could ever have
assembled as quickly.
