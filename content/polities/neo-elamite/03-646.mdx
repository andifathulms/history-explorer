Ashurbanipal took Susa in 646 and left an account of what he did to it. The
ziggurat was pulled down and its horns of shining bronze stripped. The gods and
goddesses of Elam, with their ornaments and their goods and their priests, were
carried off to Assyria. The royal tombs of Elam's kings, ancient and recent, were
opened and the bones taken to Assyria, so that their spirits would be denied
offerings and libations. The groves were cut down. Salt and thorny plants were
strewn on the ground. For a distance of a month and twenty-five days' journey the
fields were laid waste, and the noise of people, the tread of cattle and sheep and
the shouts of harvest song were banished.

Humban-haltash III, the last king this record can name, was hunted down in the
mountains, handed over, and taken to Nineveh, where he is shown pulling
Ashurbanipal's ceremonial chariot.

## How much of it to believe

Assyrian royal inscriptions are not neutral about their own thoroughness, and the
passage above is a literary set piece as much as a report — the banishing of the
sound of cattle and the harvest song is a formula. Excavation at Susa does not
show a site reduced to nothing; occupation continues, and Elamite kings are
attested afterwards.

What is not in doubt is the political result. Elam stopped being a state. The
country broke into small principalities whose sequence nobody has been able to
reconstruct, and this record stops there rather than following them, because a
span needs an end and a list of undatable local rulers is not a polity.

## What moved into the space

Within a century the region east of the Zagros was Persian, and this record's
single influence count is about how that happened.

Cyrus introduces himself, on the cylinder deposited in the wall of Babylon in
539, as king of Anshan, son of Cambyses king of Anshan, grandson of Cyrus king of
Anshan, descendant of Teispes king of Anshan. Four generations, and every one of
them styled with an Elamite royal title, in a document written in Akkadian for a
Mesopotamian audience — and no Persian title anywhere in the sequence.

Anshan is Tal-e Malyan in Fars. It had been one of the two halves of the Elamite
royal style, *king of Anshan and Susa*, for the better part of two thousand
years. What the cylinder records is that the family which produced the largest
empire the world had yet seen began as kings of an Elamite city, holding an
Elamite title, at a moment when Elam had just been destroyed by Assyria and the
title was available.

Coding rule 1 counts titulature publicly asserted, and this is the paradigm case.
It is counted once, on this record, pointing in.

## What else went west

The claim is the countable part and it is not the largest part.

The Achaemenid empire ran its administration in Elamite. The Persepolis
Fortification archive — tens of thousands of tablets recording the movement of
grain, wine, livestock and workers across the imperial heartland — is in Elamite,
written by scribes in a tradition descended from the one on this page, for a
Persian king. Elamite is one of the three languages of the Behistun inscription.
Susa became one of the Achaemenid capitals.

None of that is a successor claim, a religion carried or a descendant script, so
none of it appears in a coded field anywhere on this site. It is the single
clearest case in this corpus of a polity's most durable effect being invisible to
every instrument the corpus has, and the honest response is not to build a new
instrument for it but to say so in a chapter and let the counts stand at what they
measure.
