Ashurbanipal took Susa in 646 and left an account of what he did to it. The
ziggurat was pulled down and its horns of shining bronze stripped. The gods and
goddesses of Elam, with their ornaments and their goods and their priests, were
carried off to Assyria. The royal tombs of Elam's kings, ancient and recent,
were opened and the bones taken to Assyria, so that their spirits would be
denied offerings and libations. The groves were cut down. Salt and thorny
plants were strewn on the ground. For a distance of a month and twenty-five
days' journey the fields were laid waste, and the noise of people, the tread of
cattle and sheep and the shouts of harvest song were banished.

Humban-haltash III, the last Elamite king anybody can name, was hunted down in
the mountains, handed over, and taken to Nineveh, where he is shown pulling
Ashurbanipal's ceremonial chariot.

## How much of it to believe

Assyrian royal inscriptions are not neutral about their own thoroughness, and
the passage above is a literary set piece as much as a report — the banishing
of the sound of cattle and the harvest song is a formula. Excavation at Susa
does not show a site reduced to nothing; occupation continues, and Elamite
kings are attested afterwards.

What is not in doubt is the political result. Elam stopped being a state. The
country broke into small principalities whose sequence nobody has been able to
reconstruct. There are local rulers afterwards and no way to date them, which
is a different thing from a kingdom.

## What moved into the space

Within a century the region east of the Zagros was Persian, and the way that
happened is worth following.

Cyrus introduces himself, on the cylinder deposited in the wall of Babylon in
539, as king of Anshan, son of Cambyses king of Anshan, grandson of Cyrus king
of Anshan, descendant of Teispes king of Anshan. Four generations, and every
one of them styled with an Elamite royal title, in a document written in
Akkadian for a Mesopotamian audience — and no Persian title anywhere in the
sequence.

Anshan is Tal-e Malyan in Fars. It had been one of the two halves of the
Elamite royal style, *king of Anshan and Susa*, for the better part of two
thousand years. What the cylinder records is that the family which produced the
largest empire the world had yet seen began as kings of an Elamite city,
holding an Elamite title, at a moment when Elam had just been destroyed by
Assyria and the title was available.

It is as clear a case as exists of a new power dressing itself in the titles of
one that had just been destroyed, in front of everybody, because they were
available.

## What else went west

The claim is the countable part and it is not the largest part.

The Achaemenid empire ran its administration in Elamite. The Persepolis
Fortification archive — tens of thousands of tablets recording the movement of
grain, wine, livestock and workers across the imperial heartland — is in
Elamite, written by scribes in an Elamite tradition, for a Persian king.
Elamite is one of the three languages of the Behistun inscription. Susa became
one of the Achaemenid capitals.

None of that is a conquest, a religion spread or a script invented, and none of
it would show up in any tally of what a state exported. It is still the most
durable thing Elam ever did: a kingdom that Assyria set out to erase supplied
the next empire with its administrative language, its scribes and one of its
capitals.
