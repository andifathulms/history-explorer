Islam had been present on the coasts of South Sulawesi for generations before
Gowa ever took it up. Malay and other Muslim traders had used Makassar's own
port for decades, and the neighbouring Bugis states had had their own contact
with Muslim missionaries and merchants long before 1605. What made Gowa's
conversion that year different was not that it happened first, but what
happened immediately after: the ruler of Tallo converted, took the title
Sultan Alauddin, and Gowa followed him into the new religion as a single
political act rather than a private one.

## A conversion that came with an army behind it

Elsewhere in the archipelago, Islam had spread the way it had at Melaka and
Pasai - through marriage, trade and the slow drift of a court's own example.
Makassar's neighbours did not get that choice. Over the following six years
Gowa turned its own conversion into a campaign, bringing the interior Bugis
kingdoms of the peninsula into Islam one by one by force rather than waiting
for them to arrive at it on their own terms.

## Why the Bugis kingdoms resisted, and what breaking them meant

The three main targets were not politically insignificant: Bone, Soppeng and
Wajo were established kingdoms in their own right, with their own courts and
their own reasons to resent a rising coastal neighbour's demand that they
adopt its religion on its schedule. Their resistance made the campaign a real
war rather than a formality, and their eventual submission by 1611 left Gowa
the unrivalled dominant power of the whole South Sulawesi peninsula, not
merely of its own coastline.

## What the conquest planted

The victory was not permanent in the way it looked at the time. Bone in
particular never fully accepted the subordination the Islamic wars had
imposed on it, and the resentment the campaign left behind among the Bugis
kingdoms it had forced into submission would resurface two generations later,
when a Bugis prince of Bone found Dutch company allies willing to help him
settle the account Gowa's own religious wars had opened.
