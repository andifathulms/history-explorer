The wall of Sombaopu was made of fired brick, and at the base it was something
over three metres thick. The Dutch could not breach it with ship's guns and
took it in 1669 by mining.

There was a line of them: Sombaopu, Ujung Pandang, Panakkukang, Barombong and
others, strung along a few kilometres of coast, with the ruler's residence
inside the largest. Makassar had cannon of its own, cast locally and bought
from the Portuguese community, and Karaeng Pattingalloang — who ordered a
telescope from the Netherlands — collected European treatises on fortification.

## What that means about the sixteenth century

It means the polities of the archipelago were adopting European military
technology about as fast as it arrived, and that the outcome of the wars is not
explained by a technology gap.

Aceh sent an embassy to Istanbul in the 1560s and came back with cannon,
founders who could cast more, and gunners. Patani cast its own artillery, and
the pieces were famous enough that its chronicle names them individually. Demak
built ships in 1513 that Portuguese accounts describe as larger and
better-planked than their own.

What the Dutch company had was not better guns. It was capital, a permanent
organisation that outlived its own personnel, the ability to blockade for years
on end, and — in nearly every case in these waters — a local ally with a
grievance of its own.

## And what a fort is for

Sombaopu was also a customs house, a warehouse district and the ruler's
residence. Fortifying a trading capital is what a polity does when its revenue
is its port and its port can be reached by anybody.

The alternative is instructive. Kilwa, on the East African coast, had no army
in its sources at all and nothing in the excavated town designed to be defended
— a palace, a mosque, warehouses. For five hundred years it did not need any,
because an enemy arriving by sea still had to stop somewhere. Then the
Portuguese arrived, who did not, and took it in a morning.

Makassar built four metres of brick and had to be mined out over months. Both
were taken. The walls bought time and did not change the answer.
