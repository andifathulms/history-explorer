The Dutch East India Company's whole strategy in the eastern archipelago was to
be the only buyer. This corpus's Ternate record describes the method at the
production end: clove trees cut down and burned everywhere except the islands the
company chose, by expeditions the company made the sultan of Ternate supply.

Makassar was the hole in it.

The port on the south-west arm of Sulawesi took a toll and asked no questions.
Portuguese expelled from Melaka went there and formed a community of several
thousand. The English had a factory. So did the Danes. Chinese, Gujarati, Malay,
Javanese and Bugis shipping called, and what they bought was largely cloves and
nutmeg from Maluku that had reached Makassar in small boats through a coastline
of islands nobody could patrol.

The company complained about this for fifty years, blockaded it, and eventually
destroyed it.

## Why it could be there

`revenue_basis` on this record reads `trade-toll` and `land-tax`, and the second
value is why the first was possible.

The south-west arm of Sulawesi grows rice. A free port needs to feed the people
who come to it and the people who run it, and a polity that has to import its
food cannot afford to annoy the power that controls the sea — which is the
position this corpus's Melaka record is in, coded `trade-toll` alone, buying its
rice from Java. Makassar had a hinterland. It could take a position and hold it.

## The sea belongs to nobody

The position is on the record because a Dutch envoy wrote it down. Told that the
company had a right to exclude others from the Moluccas trade, the Makassar court
answered that God had made the earth and the sea, had divided the earth among
mankind and given the sea in common, and that it had never been heard that
anyone should be forbidden to sail it.

That is 1615 or thereabouts. Grotius had published *Mare Liberum* in Latin in
Leiden six years earlier, arguing the freedom of the seas against the Portuguese,
on behalf of the same Dutch company — which by the time it reached Sulawesi was
arguing the opposite.

This site has no field for a doctrine. What it has is a `revenue_basis` of
`trade-toll` that meant something different here from what it meant at Melaka or
Srivijaya, where the toll was the price of protection on a route the polity
controlled. At Makassar it was the price of admission to a market that was open
on principle, and the principle was stated in writing to the people who came to
close it.
