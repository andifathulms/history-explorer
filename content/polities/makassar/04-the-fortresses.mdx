The wall of Sombaopu was made of fired brick, and at the base it was something
over three metres thick. The Dutch could not breach it with ship's guns and took
it in 1669 by mining.

There was a line of them: Sombaopu, Ujung Pandang, Panakkukang, Barombong and
others, strung along a few kilometres of coast, with the ruler's residence inside
the largest. Makassar had cannon of its own, cast locally and bought from the
Portuguese community, and Karaeng Pattingalloang — who ordered a telescope from
the Netherlands — collected European treatises on fortification.

## What that means about the sixteenth century

It means the polities of the archipelago were adopting European military
technology about as fast as it arrived, and that the outcome of the wars is not explained by
a technology gap.

This corpus's Aceh record's third chapter is about an embassy to Istanbul in the
1560s that came back with cannon, founders who could cast more, and gunners. This
corpus's Patani record cast its own artillery, and the pieces were famous enough
that the chronicle names them. Demak built ships in 1513 that Portuguese accounts
describe as larger and better-planked than their own.

What the Dutch company had was not better guns. It was capital, a permanent
organisation that outlived its personnel, the ability to blockade for years, and
— in every one of the four cases the archipelago records — a local ally with its
own grievance.

## And what a fort is for

Sombaopu was also a customs house and a warehouse district and the residence.
The fortification of a trading capital is what a polity does when its revenue is
its port and its port can be reached by anyone: this corpus's Kilwa record, one
region over, is `military_basis: null` with a chapter observing that the excavated
town has a palace, a mosque, warehouses and nothing designed to be defended — and
that for five hundred years it did not need any, until somebody arrived who did
not need to stop.

Makassar built the walls. Kilwa did not. Both were taken, one in a morning and
one after a siege that had to mine four metres of brick, and the difference in
outcome between them is a matter of degree rather than of kind.
