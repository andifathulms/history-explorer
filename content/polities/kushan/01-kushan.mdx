title: The junction

The Kushans came out of the Yuezhi, a Central Asian people displaced westward by
the Xiongnu on this site's inner-asia page, and by the second century AD they
held Bactria, Gandhara and much of northern India — precisely the junction where
the routes from China, Iran and India met.

Everything about them reflects that position. Their coins carry Greek letters
writing the Iranian Bactrian language, and deities from the Greek, Iranian, Hindu
and Buddhist pantheons on the reverse, sometimes across a single reign. Kanishka
patronised Buddhism and is remembered in Buddhist tradition as a council-convening
sponsor comparable to Ashoka.

The consequence counted on this page is the transmission. Mahayana Buddhism moved
out of India into Central Asia and from there along the oasis routes into China
under Kushan rule and through Kushan territory, which is why there are Buddhist
cave complexes in the Tarim basin and why the religion reached the Han court. The
Gandharan sculptural tradition, Buddhist subject matter in Greek-derived form, is
the visible residue of the same junction.

The empire was broken by the Sasanians in the third century, which is the one
point where this site's Iranian and Indian regions touch directly.
