Every other Italian city republic lost its constitution to a family. Milan to the
Visconti, Florence to the Medici, Verona to the Scaligeri, Ferrara to the Este,
Rimini to the Malatesta, and so on across the peninsula, usually within two or
three generations of the commune's founding. Venice did not, for five hundred
years, and the machinery that prevented it is this record's `succession_rule`
field.

## How a doge was chosen

The Great Council assembled. Thirty members were chosen by lot. Those thirty were
reduced by lot to nine. The nine elected forty. The forty were reduced by lot to
twelve. The twelve elected twenty-five. The twenty-five were reduced by lot to
nine. The nine elected forty-five. The forty-five were reduced by lot to eleven.
The eleven elected forty-one. The forty-one elected the doge, with a
twenty-five-vote threshold.

Ten rounds, alternating lot and ballot. Nobody could know in advance who would be
voting at the stage that mattered, which is the entire design: a faction can buy
a council and cannot buy a lottery.

This site codes `election` on a number of records and this is what the value
looks like when it is a documented constitutional procedure rather than an
acclamation or a decree of an assembly. The Hasmonean record's `election` comes
from a decree engraved on bronze that establishes a hereditary office. This one
comes from a manual.

## And what he was allowed to do

Very little, and it was written down and got shorter.

The *promissione ducale* was an oath of limitations drafted afresh for each new
doge, read aloud at his coronation, and revised by a committee appointed to find
the previous holder's abuses. Over centuries it accumulated: the doge could not
meet a foreign ambassador alone, could not open his own official correspondence,
could not leave the city without permission, could not hold other office, could
not have his family in trade with the state, could not accept gifts beyond a
listed few. His sons were barred from certain offices.

He was elected for life and could not resign without consent, which made the
office a splendid captivity. One doge who overreached — Marino Faliero, in 1355,
who conspired to make himself prince — was beheaded on the palace stairs, and his
portrait in the council chamber is a painted black cloth with an inscription
saying this is the place of Marino Faliero, beheaded for his crimes.

## The Serrata

The other half of the design is the third turning point on this record and it is
the part that is hard to admire.

In 1297 the Great Council closed. Membership was restricted to men whose families
had already sat in it, and the qualifying families were registered in a book. The
patriciate became a hereditary caste of a few thousand people governing a city of
a hundred thousand and an empire of considerably more, and it stayed that way
until 1797.

It worked, in the narrow sense that it answered permanently the question that
destroyed every other Italian republic — who is allowed in — by answering it
once. It is also the reason the constitution is a curiosity rather than an
ancestor. Venice invented no doctrine of consent, extended nothing to anybody,
and closed rather than opened. What it built was a machine for preventing a
tyrant, run by an oligarchy that had made itself permanent.
