Venice sits on mud. The lagoon is a shallow brackish basin behind a barrier of
sandbars, with no stone, no timber, no arable ground and no fresh water beyond
what falls on it. Every building in the city stands on piles driven into silt,
and every one of those piles came from somewhere else.

That is the whole of the polity's economic constitution and it is worth putting
first, because it explains the coded fields. A city with no hinterland cannot
tax agriculture, so this record's `revenue_basis` reads `trade-toll, monopoly`
rather than the `land-tax` that leads nearly every other page on this site. It
cannot feed itself, so it must trade. And it cannot be taken by cavalry, which
is the other half of the story.

## Fish and salt

The first Venetian export was salt, and for four hundred years it was the largest
item in the accounts.

Salt was made by evaporating lagoon water in pans, and the same conditions that
made the site useless for farming made it excellent for that. What turned a
commodity into a monopoly was policy: from the twelfth century Venice
systematically bought out, undercut or destroyed rival production around the
Adriatic, and then sold to a mainland with no alternative source and an absolute
need. Cargoes coming into Venice were required to carry salt on the return leg,
subsidised, which is a state using a monopoly to underwrite the freight rates of
everything else.

It is the least romantic fact about the republic and it funded the beginning of
everything in the next four chapters.

## Byzantine, and then not

Venice was a Byzantine province. The lagoon settlements were part of the
exarchate of Ravenna; their dukes were imperial officials with Greek court
titles, appointed and confirmed from Constantinople, and this site records the
relationship as an edge — `byzantine -> venice`, type `seceded from`, dated 812
and marked contested.

The contested flag is doing real work. Nobody seceded. There is no declaration,
no war and no year in which Venice announced anything. What happened is that the
dukes started choosing themselves, then minting, then treating with the Franks
directly, while continuing to accept Byzantine titles and to describe themselves
as the emperor's servants for another two hundred years. The 812 date is the Pax
Nicephori, in which Byzantium and Charlemagne's empire settled whose the lagoon
was — a treaty between two other powers about a third.

The habit stuck. Venice went on being formally deferential to Constantinople
long after it had stopped obeying it, which was useful, and the arrangement in
this record's first turning point is what it was eventually cashed in for.

## Saint Mark

In 828 two Venetian merchants removed the body of Saint Mark from Alexandria and
brought it home, and the standard account has them concealing it under a layer
of pork in the expectation that Muslim inspectors would not search further.

This record's `legitimation` field carries `divine-sanction` beside `election`
because of it, and the reason is political rather than devotional. A city with no
Roman past, no imperial charter, no founding saint and no bishop of any
antiquity had nothing to answer a claim with. An apostle changes that. The basilica
was built to house him, the doge's chapel was the apostle's church rather than
the cathedral, and the republic's name for itself in its own documents was often
simply the commune of Saint Mark.

The lion is on every building Venice ever held, from Bergamo to Crete, which is
the other reason to mention it: it is how you can still tell, on the ground,
where this record's second turning point reached.
