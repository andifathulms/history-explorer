---
title: Taking Khurasan, and asking afterwards
drafted_from: iranica-saffarids
phase: expansion
---

Between 867 and 873 Ya'qub took Herat, Kirman, Fars and finally Nishapur,
ending the Tahirids. He sent the captured Tahirid governor to the caliph along
with, in effect, a demand that the conquest be recognised.

Baghdad's position through all of this is the interesting one. The caliph
al-Mu'tamid alternated between granting Ya'qub governorships he already held
and publicly cursing him, depending on how close he was. In 876 Ya'qub marched
on Iraq itself and was stopped at Dayr al-Aqul, fifty miles from Baghdad, by
the caliph's brother al-Muwaffaq. He withdrew and died three years later,
still, formally, a rebel.

He had held Khurasan for six years without ever holding a valid title to it,
and had come within fifty miles of taking the institution that issued titles.
His brother, in the next chapter, tried the opposite experiment.
