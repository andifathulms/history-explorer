After 901 the Saffarids stopped being a claimant to Khurasan and became what
they had started as: the power in Sistan. They stayed that, on and off, for a
further century, under Samanid and then Ghaznavid overlordship, as the maliks
of Nimruz.

This is why the end date on this page is a range rather than a year, and why
the range is unusually wide. Bosworth follows the family to 1003, when Mahmud
of Ghazna deposed Khalaf ibn Ahmad; the imperial Saffarid state, the thing that
took Nishapur and frightened Baghdad, was finished in 901. Both are true
statements about different objects. The site keeps both dates rather than
picking the one that makes a tidier bar.

The Saffarids are also the polity where this site's rating panel is emptiest.
There is no cited peak extent, no cited peak population, and no source in the
consulted set that addresses whether anyone later claimed their legitimacy.
Three of the four axes render as gaps.

That is not an accident of this project's coverage. Ya'qub's state was brief,
hostile to the caliphate that produced most of the surviving chronicles, and
based in a region that quantitative historical work has largely passed over.
Which polities scholarship has bothered to measure is itself a fact about
scholarship, and the empty bars are the most honest way to show it.
