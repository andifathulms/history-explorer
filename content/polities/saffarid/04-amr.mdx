---
title: The brother who took the paper
drafted_from: bosworth-1994
phase: peak
---

Ya'qub died in 879 having never accepted an appointment from anyone. His
brother Amr, who succeeded him, accepted one within months, and held the
Saffarid state for twenty-two years — longer than Ya'qub held it, over more
ground, with less trouble.

The reversal is the whole interest of the reign. Amr took investiture as
governor of Khurasan, had his name and the caliph's on the coinage in the
correct order, sent the tribute, and received in return the thing Ya'qub had
been offered a sword and a plate of onions to refuse. It worked. Baghdad, which
had cursed Ya'qub from the pulpit, dealt with Amr as a legitimate governor for
two decades, and the province was quieter under him than it had been under
either of them.

He also governed, which Ya'qub had largely not had time to. The sources
describe an administration in Sistan and Khurasan with a functioning fisc,
attention to the irrigation the Helmand basin lives on, and a reputation for
paying troops on time that is worth more in this corpus than it sounds — the
Samanid page two centuries of history later is about what happens when that
stops.

Then he made one mistake and it was terminal. In 900 he moved north against the
Samanids, who held Transoxiana and had just been granted Khurasan by a caliph
who had granted it to Amr first. He was beaten at Balkh, captured, and sent to
Baghdad, where he was executed in 902.

What the caliph did next is the sentence the Saffarid page exists for.
Al-Mu'tadid transferred the governorship of Khurasan to Isma'il the Samanid in
the same year, on the same terms, with the same wording. The investiture Amr
had reorganised his whole political life to obtain turned out to be
transferable to whoever won the next battle, which is exactly what his brother
had said it was worth.

The `legitimation` field on this page carries two values, conquest and
caliphal-investiture, and it is the only page in this corpus that carries the
first. The two brothers are the reason: one ran the sequence backwards on
purpose, the other put it back the usual way round, and the province ended up
in the same place either way.
