In the 970s a Fatimid envoy called Ibn Selim al-Aswani went up the Nile past
Makuria to the kingdom beyond it, and wrote a description. Alodia, he says, is
larger than Makuria, its king more powerful, its territory more extensive, and
its capital at Soba has fine houses, churches with much gold, and gardens. He
gives the number of churches as four hundred.

That paragraph is a substantial fraction of everything known about this kingdom.

## The emptiest block on the site

This record's four institution fields are all null, which puts it alongside this
corpus's Median record as the least-coded page here — and the two are empty for
opposite reasons.

The Median record is null because it is genuinely disputed whether there was a
Median state to have institutions. Nobody disputes that Alodia existed. There are
churches excavated at Soba, Greek and Old Nubian inscriptions, imported
ceramics, a royal seal, and an envoy's account of a functioning court.

What there is not is any evidence about how it worked. Not what the army rested
on, not where the revenue came from, not who inherited, not on what public
ground. So the fields stay empty.

The temptation on a page like this is to borrow from the neighbour. Makuria, a
few hundred kilometres downstream, shares the religion, the script, the
architecture and probably a great deal else, and this corpus codes three of its
four institution fields. Coding rule 7's standing requirement is that the answer
is null or a value argued on the merits and never the nearest familiar label, and
the nearest familiar label here is another kingdom's page.

## Soba

The capital is under the eastern suburbs of Khartoum.

Parts have been excavated — three churches, a large building that may be a
palace, cemeteries, a great deal of pottery — and a good deal of the rest is
built on. The site was mined for bricks for centuries.

That is the ordinary situation for a medieval African capital that happens to sit
where a modern city grew, and it is worth stating because the alternative
explanation for how little is known is the one a reader may reach for. It is not
that nobody has looked. It is that the looking has to be done between houses.

## Where it sits on this river

This corpus now carries four polities on the middle Nile.

Kush, from the eighth century BC, ruling Egypt and then Meroë, writing in a
script that can be read and not understood. Then three centuries nobody can fill.
Then Makuria and this kingdom, Christian, from the sixth century — Makuria
holding the frontier with Egypt by treaty for six hundred years, this one
upstream and reportedly richer. Then the Funj, who are not here.

Four records on one river, two of them mostly silent, and a fifth that ought to
be here and is not.
