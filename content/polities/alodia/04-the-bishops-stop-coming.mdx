The traditional date for the fall of Soba is 1504, to a coalition of the Funj and
the Abdallab Arabs, and the Funj sultanate of Sennar took the country.

The date comes from later Sudanese chronicle traditions rather than from anything
contemporary, and the process it stands for was longer than a year. Arab groups
had been moving into the Gezira for generations; the church was collapsing for
the same reason it collapsed in Makuria, because Nubian bishops were consecrated
in Alexandria and Alexandria under Mamluk rule could not supply them; and there
are indications the kingdom had already fragmented before the Funj arrived.

## Nine hundred years

Christian Nubia lasted from the sixth century to the sixteenth. That is longer
than the interval between the fall of Rome and the Norman conquest, and it
happened on a river a thousand kilometres up from a Muslim Egypt with which it
was, for most of that time, formally at peace.

The middle kingdom fell in 1365 and this one in 1504, a hundred and thirty-nine
years later. With Soba the last Christian state on the Nile above
Egypt disappears, and the Christian tradition on this river ends after nine
hundred years — not conquered in a war anybody records, but ground down by a
demographic change, a bishop shortage and a sequence of raids.

## What actually killed it

Not a war. Two slow things and a raid at the end of them.

The first was the church. Nubian bishops were consecrated at Alexandria, and by
the fourteenth century the Coptic patriarchate under Mamluk rule was in no
position to keep supplying sees a thousand miles up a river in a country it
had no leverage in. Sees went unfilled. A church with no bishops ordains no
priests; a church with no priests has, at most, two generations left in it.

The second was demographic. Arab groups had been moving into the Gezira — the
land between the Blue and White Niles — for generations, by marriage and pasture
and settlement rather than by conquest, and Nubian inheritance ran through the
mother's line, which meant that the children of Arab fathers and Nubian mothers
inherited from their Nubian uncles while identifying with their fathers. That
arrangement transfers a country in about three generations and nobody has to
fight for it.

By the time anybody arrived to take Soba there may not have been much of a
kingdom to take.
