In 1813 and 1814 a Swiss traveller calling himself Ibrahim ibn Abdallah went up
the Nile on very little money, dressed as a poor Syrian trader, writing down the
stages and the prices. Three hundred years after Soba fell, John Lewis Burckhardt
recorded what had replaced the Christian kingdoms, and he recorded it without any
sense that something had been replaced.

## One sultanate, the length of the river

At Shendy, well up the river and in what had been Alodian country, he notes the
arrangement in a sentence. The local king *is tributary to the King of Sennaar,
as are likewise all the petty rulers down the river as far as Dongola.*

That is the whole political geography of the middle Nile in 1814: one Muslim
sultanate, the Funj of Sennar, holding from the Ethiopian foothills to the
Nubian bend — ground that had carried three Christian kingdoms for nine hundred
years, now held by one Muslim one for three.

He describes the traffic on it as ordinary and busy. Caravans from Berber
reaching Upper Egypt almost every month; Shendy as the principal market for
slave traders out of Egypt, Darfur, Kordofan and Sennar; the roads secure enough
that small parties travelled them. The Funj had made the river work commercially
in a way that would have been recognisable to anyone who had used it under
Alodia, doing the same things, in a different religion, with the same geography.

Muhammad Ali's Egyptian army took the sultanate seven years after Burckhardt
passed through.

## What it had absorbed

The kingdom did not leave a remnant population identifiable as its own.

The Christian communities of the Gezira did not survive as a minority the way
the Copts of Egypt did. There is no Alodian church in exile, no diaspora, no
liturgy preserved somewhere else, and Old Nubian went out of use in that part of
the river altogether. What happened instead is what had been happening for two
centuries before Soba fell: intermarriage, in a society where descent ran through
the mother, until the grandchildren of the kingdom's own people were Muslims with
Arab genealogies.

Burckhardt met their descendants everywhere and did not know it, because there
was nothing left for them to tell him.

## And Soba

The capital is under the eastern suburbs of Khartoum.

Its ruins were quarried, for centuries, by people who needed fired brick and had
a supply of it lying in a field. Then the modern city grew out over the site.

Which is the last thing the kingdom did: it was taken apart and used, by
everybody who came afterwards, until there was not enough left above ground for
anyone to wonder what it had been.

The excavations that have been possible found three churches, a probable palace,
granite columns, painted plaster, window glass, a bronze hanging lamp, an
inscribed bishop's tombstone and a great deal of imported pottery — which is
enough to confirm that a Fatimid envoy in the 970s was describing something real,
and not enough to say what.
