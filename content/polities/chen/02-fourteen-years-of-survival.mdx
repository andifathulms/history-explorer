---
title: Fourteen years of survival
drafted_from: boulger-china-1881
phase: peak
---

Xuandi gave the Chen their longest reign, fourteen years of continued
war against the northern kingdom of Zhou, the state that had emerged
from the final collapse of the old Northern Wei. It was, even by
Boulger's own account, a period of survival rather than achievement -
the dynasty held its own without ever meaningfully expanding it, one
ruler among several small, contending states in an age that had, by
this point, been fractured for more than a century and a half. It was
the fullest reign any Chen ruler would get, and the closest this
dynasty came to the kind of peak era its four predecessors on the same
throne had each, in their own troubled way, briefly managed. The
throne passed on Xuandi's death in 582 to his son Houzhu, whose own
short reign would close out both his family's rule and the entire era
of division the Chen's four predecessor dynasties had shared with the
rival kingdoms of the north.
