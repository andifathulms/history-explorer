Haakon Haakonsson, Sverre's own grandson, was proclaimed king at the
Øre-thing in 1217 while still a child, guarded night and day by
Birchleg loyalists against the intrigues of Earl Skule, who coveted
the throne for himself and worked against Haakon for years through
allies in the church. Norway's long civil war did not end
immediately - rebel bands kept rising under one impostor after
another claiming descent from King Magnus Erlingsson, each drawing
enough restless, war-habituated men to sustain a few more years of
plunder dressed up as rebellion - but Haakon's patient policy of
conciliation, absorbing defeated rebel factions rather than
exterminating them, gradually starved the civil wars of new
recruits. What emerged from nearly a century of intermittent
fighting, under a king remembered less for brilliance than for what
Boyesen calls strong common sense, generosity and a clear-sighted
instinct for choosing the right course, was the most stable and
internationally respected Norwegian monarchy the country had yet
produced.

## A fleet that won without fighting

Haakon's reputation abroad rested chiefly on his navy. When war broke
out with Denmark in 1256, the mere sight of the Norwegian fleet was
enough to bring King Christopher to terms without a single battle
being fought. Pope Alexander IV pressed Haakon to join a crusade, King
Louis IX of France offered him command of a combined Norse-French
fleet in recognition of his naval experience, and in 1256 the Pope is
said to have put Haakon forward as his own candidate for Holy Roman
Emperor. Closer to home, the reach of his authority grew more
concretely still: Iceland, worn down by generations of internal
feuding among its own great families, formally acknowledged his
overlordship and agreed to pay him tribute in 1261, and the scattered
settlements of Greenland recognized his sovereignty as well - bringing
both North Atlantic territories under the Norwegian crown for the
first time in the country's history, a reach no earlier Norwegian
king, not even Magnus the Good ruling Denmark and Norway together,
had achieved simultaneously with such lasting effect.
