For most of the twentieth century the standard account of this polity's ending
was that the Almoravids took Kumbi Saleh in 1076, forced the kingdom to Islam,
and broke it — and that everything afterwards is a decline into the arms of Mali.

It probably did not happen.

## What the account rested on

One late source, and a habit of reading.

The claim traces to Ibn Khaldun, writing three hundred years after the event, and
to a reading of earlier notices that does not survive being looked at closely.
Al-Bakri, writing in 1068 — eight years before the supposed conquest — describes
a functioning kingdom. The contemporary Almoravid material does not claim the
conquest. Archaeology at Kumbi Saleh has produced no destruction layer of the
right date.

What the earlier evidence supports is something less dramatic and more
interesting: pressure, alliance, commercial competition, and a conversion of the
Ghanaian ruling house to Islam somewhere in this period that appears to have been
negotiated rather than imposed. The relationship between the Almoravids and Ghana
looks, on the surviving evidence, like two powers with a shared interest in the
same trade routes rather than a holy war.

## Why it is still worth naming

Because the non-event shaped the field for a century.

An Almoravid conquest of Ghana in 1076 is in a great many books. It supplied a
very tidy causal story — Berber jihad destroys pagan African empire, Mali picks
up the pieces — and the tidiness is a good part of why it lasted so long. Nobody
had to explain how an empire ends without a conquest, because a conquest had been
supplied.

The dismantling is largely the work of the 1970s and after, and Levtzion is one
of the people who did it.

What the story replaced is the more interesting account, and it is the one this
kingdom actually needs: a state built on a position in a trade, in a country
where the rains were shifting, losing its grip on its tributaries over about a
century, while a nomadic power with the same commercial interests grew stronger
to the north and a new one grew stronger to the south.
