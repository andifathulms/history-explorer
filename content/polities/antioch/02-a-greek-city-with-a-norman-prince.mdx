Antioch was not Edessa. Where the county was a thin Frankish crust over an
Armenian and Syriac countryside, the principality's capital was one of the great
cities of the eastern Mediterranean — a patriarchal see, a Greek city with Greek
institutions, walls running for miles over a mountain, and a working port at St
Symeon at the mouth of the Orontes.

That is why this record codes `land-tax` and `trade-toll` where Edessa carries
land alone. It is also why the principality's central internal quarrel was
ecclesiastical. Antioch had a Greek patriarch. The Latins installed their own
and spent a century in a dispute that was never merely about liturgy, because
the return of the Greek patriarch was one of the standing imperial demands and
conceding it meant conceding the larger claim. The `administration` coding on
this page carries Greek and Armenian beside Latin for the same reason it does on
the Edessa page, and with more force: these were not tolerated minorities but
the people who ran the city.

The principality's own expansion ran east towards Aleppo and north into Cilicia,
and it ended abruptly. In June 1119 Roger of Salerno took the field against
Ilghazi, the Artuqid lord of Mardin and Aleppo, without waiting for the king of
Jerusalem, and his army was annihilated in a morning at a place the Franks
afterwards called the Field of Blood. Roger was killed. The knighthood of the
principality was destroyed as a body.

Two things follow, and the second is why this chapter ends here rather than
later. Antioch never expanded again on its own account: from 1119 its eastern
frontier stopped moving and it was governed for long stretches by regents sent
from Jerusalem. And the man who destroyed it has a page in this corpus — the
Artuqid record, filed in this same region, which currently stands in the thread
with no edge at either end. The two polities met once, decisively, and the
meeting produced no succession, so there is no edge and there should not be one.
The relation is a fact about a battle. This site puts those in chapters.
