---
title: The broken oath
drafted_from: asbridge-2000
phase: formation
---

Before the First Crusade was allowed through Byzantine territory, its leaders
swore an oath at Constantinople: any former imperial city they recovered would
be handed back to Alexios Komnenos. Antioch was a former imperial city. It had
been Byzantine until 1084, fourteen years earlier, and there were men in the
crusading camp who had been alive when it was.

The siege lasted from October 1097 to June 1098 and was appalling — famine
inside the besiegers' lines, desertions, and then the city taken by treachery
through an Armenian tower-captain days before a relief army arrived, so that the
crusaders spent the next month besieged in the place they had just captured.
Bohemond of Taranto, a Norman from southern Italy and the son of the man who had
spent his career fighting the Byzantines in the Adriatic, organised the entry
and the defence.

He then kept the city, and the oath was the first casualty. His argument was
that Alexios had failed to bring the army the promised support and had forfeited
the claim; the emperor's was that a sworn undertaking is a sworn undertaking.
Both positions were arguable and neither was going to move, and the result was
that the second Crusader state came into existence in a state of unresolved
dispute with the greatest Christian power in the region, which is a bad way to
found anything.

The chronology is worth marking as it was on the Edessa page. Antioch fell in
June 1098 and was a functioning principality with a frontier and a chancery
inside two years. Jerusalem was not taken until July 1099. Two of the four
Frankish states existed before the crusade reached its objective, and the two
that mattered most militarily for the next century were both in the north.

What the quarrel produced is this record's oddest field. The `legitimation`
coding is `conquest` alone — the princes held Antioch because they had taken it
— and yet a Byzantine edge runs into this page typing the principality as a
`vassal of` the empire. Those are not contradictory. They are the two parties'
positions, and the site records the one the princes asserted in the coded field
and the one the emperors asserted, and twice enforced, in the edge.
