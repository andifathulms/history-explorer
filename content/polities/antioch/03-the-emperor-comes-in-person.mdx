title: The emperor comes in person

The imperial claim on Antioch was not a grievance that faded. It was pursued for
sixty years, in writing and then with armies, by three emperors in succession,
and it is the reason this page carries the only edge in this corpus that makes a
Frankish state somebody's vassal.

The document was the treaty of Devol in 1108. Bohemond had gone back to Europe,
raised a war against the empire, invaded the Balkans and been comprehensively
beaten, and the terms Alexios imposed had him hold Antioch as a liegeman of the
emperor for life, return the Greek patriarch, and pass the city to the empire
afterwards. Bohemond signed and never returned to Syria; his nephew Tancred, in
possession at Antioch, treated the treaty as a document about somebody else. In
practical terms it governed nothing.

In legal terms it governed everything that followed, because the emperors now
had it in writing. John II Komnenos came in 1137 with an army, obliged Raymond
of Poitiers to do homage and to promise the city outright, and campaigned in
Syria with the imperial standard flying over Antioch's citadel. Manuel I came in
1158 after Reynald of Châtillon had raided Byzantine Cyprus, and Reynald was made
to walk bareheaded and barefoot with a rope round his neck through the imperial
camp at Mamistra and lie prostrate before the emperor in front of an audience
that included Latin envoys. In 1159 Manuel entered Antioch in a formal
adventus, riding, with the prince of Antioch walking at his stirrup.

This record's `vassal of` edge from the Byzantine page is dated to Devol in 1108
because that is when the relation was constituted. The note says what the field
cannot: it was repudiated immediately, and then extracted twice by emperors who
brought enough soldiers to collect it.

It is worth reading against the Byzantine record's other edges. Byzantium in
this corpus is mostly a polity things happen to — partitioned in 1204,
outmanoeuvred in 1261, conquered in 1453. This is the one place where it appears
as an overlord successfully imposing terms on a Latin state, and it happened in
the century when nobody thinks of the empire as strong.
