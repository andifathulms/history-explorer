---
title: The emperor comes in person
drafted_from: lilie-1993
phase: contraction
---

The imperial claim on Antioch was not a grievance that faded. It was pursued
for sixty years, in writing and then with armies, by three emperors in
succession, and it produced the one case in the crusading period of a Latin
state being made, formally and repeatedly, somebody's vassal.

The document was the treaty of Devol in 1108. Bohemond had gone back to Europe,
raised a war against the empire, invaded the Balkans and been comprehensively
beaten, and the terms Alexios imposed had him hold Antioch as a liegeman of the
emperor for life, return the Greek patriarch, and pass the city to the empire
afterwards. Bohemond signed and never returned to Syria; his nephew Tancred, in
possession at Antioch, treated the treaty as a document about somebody else. In
practical terms it governed nothing.

In legal terms it governed everything that followed, because the emperors now
had it in writing. John II Komnenos came in 1137 with an army, obliged Raymond
of Poitiers to do homage and to promise the city outright, and campaigned in
Syria with the imperial standard flying over Antioch's citadel. Manuel I came
in 1158 after Reynald of Châtillon had raided Byzantine Cyprus, and Reynald was
made to walk bareheaded and barefoot with a rope round his neck through the
imperial camp at Mamistra and lie prostrate before the emperor in front of an
audience that included Latin envoys. In 1159 Manuel entered Antioch in a formal
adventus, riding, with the prince of Antioch walking at his stirrup.

The relation was constituted at Devol in 1108, repudiated immediately, and then
collected twice by emperors who brought enough soldiers to collect it.

That is worth setting against the way the twelfth-century empire is usually
described. Byzantium in this period is mostly written about as something things
happen to — outmanoeuvred, partitioned, eventually conquered. Here it is an
overlord successfully imposing terms on a Latin state and enforcing them in
person, twice, in the century when nobody thinks of it as strong.
