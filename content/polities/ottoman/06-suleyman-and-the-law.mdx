---
title: The lawgiver, and what he actually did
drafted_from: imber-2002
phase: peak
---

Suleyman I reigned for forty-six years and Europe called him the Magnificent and remembers the campaigns: Belgrade in
1521, Rhodes in 1522, the destruction of the Hungarian kingdom at Mohács in
1526, Vienna besieged and not taken in 1529, Baghdad in 1534, and a
Mediterranean fleet under Barbarossa that made the sea contested for a
generation. At his death in 1566 the empire ran from Hungary to Yemen and from
Algiers to Basra.

The Ottomans called him *Kanuni*, the lawgiver, and that is the more useful
name.

The problem he inherited was specific. A Muslim state's law is the sharia,
which the sultan does not make; but an empire needs land tenure, taxation,
criminal penalties, market regulation and provincial administration, and the
sharia does not supply most of that in the detail a bureaucracy requires. The
Ottoman answer was *kanun* — sultanic regulation, issued by decree, covering
what the religious law left open. It had existed since the fifteenth century.
What happened under Suleyman was its systematisation into a coherent body,
codified province by province, and — this is the difficult part — its formal
reconciliation with the sharia by the chief jurist Ebussuud Efendi, who held
the office of şeyhülislam for nearly thirty years and whose rulings made the
sultan's regulations defensible in religious terms.

That is the settlement the empire ran on, and it is the third answer Islamic
history had produced to the same question. The Umayyads claimed to be God's
deputy on earth and lost that argument. The Abbasids tried to impose doctrine by
inquisition in the *mihna* and lost it definitively; after that, religious
authority in Sunni Islam belonged to the scholars and not to the ruler, and
every ruler since has had to work around the fact.

The Ottoman solution was not to claim the scholars' authority but to employ
them: a salaried, ranked, promotable religious hierarchy with the şeyhülislam at
its head, appointed by the sultan, whose independence was genuine in doctrine and
structurally limited in everything else.

Nobody solved that problem. This empire contained it for about three hundred
years, which is longer than anybody else managed.
