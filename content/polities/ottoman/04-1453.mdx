Mehmed II took Constantinople in 1453 after a fifty-three day siege, ending the
Roman empire eleven hundred years after its eastern half had been separated from
its western one. He was twenty-one. He also took the title *Kayser-i Rum* —
Caesar of Rome — and moved his capital into the city.

<Figure id="mehmed-ii" />

It is the clearest example there is of what claiming an empire actually
consists of: not descent, not conquest by itself, but a ruler publicly taking a
predecessor's title as the description of his own authority, and then daring
anybody to dispute it. The Ottomans also kept the Greek Orthodox patriarchate in
being under their protection, which was both a practical arrangement for
governing a Christian population and a continuation of an imperial function.

It was the first of two such claims the dynasty would make on the strength of
conquest rather than descent. The second, on the Arab lands and the holy
cities, came sixty-four years later.
