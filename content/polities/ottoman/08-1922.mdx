On 1 November 1922 the Grand National Assembly at Ankara abolished the Ottoman
sultanate. Mustafa Kemal's own account of the day, delivered in a speech five
years later, is the best evidence there is of how it was done and what he
thought he was doing.

The obstacle was a committee. Three committees of the Assembly had been put in
one room to work out whether the caliphate could be separated from the
sultanate, and the religious scholars on the *Şeriye* committee were arguing that
it could not. The men who disagreed with them, Kemal says, would not come
forward and say so. He watched the debate from a corner of a crowded room and
concluded that nothing he wanted was going to come out of it.

Then he asked the chairman for permission to speak, stood on the bench in front
of him, and said this in a loud voice:

> Gentlemen, neither the sovereignty nor the right to govern can be transferred
> by one person to anybody else by an academic debate. Sovereignty is acquired by
> force, by power and by violence. It was by violence that the sons of Osman
> acquired the power to rule over the Turkish nation and to maintain their rule
> for more than six centuries. It is now the nation that revolts against these
> usurpers, puts them in their right place and actually carries on their
> sovereignty. This is an actual fact.

And then the sentence that ended the discussion:

> If those who are assembled here, the Assembly and everybody else, would find
> this quite natural, it would be very appropriate from my point of view.
> Conversely, the reality will nevertheless be manifested in the necessary form,
> but in that event it is possible that some heads will be cut off.

One of the scholars, the deputy for Ankara, replied: *Pardon me, we had regarded
the question in another light. Now we are informed.*

## The vote

The draft act was written the same day and read that afternoon. Somebody moved a
roll-call vote and Kemal went to the tribune to say the procedure was useless,
because he believed the Assembly would adopt the principles unanimously.

There were shouts of *Vote!* The chairman put the motion and declared it
unanimously agreed. One voice was heard saying *I am against it*, and was
drowned in cries of *Silence!*

Kemal's own summary of the afternoon is the last line of the section:

> In this way, Gentlemen, the curtain fell on the last act of the overthrow and
> breakdown of the Ottoman Monarchy.

## What the last sultan did

Mehmed VI did not contest it. On 17 November the British commander in
Constantinople, General Harington, notified Ankara by letter that His Majesty
had placed himself under the protection of England and left the city on a
British man-of-war.

The communiqué appended to it explains that the sultan, appreciating the danger
threatening his life and freedom, had appealed for English protection *in his
capacity as Caliph of all the Mohamedans*, and asked to be taken somewhere else,
and that his desire had been fulfilled that morning.

Kemal also prints a second letter, from Harington to one of the sultan's
daughters, relaying a wireless message from the ship as it approached Malta: the
sultan wished to know how his family were, the general had gone to the Yıldız
palace to find out, and the sultan's wife was in the best of health.

Kemal's comment on that letter is four words. *This last letter is not worthy of
notice.*

Six centuries of the house of Osman, and the reigning head of it leaving his
capital on somebody else's warship, asking by radio after his wife.
