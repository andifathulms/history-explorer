Palenque's Classic dynasty, according to retrospective texts
compiled at the end of the seventh century, began with K'uk' Bahlam
I, "Quetzal Jaguar," whose reign started in 431. For nearly two
centuries afterward the kingdom - known to its own people as Baakal,
"Bone" - governed itself from Lakamha', its capital in the foothills
above the Usumacinta basin, largely outside the reach of the great
rivalries convulsing the central Petén to the east.

## The Snake kingdom reaches across the Usumacinta

That isolation ended abruptly. On 21 April 599 Palenque was "axed" -
sacked - by forces from Calakmul's Snake kingdom, an assault
launched from a great distance and requiring a crossing of the
Usumacinta River, evidence of just how far the Snake kings' ambition
now reached. Queen Yohl Ik'nal, who had ruled through the attack,
died in 604 and was succeeded by Ajen Yohl Mat, whose eight-year
reign brought a second, still worse catastrophe: on 4 April 611,
Lakamha' was sacked again, this time by Scroll Serpent, the Snake
king, in person. Neither Ajen Yohl Mat nor his own successor-in-waiting,
Janaab Pakal, lived out the following year, both dying within weeks
of each other in 612.

## Three years without a king

What followed was recorded in language unlike anything else in the
Palenque corpus - "lost is the divine lady, lost is the lord" - and a
new ruler is named only as Muwaan Mat, the ancestral deity and
progenitor of Palenque's patron gods, rather than as any ordinary
mortal king. Whether this represents an actual interregnum under a
deity's name, a stand-in for an unnamed foreign administrator
imposed by Palenque's conquerors, or something else again, no
surviving text makes fully clear. The strange period lasted three
years, and ended only with the accession of a twelve-year-old boy.
