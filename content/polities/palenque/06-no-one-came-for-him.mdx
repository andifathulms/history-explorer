The last ruler of Palenque known to any surviving inscription was
Wak Kimi Janaab Pakal, "6 Death Shield," also called Janaab Pakal
III, who acceded in 799. The record of his reign is modest by the
standards of his great predecessor - an incised blackware vessel
found in an ordinary residential burial, not a royal monument. One
further trace of the Baakal kingdom survives after him: a clay brick
made around 814 at Comalcalco, a peripheral site far to the west at
the very edge of the Maya world, evidence that some memory of
Palenque's kingship persisted there even as the dynasty's own history
went silent at its capital.

## Depopulation and squatters

What followed at Lakamha' itself was the same story unfolding across
the Classic Maya world in the early ninth century: the sudden end of
dynastic history, a rapid fall in population, and a long afterlife as
a place inhabited only by squatters and itinerant travellers rather
than by any court. Most of the great halls of Pakal's own Palace
complex remained standing, sheltering that thin, scattered occupation
for close to a thousand years. When one such visitor was killed by
the sudden collapse of a gallery roof sometime in that long
afterward, archaeologists found his remains centuries later exactly
where he had died - no companion or relation ever having returned to
carry the body away.
