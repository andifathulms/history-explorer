K'inich Janaab Pakal - "Radiant Shield" - came to the throne in 615
at the age of twelve, the son of Lady Sak K'uk', "Resplendent
Quetzal." His inauguration is shown on the Oval Palace Tablet, where
his mother herself presents him with the royal "drum major" crown,
an image without real precedent in Palenque's earlier monuments. Sak
K'uk' lived another twenty-five years, dying in 640, and she and her
consort K'an Mo' Hix almost certainly held much of the kingdom's
practical power while the young king grew into his own authority.

## Clawing back Santa Elena

Once he came into his majority, Pakal set about reversing the losses
of the previous generation. A stairway inscription in Palenque's West
Court describes fighting six days after the death of a rival lord in
662, and credits the king with bringing the strategically important
site of Santa Elena "back under Palenque's control" - an achievement
that echoed one his own predecessor had claimed decades earlier. The
triumph proved short-lived: Santa Elena fell to Piedras Negras only
two years later, and the Snake kingdom, still commanding events from
Calakmul, installed a boy-king of its own at nearby Moral-Reforma
soon after. Most of what Pakal had recovered was quickly lost again,
but the kingdom he had inherited as an orphaned child was, by any
measure, no longer the broken state Scroll Serpent had left behind
in 611.
