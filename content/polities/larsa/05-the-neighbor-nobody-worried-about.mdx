Hammurabi's own thirty-first regnal year is when the record shows
Larsa finally falling - the campaign that ended twenty-nine years in
which Babylon had gained almost nothing worth the name. Rim-Sin, king
for six decades by that point, lost his own capital to a rival kingdom
that had spent most of his own reign looking too minor to bother
with. There is no record of a prolonged mutual conflict building
toward this moment: Babylon's own sudden turn against Larsa reads, in
the sources, as the deliberate opening move of a campaign rather than
the culmination of a long-simmering rivalry.

## Eight years, and everything else

Larsa's fall was not an isolated event but the first domino in a
much larger collapse. In the eight years that followed his conquest
of Larsa, Hammurabi took nearly every other significant power within
reach - a campaign of expansion so total that it occupies the final
twelve years of his own forty-three-year reign, following three
decades in which no one, Rim-Sin least of all, had reason to think
Babylon would ever matter beyond its own immediate neighborhood.

Rim-Sin's own fate afterward is not recorded in detail by the sources
consulted here. What can be said with confidence is only that a rivalry
begun with one governor's opportunistic break from Isin, and settled a
century later by his own dynasty's absorption of that same rival, ended
in turn with Larsa itself becoming one piece of somebody else's much
larger conquest.
