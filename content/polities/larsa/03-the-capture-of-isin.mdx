A century-old rivalry between two cities for the same old royal title ended
in Rim-Sin's twenty-ninth regnal year, and the event mattered enough to the
man who won it that he stopped naming his subsequent years after anything
else. Every year-formula of Rim-Sin's reign after this one counts forward
from the capture of Isin rather than commemorating whatever else happened
in the interval — a king who had reigned for decades still choosing, for
the rest of his life, to date everything from the single year his oldest
rival finally fell.

## What actually happened, stated plainly

Isin's own record cannot say cleanly how its dynasty ended: its last
securely attested kings, Sin-magir and Damik-ilishu, close out the royal
line, and no source states what became of the city or its throne
immediately afterward. What can be said from Larsa's own side is more
direct. By the time Rim-Sin turned his full attention to Isin, the city
was governed by a dynasty already past the point of mounting serious
resistance to a stronger neighbor, and Rim-Sin took it — not, so far as the
record shows, in a single dramatic battle, but as the culmination of
pressure a declining rival could no longer answer.

The distinction matters for what it settles. Isin's dynasty did not fall
to the later, disputed captures its own date-formulas would eventually
misattribute to Rim-Sin and to a Babylonian king a generation afterward;
those events belong to a separate contest over the same city fought long
after Isin's own kingship had already ended. What Rim-Sin's twenty-ninth
year actually ended was Isin as an independent throne, whatever exact
process had already left it too weak to remain one.

## One king, one title, one south

Rim-Sin's absorption of Isin's territory converted a rivalry that had run
since Gungunum's own secession into a single fact: for the first time in
over a century, one king held the old royal claim to Sumer and Akkad
without a rival city asserting the same title against him. Larsa's later
decades of unchallenged dominance, described in what follows, rest
directly on this one campaign — the year Rim-Sin's own chronology never
stopped counting from.
