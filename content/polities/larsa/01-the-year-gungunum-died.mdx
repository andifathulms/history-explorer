---
title: The year Gungunum died

Larsa had been a city under Isin's control for generations, governed
in part through priestly appointments the ruling family made at
neighboring Ur - one of Isin's own princes held the office of chief
priest of the Moon-god there for years. Gungunum changed that
relationship outright. On his own building inscriptions he claims the
titles King of Larsa and King of the whole of Sumer and Akkad, the
same royal style Isin's own kings had held unchallenged for nearly a
century, and the timing suggests he broke away toward the end of, or
immediately following, the eleven-year reign of Isin's King
Libit-Ishtar.

## A rebellion, or an accident of chaos

The sources leave open whether Gungunum's own secession was a
deliberate act of rebellion or simply an opportunist's response to a
crisis already underway elsewhere - there is real evidence that Isin
itself may have suffered a foreign invasion from Elam around the same
time, in which case Larsa's independence may have emerged from a
vacuum rather than from any direct confrontation between the two
cities. Either way, the result was the same: a second kingdom, with
its own king and its own royal title, standing where Isin had
recently ruled unopposed.

## A brief reign, and a violent one

Gungunum's own success did not last long. His death is recorded in a
date-formula that reads only "the year in which Gungunum died" - a
phrase the source notes was never used to commemorate a natural death,
which points instead to a killing, probably at the hands of Isin's own
King Ur-Ninib, who succeeded in briefly restoring his own dynasty's
authority not long afterward. The kingdom Gungunum had founded
outlived him regardless, and the rivalry he had opened between Larsa
and Isin would run for another century before either city's original
line was left standing.
