The kingdom Rim-Sin I eventually held for sixty years did not descend to
him from Gungunum's own family. Between the founder's violent death and
Rim-Sin's own accession, Larsa's throne changed hands several times among
kings whose relationship to Gungunum or to each other the record does not
state plainly, and the dynasty that finally produced Rim-Sin arrived from
outside the city altogether.

## A sheikh who ruled without a crown

Kudur-Mabuk, whose name is Elamite though he led an Amorite tribal group,
the Yamutbal, built his own standing in the region under the kings who
held Larsa before him, eventually taking the title Father of the Amorite
Land. He never styled himself king of Larsa. What he did instead was
install his own son.

Around 1834, Kudur-Mabuk placed his son Warad-Sin on the throne of Larsa.
Warad-Sin's own inscriptions credit his father openly, naming Kudur-Mabuk
beside himself in temple dedications in a way no previous Larsa king's
father had been credited — a public acknowledgment that the man behind
the throne was not merely royal blood but the reason there was a new king
at all.

## The brother who came after

Warad-Sin reigned about twelve years. When he died, the throne passed not
to a son of his own but to his brother, Rim-Sin — both of them Kudur-
Mabuk's sons, and both of their reigns beginning with an act of filial
deference their own father's unusual position had made necessary.

That transfer, brother to brother rather than father to son, is itself
worth noting beside the instability that preceded it. Kudur-Mabuk's family
had taken a kingdom whose recent history was five or six kings in as many
decades and given it something none of Gungunum's own successors had
managed: an orderly succession within one household, prepared for and
carried out without the record showing any dispute at all.

## What the family actually was

A tribal chief with a foreign name, ruling through sons he installed
rather than through a crown he wore himself, is not the shape of dynasty
this region's kings ordinarily advertised. Kudur-Mabuk's own inscriptions
show no embarrassment about it. He is named as what he was — a chief who
had made kings — and the arrangement worked exactly as intended: it put
his family on Larsa's throne for the whole of the kingdom's remaining
existence.
