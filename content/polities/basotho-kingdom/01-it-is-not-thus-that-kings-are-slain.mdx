Before 1820, Casalis records, the scattered Sotho chiefdoms of the
highlands lived in something like the feudal disorder of medieval
France - the house of Monaheng, of which Moshoeshoe was a
representative, held nominal supremacy, while every town's chief
pushed for as much independence as he could get, and disputes rarely
cost more than a few stolen herds. That order collapsed all at once
when Shaka's own conquests in Natal drove a defeated vassal,
Matiwane, to flee west with thousands of followers still loyal to
him - a flight that set off a chain reaction, driving one displaced
people into the next, until the peaceful valleys of Lesotho became,
in Casalis's own words, "the scene of continual slaughter."

## A famine that turned neighbors into cannibals

Years of war left fields untilled and famine layered on top of
violence, until bands answering to no chief at all took to living in
mountain caves and hunting other people for food - caves Casalis
himself later visited, still scattered with cracked skulls and
bloodstained rock. Moshoeshoe, alone among the region's chiefs, held
his ground against the tide. Cornered after a defeat with only four or
five men and a wall of enemy spears yards away, he is said to have
sat down calmly, then risen and walked straight at the encircling
warriors, announcing, "It is not thus that kings are slain" - and
they let him through.

## A word that ended what an army could not

Moshoeshoe took final refuge on the flat summit of Thaba Bosiu, a
natural fortress whose cliffs made surprise impossible, and set about
rebuilding order out of the wreckage around him. His first act was to
end the cannibalism that had taken hold among the starving and the
lawless - not by force, which he judged would only touch off a
ruinous civil war on top of everything else, but by declaring simply
that men who ate human flesh were already living corpses, unworthy of
being fought at all. The declaration alone, Casalis writes, was enough
to shame the practice into decline: those guilty of it took the
verdict as an unlooked-for path back into the community, rather than
a sentence, and returned to it.
