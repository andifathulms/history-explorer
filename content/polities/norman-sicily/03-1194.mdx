William II died in 1189 at thirty-six, childless, having made his barons swear to
accept his aunt Constance as his heir. Constance was Roger II's posthumous
daughter, thirty-five years old, and married to Henry, king of Germany and heir
to this site's Holy Roman record.

The barons and the chancellor elected Tancred of Lecce instead — an illegitimate
grandson of Roger II, competent, and not German.

## The one succession that mattered

This record's `succession_rule` reads `primogeniture` and the field is describing
a rule that was applied four times and broken on the fifth, when everything
depended on it.

Coding rule 7 takes the rule and not the outcome, which is the site's standard
practice and is worth restating here because this is a case where the outcome was
terminal. Roger II to William I to William II is descent in the male line
unproblematically. 1189 is the first succession without a son, and the men who
had to decide chose a claimant they preferred over the claimant the rule
produced.

They were not obviously wrong to. The alternative was a German king in Palermo,
which is what they got five years later.

## What Henry did with it

Tancred died in 1194. Henry VI, by then emperor, came south with an army financed
by the ransom of Richard I of England — who had been captured on his way back
from the Third Crusade — took Palermo without much fighting, and was crowned on
Christmas Day.

He had Tancred's widow and children sent to Germany, had the surviving Norman
claimants blinded or mutilated, and by some accounts had Tancred's son castrated
and blinded. The Hauteville line ended there.

Constance, forty and pregnant, gave birth the following day in the market square
at Jesi in the Marche, reportedly in a tent open to the town so that nobody could
say the child was substituted. The child is Frederick II, this site's Holy Roman
record's peak ruler, who was raised in Palermo and ruled Sicily and Germany
together for thirty years.

## `dynastic replacement`

The field says `dynastic replacement` rather than `conquest`, and the distinction
is the same one this corpus's Lombard record makes about 774.

The kingdom was not dismantled. The chancery went on issuing in three languages
for a while yet, the registers went on being kept, the administrative structure
Roger II had built went on running, and Frederick II — who was born a Sicilian
and thought of himself as one — governed it from Palermo. What changed was the
family.

`ended.by` names the Holy Roman Empire, which is a record in this same region,
and the edge `norman-sicily -> holy-roman-empire` is the Latin West's third
internal one. It is also, on this site's data, the only occasion on which one
record in this region ended another.

## The Zirid field

One other field changed when this record was written.

This corpus's Zirid record ends in 1148 with an `ended.by` of `[]` and a chapter
saying that the vocabulary could not name the power that took Mahdia, because
that power had no page. Roger II's fleet took Mahdia and the Tunisian ports in
1148 and the kingdom held them as tributaries for about a decade.

`by` now names this record, and `zirid -> norman-sicily` is in the edge list —
crossing from Africa and the Maghreb into the Latin West, drawn by neither
thread, and resolving a field that had been honestly empty for as long as it
needed to be.
