Burhan Imad Shah inherited Berar's throne as a child, and his minister
Toofal Khan, appointed regent for the duration of his minority, used
the years before the young king could rule in his own right to build a
power base with help from neighboring Khandesh and from Ahmadnagar's
own court. Before Burhan came of age, Toofal Khan had him confined in
irons in the fortress of Narnala and simply assumed the royal title for
himself.

## A usurper who outmaneuvered his enemies for years

Ahmadnagar and Bijapur combined against Toofal Khan once his ambitions
became undeniable, but he bought Bijapur's withdrawal from the
coalition with a gift of jewels, splitting his enemies apart before
they could destroy him together. Ahmadnagar's Murtaza Nizam Shah,
discovering the bribery, withdrew rather than fight alone — but
returned in 1572, this time under the declared purpose of freeing the
imprisoned rightful king from his own captivity.

## A siege won by starvation and a single trumpet

Toofal Khan sought help from Golconda and attacked Ahmadnagar's own
forces directly, but was decisively beaten and pursued into the hill
fortress of Narnala while his son held a second stronghold at
Gawilgarh. Rather than storm so strong a position outright, the
besieging general turned to attrition: he bribed deserters from the
garrison one by one until only a dozen gunners remained, dragged a
single cannon up the hillside to open a breach, and sent a small party
over the wall at night with a trumpeter to sound the alarm. Believing a
much larger force had broken in, Toofal Khan fled into the surrounding
hills with almost no escort at all, abandoning the fortress without a
further fight.

## A dynasty ended in a single order

Ahmadnagar's pursuing cavalry caught Toofal Khan within three days.
Murtaza Nizam Shah, rather than restore the freed King Burhan to his
own throne, had the usurper, his son and the rightful king alike
confined together in a Nizam Shahy fortress — where, by one account,
all three were strangled on the king's own order, and by another, their
entire households, forty people in all, died in a single night after
their jailers, denied a bribe from the prisoners' own subsistence
funds, locked them into an overcrowded cell on a hot night. Either way,
the source records the same result: "the family of Imad Shah and that
of the usurper Toofal Khan became extinct." Berar's own territory
passed into Ahmadnagar's hands without a treaty, a partition, or any
formal transfer — simply the last kingdom nearby strong enough to claim
what a murdered dynasty could no longer hold.
