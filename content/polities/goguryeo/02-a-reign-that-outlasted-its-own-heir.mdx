---
title: A reign that outlasted its own heir

By the time Goguryeo's sixth king, T'aejo, sat on the throne, the young
kingdom had shifted from consolidating its own founding territory to
actively pressing outward against China itself. T'aejo made common
cause with the Ye-măk people to his east, and together the two launched
repeated raids across Goguryeo's own northern border into Chinese
territory - not conquest in any settled sense, but a sustained
demonstration that the frontier no longer belonged to China alone to
patrol.

## A reign long enough to exhaust its own heir

T'aejo's own rule became, by Korean record, the longest ever held by
any monarch on the peninsula: ninety-four years on the throne, a span
so extraordinary that in his sixty-ninth year he handed day-to-day
administration to his brother Su-seung rather than continue managing
every affair personally. Su-seung proved just as ambitious as the king
he served, continuing the alliance with the Ye-măk and the raids on
Chinese territory - but also, less loyally, attempting to build a
faction against his own brother, an effort that came to nothing.

## An heir who waited a lifetime for his turn

When T'aejo finally died, his own heir apparent ascended the throne at
the advanced age of seventy-six, in AD 147 - a wait so long that one of
the new king's first acts was to have executed the officials who had
once criticised him for trying to unseat his elderly predecessor early.
Whatever T'aejo's own reign had cost his successor in patience, it left
Goguryeo a kingdom that had spent nearly a century testing China's
frontier defenses and finding them, again and again, unable to hold.
