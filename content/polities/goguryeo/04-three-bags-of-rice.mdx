In 612 the Sui emperor launched what stands as one of the largest
invasion forces in the history of premodern warfare against Goguryeo -
an army recorded at over a million men, advancing on multiple routes
toward the Yalu River. A detachment of roughly three hundred thousand
troops under General Yu Jung-mun was tasked with marching directly on
P'yongyang, on the assumption that so vast a force needed no support
from the rest.

## A spy who came disguised as surrender

Goguryeo's general Eulji Mundeok travelled into the Chinese camp under
a pretense of submission, ostensibly to negotiate terms but in fact to
assess the invaders' true strength. The emperor nearly had him killed
on the spot, then let him go, then changed his mind again - too late,
as Eulji had already slipped past the Chinese pickets and could not be
overtaken. What he had learned proved decisive: each Chinese soldier
had been issued three bags of rice to carry on the march, under threat
of death for discarding any of it, and most had simply buried the
surplus rather than carry the weight - leaving the army's generals
believing their men were far better supplied than they actually were.

## A retreat that was really an ambush

Eulji drew the invading force deep into Goguryeo territory through
repeated feigned withdrawals, then, once the Chinese army had crossed
the Salsu river exhausted and low on provisions, sent a second, more
convincing offer of surrender. The Chinese general, reasoning that his
own supplies could not sustain a siege of the capital, accepted and
turned his army back toward the Yalu - directly into a prepared
ambush that struck from the forests on both sides of the road before
his forces had gone a mile.

## A million men, a few thousand survivors

The retreat became a rout, and the rout a slaughter that covered four
hundred and fifty li in a single day and night of flight. Of the three
hundred thousand men who had crossed the Yalu into Goguryeo territory
under General Yu, only twenty-seven hundred made it back across the
river alive. The Sui emperor, enraged, imprisoned the general
responsible - a punishment that changed nothing about the scale of the
defeat, or the fact that Goguryeo had just destroyed one of the largest
armies ever assembled against it almost without pitched battle at all.
