Balhae spent its first two decades absorbing territory steadily in the
north, growing, in the source's words, rapidly enough to grasp "at
everything in sight." China watched this growth with rising unease, and
in 734 that unease turned to action after Balhae did something no
Korean kingdom in this history is recorded doing before or since: she
threw troops across the Yellow Sea itself and seized a foothold on the
Shandong promontory, on the Chinese mainland proper.

## An expedition that arrived too late

The raid forced China's hand. A joint expedition was organized to
punish Balhae for the incursion, and Silla — no friend to a northern
power expanding on her own frontier — sent Gen. Kim Yun-jung to join
the Chinese forces against her. But the campaign had been slow to
assemble, and by the time it was ready to move against Balhae proper,
winter's approach made further operations impractical. The expedition
that had been meant to chastise Balhae for reaching onto Chinese soil
accomplished nothing, and Balhae's own territory in the north went
untouched.

## A kingdom neither conquered nor contained

What the failed campaign left behind was a Balhae that had tested
Chinese and Silla resolve together and found neither strong enough, in
that moment, to reverse what she had already taken. For the following
decades she stood as the one power on the peninsula's own northern
frontier that Silla could neither absorb nor safely ignore — until a
different northern power, one Silla had never had to reckon with at
all, arrived to end the arrangement in Balhae's place rather than
Silla's.
