It was in Amoghavarsha's reign, and probably at his own direction,
that Manyakheta - modern Malkhed - became the Rashtrakuta capital in
place of whatever earlier seat the dynasty had used at Achalapura or
near Ellora. The Arab historian al-Mas'udi knew the city by the name
Mankir, a mark of how far its reputation had already travelled beyond
the Deccan by the time later geographers were writing.

## A synthesis of two faiths

In the latter half of his long life Amoghavarsha turned from war
toward religion and literature. He was, by his own court's account,
either the author or the inspiration behind the Kavirajamarga, the
earliest surviving work of Kannada poetics, and the Jain scholar
Jinasena, author of the Adipurana, names Amoghavarsha his own pupil.
Yet the same king kept up an intense devotion to the goddess
Mahalakshmi, once offering her a finger of his own left hand -
recorded by his court poet in the Sanjan plates and independently
confirmed by the Jain grammarian Bhattakalanka - to avert a calamity
he believed threatened his people. "Monarchs usually make others
bleed for themselves," the source observes; "rulers like Amoghavarsha,
who voluntarily bleed for others, are rare indeed in the annals of
human history."

## A throne set down and taken up again

After about 860, once he had passed sixty and the empire had settled
into comparative peace, Amoghavarsha began periodically withdrawing
from government altogether to devote himself to religious retreat,
leaving his son and eventual successor Krishna to rule in his place
until he returned. The practice explains why later charters sometimes
name father and son as reigning king in the same years - a confusion
the source resolves by treating whichever charter names an active
ruler as reflecting one of these interludes rather than a disputed
succession. Amoghavarsha's last known date is March 878; reckoning
his age at about seventy-eight that year, his death and his son's
full accession followed within two years, closing a reign that had
opened when its own occupant was still a boy of thirteen.
