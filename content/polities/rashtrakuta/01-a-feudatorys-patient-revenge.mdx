title: A feudatory's patient revenge

Dantidurga did not begin as a rebel. He served the Chalukya emperor
Kirttivarman II loyally enough at first, even marrying a Chalukya
princess he had taken by force on the battlefield at Kaira — a match
that tied his own family to the imperial line he would later
overthrow. His ambitions grew slowly and were pursued with patience
rather than a single decisive stroke.

## A rival isolated before he was ever attacked

Rather than move directly against his own overlord, Dantidurga spent
years building strength on the margins of Chalukya power: subduing the
Gurjaras of Malwa, campaigning against Kosala and Kalinga, and marching
all the way to Kanchipuram, where he struck an alliance with the
Pallava king Nandivarman Pallavamalla by giving him his own daughter in
marriage. By the time he finally turned against Kirttivarman directly,
the Chalukya emperor had already been stripped of his outlying
provinces and diplomatic support.

## A conquest declared before it was fully won

Dantidurga's own Samangad copper-plate grant, dated 753, already
describes Chalukya sovereignty as overthrown — an accomplished fact
rather than an ongoing campaign, even though Kirttivarman continued to
issue grants from his own camp for some years afterward. The source
treats this less as a single military disaster than as "a rapidly
progressing attenuation" of Chalukya power that Dantidurga's own
maneuvering had engineered over years. In declaring himself sovereign
of the Deccan, Dantidurga founded a dynasty that would hold that
position for two centuries — considerably longer than the empire it
had replaced.
