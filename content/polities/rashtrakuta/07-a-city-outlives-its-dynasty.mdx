title: A city outlives its dynasty

Manyakheta itself did not fall with the dynasty that had built it.
Taila II occupied the city in 973 and kept it as the seat of his
restored Chalukya power for a generation, and the fort on the Kagina
river continued to appear in inscriptions - a Mysore record of 902
already names it the headquarters of the "Manyakheda six thousand"
province, and it went on being graced by the presence of Chalukya
sovereigns after Taila's own conquest.

## A Chola prince's raid on the old Rashtrakuta capital

The city's second violent moment came from the south. Around 1007-8,
the Chola crown prince Rajendra - the future Rajendra I - led an army
deep into Chalukya territory in reprisal for a Chalukya invasion of
Vengi, and inscriptions of his own reign claim the capture of the
Raichur doab, the Kadamba capital Banavasi, and finally "Mannaikkadakkam" -
Manyakheta itself, by then the Chalukya king Satyashraya's own seat.
A near-contemporary Chalukya inscription, in its own "picturesque
language," accuses Rajendra's army of crossing the frontier "900,000
strong" and ravaging the country - language the source treats as
plainly exaggerated, but which nonetheless records a genuine and
deep incursion. It was not a lasting occupation: Satyashraya kept his
throne until his own death in 1008, and the Chalukya line he
represented, the dynasty that had itself displaced the Rashtrakutas
thirty-five years before, continued to rule from Manyakheta and later
from Kalyani for more than a century afterward. The city that had
once housed the court a ninth-century Arab merchant judged mightier
than any of its neighbours spent its final chapter as a prize fought
over by the two powers that came after it.
