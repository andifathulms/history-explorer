title: The book of machines

In 1206, at Amid, an engineer named al-Jazari finished a book for his patron,
the Artuqid ruler Nasr al-Din Mahmud, whose father and grandfather he had also
served. It is called *al-Jami bayn al-ilm wa'l-amal* — the compendium of theory
and practice — and it is usually known as the Book of Knowledge of Ingenious
Mechanical Devices.

It describes about fifty machines, with instructions for building them and
drawings of the parts. Water clocks and candle clocks, including one with a
figure that drops a ball onto a cymbal on the hour. Automatic vessels for
serving drink. A waitress automaton. A hand-washing device with a flush
mechanism. Fountains whose patterns change on a cycle. And, less
decoratively, the water-raising machines the region actually needed: a
crank-driven pump, a twin-cylinder suction pump with a genuine crankshaft, and
a segmental gear.

The historians of technology treat several of those as first surviving
descriptions anywhere. What matters for this page is simpler. The book was
commissioned, paid for and preserved by a minor Turkmen dynasty in the Jazira
whose political history is a list of the overlords it acknowledged, in the
century between the crusade it defeated and the Mongols it submitted to.

This corpus keeps producing the same observation from different directions and
it is worth stating plainly here. The Sultanate of Rum's cultural peak came
while it was a Mongol tributary; the Ilkhanate produced the first world history
after destroying the caliphate; the Ma'munids of Khwarazm held al-Biruni and
Ibn Sina for fifteen years and lasted twenty-two; the Kakuyids gave Ibn Sina
the only settled decade he had. The relationship between political weight and
what gets made is much weaker than a site organised around reach and longevity
implies.

This page has no cited extent, no cited population, and three influence counts
that are all gaps. It also has, on the strength of one patron in one small
city, a claim on the history of mechanical engineering that nothing else in
this corpus can match.
