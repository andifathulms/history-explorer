---
title: Why it took twenty-five years
drafted_from: gammer-1994
phase: contraction
---

Russia in 1839 had a professional army of enormous size, artillery, and the
resources of an empire. The imamate had mountains, forests and perhaps a few
tens of thousands of fighting men. The war lasted until 1859, and the reason is
worth setting out because it is the same reason this corpus gives for a dozen
polities in similar country.

In 1839 the Russians besieged Akhulgo, Shamil's stronghold, for eighty days and
took it at heavy cost. It was reported in St Petersburg as the end of the war.
Shamil escaped through the cordon with a handful of men, and his son Jamaluddin
was handed over as a hostage and raised as a Russian officer. Within a year
Chechnya had risen and the imamate was larger than it had ever been.

In 1845 the viceroy Vorontsov marched on Dargo, Shamil's capital, with a large
force and reached it. It was empty and burning. The retreat through the forests
is one of the notorious episodes of Russian military history — the column
harassed continuously, its supply train destroyed, and something approaching a
third of it lost.

The lesson both sides drew was the same and the Russians acted on it. An army
that marches at a mountain polity arrives at an empty village and is destroyed
on the way home; the polity is not a place and cannot be captured. So from the
late 1840s Russia stopped invading and started building — clearing the beech
forests of Chechnya that made ambush possible, cutting roads through the cleared
ground, planting forts at the ends of the roads, and moving the line forward a
few kilometres a year.

It is a strategy of engineering rather than of battle, and it is the answer to
mountain warfare that this corpus's other pages describe being reached
independently elsewhere: Ibrahim Pasha took the Najd in 1818 by taking the
walled towns and the wells one at a time rather than chasing the tribes, and the
Ottoman reconquests of Yemen ran on the same principle.

The imamate could not answer it. Every kilometre of cleared forest removed a
piece of the ground its method depended on, and the population that had to be
taxed to fight was the same population being progressively enclosed.
