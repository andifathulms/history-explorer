In 1584 the male line of Patani failed and the dead sultan's sister took the
throne.

Raja Hijau reigned thirty-two years. Her sister Raja Biru followed, then a second
sister, Raja Ungu, then Raja Ungu's daughter Raja Kuning. Four queens in
succession, from 1584 to 1688: a hundred and four years.

## What this does to another record

This corpus's Aceh record has a chapter called "Four queens" about the women who
held that throne from 1641 to 1699. It says that fifty-eight years of women
ruling in succession was "the longest run of reigning women anywhere in this
corpus by a wide margin", and the record's data comment says the same thing.

That was written before this page existed and it is wrong. Patani's run is
almost twice as long and starts fifty-seven years earlier. Both records have been
corrected.

The rest of that chapter's argument is unaffected, and it is worth restating
because this record strengthens it. Aceh's queens were installed by the
*orangkaya*, the merchant aristocracy, because a queen could not lead an army or
build a personal following among the war captains — a mechanism for keeping the
throne weak, run four times. The vocabulary value `matrilineal` was defined on
this site by descent rather than by the sex of the ruler, on an audit of Ghana,
Makuria and Asante, and the Aceh chapter argues that had it been defined the
other way the record would now be carrying a value that said something false.

Patani makes that argument twice as strong and for a different reason. Here the
succession ran through sisters and then a daughter — women succeeding women, in a
line, for a century — and it was still not matrilineal in the sense the rulebook
means. Descent at Patani ran through the founding dynasty; what happened in 1584
is that the dynasty had no men left, and then kept not having them.

`succession_rule` on both records is null. Two of the longest runs of reigning
women in the corpus, on two Malay-speaking sultanates four hundred miles apart,
and neither of them a rule about women.

## The chronicle

The *Hikayat Patani* is the internal source, and like the *Sejarah Melayu* on
this corpus's Melaka record it was written by people with a position in an
argument that was still open. Both are court chronicles of Malay sultanates
composed while a succession was contested; both are the only inside view of what
they describe; and this site cites both while saying so.
