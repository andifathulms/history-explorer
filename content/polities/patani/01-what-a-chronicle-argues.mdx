The *Hikayat Patani* is the internal source for almost everything on this record,
and this site cites two Malay court chronicles of the same kind — the other being
the *Sejarah Melayu* on this corpus's Melaka and Johor records.

Both were written by people with a position in an argument that was still open.
Both are the only inside view of what they describe. Neither is a chronicle in
the sense of a year-by-year register; both are structured as a sequence of
episodes with a moral shape.

## What this one is arguing about

Succession, and specifically the legitimacy of a line that ran through women for
a century.

The text spends a great deal of its length on the four queens and on the events
around each accession, and it is at pains to establish that each was the right
person. That is not how a chronicle behaves about an uncontroversial rule. It is
how a chronicle behaves when the arrangement it is describing needs defending —
and the *Hikayat* was compiled, in the form we have, after the queens were over
and a different dynasty from Kelantan held the throne.

So the most detailed evidence this record has for its most distinctive feature
comes from a document produced by people who had an interest in how that feature
was remembered.

This corpus's Melaka record makes the same observation about the *Sejarah
Melayu*, and this site's source note on that text compares it to the Timbuktu
chronicles on the Songhai record: elegy written by the survivors of the thing it
describes. The *Hikayat Patani* is not elegy. It is closer to a brief.

## What that does to the coding

`succession_rule` on this record is null, and this chapter is a second reason for
it beyond the one this record's first chapter gives.

The first reason is that women succeeding women for a hundred and four years was
not a rule about women — descent ran through the founding dynasty and the
dynasty had run out of men. The second is that the source which would tell us
whether there was a rule is the source with the strongest motive to say there
was.

Coding rule 7 asks for the rule rather than the outcome. Where the only witness
to the rule is arguing a case, this site's answer has been a null and a
paragraph, and this is the paragraph.
