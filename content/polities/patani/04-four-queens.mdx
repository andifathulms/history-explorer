In 1584 the male line of Patani failed and the dead sultan's sister took the
throne.

Raja Hijau reigned thirty-two years. Her sister Raja Biru followed, then a
second sister, Raja Ungu, then Raja Ungu's daughter Raja Kuning. Four queens in
succession, from 1584 to 1688: a hundred and four years.

## Not a rule about women

Four queens in a line invites a conclusion it will not support, and Patani is
the sharpest case of it anywhere in these waters.

Descent at Patani ran through the founding dynasty, not through its women. What
happened in 1584 is that the dynasty had no men left, and then kept not having
them: a sister, a second sister, a third, and then a daughter. Nothing was
inherited through the female line and nothing in Patani's law made women
eligible as women.

Aceh, four hundred miles across the water, did the same thing for fifty-eight
years from 1641 and did it for a different reason again — there the merchant
aristocracy installed queens deliberately, four times running, precisely
because a queen could not lead an army or build a following among the war
captains. A mechanism for keeping the throne weak.

Two of the longest runs of reigning women anywhere in the Malay world, on two
sultanates in the same century, and neither of them a rule about women. Genuine
matrilineal succession exists — in Ghana, where al-Bakri states it outright, in
Makuria, in Asante, where the matriclan decided who could rule at all — and it
looks nothing like either of these.
