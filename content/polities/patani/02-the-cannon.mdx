Patani cast its own artillery, and the pieces were famous enough that the
*Hikayat Patani* names them: Seri Negara, Seri Patani, Nang Liu-liu. They were
large bronze guns, made in the town by a founder the chronicle identifies, and
they were what made a Siamese siege an expensive proposition rather than a
formality.

When Siam finally took the town in 1786 it carried them off. Seri Patani is in
Bangkok, outside the Ministry of Defence, under a Thai name.

## Why a port had guns

Patani was on the Gulf side of the peninsula, which put it on the route between
the China trade and the Siamese and Malay ports, and it did well out of the
period when Portuguese Melaka was driving Muslim shipping elsewhere. Chinese,
Japanese, Dutch and English trading posts were established there in the first
decades of the seventeenth century; the Dutch and English factories at Patani
predate their factories almost anywhere else in the archipelago.

`revenue_basis` on this record reads `trade-toll` alone, which makes it the
twelfth record on this site coded that way and the fifth in this region, after
Srivijaya, Melaka, Pasai and Johor. It is the standard coding for a port with no
hinterland worth taxing, and Patani had a hinterland but not a productive one.

What is unusual is what the money bought. The other four in this region bought protection from
somebody: the Melaka record bought a Ming guarantee, the Srivijaya and Johor
records bought the orang laut, and the Pasai record bought nothing and was taken
twice in three years.

Patani bought guns and a wall, and used them.

## 1634

Raja Ungu, the third of the four queens, refused the Siamese overlordship her
predecessors had accepted. A large Siamese army and fleet came south and were
beaten off.

The turning point on this record calls that the ordinary condition of the
peninsula rather than a contradiction — tribute bought the appearance of
submission and the substance was negotiable until somebody tested it. What made
Patani able to test it, in a way that Kedah and Kelantan and Terengganu could
not, was a foundry.
