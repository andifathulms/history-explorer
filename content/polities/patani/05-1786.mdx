Patani spent two hundred and eighty years as a nominal tributary of Ayutthaya
that behaved, in practice, as an independent state.

In 1634 Raja Ungu refused the overlordship her predecessors had accepted, put
that independence to an actual test, and a large Siamese army and fleet were
beaten off.

In 1786, nineteen years after Ayutthaya itself was destroyed by a Burmese army,
the dynasty that had replaced it at Bangkok took Patani, deported a large part
of the population north, and carried off the town's great cannon.

## Who did it

The polity that took Patani was not quite the one it had spent two centuries
managing.

Ayutthaya had been destroyed by a Burmese army in 1767, nineteen years earlier.
The Siamese state that reassembled itself afterwards under a new dynasty at
Bangkok was a different and more centralising thing than the one that fell: it
had lost its capital, its archive and much of its literature, and what it built
back was tighter.

Patani's two hundred and eighty years of negotiable tribute ended because the
party on the other side of the negotiation had been replaced by one that did
not negotiate. The new dynasty rebuilt Ayutthaya's city plan on a fresh bend of
the river and re-promulgated its law code, and then went south and collected
what the old arrangement had only ever pretended to owe.
