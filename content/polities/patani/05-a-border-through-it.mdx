This record stops in 1786. What followed is short and is on this page because the
sultanate's ending is not really 1786 and this site's fields cannot say when it
was.

Siam took the town, deported part of the population north, and carried off the
guns. In 1808 the territory was broken into seven small provinces under separate
rulers, which is a standard device for making a place ungovernable as a unit. In
1902 the last of the ruling line was removed and the area was absorbed into the
Siamese administrative system.

And in 1909, under an Anglo-Siamese treaty, Britain and Siam drew a line. Kedah,
Kelantan, Terengganu and Perlis went to the British side and are now in Malaysia.
Patani stayed on the Siamese side and is now the southern provinces of Thailand.

The line runs through what had been one Malay-speaking, Muslim, Jawi-writing
world, and it was drawn by two states neither of which was in it.

## Three records, one boundary

This corpus keeps producing these and it is worth counting them.

The Brunei record ends in 1888 having been given away in pieces to two chartered
companies and a private person, and the pieces are now in three countries. The
Sulu record's fourth chapter is about a word in an 1878 contract whose
translation determined which state a coastline belongs to. This record ends with
a boundary agreed in a European capital between an empire and a kingdom.

None of the three endings is a conquest in the sense this site's `ended.type`
vocabulary is built around. All three are documents, and this corpus's Sulu record
counts seven records across five regions whose ending is a signature and notes
that the vocabulary offers six values, all of them about force or dissolution,
and none about signature.

Patani's `ended.type` reads `conquest` for 1786, which is accurate about that
year and is not what ended the polity. What ended it was administrative — 1808,
1902, 1909 — and this site has no way to record an ending that took a hundred and
twenty-three years and three separate instruments.
