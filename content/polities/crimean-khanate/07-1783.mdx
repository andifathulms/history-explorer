On 8 April 1783 Catherine II signed a manifesto announcing that the Crimea, the
Taman peninsula and the Kuban were taken under Russian rule. There was no battle
and no treaty of cession. A document was issued, and the oldest surviving state
founded by the house of Chinggis stopped existing.

## The sequence

It took twelve years and almost no fighting.

In 1771 a Russian army forced the isthmus and occupied the peninsula. In 1774 the
treaty of Küçük Kaynarca declared the khanate independent of the Ottoman empire
and left Russia in possession of the keys to the Black Sea. Between 1774 and 1783
Russia installed and reinstalled Şahin Giray, a khan who had been educated partly
in Europe and who attempted to reform the state along Russian lines — a standing
army, a treasury, taxes on the clans — and provoked exactly the revolt that any
Crimean khan attacking the clan begs would have provoked. Russian troops put the
revolt down on his behalf, twice.

<Figure id="cr-potemkin" />

By 1783 there was a khan who could not govern without a foreign army, a clan
aristocracy that had twice risen against him, and no protector. The manifesto
recorded a fact.

## What was lost with it

A dynasty, a language of government, and about three hundred thousand people who
subsequently left.

The emigration is the part that is usually passed over. Tatars left the peninsula
for Ottoman territory in waves from the 1780s and in very large numbers after the
Crimean War in the 1850s and 1860s; the modern Crimean Tatar population of Turkey
and the Dobruja is the result. The peninsula was then settled with Russians,
Ukrainians, Greeks, Bulgarians and Germans under a deliberate policy, and the
Tatars were a minority in their own country within a century of the annexation.

What was also lost was the slave trade, and the honest account of the annexation
has to say so. The raids had stopped a generation earlier, under Ottoman and then
Russian pressure; the market at Caffa was finished; and the Russian frontier
settlement of the southern steppe, which had been impossible for three centuries,
began immediately.

## The last Chinggisid state in Europe

Şahin Giray left for Russia, then for Ottoman territory, and was executed on
Rhodes in 1787.

He was the fortieth-odd khan of a line that had ruled continuously since the
1440s, in unbroken male descent from Jochi, the eldest son of Chinggis Khan.
There had been Chinggisid rulers somewhere on the Eurasian steppe since 1206. In
the Kazakh hordes there would be khans of the same descent until the 1840s, but as
subjects of the Russian empire.

The state that Hacı Giray was brought from Lithuania to rule outlasted the Golden
Horde by two hundred and eighty-one years, the khanate of Kazan by two hundred and
thirty-one, and the Ottoman capacity to protect it by nine.
