Every other state founded by the house of Chinggis was gone by 1600. The
Crimean khanate was abolished in 1783, by a manifesto, three hundred and forty
years after it was founded and a century and a half after the last Chinggisid
had ruled anywhere else in Europe.

That is the fact worth putting first, because everything else about the khanate
is usually described as decline.

## Where it came from

The Golden Horde broke up in the fifteenth century into hordes that were not
territorial units so much as followings — a prince, his clans, and the pasture
they used. The Crimean peninsula had been a Jochid province with a governor, a
mint and a Genoese coast; what made it a separate state was that one branch of
the family settled on it and stopped moving.

Hacı Giray was brought in from exile in Lithuania in the 1440s by the peninsula's
great clans, of which the Şirin were the first. He was a Jochid, which the clans
required; he had no following of his own, which suited them; and the arrangement
that resulted — a khan who could not rule without the clan begs and clan begs who
could not rule without a khan — outlived him by three centuries.

## What made it permanent

The peninsula itself. Crimea is joined to the steppe by an isthmus a few miles
wide, and the Tatars fortified it and called it Perekop, the ditch. A horde on
open grass can be ridden down by another horde; a horde behind a ditch cannot,
and neither the Great Horde nor the Nogai nor Muscovy ever forced it.

Behind the ditch was something no other steppe polity had: agriculture, towns,
a coastline with ports on it, and a settled tax base. The khanate was not a
nomadic state that happened to own a peninsula. It was a peninsula with a
nomadic army attached, and the two halves paid for each other.

## The three relationships

From 1475 the khan was a vassal of the Ottoman sultan, who took the coastal
cities and left him the interior. From the 1480s Moscow was an ally against the
Great Horde and then an enemy. From the 1490s Poland–Lithuania was whichever of
those Moscow was not.

Each of the three paid the khan. The Ottomans paid in subsidy and appointments;
Moscow and Poland paid in what they called gifts and the khan's chancery called
tribute. None of them could afford to have him on the other side, and he was
perfectly aware of it.

## What the state was for

The other income was slaves, and it is not possible to write about this khanate
honestly without putting that near the front.

Crimean and Nogai horsemen raided the Russian, Ukrainian and Polish lands
annually for two and a half centuries and took captives on a scale that
depopulated the frontier. The captives went down to the coast, were sold in the
Ottoman ports, and were shipped across the Black Sea. Moscow and Warsaw ran
standing ransom funds. The steppe between the forest and the Black Sea was empty
in the sixteenth century because of it, and the Russian advance south in the
seventeenth and eighteenth was in the first instance an attempt to stop it.

A state can be described by its exports. This one exported people.
