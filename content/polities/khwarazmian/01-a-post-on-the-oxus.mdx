Khwarazm is the delta where the Oxus reaches the Aral Sea: irrigated, wealthy in
proportion to its size, and physically separate from everywhere else by desert.
The title *Khwarazmshah* is old, predating Islam by centuries, and the Seljuks
inherited it as the name of a provincial post.

In 1077 Malik-Shah gave the post to Anushtigin Gharchai, who was a Turkish
slave of the royal household — the man in charge of the sultan's washing bowl,
according to the tradition, which the sources repeat because it makes the ending
better.

His son held it after him. So did his son. By the mid-twelfth century the
Khwarazmshahs were hereditary governors paying tribute to a sultanate whose
capacity to collect it was declining, and the sequence from there is the one
this entire site is about. Atsiz rebelled against Sanjar repeatedly and was
repeatedly forgiven, because Sanjar had other problems and Khwarazm was far
away. Il-Arslan stopped pretending. Tekish stopped paying.

The Samanids did this to the Tahirids and the caliphate. Alptigin did it to the
Samanids. The Ghurids did it to the Ghaznavids. The difference here is only that
Khwarazm was a Seljuk creation from the first day — not an inherited province,
not a client house, not a defection, just an appointment that kept being
renewed until it did not need renewing.
