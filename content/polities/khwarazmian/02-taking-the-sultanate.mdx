In 1194 Ala al-Din Tekish met Tughril III, the last Great Seljuk sultan, in
battle near Rayy. Tughril was killed. The Seljuk sultanate of Iran ended there,
and Tekish took its territory and then its titulature: the Khwarazmshahs styled
themselves sultans in the Seljuk manner and presented their rule as the
continuation of the sultanate rather than as its replacement.

This site counts that as a successor claim on the Seljuk record. It is worth
being precise about why it counts when so many similar-looking things do not.
The Khwarazmshahs were descended from a Seljuk household slave, which under
these coding rules is genealogy and counts for nothing. What counts is that they
made a public claim, in their own titles and chancery style, on the legitimacy
of the polity they had just extinguished.

Tekish also did what everyone in this thread eventually does. He applied to the
caliph al-Nasir for investiture as sultan. Al-Nasir, who was engaged in the
unusual project of reconstructing actual Abbasid power in Iraq after three
hundred years, refused to be helpful about it, and the relationship between
Khwarazm and Baghdad was hostile from then on.

Under Ala al-Din Muhammad II the empire reached from the Aral Sea to the
Persian Gulf and from the Punjab frontier to Anatolia. In 1217 he marched on
Baghdad to install a caliph of his own choosing and was turned back by snow in
the Zagros passes.

There is no cited figure on this page for how large that empire was. It held
its greatest extent for about eight years, and the datasets this site uses do
not carry it.
