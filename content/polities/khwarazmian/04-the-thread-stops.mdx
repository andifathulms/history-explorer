Every ending in this corpus until now has been a handover. The Tahirids fall and
the Saffarids take Khurasan; the Samanids fall and Ghazna keeps the
administration, the chancery Persian and the slave-soldier system; the
Ghaznavids fall and the Ghurids inherit their Indian project; the Seljuks fall
and Khwarazm takes the sultanate and the titles.

1231 is not that. The Khwarazmian influence counts on this page are zero across
all three, and unlike the Saffarids, where the counts are null because nobody
looked, these are genuine zeroes: no descendant script, no religion carried
beyond a boundary, and no later polity that found the Khwarazmian name worth
claiming.

The Mongols did not continue the Khwarazmshahs in the way each previous
successor continued its predecessor. They dismantled the state, and what
eventually rose on the same ground — the Ilkhanate — was built by the
conquerors on their own terms, from a different direction, with a different
legitimating story.

That is why the eight polities on this site stop here rather than continuing.
The thread is a single mechanism running through four hundred years: an
instrument of a state — a governor, a guard corps, a client house, an appointed
post — becoming heavier than the thing it served, and taking its place while
keeping its forms. In 1231 something arrived from outside that was not an
instrument of anything here, and the mechanism had nothing to work on.

The site is not making the claim that history stopped. It is marking the point
where this particular pattern did.
