In 1218 a trade caravan under Mongol protection arrived at Otrar, on the
Khwarazmian frontier. The governor, Inalchuq, had the merchants killed as spies
and kept the goods. Chinggis Khan sent envoys to demand redress. Ala al-Din
Muhammad had the senior envoy killed and the others sent back shaved.

The Mongol invasion arrived in 1219. It took about two years.

The military decision is the part historians dwell on. Muhammad had a large army
and chose to distribute it across his cities rather than concentrate it, on the
reasoning that the fortified places of Transoxiana could not all be taken.
Otrar, Bukhara, Samarkand and Gurganj were reduced one after another. Nishapur,
Merv and Herat followed, and the destruction in Khurasan was of a different
order from anything else in this corpus — cities that had been continuously
central to the region since the Tahirids ceased to be central.

Muhammad fled west and died of pleurisy on an island in the Caspian in 1220,
with no territory left.

His son Jalal al-Din Mingburnu is the reason this page gives an end range of
1220 to 1231. He fought the Mongols at the Indus in 1221 and, when the line
broke, rode his horse off the bank into the river and swam it — the one moment
of Khwarazmian history everybody knows, and one that Chinggis is said to have
admired. He spent the next decade in India, Iran and the Caucasus, gathering
forces, fighting Mongols, Seljuks of Rum and Ayyubids in turn, and was killed by
a Kurdish villager in 1231 while in flight.

Whether the empire ends in 1220 with the state or in 1231 with the man is the
same question the Samanid page asks about al-Muntasir, and this site answers it
the same way: it keeps both and does not choose.
