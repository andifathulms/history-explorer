Every dynasty in this part of the world for four hundred years had been
succeeded by something that kept its forms. The Tahirids fell and the Saffarids
took Khurasan. The Samanids fell and Ghazna kept the administration, the chancery
Persian and the slave-soldier system. The Ghaznavids fell and the Ghurids
inherited their Indian project. The Seljuks fell and Khwarazm took the sultanate
and the titles.

1231 is not that, and the difference is the whole point of the chapter.

## Three zeroes

The Khwarazmshahs left no descendant script, carried no religion beyond a
boundary, and had no successor that thought their name was worth claiming.

That last one is the striking absence. Claiming a predecessor was the standard
move of the period — the Khwarazmshahs had done it themselves to the Seljuks
twenty-five years earlier, in their titles and their chancery style, on a state
they had just destroyed. Nobody did it to them. The Mongols did not need to and
the local dynasties that came up afterwards under Mongol licence had a Mongol
patron to point at instead.

There was, briefly, a Khwarazmian afterlife of a sort in the wrong place. The
remnants of Jalal al-Din's army wandered into Syria as a mercenary corps, took
Jerusalem in 1244 for an Ayyubid employer, and were destroyed as a fighting
force within two years. That is what a dynasty's inheritance looks like when
there is nothing to inherit: several thousand horsemen for hire, a long way from
home, remembered for sacking a city they had no interest in.

## What was actually different

The mechanism that had run the eastern Islamic world for four centuries was
always the same one. An instrument of a state — a governor, a guard corps, a
client house, an appointed post — becomes heavier than the thing it serves and
takes its place, keeping the forms because the forms are what make the new
holder legible.

Every dynasty in the sequence is a version of it. The Tahirids were governors.
The Samanids were governors. The Ghaznavids were a guard. The Seljuks were
frontier auxiliaries. The Khwarazmshahs were an appointed post, held by the
descendants of a household slave.

In 1219 something arrived that had never been anybody's instrument. It had not
been recruited, appointed, invested or paid; it owed nobody a form; and it had
no use for the titles, which is why it did not take them.

## What that cost

The cities are the measure. Nishapur, Merv, Herat, Gurganj, Bukhara — the
places where the Persian revival had been paid for, where the libraries were,
where the canal systems had been maintained since before Islam — were reduced
inside two years, and several of them never recovered their position.

The eastern Iranian world after 1231 was governed from Tabriz and Sultaniyya by
Mongols, and later by their descendants who had become Muslims and Persian
speakers and patrons of exactly the kind of court their great-grandfathers had
destroyed. That is a continuity of a sort, and it is not the same thing. It was
built by the conquerors on their own terms, from a different direction, with a
different story about why they were entitled to it.
