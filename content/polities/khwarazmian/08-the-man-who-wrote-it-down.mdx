---
title: The man who wrote it down worked for them
drafted_from: morgan-2007
phase: aside
---

Almost everything known about the destruction of this polity comes from
Persians employed by the people who destroyed it.

Juvayni was born into a family of Khurasani administrators who had served the
Khwarazmshahs, entered Mongol service as a young man, travelled to Mongolia
twice, and wrote his history of the conquests — the *Tarikh-i Jahangusha*, the
history of the world-conqueror — in the 1250s while working for Hulegu. He was
present at the reduction of Alamut in 1256, went through the Isma'ili library
there and chose what to save before the rest was burned, and was later governor
of Baghdad.

His position is therefore impossible and he knows it. He is a Muslim writing
about the destruction of Muslim cities for a patron whose grandfather ordered
it, in Persian, for readers who had lived through it. What he produces is not
propaganda and not lament but something stranger: a work that gives the
casualty figures and the sack of Merv and Nishapur in full, calls the invasion
a punishment God visited on Islam for its sins — which relieves the Mongols of
agency without excusing them — and then argues that Mongol rule is now
restoring order and that men like himself should serve it.

Two things follow. The first is that the enormous casualty figures this period
is famous for come substantially from him and from writers using him, and modern
historians treat them as literary rather than statistical.

The second is subtler and worse. A history written by the losers' administrators
for the winners will register the destruction of a state and not the state
itself. Nobody had a reason to write down how Khwarazm was governed, what it
raised in revenue, or how large it was, because the book that had to be written
was about how it fell. An empire that briefly ran from the Aral Sea to the
Persian Gulf has no figure attached to it anywhere, and that is not an accident
of survival. It is what happens when the only man to write the account is
employed by the people who ended the subject.
