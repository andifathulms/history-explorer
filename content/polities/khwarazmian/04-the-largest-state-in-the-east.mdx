By 1217 the Khwarazmshah ruled from the Aral Sea to the Persian Gulf and from
the Indian frontier to the edge of Anatolia. On a map it is the largest thing
between the Mediterranean and China, and it had been assembled in a single
reign.

It is worth setting out what that empire actually consisted of, because the
speed with which it disappeared two years later is not a mystery once you have.

## Assembled, not built

Everything in it had belonged to somebody else within living memory. The Seljuk
sultanate had been taken in 1194. The Qara Khitai protectorate over Transoxiana
had been broken by about 1210, and the Karakhanids who had held Samarkand under
it were extinguished in 1212. The Ghurid empire in the east came apart after
1206 and Khwarazm took Khurasan out of the wreck; Firuzkuh itself fell to
Muhammad in 1215.

Three dynasties in twenty-one years, and none of the territory had been governed
from Gurganj for longer than a few years when the Mongols arrived. There had
been no time to make anybody's grandchildren loyal.

## The army it was held with

The core of the army was Qangli and Qipchaq Turkmen from the steppe north of the
Aral, and they came in with the sultan's mother.

Terken Khatun was of that stock, and she kept her own court, her own chancery
and her own patronage, and a faction of Qangli commanders who answered to her
rather than to her son. Orders went out from Gurganj under two seals. The
commanders in the great cities of Transoxiana were her kinsmen, appointed by
her, with a claim on her protection.

An army recruited from the same steppe that the Mongols came out of, commanded
by men whose loyalty ran to the sultan's mother, garrisoning cities conquered
within the last decade, on behalf of a ruler who had just quarrelled with the
caliph. That is the state that met Chinggis Khan.

## Why it looked strong

None of this was obvious at the time, and it should not be made to look obvious
now. Muhammad had beaten the Qara Khitai, who had beaten Sanjar. He had
swallowed the Ghurids, who had swallowed the Ghaznavids. His cities were the
richest in the eastern Islamic world and his revenues were enormous.

What he did not have was any relationship in his empire that was older than his
own reign, and no way of finding out that this mattered until it was tested.
