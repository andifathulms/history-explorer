This record had no institutions block at all until this pass. Four centuries of
the Han, on a site that codes four fields for two hundred polities, carrying
none of them.

The worst of the four omissions was `revenue_basis`, because fourteen records
here already carried `monopoly` and the Han carried none — and the Han salt and
iron monopolies are the most thoroughly argued fiscal institution anywhere in the
ancient world.

## What was taken over

From 117 BC the state took the production and sale of salt and of iron, and
later of liquor, out of private hands. Emperor Wu had been at war with the
Xiongnu for twenty years by then and the ordinary taxes did not cover it: a land
tax at a thirtieth of the harvest was deliberately light, and the poll tax fell
on people who could not pay much more.

Salt and iron were different. Everybody needs salt, nobody can make it at home,
and it is produced at a small number of identifiable places — coastal pans and
inland wells. Iron is the same shape of commodity. A state that controls a few
hundred production sites can tax the whole population without knowing where any
of them live.

## The transcript

What makes this record's `monopoly` value unusual is that the argument about it
survives.

In 81 BC, six years after Wu died, the court held a debate on whether to keep the
monopolies. On one side the officials who ran them, arguing fiscal necessity and
frontier defence; on the other a delegation of Confucian scholars, arguing that
the state had gone into business, was competing with its own people, and had
made profit a respectable motive at court. A version of it was written up a
generation later as the *Discourses on Salt and Iron*, and it is a substantial
book.

The scholars lost on salt and iron and won on liquor.

The `revenue_basis` field on this site holds seven possible values and no room
for any of that. What it can say is `land-tax`, `poll-tax`, `monopoly` — three
values, which is more than most records here carry, and the least informative
description of this state's finances that could be true.
