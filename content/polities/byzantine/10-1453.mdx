By 1400 the emperor was an Ottoman vassal who had campaigned in Anatolia in the
sultan's army. Manuel II spent the years from 1399 to 1403 travelling in western
Europe asking for help, was received in Paris and London with great courtesy, and
got very little. What saved the city was Timur, who destroyed the Ottoman army at
Ankara in 1402 and set off a civil war among Bayezid's sons that bought
Constantinople half a century.

In 1439 the emperor and the Greek church accepted union with Rome at the Council
of Florence, in exchange for the help that was the only way the city could be
held. The population of Constantinople substantially rejected it. The remark
attributed to a senior official — better the sultan's turban than the cardinal's
hat — is probably not authentic and is an accurate summary of the position.

## Fifty-three days

Mehmed II brought something in the order of eighty thousand men and a siege train
including a gun cast by a Hungarian engineer who had offered it to the emperor
first and had not been able to be paid.

The defence was about seven thousand, of whom perhaps two thousand were foreign,
mostly Genoese and Venetian. The chain across the Golden Horn held until the
Ottomans dragged ships overland into the harbour. The land walls of Anthemius,
which had not been breached in a thousand and forty years, were breached on 29
May 1453. Constantine XI was killed somewhere in the fighting and his body was
not identified.

## What the fields say and do not

This record's `ended.type` is `conquest`, `by` names the Ottoman record, and its
single `successor_claims` entry is also the Ottomans, on the title Kayser-i Rûm
that Mehmed took with the city — the same claim the Roman record on this site
counts, made once and counted twice, because both records existed to be claimed.

What the fields cannot say is the thing the whole record is about, and the aside
on this page's name says it: the state that ended in 1453 called itself the
empire of the Romans, its inhabitants called themselves Romans, and its
neighbours agreed. There is no field on this site for *this is the same polity as
the one three records up*, and the `resumes` field that exists for the two
Babylons will not take it, because the two records are contiguous rather than
separated by a dark age.

So the corpus carries Rome and Byzantium as two records joined by an edge that
says `partitioned from`, and every chapter on both of them that touches the
question says the division is a bookkeeping convenience. That is the best this
data model can do, and stating it repeatedly is cheaper than pretending the model
is neutral.

## Afterwards

Trebizond, the other Greek successor state, fell in 1461. The Morea in 1460.
Mehmed rebuilt and repopulated Constantinople as his capital, kept the Greek
patriarchate and appointed a patriarch, and used the imperial title. This
corpus's Ottoman record is the one that carries what he did with it.

Hagia Sophia became a mosque, which is why the dome the second chapter of this
record describes is still standing: a building in continuous use is maintained,
and a building nobody needs is quarried.
