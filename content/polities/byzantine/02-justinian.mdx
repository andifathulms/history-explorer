Justinian reigned thirty-eight years from 527 and spent them trying to be the
Roman emperor of the record above this one.

Africa was taken from the Vandals in 534 in a campaign of a few months. Sicily
followed, then Italy, in a war against the Ostrogoths that Belisarius began
briskly in 535 and that then ran for eighteen years, wrecked the Italian
peninsula, depopulated Rome and ended with an exhausted province that could not
pay for its own defence. A strip of southern Spain was taken in 552. At his
death the Mediterranean was again a Roman sea on three sides, and the empire was
at its largest since the fourth century.

## What it cost

The eastern frontier was left to be managed by payment: Justinian bought two
peaces from Khusrau I and the second was called the Eternal Peace and lasted
eight years. The Balkans were raided repeatedly by Slavs and by Kutrigurs, once
to the walls of the capital. The taxation required to fund the western wars is
the loudest complaint in the sources — including in the *Secret History* of
Procopius, who wrote the official campaign narrative and then wrote a second book
calling the emperor a demon.

And in 541 the plague arrived. It reached Constantinople in 542, killed on a
scale the sources describe in the language of catastrophe and modern estimates
cannot fix, and recurred in waves for two centuries. Whatever the number, the
empire that fought the Persians in the 570s was not the empire that had taken
Africa forty years earlier, and the Italy that had been reconquered was taken by
the Lombards within three years of Justinian's death.

## What survived him

Two things, and neither is territory.

The law. A commission under Tribonian compressed a thousand years of Roman
jurisprudence into the Code, the Digest, the Institutes and the Novels in six
years. It may be the single most consequential document produced by any European
state: rediscovered in eleventh-century Bologna, it is the foundation of the
civil law tradition that governs most of continental Europe, Latin America and a
good deal else. A legal system is not the kind of thing that shows up in an
account of territory or religion or script, and this one outlasted every empire
that ever used it.

And Hagia Sophia, rebuilt in five years after a riot burned the previous church,
with a dome on pendentives over a nave nobody had attempted to span that way.
It was the largest enclosed space in the world for eight hundred years, it is
what the Rus envoys in the tenth century reported having seen, and it is a
mosque now because of what happened in 1453.

## The word reconquest

It is worth noticing that nobody at the time called it that.

Justinian's own legislation describes the African campaign as the recovery of
provinces from usurpers, and the legal position throughout was that the western
territories had never stopped being Roman — they had been under people who had
no right to them. The same assumption runs through everything the empire said
about itself: there had been no break, in 395, in 476, or afterwards.

The division of 395 is a bookkeeping convenience.
This chapter is what that looks like from the eastern side, a hundred and forty
years later, with an army in Carthage.
