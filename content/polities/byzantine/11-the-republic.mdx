This record's `legitimation` field reads `divine-sanction, descent, election`,
and the third value is doing something unusual. This aside is the argument behind
it, and the argument is contested.

## The old picture

The standard account of Byzantine government, for most of the last two hundred
years, is a sacral autocracy: an emperor chosen by God, ruling without
constitutional limit, surrounded by a court ceremonial designed to make him
appear more than human, in a state where church and throne were fused and
politics consisted of palace intrigue. The frequent depositions are read as
evidence of instability — a system that did not work.

## Kaldellis's argument

His case is that this gets the constitution backwards. The eastern Roman empire
was a *res publica* — the word is in the sources, in Greek as *politeia* —
whose emperor held power on behalf of the people and could lose it. The
acclamation by senate, army and people at every accession was not decoration but
the constitutive act. The emperor was not above the law in the sources' own
account of him. And the depositions are not evidence of a broken system: they are
what a constitution without a formal removal mechanism looks like when it removes
somebody, and they follow a recognisable pattern — the emperor loses a war or
debases the coinage or raises taxes, the population of the capital riots, the
army or the senate acts, and the successor is acclaimed.

On this reading, roughly half the emperors between 395 and 1453 being deposed is
not chaos. It is a republic with an unusually violent recall procedure.

## Why the field carries `election` anyway

Because the coding does not require the argument to be right.

Coding rule 8's test is publicity: on what public ground was the right to rule
asserted. What is not in dispute is that every accession involved an acclamation,
that the ceremony was recorded and described, that usurpers sought it, and that
the sources treat an unacclaimed emperor as lacking something. That is the same
test and the same evidence on which this site's Roman record carries `election`,
where the mechanism is the law voted by senate and people and the bronze of one
survives.

What the field is not asserting is that the acclamation had teeth, which is
exactly where Kaldellis and his critics disagree. A coded value records what
ground was claimed, not whether the claim was honoured — the same reason coding
rule 1 counts unrecognised successor claims alongside recognised ones.

## Why it matters for this site

Because the alternative reading is a specific historiographical position with a
history, and the aside on this record's name is about that history.

The picture of Byzantium as an oriental theocracy in permanent decline was built
by western scholarship that had reasons to want the Roman inheritance for the
Latin west, and it is the same picture that produced the name. A site that codes
`divine-sanction` and stops has adopted half of it without noticing. Coding
`election` beside it, and writing this chapter, is the alternative — and the
honest form of that alternative is to say the argument is live rather than to
present it as settled.
