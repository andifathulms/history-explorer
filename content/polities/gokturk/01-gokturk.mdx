---
title: The first people to be called Turks
drafted_from: golden-1992
phase: formation
---

In 552 a subject people of the Rouran, ironworkers of the Altai, revolted under
Bumin and destroyed their overlords. The polity they created is the first in
history whose people called themselves Turk, and within twenty-five years it
reached from Manchuria to the Black Sea — the largest steppe empire before the
Mongols.

Its western half, under Bumin's brother Istami, destroyed the Hephthalites in
alliance with the Sasanians, then fell out with them over the silk trade and sent
an embassy to Constantinople in 568 proposing a joint attack on Persia. That
embassy is the point at which the steppe becomes a party to the diplomacy between
the Mediterranean and Iran rather than a frontier problem for both.

The khaganate carried the runiform Old Turkic script counted on this page — the
Orkhon inscriptions, raised by a later revival of the same house, are the oldest
substantial texts in any Turkic language and are notable for being a steppe
empire's account of itself rather than a Chinese description of one.

It split into eastern and western halves in 603 and both were eventually absorbed
by Tang China, before a second khaganate and then the Uyghurs on this site's
inner-asia page took the same ground.
