Almish, king of the Bulghars on the middle Volga, had written to the
Abbasid caliph al-Muqtadir asking for teachers of Islamic law, a
mosque, a minbar so the Friday sermon could be read in his name, and
a fortress against the kings who threatened him. The caliph agreed,
and the embassy that carried his answer - a party that included the
diplomat Ahmad ibn Fadlan, whose own account of the journey
survives - reached Almish's camp after a journey of many months, on
12 May 922. Four kings rode out to escort the travellers in; the
next day Almish himself rode out to meet them, dismounted, and
prostrated himself before the caliph's letter, scattering coins over
his visitors as a sign of welcome.

## A sermon read wrong

The party rested four days while Almish summoned the leaders of his
people to hear the letter read. It was only once the formalities
were under way that Ibn Fadlan discovered a problem: the Friday
sermon at Almish's own camp had been calling him "King Yiltawar," a
form of royal address Ibn Fadlan judged inappropriate, since in his
view only God should be addressed as king from a pulpit. He told
Almish the caliph's own preferred formula named the caliph as God's
servant and slave, never as a king in his own right, and asked what
name should be used for Almish instead. Almish's answer went further
than Ibn Fadlan had expected.

## Ja'far, son of Abd Allah

Almish refused to have his own father's name read out, because his
father had died an unbeliever, and asked instead for the caliph's own
name, Ja'far. Told he could take it, he chose it on the spot, naming
his father Abd Allah - "servant of God" - in place of the name his
unbelieving father had actually held. The sermon was corrected on
the spot: "O God, preserve in good health your slave Ja'far, son of
Abd Allah, amir of the Bulghars, client of the Commander of the
Faithful." It was a small change in wording, made in an afternoon,
but it bound the Bulghar kingship formally to Baghdad rather than to
the customs of the steppe kings around it - the same investiture that
had already brought Almish an official mosque and the right to have
his own minbar built.
