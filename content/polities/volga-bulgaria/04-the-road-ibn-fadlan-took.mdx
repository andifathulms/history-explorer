The Mongol advance into the lands Ibn Fadlan's embassy had once
crossed began at the western end of the same road. In 1220 the
geographer Yaqut, then sheltering at Gurganj - the Khwarazmian
capital where Ibn Fadlan's own party had once waited three frozen
months for the ice to break before pushing north to Bulghar - heard
rumours of the Mongols' approach and fled for Aleppo in time. The
following year the Mongol army razed Gurganj to the ground and
flooded the ruins by turning the Oxus itself against the city.

## The kingdom Ibn Fadlan had recognized

The Bulghar kingdom that had received Ibn Fadlan's embassy three
centuries earlier, survived the Rus sack of 969, and grown by the
1130s into the leading fur market in the world did not escape the
same armies for long. In 1237 the Mongols conquered the Volga
Bulghars outright, ending Bulghar kingship on the middle Volga less
than two decades after the embassy road itself had been broken at
its western end. The kingdom that Almish had bound to Baghdad with
a change of wording in a Friday sermon ceased, after that, to answer
to a king of its own.
