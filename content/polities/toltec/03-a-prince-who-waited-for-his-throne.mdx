title: A prince who waited for his throne

The generations of kings who followed Chalchiuh Tlatonac left the
Toltec chronicles thin on specific events, but by the ninth century the
throne had passed, through a line the sources trace only unevenly, to a
king whose murder left his own young son unable to claim the crown
outright. Rather than press an immediate and probably hopeless claim,
the prince withdrew to a city near Tollan to wait, quietly building
support among sympathetic nobles across the wider Toltec domain and
working out, in the meantime, the religious reforms he intended to
pursue once in power.

## A throne won by reputation rather than force

The prince's patience paid off. On the death of the king who had
succeeded his murdered father - possibly his own brother, the sources are
not certain - broad support for his own claim, built as much on
religious conviction as on hereditary right, carried him to the throne
at Tollan. He took the name Topiltzin Ceacatl Quetzalcoatl, and the
chronology later chroniclers assign to his accession, however uncertain
in its particulars, is usually placed around 873.

## A reign every later tradition would remember as golden

Under Quetzalcoatl, Toltec influence extended, by voluntary allegiance
rather than conquest, across an enormous stretch of territory - from
Tehuantepec in the south to Zacatecas in the north, few provinces
between the two seas failing to acknowledge Tollan's own primacy. The
city itself, already the political capital under the older alliance
with Culhuacan, now surpassed every rival in the magnificence of its
temples and palaces and the skill of its own artists, becoming in
practice, if not always in formal precedence, the true metropolis of the
whole Toltec world.
