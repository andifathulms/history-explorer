Acxitl, ruling as the successor of Quetzalcoatl's own line, marched out
to meet the invading forces directly but was defeated in every
engagement he fought. While he campaigned, a rival faction inside Tollan
opened the city itself to a band of invaders under their own leader,
and civil war broke out in the streets between three rival factions
until the capital's noble buildings were, once again, badly damaged.
Fighting between the allied Toltec kingdoms and pestilence and famine
returned to the land at the same time.

## A last stand that could not hold

Acxitl ordered a final stand at Tultitlan, where the battle raged for
days before the Toltec forces were driven back step by step - first to
Tollan itself, then to Xaltocan, Teotihuacan and Xochitlalpan in
succession. Huemac and Xochitl were killed in this retreat, along with
several of the kingdom's remaining generals. Acxitl himself escaped the
rout by hiding in a cave on the island of Xico in Lake Chalco, while
what remained of the Toltec army scattered into the mountains and lake
marshes.

## A kingdom's children entrusted to strangers

From his hiding place, Acxitl made a final secret visit to Culhuacan,
gathered what loyal followers remained to him, and announced his own
intention to leave the country for the homeland his ancestors had once
been exiled from, promising to intercede there on his people's behalf.
He entrusted his two infant children to guardians who would raise them
without revealing their royal birth, and left in 1062. Other Toltec
nobles scattered in different directions rather than follow him -
some to the coasts, from which their own descendants would later send
parties back to resettle the valley; others remained where they were,
absorbed into the cities that had once answered to Tollan. What
disappeared with Acxitl's own flight was a ruling dynasty, not a people:
most Toltecs stayed in the land their ancestors had held, and simply
became subjects, in the end, of whichever chiefs the invasions had left
standing.
