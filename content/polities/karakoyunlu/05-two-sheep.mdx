title: Why there were two
drafted_from: chi-6
phase: aside

The Kara Koyunlu and the Ak Koyunlu were not opposed for any reason a doctrine
or a dynasty explains. They were two pastoral confederations competing for the
same summer and winter pastures across eastern Anatolia and Azerbaijan, in
country that will support a limited number of flocks, and everything else
followed from that.

It shaped their politics entirely. Because they were competing with each other,
each was available as an ally to anyone fighting the other: the Ak Koyunlu took
Timur's side and were given Diyarbakır for it; the Kara Koyunlu took the
Ottomans' and later the Mamluks'. Because their strength was mounted tribal
manpower rather than revenue, both could win battles far from home and neither
could garrison what it won. And because both were confederations rather than
states, each was one succession away from coming apart, which is what happened
to this one in 1467 and to the other in the 1490s.

The two names have obscured this for a long time. Black Sheep and White Sheep
sound like factions in a quarrel, and the quarrel gets explained by dynastic
ambition or by confessional difference — the Kara Koyunlu are often called
Shi'i and the Ak Koyunlu Sunni, which oversimplifies both. The competition was
older than either dynasty and would have existed without them.

That is the pattern the fifteenth century in this region actually has, and it
is the background to two things this site records elsewhere.

The first is on the Trebizond page, where the last emperor's father married a
daughter to Uzun Hasan of the Ak Koyunlu in the hope that a Turkmen
confederation in western Iran would come west against the Ottomans.

The second is the Safavids. This site's Safavid page has Isma'il taking Tabriz
in 1501 at the head of Qizilbash tribal followers — Turkmen, from exactly this
world, organised by a Sufi order rather than by a pasture dispute. The
confederations were the material the Safavid state was made out of, and the
thing Isma'il added was the one thing neither sheep ever had, which was a
reason for the tribes to hold together that outlasted a defeat.
