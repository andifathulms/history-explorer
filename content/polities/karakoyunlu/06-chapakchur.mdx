In the autumn of 1467 Jahan Shah moved west against Uzun Hasan of the White
Sheep, wintered in the field, and was surprised near Chapakchur in the country
between Mush and Bingöl. His army was scattered and he was killed.

What happened next is the clearest illustration in this corpus of what a
confederation is. Jahan Shah had ruled thirty years, held Azerbaijan, Iraq, Fars
and Kerman, and had a son, Hasan Ali, who inherited a treasury, the fortresses
and a claim. Within a year there was nothing. Hasan Ali could not hold Tabriz,
could not raise the tribes his father had raised, was beaten, imprisoned by his
own side and killed in 1469. Uzun Hasan took the whole territory.

A dynastic state loses a battle and continues; the administration keeps
collecting, the garrisons stay in place, and a defeated king can raise another
army from the same country. A confederation loses a battle and stops, because
what it was in the first place was an agreement among tribal leaders that this
family's fortune was the one worth following, and the battle is the evidence
that it is not. This site codes both sheep `tribal-levy` and neither with a
revenue basis its own sources describe in detail, and the two facts are the same
fact.

The Black Sheep do not reappear. Some of the tribes are visible afterwards
inside the Ak Koyunlu and then inside the Qizilbash, which is where the human
material of both confederations ended up. But there was no restoration and no
claimant, and ninety-four years of a power that at one point held Herat left no
successor claim on this site's record, because nobody afterwards thought its
legitimacy was worth asserting.
