---
title: A steppe dynasty that took all of China
drafted_from: franke-twitchett-1994
phase: peak
---

The Liao took part of northern China. The Jin took more. Kublai took all of it,
finishing the Southern Song in 1279 and becoming the first ruler from the steppe
to govern the entire country.

He did it by becoming, in part, a Chinese emperor: proclaiming a dynastic name in
the Chinese manner in 1271, building a capital at Dadu on the site of modern
Beijing, performing the imperial rites. He also did not: Mongols and Central
Asians were kept as a separate and privileged legal category above northern and
then southern Chinese, the civil service examinations were suspended for decades
because they would have staffed the administration with the wrong people, and
much of the revenue apparatus was run by Muslim and Uyghur financial
administrators brought from the west.

That deliberate refusal to assimilate is the same problem the Liao solved with
two administrations and the Chagatais failed to solve at all, and the Yuan
answer — rule China with Chinese methods but not with Chinese people — held for
under a century.

The Phags-pa script counted on this page is the sharpest illustration of the
ambition. Kublai commissioned a Tibetan monk to design a single alphabet capable
of writing Mongolian, Chinese, Tibetan and Uyghur in one system, for an empire
that spanned all four. It was promulgated in 1269, used on seals and coinage and
official documents, and abandoned when the dynasty fell.
