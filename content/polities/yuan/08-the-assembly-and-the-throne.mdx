Every other dynasty that held the whole of China lasted two or three centuries.
The Yuan lasted less than one, and the largest single reason is that it never
changed how it chose an emperor.

The Mongol rule was an assembly of the eligible, raising a successor from among
all the eligible.

## What that is for

On the steppe it is a good rule. A confederation whose survival depends on the
competence of the man leading it cannot afford to be handed a child, and an
assembly of the leading families choosing among adult claimants will usually
produce somebody who can command. It is a large part of why the Mongol empire
reached the size it did.

In a settled empire it is a war. The pool of eligible Chinggisids is enormous,
every one of them has a following, the assembly can be packed or convened twice,
and the loser of a disputed succession has an army rather than a monastery.

The Yuan never replaced it. Kublai's own accession in 1260 was decided by two
rival assemblies and four years of fighting his brother. Between 1307 and 1333
the throne changed hands eight times, and in 1323 an emperor was murdered by his
own guard commanders at the summer camp.

## Why they did not fix it

This is the part worth dwelling on, because the alternative was neither obscure
nor untested.

The Chinese answer — a designated heir apparent, named in advance, with the rule
written down — was available, thoroughly documented, and being used by every
dynasty whose records the Yuan's own historians were at that moment compiling.
The Ming would codify it within a generation. The Han had run on it for four
centuries, at the cost of a great many child emperors.

Adopting it would have meant conceding that the emperor of China was something
other than the Great Khan: that the throne descended by a Chinese rule rather
than being conferred by Mongol nobles. And the Yuan ruler was answerable to three
constituencies at once — the Chinggisid princes who elected him, the Chinese
bureaucracy that administered for him, and the population being ruled. Each
required a different account of why he held the throne. Fixing the succession
meant choosing among them.

They did not choose. They got eight emperors in twenty-six years, and then a
rising among conscripted labourers on the Yellow River, and then the Ming.
