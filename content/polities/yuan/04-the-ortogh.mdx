`revenue_basis` on this record reads `land-tax` and `monopoly` — a Chinese land
tax and the salt monopoly, which supplied the largest single share of the money.
Both values are accurate and both would fit any dynasty on this site's East Asia
pages.

What they leave out has no value in the vocabulary at all.

## Merchant partnerships with imperial capital

The *ortogh* were associations of merchants, largely Central Asian and mostly
Muslim, financed directly by Mongol princes and by the imperial household. A
prince put up silver; the partnership traded with it across the length of the
empire and beyond it; the profits were divided.

They also farmed taxes. A partnership would advance the state a sum against the
revenue of a district and then collect it, at whatever it could extract. And they
lent — to peasants, at rates the Yuan's own edicts periodically tried to cap,
against the security of land and people.

So the imperial family was simultaneously the government, the largest investor in
the empire's long-distance trade, the creditor of its taxpayers, and the
beneficiary of the tax-farming contracts. This corpus's Venice and Genoa records
describe states run by merchants. The Yuan is the inverse: merchants run as an
arm of the state's private capital.

## Why the field cannot say it

The seven values in `revenue_basis` describe where a state's income comes from —
land, heads, tolls, tribute, plunder, mining, monopoly. They are categories of
*extraction*. The ortogh are a category of *finance*, and the schema has no place
to record that a polity's rulers were also its principal investors.

This corpus's Kediri record has a chapter about a kingdom whose bequest was a
literature and whose three influence counts read zero, and it says the counts
were chosen so they could be applied uniformly to two hundred records, that
literature cannot be counted that way without becoming an opinion wearing a
number, and that the rulebook is right and the record is poorer for it.

The same holds here, with less comfort. `land-tax` and `monopoly` are not wrong
about the Yuan. They are simply what any other dynasty would say, on a record
where the interesting fiscal fact is one nobody else on this site has.
