What followed Wuti's death unraveled fast. His third son Wenti,
installed by Hou Jing as a puppet, was murdered by his own minister
within three years; Wenti's brother Yuandi overthrew Hou Jing in turn
and had his body publicly displayed, only to be besieged himself within
three more years by the general Chen Baxian.

Yuandi's own surrender came with a gesture that outlasted the man who
made it: he broke his sword and burned a library of a hundred and forty
thousand volumes, declaring "all is over! All my skill in war and
letters henceforth becomes useless to me" - and was killed shortly
afterward, his capital handed over to plunder.

The last Liang ruler, Jingdi, Yuandi's own ninth son, held the throne
two more years before Chen Baxian murdered him in 557 and founded his
own dynasty in the Liang's place - closing a fifty-five-year span in
which one exceptional forty-seven-year reign was followed, within a
single decade, by the same cycle of murder and usurpation that had
ended every dynasty before it.
