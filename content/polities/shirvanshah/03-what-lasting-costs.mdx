title: How to last seven hundred years

The Shirvanshahs ran from 799 to 1538. Almost nothing else in the medieval
Islamic world lasted that long, and the ones that did were empires.

Ask how they governed, how they raised troops, or how the throne passed, and the
answer is that it depends entirely on which century you mean. Across those seven
hundred years this polity was an Abbasid governorship, an independent
principality, a Seljuk vassal, a tributary of the Ilkhanate, a Timurid client and
a Safavid dependency. The way it raised troops in the tenth century against Rus
raiders coming down the Volga has nothing in common with the way it did in the
fifteenth. There is no arrangement that held for most of it.

## The method

What can be described is not an institution but a technique, and it is consistent
even when nothing else is.

Submit early, before being attacked. Pay. Be useful on the frontier to whoever is
currently the power — against the Khazars for the caliphate, against the
Georgians and the Kipchaks for the Seljuks, against the Golden Horde for Timur.
Hold the mountains and the passes, which are expensive to take. Be cheap enough
to leave alone that nobody bothers. Do not fight for independence, and do not
lose the country.

It is the opposite of everything a dynasty is usually praised for, and it worked
for longer than any of the empires it submitted to.

## Others who tried it

The Artuqids acknowledged nine sovereignties in two hundred and seventy-five
years, and recorded them on a rock at Mardin. The emperors of Trebizond married
their daughters to whoever might protect them, and outlived Constantinople. The
Bavandids in the Caspian mountains persisted through six centuries of everybody.

What these have in common, besides longevity, is that they are hard to say much
about. A polity that survives by never committing to anything leaves behind no
characteristic institution, no reforming ruler, and very little that a historian
can hold up and describe. The Shirvanshahs are the most successful practitioners
of the method and the least visible dynasty in the region — and those two facts
are the same fact.
