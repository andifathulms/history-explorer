---
title: A Timurid with nowhere to go
drafted_from: richards-1993
phase: formation

Babur was a great-great-great-grandson of Timur and, on his mother's side, a
descendant of Chinggis Khan. He spent his youth failing to hold Samarkand — he
took it three times and lost it three times to the Uzbeks — and settled for Kabul
instead, which is not what a Timurid prince considered a career.

India was the second choice. He beat the last Lodi sultan of Delhi at Panipat in
1526 with artillery and a smaller army, and disliked the country intensely,
saying so at length in a memoir that is among the most candid written by any
ruler on this site.

What his grandson Akbar built out of that conquest was different in kind from the
Delhi Sultanate it replaced. The *mansabdari* system ranked every officer in a
single imperial hierarchy with a numerical rank, paid from assignments of land
revenue that were rotated so that no family could root itself in a province. It
was a deliberate solution to the problem every polity on this site has run into:
that a military class paid from land eventually owns the land and stops taking
orders. Combined with a systematic revenue survey, it produced the most
centralised state the subcontinent had seen.

Akbar also abolished the *jizya* on non-Muslims and married into Rajput houses,
governing a majority-Hindu empire through a nobility that was substantially not
Muslim. That settlement held for roughly a century.
