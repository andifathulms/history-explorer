Alfonso VI took Toledo in 1085. It was the largest of the taifa capitals, an old
Visigothic royal city, and its fall was the moment the tribute the taifa kings
had been paying Castile for thirty years stopped being protection and became
instalments.

## What the parias were

Tribute, paid annually in gold by Muslim kings to Christian ones, in exchange
for not being attacked.

It had begun as protection money and it worked for a while: Ferdinand I and then
Alfonso VI of Castile took large sums from Seville, Toledo, Zaragoza and Badajoz,
and in return left them alone and occasionally lent them troops against each
other. For a taifa king it was a rational purchase. Paying a neighbour not to
invade is cheaper than an army, and the money came from cities that were still
extremely rich.

The problem is what it did over thirty years. The gold went north, where it
financed exactly the military establishment that was collecting it; the taifas
grew poorer and Castile grew stronger on their own money; and the tribute had to
be raised by taxes the religious law did not authorise, which handed the jurists
an argument.

Toledo in 1085 is where the arrangement was shown to be an instalment plan. A
king who has been paid not to take a city for twenty-five years can still take
it, and now he can afford to.
