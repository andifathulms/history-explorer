Seville became the largest of the taifas by eating the small ones, and the
method was not usually a battle.

The western taifas were mostly single cities with a district attached — Mértola,
Huelva, Silves, Niebla, Santa María del Algarve, Ronda, Morón, Arcos — several of
them held by Berber commanders left over from the caliphal army, none of them
individually able to resist a rich neighbour with a bought army.

The Abbadids took them one at a time across forty years, by purchase, by
marriage, by installing a claimant, by inheritance arranged in advance, and where
necessary by siege. By the 1060s Seville held most of what is now southern
Portugal and western Andalusia.

## Córdoba

The prize was the old capital, and taking it in 1070 was the moment Seville
stopped being a large taifa and started being the plausible successor to the
caliphate.

Córdoba in 1070 was not what it had been. The city had been sacked in the civil
wars of the 1010s, the palace-city of Madinat al-Zahra outside it had been
destroyed and quarried, and the population had fallen a very long way from what
it had been under Abd al-Rahman III. What it retained was the name, the great
mosque, and the fact that everybody in al-Andalus knew what it had been.

Al-Mu'tamid took it, lost it to Toledo, and took it back. His son governed there.
It is the only taifa acquisition that carried an argument with it rather than
just revenue.

## What the expansion was for

Money, and the fact that money was what the state ran on.

An army that is entirely bought has to be entirely paid, every year, whether it
fights or not. Seville's revenue came from an exceptionally rich agricultural
hinterland — the Guadalquivir valley, olives and grain and horses — and from the
trade of a river port, and neither of those grows quickly. Territory did.

So the logic of the state pushed outward continuously, and it was still pushing
when the parias started going north. A polity that needs a rising income to pay
a standing army, and that is simultaneously paying tribute to Castile out of the
same revenue, has an arithmetic problem that only ends one way.
