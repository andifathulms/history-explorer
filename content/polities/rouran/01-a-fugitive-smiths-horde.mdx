The Chinese record traces the Rouran to a captured slave who had
forgotten his own name. Seized during a raid by the people then
called the Jeu-jen, he was given the name Mukkuru - "bald," for the
peculiar way his hair grew - and put to work as an ironworker at the
court of a Toba prince in the region of modern Shanxi. Made a free
trooper for his own valor, Mukkuru fled into the desert rather than
face execution for missing a military muster, gathering a few hundred
vagabonds like himself around him. It was his son Cheruk, a more
warlike man, who first organized anything deserving the name of a
horde, and gave it the name Jeu-jen.

## A people who first enter the record by name

Distinct information about this people appears only from the year
394, by which point four generations had already passed since
Cheruk's own horde first took shape - a span placing its true
beginning around the year 300, during the same general collapse of
Xiongnu power that scattered every steppe people into fragments
competing to build something new. A descendant remembered only as the
"sixth grandson" crossed north of the desert that year and conquered
several Kanguly tribes, the first sign that Mukkuru's fugitive band of
vagabonds had grown into something worth a Chinese chronicler's
notice.
