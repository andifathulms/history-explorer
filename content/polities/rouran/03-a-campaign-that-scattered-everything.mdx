Zarun's own habit of harrying the Toba frontier outlived him. His
successor, his uncle or cousin Dadar, faced the consequences in the
summer of 429, when the third Toba emperor led an army of more than a
hundred thousand men against him. Dadar was caught completely by
surprise. He set fire to his own encampment and fled into the desert
without a trace, and his people's settlements scattered in every
direction, leaving vast flocks and herds to wander the country
uncared for. The Toba army pursued him twelve hundred miles from
where the first Han emperor had once been trapped by a Xiongnu
chieftain centuries before, scouring fifteen hundred miles of Mongolia
from east to west. The old Kanguly enemies, seeing their chance, hunted
down the fugitive Dadar themselves and killed him. The campaign left
the Tobas a million captured horses and men and three hundred thousand
more surrendered voluntarily.

## Two more campaigns, and a wall to hold what was won

Dadar's grandson Tughochir fared no better. A second Toba campaign
captured the whole of his tents and flocks, numbering over a million
head by the Chinese record's own vague but consistent reckoning; when
Tughochir's own son kept trespassing on Toba territory in the years
that followed, the Toba court answered by proposing a fresh Great Wall
three hundred miles long, built by soldiers idle between campaigns,
and by launching a third expedition that cut off fifty thousand heads,
took ten thousand prisoners, and pursued the survivors for nineteen
days without pause.

## An alliance no one would answer

Crushed by three successive disasters, the Jeu-jen tried what force
could no longer achieve: an alliance with the rival Chinese dynasties
south of the Yangtze, sending advances in succession to the Song, the
Southern Qi, and the Liang courts to try to bring the all-powerful
Tobas down between two fires. Nothing came of it. Distant dynasties
with no army north of the river had no reason to gamble one on a
steppe people's own quarrel, and the Jeu-jen were left to face the
Tobas' continued dominance with whatever scattered strength three
ruinous campaigns had left them.
