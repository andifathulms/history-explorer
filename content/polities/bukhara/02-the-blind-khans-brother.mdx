Baki Muhammad's own line lasted two reigns and ended in torture chambers.
What followed was the longest stretch of ordinary government Janid
Bukhara ever had, run by a pious khan who gave up his throne the moment
he went blind rather than pretend he could still hold it.

## A founder and a monster

Baki Muhammad took the throne in 1599, broke the Karai Turkomans who
had killed his own brother, and beat back a Persian punitive army under
Shah Abbas himself before dying of illness in 1605 after a doctor's
misdiagnosis had him floated down the Oxus in a litter chasing fresh
air. His brother Vali Muhammad succeeded him and turned out to be, by
every surviving account, a sadist: victims drawn through holes in walls
by oxen, others boiled in oil or flayed with wool-cards. A revolt led by
his own nephews Imam Quli and Nadir Muhammad drove him into exile in
Persia; six years after taking the throne he was captured and beheaded
on the order of the very holy man he had once mocked for underestimating
his army.

## Imam Quli

What followed was thirty-five years without a comparable atrocity. Imam
Quli ruled simply - few horses in his own stables, largesse distributed
rather than hoarded, his court's chief entertainments a running
competition between two poets nicknamed the Palmy and the Earthy. He
walked Bukhara's streets in disguise with his vizier to see how the city
actually worked, an experiment immortalized in a story about a
lovesick theology student, a robbed jeweller, and a watchman too
terrified of a royal practical joke to arrest anyone. His single
recorded massacre - Tashkent, 1612, sacked after a revolt killed his
own son's governor - ended in a lawyer's fetva letting him keep an oath
to flood the streets with blood by riding his horse into a tank already
stained red, rather than finishing the job literally. Mughal and
Safavid embassies traded jokes and unsheathed ceremonial swords at his
court for two decades of real peace on both frontiers.

Then Imam Quli went blind. Rather than reign sightless, he stood beside
his brother Nadir Muhammad at Friday prayers and had the khatib
proclaim Nadir's name instead of his own - a voluntary abdication no
one in the mosque expected - and set out for Mecca, escorted part of the
way by Shah Abbas II in person. He died at Medina in 1642, in his
sixty-second year.

## What Nadir Muhammad inherited and lost

Nadir Muhammad's own reign opened well - immense wealth, six thousand
camels needed just to move his treasury - and ended in a war against
his own son Abdul Aziz that gave the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan his
opening. Aurangzeb and his brother marched an Indian army into Balkh in
1646 under the pretence of helping Nadir against his rebellious sons,
occupied the province for two years, and then retreated in a winter so
savage that travelers reported human bones piled along the roads for
years after. Nadir returned to a country Abdul Aziz now effectively
ruled, tried and failed to reconcile with his sons, and set out for
Mecca himself in 1657, dying on the road and being buried beside his
own brother at Medina - the second khan in a row this family sent to
its grave still walking toward it rather than fighting to the last for
a throne it had already lost the will to hold.
