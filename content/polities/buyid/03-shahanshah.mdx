title: Adud al-Dawla takes an old title

Adud al-Dawla ruled Fars from 949 and Iraq from 977, and briefly held the whole
Buyid domain as one thing, which none of his relatives managed before or after.

He is remembered for hydraulic works — the Band-e Amir on the Kur river — and
for the Bimaristan al-Adudi, the great hospital he endowed at Baghdad. He is
remembered here for a title. Adud al-Dawla styled himself *shahanshah*, king of
kings.

That is a Sasanian title, unused by a Muslim ruler before him. It is a public,
formal, unmistakable claim on the legitimacy of a pre-Islamic Persian empire,
made by a man who simultaneously held a caliphal investiture and struck coin
in the caliph's name.

Under this site's coding rules, that registers as a successor claim — but on
the Sasanian ledger, not the Buyid one. The counts record who claimed a
polity's legitimacy, not whose a polity claimed. The Buyids' own
successor_claims entry is 0: nobody afterwards found the Buyid name worth
borrowing. The Seljuks who replaced them claimed the caliphate's blessing and
their own conquest, and never Buyid descent.

Holding both facts at once is the accurate picture of what Adud al-Dawla was.
He reached back four hundred years for a title that carried weight and left
behind a dynastic name that carried none.
