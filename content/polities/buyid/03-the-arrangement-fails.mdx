title: What the arrangement could not survive

The Buyid settlement was three or four related principalities — Fars, Jibal,
Iraq, Kirman — held by cousins who cooperated when it suited them. It had no
succession law, no capital, and no mechanism for producing another Adud
al-Dawla. After 983 it produced a hundred years of family war instead.

The military basis eroded from underneath at the same time. Daylamite infantry
had been the Buyid instrument; by the eleventh century Buyid armies were
increasingly Turkish cavalry, whose commanders had the same relationship to
their employers that the Buyids had once had to theirs.

In 1055 Tughril Beg entered Baghdad. Al-Malik al-Rahim was arrested. The caliph
al-Qa'im, who had spent his reign under Buyid control, received Tughril and
granted him the sultanate.

The caliph's position had not changed at all. He was still the source of
legitimacy and still not the source of power, still issuing investitures to the
man who already held the city. Only the identity of the man had changed: Shi'i
Daylamite out, Sunni Turk in, the same office, the same grant, the same
building.

A branch held on in Fars until about 1062, which is why this page gives the end
as a range. The thing that ended in 1055 was the arrangement.
