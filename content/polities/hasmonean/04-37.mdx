Antigonus II Mattathias, the last Hasmonean, held Jerusalem for three years with
Parthian backing. In 37 BC Herod took the city after a five-month siege with a
Roman army under Sosius, and Mark Antony had Antigonus scourged and beheaded at
Antioch.

Josephus, quoting Strabo, says this was the first time the Romans had executed a
foreign king, and gives the reason: Antony judged that no other punishment would
make the Jews accept Herod, because as long as Antigonus lived the Hasmonean
would be the king and Herod would not.

That judgement was correct and it did not work. Herod spent thirty-three years
being the man who was not a Hasmonean.

## The edge, and what it does to this region

This site records `hasmonean -> herodian`, type `overthrew`, dated 37 BC, and it
is the first edge in this region with both ends inside it.

That is worth pausing on, because it took seven records to get one. The Eastern
Mediterranean after Alexander has Ptolemaic Egypt, partitioned from a Macedonian
record filed under Iran before Islam. It has Palmyra, which ends against Rome.
It has the Nabataeans, absorbed by Rome. It has the Jafnids, dismantled by Rome,
and the Nasrids of al-Hira, dismantled by Iran. Every one of those points out of
the region, because the region's subject is powers that grew in the space
between two empires, and a polity defined by that space is succeeded by one of
the empires rather than by its neighbour.

Judaea is the exception because Judaea produced two of them in a row, and the
second took the first by siege.

The region's `thread` flag is now true on this single edge. Six of the seven
records are still drawn by nothing, which is the honest picture and not a
backlog.

## What Herod married

He had been betrothed to Mariamme, granddaughter of both Hyrcanus II and
Aristobulus II and therefore the Hasmonean line doubled, before he took the
city. He married her in the year he took it. He later made her seventeen-year-old
brother high priest, had him drowned in a swimming pool at Jericho within the
year, and eventually executed Mariamme, her mother, and the two sons he had by
her.

This record's `successor_claims` count is 0, and Herod is the reason the count
has to be argued rather than assumed. The marriage is real, the motive is
obvious, and Josephus is explicit that Herod wanted what the connection
conferred.

Coding rule 1 excludes dynastic descent on its own, and counts it only where it
is invoked publicly as the basis of a right to rule. Herod never did that. His
coins name him king and give no genealogy. His account of himself, as
reconstructed from Nicolaus of Damascus through Josephus, rests on the Roman
grant. He married into the house and then destroyed it, which is a use of a
connection and not an assertion of a claim.

The rule is the one this site's coding rulebook says is most likely to feel
wrong in a given case, and it is applied anyway, because who was related to whom
is a genealogy and genealogies are not claims. This is the case it feels wrongest
in, and the outcome is still right: a man who kills everyone with the better
claim is not asserting their legitimacy.
