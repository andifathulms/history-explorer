Judaea in the second century BC had two hereditary titles that mattered and the
Hasmoneans held neither.

Kingship belonged to the house of David, and there had not been a king in
Jerusalem since the Babylonians took it in 587, and the consequences of that
were still running four hundred and fifty years later. The high
priesthood belonged to the line of Zadok, which the Seleucids had been selling
to the highest bidder for a generation, but which was at least a line.

The Hasmoneans were a priestly family from Modein, of the course of Jehoiarib,
with no connection to either. What they had was that they had won.

## The decree

In 140 BC an assembly described as a great gathering of priests, people, rulers
of the nation and elders of the country made Simon leader and high priest — the
Greek word is *ethnarch* — and commander of the army, and decreed that the
offices should be his and his sons' after him. It forbade anyone, priest or
layman, to contest the arrangement or to convene an assembly without him. It was
engraved on bronze tablets and set up in the Temple precinct, and the text is
quoted in the fourteenth chapter of 1 Maccabees.

One clause in it is doing a great deal of work: Simon holds the offices *until a
trustworthy prophet shall arise*. That is the assembly conceding that it does not
have the authority to settle the question permanently and settling it anyway.

## A written constitution

Very few ancient states left a document setting out how their ruler was to be
chosen. The ones that did — the Hittites with Telipinu's edict, the Assyrians
with Esarhaddon's succession oath — generally wrote it after being badly hurt by
not having one: Telipinu legislated after a century of murdered kings,
Esarhaddon after a civil war caused by his father's assassination.

The Hasmoneans wrote theirs at the outset, before anything had gone wrong,
because they had no lineage and a document was the only thing they could put in
its place. A state can legislate a succession out of injury or out of
illegitimacy, and this is the second kind.

What the decree does not do is name an order. It says the office is Simon's and
his sons' until a trustworthy prophet should arise, and it says nothing about
which son. The century that followed ran son, son, brother, widow, and then a
civil war between two brothers that handed the country to Rome.

## What they built instead

Regev's argument is that the Hasmoneans knew exactly what they were missing and
spent a hundred years compensating for it.

The coinage is legible in that light: paleo-Hebrew letters revived from a script
five centuries out of use, no human image, the legend naming the high priest and
the *hever* — the assembly — of the Jews, in a deliberate archaism that looks
past the Seleucids to something older. The palaces at Jericho are Hellenistic
buildings with ritual baths in them. Hanukkah, as a festival, is a Hasmonean
institution about the Temple they had recovered.
