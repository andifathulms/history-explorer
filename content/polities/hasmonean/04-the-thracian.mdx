The Hasmonean kingdom reached its largest extent under Alexander Jannaeus, who
reigned twenty-seven years, took most of the coast and much of the country east
of the Jordan, and spent six of those years at war with his own subjects.

<Figure id="alexandrium" />

Josephus, himself of Hasmonean descent and not a hostile witness, tells the
story of how that ended.

Jannaeus beat the rebels in the field, shut the most powerful of them up in the
town of Bethome, besieged it, took it, and brought the men back to Jerusalem.

Then, while he was feasting with his concubines in a place where the whole city
could see, he ordered about eight hundred of them to be crucified. And while
they were still living he had the throats of their wives and children cut in
front of them.

<Figure id="crucifixion-nail" />

Josephus reports the provocation fairly — his subjects had brought foreigners
into the country against him, and had reduced him to real danger of losing his
life and his kingdom — and then says that the barbarity seems to have been
without any necessity. The Jews, he adds, called him a Thracian.

## What a peak looks like from inside

That is the largest the state ever was, and the same paragraph explains what it
cost.

To stop the Nabataeans joining his enemies, Jannaeus had to hand back Moab and
Gilead — territory he had conquered — to the king of Arabia. So the maximum
extent of the kingdom was already being traded away to buy off one neighbour
while its ruler fought a civil war against his own population.

<Figure id="machaerus" />

This is the Hasmonean problem in its finished form. The family had come to power
by leading a religious revolt against a Hellenistic king who was interfering
with the Temple. Four generations later its head was a Hellenistic king in
everything but name, wearing a diadem, employing foreign mercenaries against
Judaeans, holding the high priesthood, and crucifying Pharisees in batches while
dining in public.

## The obvious question

Josephus does not ask it directly, and the material asks it for him: at what
point did the dynasty stop being the thing it was founded to be?

There is no single point. Each step was defensible and the sequence was not.
Take the high priesthood because somebody has to hold it. Take the crown because
your neighbours have crowns and a priest cannot command troops abroad. Hire
mercenaries because a citizen levy will not campaign in Transjordan for years.
Use the mercenaries against dissidents at home because they are the only troops
who will. Execute the dissidents publicly because the alternative is being seen
to be afraid of them.

The Hasmonean state at its greatest reach was a Hellenistic monarchy of the
ordinary type — the type the family had risen against — and about eight hundred
of the people who noticed were on crosses outside the palace.
