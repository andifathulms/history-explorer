The Hasmoneans were not conquered out of existence. They were married into and
then killed off, one at a time, over about thirty years, by a man who needed
their name and could not afford their existence.

Herod married Mariamne, granddaughter of the high priest Hyrcanus II and the
Hasmonean heiress. It was the whole basis of his standing in Judaea: he was an
Idumaean whose father had been an administrator, and she was the last legitimate
representative of the family that had ruled for a century.

Josephus records what happened to the rest of them.

## The boy at the altar

Mariamne's brother Aristobulus was made high priest at the age of seventeen.

When he put on the holy vestments and came forward to the altar at a festival,
the crowd — Josephus says the multitude, in great crowds — fell into tears at
the sight of him.

That was the whole of his offence. The child was sent by night to Jericho, and
there, at Herod's command, he was held under the water of a pool by Gauls until
he drowned.

<Figure id="jericho-pools" />

Then the grandfather. Hyrcanus, old and long since mutilated out of the
priesthood, had been living safely in exile in Parthia; Josephus is explicit
that his friends warned him not to go over the Euphrates to Herod and that had
he listened he would have survived. What tempted him back was his
granddaughter's marriage. Herod's provocation, Josephus says, was not that
Hyrcanus made any attempt on the throne, but that it was fitter for him to be
king than for Herod.

<Figure id="jericho-palace" />

## Mariamne

She knew all of it and said so. Josephus describes her reproaching Herod openly
about her grandfather and her brother, in front of his sister and his mother,
while he stood there unable to answer because of how much he loved her — and
records, in the same passage, that her hatred of him was not less than his love
for her, and that she had only too just a cause.

The women of the court then got up the charge that ruined her: that she had been
unfaithful, and had sent her portrait into Egypt to Antony. It fell on Herod,
Josephus says, like a thunderbolt.

He had her killed, and afterwards the two sons she had borne him, who were the
last people alive with a claim by blood to the Hasmonean succession.

<Figure id="herodium" />

## What was left

Nothing dynastic. No exile branch, no pretender, no later house claiming the
name.

What survived was the festival. The rededication of the Temple that the first
generation of the family had carried out in 164, before there was a Hasmonean
kingdom or a Hasmonean crown, is kept annually and has been ever since —
outlasting the dynasty by two thousand years, in a form that commemorates the
revolt and not one of the kings it produced.

<Figure id="titus-relief" />

That is an unusually exact division. The Hasmoneans as rulers left a
crucifixion, a civil war, two brothers who each invited Rome in, and a
son-in-law who drowned the last of them in a swimming pool. The Hasmoneans as a
rebellion left a holiday.
