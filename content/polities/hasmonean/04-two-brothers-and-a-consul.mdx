Alexander Jannaeus died in 76 and his widow Salome Alexandra ruled nine years,
which the later rabbinic tradition remembers as the good period — she made peace
with the Pharisees, and Josephus says the country prospered. She could not hold
the high priesthood herself, so she gave it to her elder son Hyrcanus II and
intended the kingship to follow. The army and the Sadducees preferred her
younger son Aristobulus II.

When she died in 67 they fought. Aristobulus won, Hyrcanus abdicated, and then
Antipater the Idumaean — Herod's father, a Hasmonean official governing the
country his own people had been conquered into fifty years earlier — persuaded
Hyrcanus to reopen it with Nabataean help.

That is the war the Nabataeans record from the other side: Aretas III besieging
Jerusalem on behalf of one Jewish claimant, in the last
year in which the affairs of Judaea were decided by anybody local.

## Both of them wrote to Rome

Pompey was in Syria winding up the Seleucid empire. Both brothers appealed to
him, and a third delegation came from Judaea asking for neither of them and for
the restoration of the priestly constitution without a king.

Pompey heard all three, took his time, and then took Jerusalem for Hyrcanus in
63 after a three-month siege of the Temple mount. He entered the Holy of Holies
— which is remembered — and took nothing from the treasury, which is also
remembered and by a different set of people.

The settlement left Hyrcanus II as high priest and ethnarch of a much reduced
Judaea, stripped of the coastal cities, the Greek cities of the Decapolis and
most of what four generations had taken, and paying tribute. The title of king
was gone. The dynasty lasted another twenty-six years and nothing in them was
independent.

## The rule, and the outcome

The Hasmoneans had a written rule of succession — the decree of 140, published
on bronze — and the century it governed produced a son, a son, a brother, a
widow, and this.

It is tempting to conclude that they had no rule at all. They did. What they had
was an inadequate one, and the difference matters. A dynasty with no rule is one
where the strongest claimant simply takes the throne and everybody expects it. A
dynasty with a rule that says who may inherit and not in what order is one where
two brothers can each be right, each know they are right, and each find people
to say so.

That is what produced 63 BC. Not a coup, but a legal dispute that neither party
could win and neither could abandon, referred at last to the only authority in
the region big enough to settle it.
