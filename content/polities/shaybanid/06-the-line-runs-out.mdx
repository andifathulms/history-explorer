Abdullah Khan II died in 1598 and his son Abd al-Mu'min was murdered by his own
amirs after six months. There was no Shaybanid of sufficient standing left; the
male line of the house had been thinned by a century of appanage quarrels and by
Abdullah Khan's own campaigns against his relatives.

What followed is unusual enough to be worth the `ended` type it gets here. The
khanate did not fall, fragment or get conquered. Its throne passed by marriage
to the Toqay-Timurids — also called the Janids or Astrakhanids — who were
descendants of Toqay Timur, a different son of Jochi, and who had arrived in
Bukhara as refugees when Russia took Astrakhan in 1556. They ruled the same
state from the same capital with the same institutions until 1785.

That is a `dynastic replacement` with an empty `by`, and the empty field is
correct rather than lazy: nobody conquered anything, and the Toqay-Timurids are
not in this corpus.

The reason the transfer was possible without a war is the reason this whole page
exists. What made a man eligible to be khan in Transoxiana was Jochid descent,
and the Toqay-Timurids had it. Any of Chinggis Khan's descendants would do; a
Timurid, however capable, would not. That rule had governed the politics of this
country since the thirteenth century, it had forced the greatest conqueror of
the fourteenth to spend his life pretending to serve a nonentity, and it was
still deciding who sat in Bukhara in 1599.

For this site's records it also fills a field. The Timurid page's `ended.by` had
been empty with a comment saying the Uzbeks were not in the corpus. They are
now.
