In 1663 a Maloango accepted Christian baptism - the only conversion of a
Loango king noted anywhere in the surviving accounts of the kingdom. To a
reader familiar with Kongo's own religious history, where
Afonso I's conversion in 1506 had, within a generation, remade the entire
royal court and reached deep into the provinces, the natural expectation
might be that Loango's own king had set something similar in motion.

## A conversion that stopped at the throne

It had not. Christianity did not spread in Loango the way it had in Kongo,
despite the king's own example. No source records mass baptism among the
Loango nobility, no bishopric, no royal patronage of missionary
institutions comparable to what Kongo's kings built in the decades
following Afonso's own conversion. Whatever the Maloango's own motives for
accepting baptism in 1663 - diplomatic calculation in dealing with
European traders is at least as plausible a motive as personal conviction,
though no source states which it was - the conversion remained, in
practical terms, an act of one man rather than the opening move of a
national religious transformation.

## A kingdom whose sacred fire outlasted a foreign one

Loango's own religious life continued to run, before and after 1663,
through the ntufia and the rainmaking rite rather than through any church.
The kingdom's ritual kingship, built by a family of smiths generations
before any European missionary reached this stretch of coast, proved
considerably more durable in Loango than the newly introduced faith one
of its own kings had personally adopted.
