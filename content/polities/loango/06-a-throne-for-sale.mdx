The same slave trade that made Loango wealthy through the eighteenth
century enriched, above all, people who were not the king. Brokers,
caravan leaders and other trading middlemen - some of them former slaves
themselves - accumulated fortunes large enough to buy land directly from
the crown and to surround themselves with retinues of dependants, free and
servile alike. By the closing years of the eighteenth century, sources
describe commoners commanding as many as seven hundred dependants,
capable of waging war or working the land entirely on their own account,
independent of any noble title or royal favor.

## Territorial nobles replaced by paying customers

The kingdom's own royal council registered this shift directly. Titled
positions that had once belonged to the territorial nobility of the royal
house were, one by one, taken over by these newly wealthy traders - not
through inheritance or royal appointment on merit, but through simple
purchase, the crown selling high office to whoever could pay for it. A
king whose own religious authority depended on ritual performance rather
than on independent coercive force had little leverage to resist a
transaction that filled his own treasury even as it hollowed out his own
nobility's ranks.

## A justice system that stopped protecting everyone equally

The change reached even the kingdom's own system of justice. Loango had
traditionally guaranteed anyone the right to appeal directly to royal
justice, traveling to the capital along protected roads known as Nzila zi
Nzambi or Nzila Ivanga Nzambi - "God's paths" - which carried total
immunity for whoever walked them. By the eighteenth century, numerous
accounts describe that guarantee breaking down in practice, alongside a
documented rise in new magical beliefs centered on protecting individuals,
rather than families, from attack - a shift historians studying the
period read as a symptom of exactly the growing insecurity a weakening
royal authority and a newly powerful merchant class, competing openly for
influence, had together produced.
