The same mid-century crisis that left Loango's own throne in the hands of
a religious official's compromise candidate gave the kingdom's outlying
provinces an opening several of them took. Mayumba, a distant territory
on the kingdom's own northern reach, began seceding from around 1750
onward - the earliest and most direct case of a province simply declining
to answer to Mbanza Loango any longer once the royal line at the center
had visibly failed.

## Ports that eclipsed the capital that once ruled them

Kakongo and Ngoyo, both former vassals of the Maloango, followed a
different but related path: rather than merely breaking away, their own
ports at Malemba and Cabinda grew to overtake Loango's own port in the
volume of trade passing through them, especially after 1780. A kingdom
that had once been the dominant name European traders associated with
this stretch of coast found its own former subordinates handling more of
the trade that had built Loango's wealth in the first place than Loango
itself now did.

## Independence without a single battle

None of this separation required outright warfare in the way conquest
elsewhere in the region so often did. Mayumba, Kakongo and Ngoyo did not
need to defeat a Loango army to make their own independence real - a
royal center too weakened by its own succession crisis, and increasingly
dependent on the same merchant wealth its former vassals were now
accumulating on their own account, had little practical way left to
enforce a subordination that had, in earlier centuries, gone unquestioned.
