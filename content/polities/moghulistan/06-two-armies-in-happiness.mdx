In Rajab of the year 920 — the late summer of 1514 — Sultan Said Khan took the
Kashgar country, and the khanate acquired the thing it had never had.

## The conquest

Mirza Aba Bakr was a Dughlat who had made himself ruler of Kashgar, Yarkand and
Khotan and held them for decades; Mirza Haydar, whose own family he had ruined,
devotes a chapter to his evil deeds and wicked ways. Sultan Said came over the
mountains with a small force, drove Aba Bakr's people inside the walls of Kashgar,
and besieged Yangi Hisar for three months until the inhabitants capitulated.

When that news reached Kashgar its garrison simply abandoned the town. The
fugitives joined Aba Bakr at Yarkand and he resolved on flight too, and took the
road to Khotan with the khan's troops after him as far as the mountains of Tibet.
Cloth, goods, cattle and horses in great quantity fell to the Moghul army; the
flocks of sheep collected at the conquest, Mirza Haydar says elsewhere, were
beyond counting.

The khanate now had oasis cities, irrigated land, a land revenue, caravan tolls
and a capital at Yarkand.

## The brother

It also had a rival. Sultan Said's elder brother Mansur held Turfan and the
eastern end of the country, and there was old enmity between them — Mansur had
beaten him in Moghulistan and driven him into exile.

When his younger brother Aiman Khwaja came from Turfan and urged the amirs to
finish Mansur off, arguing that his power was in decline, Sultan Said refused:

> He is the elder brother; in former times I did not pay him due respect, and
> consequently he punished me. I ought on this account to bear him no enmity.

He sent ambassadors with words of peace and submission instead. Mansur, whose
soul, in the historian's phrase, had come to his lips from fear and foreboding,
received new life and joy without bounds, and came in fear and trembling to a
meeting between Aksu and Kusan. Sultan Said showed him great honour and agreed to
read the Friday sermon and strike his coins in his brother's name — the formal
marks of subordination, conceded by the stronger party.

## What it bought

Security on a scale nobody in that country remembered. Any man could travel alone
between Hami or China and Farghana without provisions and without fear of
molestation.

Somebody made a chronogram of it — *du lashkar ba nishat*, two armies in happiness
— which adds up to 922.

## The cost, which nobody saw yet

The khanate now had two capitals, both south of the mountains, and the thing it
was named for lay to the north.

Moghulistan was pasture: the Ili, the Chu, the Yulduz, Kochkar, At-Bashi, Issyk
Kul. It was where the Moghuls' herds were and where their amirs' power came from,
and from 1514 onward it was the poorer half of the state and the harder to defend.

Sultan Said went back into it in 1522 with his son Rashid and subdued it and the
Kirghiz with it. He was there because it was his, not because he needed it. That
distinction turned out to matter.
