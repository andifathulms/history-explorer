Umur Bey, who ruled from 1334, used it. Aydinid squadrons raided the Aegean
islands, the Greek mainland, Thrace and the Black Sea approaches, and for about
a decade were the strongest naval force in those waters. This is a Turkmen
warband from the interior of Anatolia, two generations off the plateau, running
a maritime raiding empire — with the ships, the sailors and the anchorages of
the people it had taken the coast from, and largely with their crews.

Umur is also the beylik's best evidence for what fourteenth-century Anatolia
actually looked like. He was a close ally and personal friend of John VI
Kantakouzenos, the Byzantine claimant, and brought fleets and armies into the
Byzantine civil war on his side in the 1340s — a Muslim Turkish emir
campaigning in Thrace for one Christian Roman emperor against another. The
Greek chronicler who records it, Kantakouzenos himself, writes about him warmly.
Meanwhile the pope was preaching a crusade against him.
