In September 1403 a Castilian embassy bound for Timur's court beat its way up
the Anatolian coast against contrary winds, and the ambassador kept a journal.
He had no interest in the emirates he was passing. He wrote down where the ship
anchored and what he was told there, and in three entries he records what had
happened to this coast in the year since Ankara.

Off a city on the Turkish mainland called *Palacia nueva* — Balat, at the mouth
of the Maeander, in the neighbouring emirate — they were told that Timur had
been in that city when he conquered the Turk and seized Turkey. The conqueror of
Bayezid had wintered on the Aegean shore.

A few days later they anchored at a Hospitaller island for water, and the people
there gave them the local news. The Turks of Palacia had ravaged the island and
done much mischief; that very year a galliot had come over from Palacia and the
raiders had carried off many sheep, and the men who were working in the fields.

## What that meant

For nearly sixty years the Aegean beyliks had been contained. One fortified harbour
mouth at Smyrna, held by a papal league, had been enough to end the raiding
economy the Aydinid state was built on, and Umur Bey had died under its walls
trying to take it back. Then Timur arrived, and in a single season the
arrangement that had held since 1344 was gone. The emirates Bayezid had
swallowed were handed back to their families, and the harbours were open again.

The Castilian journal shows what the coast did with that. Within a year the
galliots were out, and the islands that had stopped paying to be left alone were
losing their sheep and their farm workers again.

## The embassy that went the other way

The journal's last observation on the subject comes from the return march. When
the ambassadors left Samarkand on 21 November 1404 they travelled in company
with the other envoys going west: one from the sultan of Egypt, one from the
brother of a great lord of Turkey, one from Sivas, one from the city of Altologo
and one from the city called Palatia.

Altologo is what Italian merchants called Ayasoluk, the Aydinid capital below
the ruins of Ephesus. Palatia is Balat again. So within two years of Ankara both
emirates had sent men the length of Asia to Samarkand and kept them there long
enough to travel home with the Castilians, in a caravan of ambassadors from
Cairo, central Anatolia and the Aegean, all of them attending on the same
elderly conqueror.

That is what a restoration cost. The beylik that had once made the papacy
assemble a fleet against it got its harbours back as a gift, and paid for them
by maintaining a permanent presence at a court on the other side of Asia. The
gift was also short. Timur was dead by the following February, and the Ottoman
state the Aydinids had been rescued from spent the next decade putting itself
back together.
