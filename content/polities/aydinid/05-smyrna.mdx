In October 1344 a naval league assembled by the papacy — Venice, Cyprus, the
Knights Hospitaller — attacked Smyrna and took the harbour castle. They held
it, on and off, for the next fifty-eight years.

That single fortification ended the beylik as a power. The Aydinid state was
built on raiding, and raiding required a defensible harbour to sail from and
return to; without Smyrna the fleets could not operate at scale, and the
revenue that had paid the crews stopped. Umur Bey died in 1348 assaulting the
walls to get it back.

## What a single fort was worth

It is worth dwelling on the scale, because it is small and the effect was total.

<Figure id="ephesus-arcadian" />

The crusaders took one castle, at the mouth of one harbour, and held it. They
never took Birgi, or Ayasoluk, or Tire, or the Cayster valley, or any of the
territory the beylik actually governed. The Aydinid state kept its towns, its
land revenue, its court and its emirs for another eighty years.

What it lost was the ability to be dangerous, and with that the ability to be
paid. A raiding fleet needs a harbour it can sail from loaded and return to
loaded, and one fortified position at the harbour mouth removes it. The crews
went elsewhere. The islands stopped paying to be left alone. Umur Bey spent the
rest of his life trying to get the castle back and was killed under its walls in
1348.

Fifty-eight years is a long time to garrison one tower on a foreign coast, and
it was the correct investment: the league that took it never had to fight the
fleet again.
