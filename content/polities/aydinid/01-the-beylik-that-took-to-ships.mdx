A dozen Turkmen principalities replaced the Sultanate of Rum. One of them went
to sea.

When Byzantine authority in western Anatolia dissolved after 1261 — the restored
empire at Constantinople had its attention and its money on Europe, and the
Anatolian frontier troops it had disbanded went over to the other side — the
Aegean coast fell to Turkmen bands under their own leaders.

The Aydinids took the Cayster valley,
Birgi and then Ayasoluk, which is Ephesus, and in 1329 the harbour and lower
town of Smyrna.

At which point they inherited a fleet, some Greek crews, and a coastline facing
several hundred islands.

That is the whole of the formation and it took about forty years. What makes
this beylik different from the other eleven is what it did with a harbour: every
other Turkmen principality in Anatolia was a land power that happened to reach
the sea, and this one turned into a naval state, using the ships, the sailors
and the anchorages of the people it had taken the coast from.
