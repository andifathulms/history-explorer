In 1222 Ken Arok, a man of obscure origins in the service of the lord of Tumapel,
defeated and killed Kertajaya at Ganter. Kediri had held eastern Java for a
hundred and eighty years, and stopped.

This corpus's Singhasari record's second chapter is about the same battle from
the winning side, and its first is about the *Pararaton*'s account of Ken Arok —
the cursed dagger, the murdered smith, the four generations of revenge killing
that follow. Ganter is where the two records meet, and the edge `kediri →
singhasari`, `conquered by`, 1222, is now drawn between them.

`ended.type` here is `conquest` and `ended.by` names a polity with a page. That
is rarer on this site than it should be, and across the archipelago it was, until
recently, unheard of: the first six records written there had an empty
`ended.by`, six for six.

## What happens next, and why it is a different record

Seventy years later a lord of Kediri named Jayakatwang killed Singhasari's last
king and restored the kingdom that had ended here.

He is not on this page. He has his own record — `second-kediri` — joined to this
one by the `resumes` field rather than by an edge, and its first chapter argues
the case for that arrangement. The short version is that the alternative would
have been to give this record a span of 1042 to 1293 with a seventy-year hole in
the middle that nothing on the page could show.
