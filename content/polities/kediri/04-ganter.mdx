In 1222 Ken Arok, a man of obscure origins in the service of the lord of
Tumapel, defeated and killed Kertajaya at Ganter. Kediri had held eastern Java
for a hundred and eighty years, and stopped.

<Figure id="singosari" />

Ken Arok's own story, as the *Pararaton* tells it, is one of the strangest in
Javanese literature: a man of obscure and possibly divine parentage, a cursed
dagger commissioned from a smith whom he then murdered with it, and four
generations of revenge killings running down from that weapon through the
dynasty he founded.

Ganter is where a hundred and eighty years of Kediri end and that story begins.

## And then it came back

Seventy years later a lord of Kediri named Jayakatwang killed Singhasari's last
king and restored the kingdom that had ended here.

It lasted about a year. Kublai Khan's expedition to Java arrived in 1293
looking for Singhasari's king, found Jayakatwang on the throne instead, and was
manoeuvred by Raden Wijaya into destroying him — after which Wijaya turned on
the Mongols, drove them out, and founded Majapahit.

So Kediri ends twice, seventy-one years apart, and the second ending is the
more consequential: it is the accident that produced the largest state Java
ever had.
