In 1042 Airlangga, who had spent thirty years putting eastern Java back together
after it fell apart, divided the kingdom he had rebuilt between his two sons and
retired to live as an ascetic.

The Javanese account gives the reason as a father's even-handedness. The result
was two kingdoms, Janggala and Panjalu, which fought each other; Panjalu won,
took the name of its capital's district, and is the record on this page.

Kediri is here for two reasons. The first is that this corpus's Singhasari
record names it three times and could not link to it — its second chapter is
about Ganter, its fifth is about Jayakatwang, and the data comment on that
record's `ended.by` said in as many words that Kediri "is not one" of the
polities on this site. The second is that Java's dynastic chain runs Kediri →
Singhasari → Majapahit, and this site had the last two.

## The founder field is null

`rulers.founder` on this record has no name.

Airlangga is the obvious candidate and he is the wrong one: he ruled Kahuripan,
the kingdom that was divided, and Kediri is one of the halves. The first ruler of
Panjalu is named in the inscriptions, and there is nothing attached to the name —
no reign this site can date, no act, no source that puts him in a sentence.

This corpus's Aksum, Srivijaya and Champa records take the same position for the
same reason, and the reason is worth repeating on each of them: a name entered
with an invented reign is a placeholder wearing the shape of a fact, and it reads
on the page exactly like a sourced one.
