Almost everything datable about this kingdom comes from copper plates and stones
issued by its kings, and they are nearly all the same kind of document: a grant
of *sima* status.

A *sima* is land whose revenue is assigned away from the crown — to a temple, to
a religious community, sometimes to a village in return for a service performed.
The charter records who granted it, which land, what the boundaries are, what
obligations are lifted, what the ceremony cost, who witnessed it, and a curse on
anyone who interferes.

They are, functionally, the state's tax records written from the receiving end,
and this corpus's Medang record is coded from the same class of document three
centuries earlier and says so in the same words.

## What that does to what can be known

It means the coded fields on this record are inferences from a documentary genre
rather than descriptions of a system anybody wrote down.

`revenue_basis: land-tax` is on this record because the charters exempt land from
obligations that must therefore have existed and are listed by name. `military
_basis: conscript` is on it because those obligations include labour and service
rendered through territorial officials. Neither is stated anywhere as a rule.

`succession_rule` is null, and one reason among several is that charters do not
discuss succession. They give a reigning king's titles and his ancestry as he
wished it stated, and a king who took the throne irregularly does not say so in
the preamble to a land grant.

## The general point

This is worth a chapter because it is true of a large fraction of this site.

Records built from epigraphy — this one, Medang, Champa, Angkor, Kush, Makuria,
Urartu — are built from whatever genre of text that culture happened to inscribe
on durable material, and the genres differ. Angkor inscribed temple foundations,
so this site knows how many people were assigned to a shrine. The Assyrian
records inscribed royal annals, so that region's records are full of campaigns.
Kediri inscribed tax exemptions.

The fields are the same on all of them. The confidence behind each field is not,
and there is no column for it.
