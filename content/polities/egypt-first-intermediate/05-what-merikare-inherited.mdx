Merikare is the last Herakleopolitan king any source consulted here can
name with confidence, and his own reign is where the Siut inscriptions -
the fullest surviving record of his kingdom, kept by a family who had
fought and governed for it across two generations - simply stop.
Nothing in what survives describes a final battle, a siege, or a
surrender. The tombs that had proudly dated a king's rejoicing at
Herakleopolis, and boasted of a nomarch's fleet stretching for miles up
the river, do not go on to describe how the war they had been winning
by inches turned into one they lost outright.

## A kingdom without an ending on record

This is not the same silence that opens the period. The silence around
the Sixth Dynasty's fall was a silence about everything - no monuments,
no inscriptions, no named kings at all for a generation. The silence
around Merikare's own end is narrower and stranger: a well-documented
dynasty, served by a loyal and literate provincial nobility that had
recorded two generations of campaigns in careful detail, simply runs out
of things to say about itself at the one moment a reader most wants to
know what happened.

What can be said is only the shape of it. Thebes held the frontier
Tefibi had once pushed back to Herakleopolis's advantage; the Theban
kings who followed continued pressing north; and at some point in that
pressure, the Herakleopolitan throne Merikare had inherited from a line
of eighteen kings ceased to exist. No source consulted here names the
battle, if there was a single battle, or the terms, if there were any
terms to be had.

## Where the rest of the war is told

The country Mentuhotep II inherited when Thebes finally took the north
is a story told from the winning side, on the record of the kingdom that
followed this one - the same war, the same frontier, the same two rival
thrones, narrated from Thebes rather than from Herakleopolis. What
Merikare himself lost, and how he lost it, is not recorded by anyone who
was there to write it down. A century and a quarter that had begun with
a kingship nobody could reassemble ends with one that nobody thought to
finish describing.
