---
title: The centre does not hold
drafted_from: breasted-1905

Pepi II reigned for over ninety years, and when he died the office he had
held for so long simply had nowhere obvious to go. Manetho's king-lists
credit Memphis with a Seventh Dynasty and an Eighth after him, but neither
left a monument, a tomb, or an inscription that any source consulted here
can securely date. The mines and quarries of Sinai and Hammamat, which
record the passage of every prosperous line of kings before and after this
one, fall silent. For something under thirty years, Egypt had kings whose
names survive in a list and nothing else.

## What was destroyed

Archaeology, rather than any surviving text, supplies the one fact the
king-lists cannot: somebody spent real effort tearing the Old Kingdom down.
The finest granite and diorite statues of its greatest monarchs were not
merely looted but smashed, some of them thrown into the wells built into
their own pyramid causeways. Temples were not just stripped of their
portable wealth but violated in a way that looks like a settling of
accounts rather than ordinary plunder.

No text names who did this or why. The destruction has no author on
record - a violence done to a kingship that had just lost the son who
still recognised its authority, by people the sources never identify
because the sources themselves stopped being written.

## What did not stop

What the collapse of the centre did not do is empty the country of
government. The nomarchs - hereditary provincial governors whose offices
had been quietly becoming family property for two generations before
Pepi II's death - kept administering their own nomes through the silence
exactly as they had while a king in Memphis still claimed authority over
them. Some of them now dated documents by their own years of local rule
rather than a Pharaoh's regnal count, which is itself a form of evidence:
a scribe only stops counting by the king's years once counting by the
king's years has stopped meaning anything.

Egypt did not have no government in this period. It had dozens of small
ones, each legitimate within its own nome and answerable to nobody above
it, waiting to find out which of them, or which outside candidate, would
eventually reassemble the rest.
