Tefibi's victories bought Herakleopolis time rather than security. The
Theban line that had been checked on both riverbanks did not disappear;
it produced, within a generation, a nomarch who took royal titles
outright. This Intef - counted by later tradition as the first king of
a new Theban dynasty - resumed the war his predecessor had lost, and
this time the direction of the frontier did not reverse.

## What changed hands

Intef pushed north and took Abydos, capital of the Thinite nome and one
of the oldest religious centres in Egypt, holding the tombs of kings
from the earliest dynasties. He fixed his own new frontier there,
calling it the "Door of the North" - in deliberate answer to
Elephantine's old title as the "Door of the South" at the opposite end
of the kingdom - and this was, in all likelihood, the very fortress
Tefibi's own inscriptions had called the "Port of the South" when his
army still held it for Herakleopolis. The same position, named from two
directions by two rival courts, is as clear a marker as this war left of
which side was advancing and which was not.

Intef reigned over fifty years. Real detail about his successor is thin,
but the Theban line did not need to press every year to win: it needed
only to hold what it took and wait for the north's own resources -
never as deep as the south liked to claim, and now permanently
committed to a two-front defence against a confederation that showed no
sign of losing interest - to run out.

## What the Herakleopolitan side could still do

Nothing in the sources suggests panic at Herakleopolis. Merikare's
kingship, the buffer of loyal nomarchs at Siut, and whatever remained of
the old bureaucratic habits inherited from the Old Kingdom continued to
function through this reversal much as they had through the earlier
Theban rebellion. What had changed was not the Herakleopolitan
kingdom's internal order but its position in a war it was now losing by
inches rather than winning by battles - a difference no scribe at
Herakleopolis or Siut seems to have thought worth recording while it was
still happening.
