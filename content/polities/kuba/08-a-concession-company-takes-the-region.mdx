European contact with the Kuba kingdom, when it finally came, arrived
slowly and at second hand. Ludwig Wolf, a doctor traveling as second in
command on the explorer Hermann Wissmann's own expedition, reached the
fringe of the kingdom's territory in 1885 without ever penetrating to the
capital itself. It was an African-American Presbyterian minister, William
Sheppard, who first reached Mushenge directly, in 1893 - and it was not
until 1907 that Emil Torday and T. A. Joyce arrived to produce the first
full ethnographic and historical account of the kingdom's own
institutions, with M. W. Hilton-Simpson accompanying their expedition.

## A concession that answered to shareholders, not to any king

Whatever protection distance and difficult terrain had given the kingdom
against earlier colonial pressure did not survive the Congo Free State.
Between 1885 and 1908, the entire Kasai region - Kuba territory included -
was granted as a single concession to a company called the Compagnie du
Kasai, whose rubber collection methods were brutal enough to draw
international attention. A British consul, Wilfred Thesiger, produced an
eyewitness report in 1909 documenting the concession's impact on the Kuba
directly - one of the relatively few outside accounts to describe, in
real time, what Free State rubber extraction actually did to a specific,
named kingdom rather than to the Congo in the abstract.

## Institutions that kept functioning under the weight

What makes the Compagnie du Kasai period distinctive in Kuba's own
history is not that the kingdom escaped its consequences - the available
evidence suggests ordinary Kuba subjects suffered under the concession in
much the same ways as their neighbors elsewhere in Kasai did - but that
the kingdom's own governing institutions, the nine provinces, the courts,
the councils of kolm, kept operating underneath the extraction regime
rather than collapsing under it. A concession company answerable to
distant shareholders had taken control of what the kingdom's own economy
produced. It had not yet taken control of how the kingdom governed
itself.
