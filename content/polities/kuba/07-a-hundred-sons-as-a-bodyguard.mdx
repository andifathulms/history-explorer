Bope Mobinji, remembered by the Bushong themselves as the harshest king
in their own oral record, was still on the throne - blind with age, by
the time Torday's informants could picture him clearly - when the first
European reached the kingdom's own frontier. His reign is the last one
the record describes in any real detail before European contact
changes what kind of history gets recorded at all.

## A king enforced by his own sons

Bope Mobinji had, by report, a hundred sons, and he turned them into
an instrument of rule: whenever anyone was judged to have offended the
king or defied his orders, his sons descended on the offender's own
village, killed him, plundered the settlement and carried off its women
and children into slavery. It was a severity that provoked repeated
revolts, each one put down, by the same account, with a cruelty to
match the offense. When Bope Mobinji later learned that one of his own
sons had allowed the German traveler Ludwig Wolf to visit a Bushong
border village in 1885, he had a slave killed on the spot to purify the
contact - the kingdom's first recorded brush with a European visitor
answered with an execution rather than curiosity.

## A death and a funeral remembered for its scale

Bope Mobinji's own funeral became, in later Bushong memory, the
measure against which every later king's more restrained rule was
judged. His son and heir, following the old custom that gave a dead
king's eldest son absolute power over the kingdom during the
interregnum before a successor's coronation, chose the victims to be
sacrificed at the grave - and by the figure later Bushong elders gave
Torday, had two thousand people killed in Bope Mobinji's honor, not
counting the wives and slaves buried alongside him. Torday himself
treated the figure as very probably exaggerated, but its scale was
still being invoked a generation later, when the current king's own
much smaller funeral for his sister - not a single person sacrificed -
struck the same elders as proof that the kingdom's old severity, and
with it something of its old greatness, had gone soft.

## What came after him

Bope Mobinji's own immediate successor is not named in the record
Torday collected, but by the start of the twentieth century the throne
had passed to Kwete Peshanga Kena, the gentle, conflict-averse king
Torday would come to know personally during his 1907-08 stay at
Mushenge - a ruler shaped, by his own court's estimation, into exactly
the kind of monarch Bope Mobinji's reign of terror had never allowed
the kingdom to need.
