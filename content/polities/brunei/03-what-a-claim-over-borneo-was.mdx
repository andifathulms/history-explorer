Brunei under Bolkiah, around 1500, is usually described as controlling most of
the coastline of Borneo, and this record's `core_region` says so with a
qualification — "with claims at their widest around most of the island".

Borneo is the third largest island in the world. Nothing that happened in the
sixteenth century controlled it.

## What was actually held

A river mouth, and a network.

The pattern is the one this corpus's Srivijaya record established seven hundred
years earlier and called power over a passage. A coastal capital with ships;
downstream chiefs at other river mouths who acknowledged it, sent tribute, took
titles from it, and traded through it; and behind them, up the rivers, populations
that supplied the forest produce the trade ran on — camphor, rattan, birds' nests,
gutta-percha, gold — and that dealt with the coast and not with Brunei.

The title granted, the tribute paid and the marriage arranged are the whole of
the relationship. When a downstream chief stopped sending, the sultanate's options
were a punitive expedition or nothing, and often it was nothing.

## Why the schema flatters it

This site's fields cannot express that, and the reason is worth naming because it
applies to half the records in the archipelago.

`core_region` is prose, and it is the only field that can carry a qualification at
all. `reach_km2` is null here, and on all twenty-two records in the archipelago:
not one of them carries a reach figure, because the tabulations this site draws those
figures from are tabulations of territorial empires and nothing here is one. So
at least there is no number to overstate. But `military_basis: client-levy` and
`revenue_basis: trade-toll, tribute` both read like descriptions of a functioning
apparatus, and what they describe is a set of arrangements that had to be renewed
every generation and frequently were not.

The Majapahit record has the same problem with the *Nagarakertagama*'s list of
dependencies, and its fourth chapter is about it. So does the Srivijaya record.
So does Sulu's claim over north-east Borneo, which is on this island too and
overlapped with this one — two records in this corpus claiming the same coast at
the same time, both of them accurately.

Overlapping claims are the normal condition of maritime Southeast Asia and there
is no field for them. A polity page implies exclusive tenure of a territory,
which is a European idea about states arriving four hundred years after the fact.
