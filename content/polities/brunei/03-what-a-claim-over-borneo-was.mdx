Brunei under Bolkiah, around 1500, is usually described as controlling most of
the coastline of Borneo.

Borneo is the third largest island in the world. Nothing in the sixteenth
century controlled it, and the word doing the damage in that sentence is
"controlling".

## What was actually held

A river mouth, and a network.

It is the pattern Srivijaya had run on the same seas seven hundred years
earlier: a coastal capital with ships; downstream chiefs at other river mouths
who acknowledged it, sent tribute, took titles from it, and traded through it;
and behind them, up the rivers, populations that supplied the forest produce
the trade ran on — camphor, rattan, birds' nests, gutta-percha, gold — and that
dealt with the coast and not with Brunei.

The title granted, the tribute paid and the marriage arranged are the whole of
the relationship. When a downstream chief stopped sending, the sultanate's
options were a punitive expedition or nothing, and often it was nothing.

## Why maps of it are misleading

Draw that on a map and you get a solid block of colour around a coastline. What
was actually there was a set of arrangements that had to be renewed every
generation and frequently were not.

Majapahit's court poem lists dependencies across the whole archipelago in
exactly the same way, and Srivijaya's inscriptions do it seven centuries
earlier. It is the standard form. A tributary list is a statement of who
currently acknowledges you, written by the party being acknowledged, and it
goes out of date silently.

Sulu makes the point sharper still. Sulu claimed the north-east coast of Borneo
at the same period, and its claim and Brunei's overlapped — both of them
accurately, because what each was claiming was the allegiance of particular
chiefs at particular river mouths rather than the ground between them. Two
sultanates could hold the same coast at once without either of them lying, and
did.

Exclusive tenure of a bounded territory is a European idea about states. It
arrived in Borneo four centuries after this, with people who then drew the
lines that are still there.
