Brunei at its height under Bolkiah, around 1500, claimed most of the coastline of
Borneo — an island the size of France and Germany together — and had installed a
related Muslim ruler at Manila.

Spain took Manila bay in 1571, attacked Brunei itself in 1578 and held the town
for a few weeks before disease drove the force out. That decade cost Brunei the
northern half of its reach. What was left, it kept for nearly three hundred more
years, and then gave away.

In 1841 the sultan granted Sarawak to James Brooke, an English adventurer who had
helped put down a rebellion there. Brooke's family ruled it as a private kingdom
until 1946. North Borneo went to a chartered company in 1881. The protectorate
treaty of 1888 covered what was left, which was a strip of coast.

## The only ending of its kind here

This site carries around two hundred endings. They are conquests, fragmentations,
usurpations, dynastic replacements and absorptions, and the thing on the other
side is almost always a polity — or, on fourteen records in this region, someone
this corpus does not carry at all.

This one was delivered piecemeal, by signature, over fifty years, to two
chartered companies and one private person. `ended.type` is `gradual absorption`
and `ended.by` is empty, and the emptiness here is not the usual kind: a list of
polity ids could not hold James Brooke under any circumstances.

## And the state is still there

Brunei exists. The dynasty this record's `founder` field names is the one on the
throne now, which makes it among the longest continuously reigning houses
anywhere.

The record stops in 1888 on the test this site applied to Surakarta and
Yogyakarta after the division of Mataram: what a polity page requires is the
disposal of its own external relations, and a state under protection does not
have it. The Brunei that got that back in 1984 is a different kind of object from
the one on this page, in the same way and for the same reason.

That test is doing a lot of work in this region now — on the two Javanese courts,
on Ternate after 1683, on Banten after 1684, on Tidore after Nuku, and here. It
is the same test each time, which is the only thing that makes it defensible.
