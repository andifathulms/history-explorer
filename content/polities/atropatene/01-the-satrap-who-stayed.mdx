---
title: The satrap who did not hand it over
drafted_from: wiesehofer-1996
phase: formation
---

Atropates was the Achaemenid satrap of Media. He commanded the Median
contingent against Alexander at Gaugamela, and after the empire fell he did what
this site's Macedonian record describes Alexander doing with a number of Persian
governors: he submitted, and was confirmed in his own province.

When Alexander died in 323 the settlement at Babylon divided the satrapies among
the officers, and Media was split. The southern part went to a Macedonian. The
northern part — Media Minor, the mountain country from the Araxes down to the
Zagros — stayed with Atropates, and he never handed it over.

His descendants ruled it for five hundred and fifty years.

That makes this the strangest entry in this corpus's Iranian region, and the
claim in its `legitimation` field is unique on the site. Every other polity here
asserting `descent` is claiming a connection to something that had ended: the
Parthians and Sasanians to the Achaemenids, the Shirvanshahs to the Sasanians,
the Rasulids to the Ghassanids, the Bagratids to King David. Atropatene's
dynasty was not claiming succession from the Achaemenid empire. It was a piece of
it that had not stopped.

The kingdom was a client of whoever was strongest — Seleucid, then Parthian, with
periods of effective independence and one Roman alliance — and the arrangement
this corpus records for the Shirvanshahs, the Artuqids and the Bavandids applies
exactly: submit early, pay, hold mountain country that is expensive to take, and
outlast the people who take it. Atropatene did it longer than any of them.
