Ardashir took the kingdom around 226, two years after taking the Parthian empire
this record had been a client of, and Atropatene became a Sasanian province
governed by an appointed marzban. This corpus's Sasanian record picks it up as
the province containing the royal fire.

That is a five-hundred-and-fifty-year span ending in a paragraph, which is
proportionate: the kingdom's political history is a sequence of accommodations
and almost none of it is recorded from inside. This record's `peak` and `last`
ruler fields are both null, its `military_basis` and `succession_rule` are null,
and it has one turning point. What it has instead of a narrative is a name.

Atropatene is Greek for the land of Atropates. In Middle Persian it becomes
Adurbadagan — which the Sasanians also glossed as the land of the fire, since
*adur* is fire and the great sanctuary was there, and the two etymologies have
been braided together ever since. In Arabic it is Adharbayjan. In modern Persian
and Turkish, Azerbaijan.

So a satrap who declined to hand over his province in 323 BC named a region that
is now a country of ten million people and a province of Iran with more people
than that, and the name has been continuously in use for twenty-three centuries.

This site has no field for that either, and it is the third time on four pages
that this record has had to say so. The influence counts measure scripts,
religions carried and successor claims. Naming a country is none of the three,
and neither is housing the fire that crowned an empire, and neither — on the
Marwanid record in the Jazira — is changing who lives somewhere.

The corpus's rulebook is right that a fourth count would need a rule applicable
to sixty polities and that no such rule exists. What follows from that is not
that the effects do not matter. It is that the chapters carry them, and that a
reader working from the measures block alone would find this page empty.
