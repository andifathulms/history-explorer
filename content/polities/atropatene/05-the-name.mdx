Ardashir took the kingdom around 226, two years after taking the Parthian
empire it had been a client of, and Atropatene became a Sasanian province
governed by an appointed marzban. The fire stayed where it was and the new kings
of kings came to it exactly as the old ones had.

That is a five-hundred-and-fifty-year span ending in a paragraph, and the
proportion is honest. Nobody in the kingdom wrote its history; nobody outside it
was interested except when passing through with an army. There is no name for
its greatest king, no account of how it raised troops, no rule for how a throne
passed from one man to the next. What survives instead of a narrative is a
name.

Atropatene is Greek for the land of Atropates. In Middle Persian it becomes
Adurbadagan — which the Sasanians also glossed as the land of the fire, since
*adur* is fire and the great sanctuary was there, and the two etymologies have
been braided together ever since. In Arabic it is Adharbayjan. In modern Persian
and Turkish, Azerbaijan.

The name has been in continuous use for twenty-three centuries. It is on the map
now, attached to a country of ten million people and to a province of Iran with
more people than that.

Set that beside what the kingdom did not do. It founded no religion and
exported none. It conquered nothing. It produced no literature anyone kept, no
script, no law, and no claimant to anybody else's throne. By every ordinary
measure of consequence it is a small mountain client that spent five and a half
centuries agreeing to terms.

And it holds the fire that crowned the kings of Iran, and it gave two countries
their name.

Those are not consolation prizes for a polity that failed at something else.
They are what this particular kind of durability produces. A state that survives
by never being worth conquering accumulates nothing that can be counted and
outlasts everything that can. Atropates declined to hand over a province in 323
BC, and the label he left on it has proved harder to remove than every empire
that has since governed it.
