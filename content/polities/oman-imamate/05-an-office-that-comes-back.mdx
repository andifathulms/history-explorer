893 is where this record ends, and it is worth being explicit about why it ends
there rather than in the twentieth century.

The Zaydi imamate on this page's neighbouring record runs from 897 to 1962 as a
single span, because its own historiography treats it as one institution with a
continuous line of imams and long interruptions. Omani historiography does the
opposite: it counts imamates, in the plural, as distinct constitutions of the
same office separated by periods of dynastic or foreign rule — the Nabhani
kings, the Buyids, the Portuguese on the coast, the Ya'ariba imams of the
seventeenth century who took Mombasa and Zanzibar, the Al Bu Sa'id sultanate,
and an imamate revived in the interior as late as the twentieth century against
a sultan in Muscat.

So the two Arabian imamates on this site are bounded differently, and the
difference is not inconsistency but deference: each follows the periodisation
its own sources use. The Zaydi page carries a chapter arguing that its
thousand-year figure measures an institution rather than a state. This page
carries the opposite problem — a hundred and forty-three years that understate
an office which kept being reconstituted in the same mountains for another
millennium.

The two records together are the honest position, and neither of them fits the
longevity axis comfortably. That is a fact about the axis.
