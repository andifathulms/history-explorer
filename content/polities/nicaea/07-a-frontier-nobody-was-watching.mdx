That is the argument for giving this fifty-seven-year state a page of its own.
The empire of Nicaea is the last period in which the Roman state governed
Anatolia effectively, and the decision that ended it — to spend everything on
recovering the capital — is the decision that gave Anatolia to the Turks. The
The Byzantine empire ends in 1453 in Constantinople. The decision that made that
ending inevitable was taken here, two hundred years earlier, and was technically

## What Nicaea had actually built

It is worth being specific about what was given up, because the usual account of
the thirteenth century treats Nicaea as a waiting room.

For fifty-seven years the Laskarid emperors governed western Anatolia as a
going concern. They kept the frontier garrisoned with a settled soldiery holding
land in return for service. They ran a treasury with a surplus — John Vatatzes is
remembered for a state farm whose egg money paid for a crown — and they enforced
a policy of buying nothing from foreign merchants that could be produced at home.
They kept the patriarchate, the schools and the law courts in exile and
functioning.

That was the last time a Roman government administered Anatolia in a way that
worked. Everything after 1261 is a coastal empire on the wrong side of the water,
holding Anatolian territory it could not garrison from a capital it could not
afford.

## The exchange

So the balance is unusually clean, and unusually harsh.

Nicaea traded a functioning provincial state for a ruined capital and a name. It
got the name — Michael VIII entered Hagia Sophia and the Roman empire was
formally restored — and within a lifetime the provinces that had paid for the
whole enterprise were gone, and the empire was a city and its suburbs again,
which is what the Latin empire had been.

The restoration succeeded completely and cost more than the thing it recovered
was worth. That is not hindsight applied afterwards: contemporaries in Anatolia
said so at the time, loudly enough that Michael's dynasty spent decades dealing
with Laskarid loyalism in the provinces it had stopped defending.
