By 1102, Florence was already governing itself through consuls -
magistrates who "guided everything, and governed the city, and decided
causes, and administered justice," in the words of the chronicler
Villani, serving one-year terms and answerable to a senate of a hundred
"buoni uomini," worthy citizens of standing. How much earlier this
system had taken root is impossible to say with certainty; it did not
yet exist in 897, and the record of individual consuls' names grows
reliable only from 1138 onward, when their number is known to have
ranged from four to as many as eleven depending on the year.

## An overlord who barely appears in the record

For a city whose own later history is inseparable from the great
Countess Matilda of Tuscany in the popular imagination, Florence's own
chronicles say remarkably little about her directly. The brief notices
that survive record chiefly her wars against the German emperor Henry
IV on behalf of the Church, and finally, in a single terse line: "In this
year (1115) died the Countess Matilda." That sparse treatment is itself
telling. Whatever feudal claim Matilda's own office carried over
Florence, the city's actual government - the consuls, their senate, their
year-by-year administration of justice - had already been running for a
decade or more before her death, largely independent of whatever
authority she nominally held.

## Independence as an accomplished fact rather than a single grant

Matilda's death is remembered as the moment that removed the last
feudal claim standing above the commune, but it did not create Florence's
own self-government - it simply cleared away whatever outside authority
might otherwise have contested a system already functioning in practice.
The city that emerged from this period was, by every account, prosperous
and expanding: its walls were extended in 1078 to accommodate a
population historians estimate at seventy to eighty thousand, and its
consuls, whatever the looseness of the record naming them, had already
begun sending expeditions against rival strongholds in the surrounding
countryside years before Matilda's own death removed any question of
whose authority those expeditions answered to.
