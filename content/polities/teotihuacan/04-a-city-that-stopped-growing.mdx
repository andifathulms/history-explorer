---
title: A city that stopped growing
drafted_from: cowgill-1997
phase: contraction
---

Teotihuacan's own later centuries show no dramatic single crisis, only
a slow loss of momentum. The city's population, after reaching a
plateau of a hundred thousand or more by the 300s, may have begun
declining significantly in its final century, though archaeologists
still dispute how sharply, since the pottery evidence that would settle
the question has not yet been dated precisely enough. Some households
remained clearly prosperous even as this happened, and the gap between
the wealthiest and poorest residents appears to have widened rather than
narrowed as the city's fortunes turned.

## Rivals rising as the centre weakened

Elsewhere in central Mexico, new centres such as Xochicalco and Cacaxtla
began developing influence of their own - possibly only after
Teotihuacan's own fall, but plausibly earlier, as a weakening capital
lost its ability to punish upstart neighbours the way it once had. A
government that had once relied on conquest and the steady flow of new
tribute to fund its own building projects had, without either, few good
options: bureaucratic institutions built for growth do not always adapt
gracefully to its absence, and there is no sign the city's own rulers in
this period found a way to break free of constraints - religious,
institutional, or possibly environmental - that a more centralized
regime in an earlier age might have simply overridden by force.

## A capital living on inherited prestige

Whatever combination of causes was at work, none of it was sudden.
Teotihuacan in its last century remained, by any regional standard, an
enormous and imposing city, its great avenue and pyramids still the
largest architectural achievement anyone in central Mexico had ever
seen. What it had lost was the ability to keep expanding that prestige
into new conquest, new tribute, or new construction - a capital
increasingly living on the accumulated weight of what earlier
generations had already built rather than adding meaningfully to it.
