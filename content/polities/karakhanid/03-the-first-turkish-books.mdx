---
title: Two books, and what they were claiming
drafted_from: golden-1992
phase: aside
---

Within seventy years of taking Bukhara, the Karakhanid courts produced the two
foundational works of Islamic Turkic letters. They were written a few years
apart, in different languages, for different audiences, and between them they
say something no chronicle on this page says.

The *Kutadgu Bilig* — the knowledge that brings good fortune — was completed in
1069 by Yusuf of Balasaghun, who presented it at Kashgar and was made a
chamberlain for it. It is a long didactic poem in Turkic, a mirror for princes
of the kind the Persian and Arabic traditions were full of, arguing through
four allegorical figures about justice, fortune, intellect and contentment. The
form is the point: it is written in the metre of the *Shahnama*, in Turkic, by
a man who plainly knew the Persian and Arabic models and chose not to use their
language. This site's Samanid page describes a court making Persian into a
literary language a century earlier. This is the same operation performed on
Turkic, by the dynasty that ended the one that did it for Persian.

Mahmud al-Kashgari's *Diwan Lughat al-Turk*, compiled in the early 1070s, went
the other way. It is a dictionary and encyclopaedia of the Turkic dialects —
vocabulary, grammar, proverbs, verse, tribal geography and a circular world map
— written in Arabic and dedicated to the caliph at Baghdad. It exists to teach
Arabs Turkish.

That is the claim, and it is worth stating plainly because Kashgari does. He
argues that learning Turkish has become necessary: the Turks now hold power,
God has given them rule, and men who deal with government will need their
language. Consider what that meant in the 1070s. The caliph in Baghdad had
been under Turkish protection since the Seljuks arrived; Khurasan was
Ghaznavid; Transoxiana was Karakhanid; and the slave soldiers running the armies
of Egypt and Iraq had come from the same steppe. A Turk was writing to the
caliph, in the caliph's language, to explain that the caliph's world now
required his own.

Everything after this page in the corpus is ruled by Turks or by Mongols. These
two books are the moment that becomes something the people concerned wrote
down.
