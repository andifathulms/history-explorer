---
title: Taking Bukhara
drafted_from: ei2-karakhanids

In 999 the Karakhanid ruler Nasr b. Ali took Bukhara from the Samanids and
extinguished the dynasty, having taken it once already in 992 and withdrawn.
There was very little fighting. The Samanid state had been dissolving for a
decade in factional wars among its own Turkish commanders, and the *ulama* of
Bukhara declined to authorise resistance to a Muslim ruler.

The Karakhanids then divided Transoxiana with the Ghaznavids along the Oxus —
Mahmud of Ghazna taking Khurasan, the Karakhanids taking everything north of the
river. The two dynasties that partitioned the Samanid inheritance had both come
out of it: one from its slave guard, one from the steppe beyond its frontier.

What the Karakhanids did with Transoxiana afterwards is the subject of the two
chapters after this one. It was not what the Samanids had done with it.
