Khufu's pyramid is 146 metres high as built, about 2.3 million blocks, the base
level to within a couple of centimetres across thirteen acres, the sides aligned
to true north to within a twentieth of a degree. It was the tallest structure
made by human beings for something like thirty-eight centuries, until a
cathedral spire in Lincolnshire in the fourteenth century AD. His son and
grandson built the second and third pyramids beside it; the Sphinx is carved from
a knoll left in Khafre's quarry.

The Fourth Dynasty covers about a hundred and twenty years and it is the peak of
this record by every measure the site has and several it does not.

## Not slaves

The workforce question has a clear answer and it is worth stating because the
wrong one is so widely held. Excavation of the workers' settlement south-east of
the pyramids has produced bakeries, breweries, dormitories, a cattle and sheep
supply operation on an industrial scale, and a cemetery of workers buried in
small tombs of their own with their titles on them. The picture is of a permanent
skilled core of a few thousand and a much larger rotating corvée drawn from the
provinces, fed by the state, organised in named gangs whose graffiti survive on
the blocks.

That is this record's `military_basis` as well as its labour system, and they are
the same system. Old Kingdom Egypt has no standing army. Expeditions to the Sinai
turquoise mines, to the Nubian quarries and up the Levantine coast were raised
out of the same provincial apparatus that raised the pyramid gangs, and the men
who led them describe themselves in their tomb autobiographies as officials who
conducted an expedition, not as soldiers. `conscript` is the value, and it covers
both the army and the pyramid.

## What a state that does this looks like

Kemp's account, which is where this record's institutions come from, treats the
pyramid not as an extravagance a strong state could afford but as the thing the
state was doing. The surplus was extracted, moved to the capital, converted into
a monument and an endowed mortuary cult, and the cult then continued to consume
revenue in perpetuity — every dead king kept his estates, his priests and his
offerings, forever, in principle.

That is the mechanism the last chapter is about, because the compounding is
obvious once stated. A state that permanently alienates a share of its land and
labour to each dead king, and never releases any of it, has a fixed and rising
charge on a fixed agricultural base. The Old Kingdom ran that arrangement for
five centuries.

## The scale afterwards

The Fourth Dynasty is the maximum and nothing later approaches it. Fifth and
Sixth Dynasty pyramids are smaller, built with rubble cores that have largely
collapsed, and the effort visibly shifts from the size of the structure to what
is written inside it: the Pyramid Texts, first cut in the burial chamber of Unas
around 2350, are the oldest substantial religious literature in the world.

Whether that shift is a state with less to spend or a state choosing to spend it
differently is exactly the kind of question this site's coded fields cannot
answer and its chapters exist for.
