There is a second explanation for the collapse and this site codes it as a
turning point marked `contested: true`, which is worth unpacking, because how a
project like this handles a climatic argument is a methodological question and not
only a historical one.

The claim: around 2200 BC there was a sustained arid episode across the eastern
Mediterranean and the Near East — the event that appears in the palaeoclimate
literature under a decidedly unpoetic name — which reduced the Nile flood for a
run of years. Lower floods mean smaller harvests on a system with no storage
margin to speak of, and famine follows.

The Egyptian evidence adduced for it is textual and comes from the wrong side of
the collapse: provincial tomb inscriptions of the First Intermediate Period in
which governors say, in the first person, that they fed their people in years of
hunger, gave grain to the starving, and kept their nome alive when others died.
Ankhtifi of Mo'alla says the whole of Upper Egypt was dying of hunger and that
men were eating their children.

## Why the site marks it contested rather than either accepting or dropping it

Coding rule 4.4 is specific: `contested: true` goes where scholarship disputes
that the event was a hinge, not where it disputes the date or the details. That is
exactly the situation. Nobody serious denies that the climatic downturn happened.
What is disputed is how much explanatory weight it should carry against the
internal mechanism the previous chapter describes — hereditary provincial office,
compounding mortuary endowments, a ninety-year reign — which is sufficient on its
own and does not need a drought.

And the boasting-about-famine inscriptions are not neutral evidence for famine.
They are a genre. A provincial governor asserting that he alone fed his people
while everyone else starved is making a political claim in a period when the
central state had stopped being the thing that fed anybody, and one would expect
the genre to exist whether or not the Nile was low.

## The general point

Hard rule 2 on this site forbids estimating a number, and the reason given is
that an estimate presented in a data field renders as a fact. The same hazard
exists for causes. A single-cause climatic explanation is unusually attractive
because it is clean, quantitative-sounding and external, and because the
palaeoclimate record is genuinely good — but the strength of the evidence that a
drought *happened* is not evidence about what it *did*, and running the two
together is the same move as averaging two sources into one figure.

So the turning point stays, because the downturn is real and dated and this
record's ending cannot be told without it, and the `contested` flag carries the
argument. This is the same instrument the site uses on the Ur III record's letters
about the fall of Ur and on the Middle Elamite record's new capital: a hinge
entered, and its status as a hinge marked as disputed, rather than a decision
smuggled in by omission.
