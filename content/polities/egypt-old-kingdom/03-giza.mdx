Khufu's pyramid is 146 metres high as built, about 2.3 million blocks, the base
level to within a couple of centimetres across thirteen acres, the sides
aligned to true north to within a twentieth of a degree. It was the tallest
structure made by human beings for something like thirty-eight centuries, until
a cathedral spire in Lincolnshire in the fourteenth century AD. His son and
grandson built the second and third pyramids beside it; the Sphinx is carved
from a knoll left in Khafre's quarry.

The Fourth Dynasty covers about a hundred and twenty years, and by any measure
— resources commanded, ground held, and what it left standing — it is the peak.

## Not slaves

The workforce question has a clear answer and it is worth stating because the
wrong one is so widely held. Excavation of the workers' settlement south-east
of the pyramids has produced bakeries, breweries, dormitories, a cattle and
sheep supply operation on an industrial scale, and a cemetery of workers buried
in small tombs of their own with their titles on them. The picture is of a
permanent skilled core of a few thousand and a much larger rotating corvée
drawn from the provinces, fed by the state, organised in named gangs whose
graffiti survive on the blocks.

The labour system was also the military system. Old Kingdom Egypt had no
standing army. Expeditions to the Sinai turquoise mines, to the Nubian quarries
and up the Levantine coast were raised out of the same provincial apparatus
that raised the pyramid gangs, and the men who led them describe themselves in
their tomb autobiographies as officials who conducted an expedition, not as
soldiers. One conscription, two uses.

## What a state that does this looks like

Kemp's account treats the pyramid not as an extravagance a strong state could
afford but as the thing the state was doing. The surplus was extracted, moved
to the capital, converted into a monument and an endowed mortuary cult, and the
cult then continued to consume revenue in perpetuity — every dead king kept his
estates, his priests and his offerings, forever, in principle.

That is the mechanism the last chapter is about, because the compounding is
obvious once stated. A state that permanently alienates a share of its land and
labour to each dead king, and never releases any of it, has a fixed and rising
charge on a fixed agricultural base. The Old Kingdom ran that arrangement for
five centuries.

## The scale afterwards

The Fourth Dynasty is the maximum and nothing later approaches it. Fifth and
Sixth Dynasty pyramids are smaller, built with rubble cores that have largely
collapsed, and the effort visibly shifts from the size of the structure to what
is written inside it: the Pyramid Texts, first cut in the burial chamber of
Unas around 2350, are the oldest substantial religious literature in the world.

Whether that shift shows a state with less to spend or a state choosing to
spend it differently is not a question any measurement settles, and it is the
question that matters most about the fifth and sixth dynasties.
