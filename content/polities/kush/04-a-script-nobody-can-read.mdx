Around 200 BC the Kushites stopped writing in Egyptian and started writing in
their own language, in a script they made for it.

Meroitic has twenty-three signs. There are two forms — a hieroglyphic one for
monuments, using Egyptian sign-shapes, and a cursive one derived from demotic for
everything else — and they map one to one. It is alphabetic with syllabic
features: consonant signs carry an inherent vowel, four vowel signs modify, and a
word-divider of two or three dots separates units.

Francis Llewellyn Griffith worked out the sound values in 1909, from a bilingual
inscription and from Egyptian transcriptions of Meroitic names. So the signs can
be read aloud.

Nobody knows what the words mean.

## A hundred and sixteen years

Meroitic is not a mystery like Linear A, where the script itself is unread. The
phonetics have been settled for over a century. The problem is the language: it
has no established relatives.

Egyptian is Afroasiatic and has cousins. Sumerian is an isolate but has a vast
bilingual corpus, because the Akkadians who took over Mesopotamia went on
learning it in schools and produced dictionaries — this corpus's Ur III and Old
Babylonian records are legible because of that. Elamite is an isolate and is
readable because the Achaemenids published in three languages at Behistun.

Meroitic has no long bilingual and no successor state that kept it as a learned
language. The corpus is a few thousand short texts, mostly funerary, mostly
formulaic. Perhaps a couple of hundred words are understood — enough to tell that
a stele records somebody's mother and father and the offerings they should
receive, and not enough to read a sentence with confidence.

Proposals connecting it to Nilo-Saharan languages have been made and are not
settled.

## What that costs this record

Everything the coded fields would otherwise carry.

This record's `succession_rule` is null and the third chapter argues that the
selection mechanism is an oracle and the eligibility rule may be matrilineal.
Both of those come from Egyptian-language stelae of the Napatan period and from
Greek and Latin outsiders. For the six hundred years after the script changes,
the kingdom is writing continuously about itself and this site cannot use a word
of it.

The Mitanni record in this corpus has one coded institution out of four because
its capital has never been found. The Median record has none because it is
disputed whether there was a state. This one has three of four, and the missing
material is not lost — it is on museum walls in Khartoum, Berlin and London,
legible, and unintelligible.

## And what it left

Three letters.

When the Christian kingdom downstream needed to write Old Nubian, it took the
Coptic alphabet and added three signs from Meroitic for sounds Coptic had no
letters for. This corpus's Makuria record counts Old Nubian as its single
influence claim, and those three borrowed characters are the only part of this
kingdom's writing system that anybody can currently understand in context.

Egyptian hieroglyphs to Meroitic to Old Nubian, on one river, over two and a half
thousand years. This site now carries all three polities, and the middle one is
the only one whose documents nobody can read.
