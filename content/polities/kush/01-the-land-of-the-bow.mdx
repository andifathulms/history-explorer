Egypt's name for the country upstream of the first cataract was Ta-Seti, the land
of the bow. Egypt's word for gold was *nub*, and Nubia is named from it.

Those two words are most of the relationship. For three thousand years, Egypt
went south for gold and came back with archers, and periodically tried to hold
the country directly — under the New Kingdom, Nubia was governed by a viceroy as
far as the fourth cataract, because Nubia had gold, and it slipped away when the
New Kingdom's grip failed.

When the New Kingdom's grip failed, what grew in the space was a Nubian kingdom
that in about 727 BC came down the river and took Egypt.

## The kingdom at Napata

What grew there is called the kingdom of Kush, ruled from Napata under the flat
sandstone bluff of Gebel Barkal, which Egyptian religion had held for eight
hundred years to be a dwelling of Amun. The Kushite kings took that over
entirely: Amun of Napata was their god, they built him a temple under the
mountain, and they buried their kings in pyramids nearby.

A Nubian dynasty adopting the gods, the script and the funerary architecture of
the empire that had ruled and taxed them is not obviously what one would predict.
What it produced was a line of kings who could argue, when they went north, that
they were more properly Egyptian than the Egyptians.
