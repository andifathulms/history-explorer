Esarhaddon left the empire divided between two sons on purpose. Ashurbanipal took
Assyria; Shamash-shum-ukin took Babylon. It held for sixteen years and then the
brother in Babylon raised the whole south against the brother in Nineveh.

The account of what followed is Ashurbanipal's own, and he is not defensive about
any of it.

## The siege

The war was decided by starvation, and the annals say so directly.

> At this time, the people of Akkad, who had cast in their lot with
> Shamash-shum-ukin, and plotted evil — famine laid hold upon them. They ate the
> flesh of their sons and daughters to stay their hunger, they gnawed leather
> thongs.

Then the gods — Ashur, Sin, Shamash, Adad, Bel, Nabu, Ishtar of Nineveh, the
queen of Kidmuri, Ishtar of Arbela, Urta, Nergal and Nusku, listed in full, who
march before me slaying my foes — cast Shamash-shum-ukin *into the burning flames
of a conflagration and destroyed him*.

Ashurbanipal's comment on the men who had organised the revolt is that they were
afraid of death and valued their lives highly, and therefore did not throw
themselves into the fire alongside their lord. He regards this as discreditable.

## Afterwards

The reprisals are itemised with the same care as the tribute lists.

Those who had spoken against Ashur had their mouths slit. The remaining prisoners
were taken to the place between the colossi where Sennacherib — *the father of the
father who begot me* — had been cut down forty years earlier, and were killed
there as an offering to his shade; their dismembered bodies were given to dogs,
swine, wolves, eagles, the birds of heaven and the fish of the deep.

And then, in the next breath and with no change of tone, the corpses of those who
had died of plague and famine — what was left of the feast of the dogs and swine,
the annals say, the limbs that blocked the streets and filled the squares — were
ordered cleared out of Babylon, Kutha and Sippar and heaped up outside.

Then the shrines were purified by priestly rite and the filthy streets cleansed.
The angry gods and wrathful goddesses of Babylon were appeased with penitential
prayer and psalm. The temple revenues, which had fallen off, were restored in
full as in the days of old. And on the survivors of the slaughter and the famine
the king *took pity and ordered their lives to be spared*, and settled them in
Babylon.

That sequence — the mass execution, the street-clearing, the liturgy, the
restored endowments, the mercy — is one continuous passage, and it is the clearest
statement in Assyrian writing of what the empire thought it was doing. Babylon
was not an enemy city. It was Assyria's own holy place in revolt, and it had to be
punished, cleaned, reconsecrated and refinanced, in that order, by the same king.

## What it cost

Chaldea, Aram and the Sea-land, which Shamash-shum-ukin had brought to one mouth,
were trodden under foot and given governors and prefects who were, in the phrase,
*creatures of my hand*. Yearly tribute was imposed without ceasing.

The empire won. Ashurbanipal went on to break Elam so thoroughly that it stopped
being a power, and Assyria in his last decades looks on the map like the largest
state the world had yet seen.

But the reserves had gone into a war against itself. The southern provinces had
been governed by an Assyrian prince and had still risen; Egypt, taken a
generation earlier, was already slipping; and the settlement in Babylonia now
rested on garrisons and appointees rather than on a brother of the king. Within
about twenty years of Ashurbanipal's death, Babylon and the Medes together took
Nineveh, and the state that had eaten its own second capital to hold it did not
survive the sequel by long.
