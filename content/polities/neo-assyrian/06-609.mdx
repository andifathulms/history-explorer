Ashurbanipal died around 631. Nineveh fell in 612. The Assyrian state was gone
by 609.

That is the fastest collapse of a great power anywhere in this corpus, and no
single cause explains it. What can be listed is what was going on.

There was a civil war in the previous generation. Esarhaddon had divided the
succession between two sons — Ashurbanipal in Assyria and Shamash-shum-ukin in
Babylon — under the elaborate loyalty oath this record's `succession_rule` field
is coded from, and the two of them fought a four-year war from 652 that ended
with Babylon starved into surrender and Shamash-shum-ukin dead in his burning
palace. The most carefully documented succession instrument in the ancient world
had failed at exactly the thing it was designed to prevent.

There was an exhausted army and an overextended frontier. Egypt had been taken
and lost; Elam had been destroyed, which removed a rival and also removed the
buffer between Assyria and the peoples of the Iranian plateau; and the annual
campaign that this record codes `plunder` for had been running for three
centuries.

There was Babylon, which had been sacked in 689 and rebuilt and revolted
repeatedly and finally, under Nabopolassar from 626, permanently.

And there were the Medes, on the record this corpus now files at the head of its
Iranian region, about whom the honest position is that we do not know what they
were. Something out of western Iran joined the Babylonians and helped take
Ashur in 614 and Nineveh in 612.

What is striking is how completely it went. Assyria did not become a rump or a
client kingdom; the great cities were destroyed and not reoccupied, the language
of the court stopped being written, and within two centuries Greek writers
passing through the region were recording legends about the ruins. Xenophon
marched an army past Nimrud in 401 and was told it had been a Median city.

This site records the ending as `conquest`, and its `by` field named one of the
two coalition partners for as long as the other one had no page. That has been
fixed. The Neo-Babylonian empire — the polity that had been at war with this one
since 626, that led the coalition, that then took Jerusalem and was itself taken
by Cyrus — is now a record here, and `by` names both. The change is small in the
file and is the reason the region draws a thread again: `neo-assyrian ->
neo-babylonian` has both ends inside the Ancient Near East, and the older edge to
the Medes, which is the same siege and the same year, now crosses into Iran and
is not drawn by any thread. Two edges, one event, both true.
