Ashurbanipal died around 631. Nineveh fell in 612. The Assyrian state was gone
by 609.

That is about as fast as a great power has ever collapsed, and no single cause
explains it. What can be listed is what was going on.

There was the civil war between the two brothers a generation earlier, fought
under the most elaborate loyalty oath the ancient world produced and won only
by starving out Assyria's own second capital. The most carefully documented
succession instrument in the ancient world had failed at exactly the thing it
was designed to prevent, and the empire that emerged from winning had spent
reserves a war against itself does not give back.

There was an exhausted army and an overextended frontier. Egypt had been taken
and lost; Elam had been destroyed, which removed a rival and also removed the
buffer between Assyria and the peoples of the Iranian plateau; and the annual
plundering campaign had been running for three centuries.

There was Babylon, which had been sacked in 689 and rebuilt and revolted
repeatedly and finally, under Nabopolassar from 626, permanently.

And there were the Medes, about whom the honest position is that nobody knows
what they were. Something out of western Iran joined the Babylonians and helped
take Ashur in 614 and Nineveh in 612.

<Figure id="nineveh-chronicle" />

What is striking is how completely it went. Assyria did not become a rump or a
client kingdom; the great cities were destroyed and not reoccupied, the
language of the court stopped being written, and within two centuries Greek
writers passing through the region were recording legends about the ruins.
Xenophon marched an army past Nimrud in 401 and was told it had been a Median
city.

Two powers took Nineveh, and both claims are true. The Babylonians under
Nabopolassar had been at war with Assyria since 626 and led the coalition; the
Medes came down out of western Iran and joined them. Neither could have done it
alone, and the empire that inherited the Assyrian position was the Babylonian
one, which went on to take Jerusalem and was itself taken by Cyrus within
seventy years.
