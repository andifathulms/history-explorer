---
title: A script built for an empire
drafted_from: bell-1924
phase: aside

The closeness between the Sakya hierarchy and the Mongol imperial
court produced more than a political arrangement; it produced a piece
of writing technology commissioned specifically to serve an empire far
larger than Tibet itself. A new script, devised under Mongol
patronage and associated with the Sakya lineage's own religious
authority at court, was intended to render every language spoken
across the Mongol domains - Mongolian, Chinese, Tibetan and more - in
a single unified system.

## An ambition the empire itself could not sustain

The project reflected the same logic that had produced the household
census and the myriarchy system: a belief that a single administrative
or technical solution, applied uniformly, could hold together an
empire spanning wildly different languages and cultures. In practice,
the script never displaced the established writing systems it was
meant to unify. Chinese continued to be written in Chinese characters,
Tibetan in its own alphabet, and the new script survived mainly in
official and ceremonial contexts rather than becoming anyone's
everyday tool.

## A byproduct that outlasted its own purpose

What the script does demonstrate, regardless of its practical failure,
is how far the relationship between Sakya and the Mongol court had
moved beyond simple religious patronage. A monastery's own hierarchy
had become embedded closely enough in the machinery of a
continent-spanning empire that the empire commissioned a new alphabet
through channels connected to that monastery's own religious
authority - a level of institutional entanglement no earlier Tibetan
religious establishment had ever approached, and none would approach
again in quite the same way after Sakya's own political power ended.
