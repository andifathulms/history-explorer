`successor_claims` on this record read null until this pass. It reads one, and
the gap between the claim and the thing claimed is three hundred and eighty-two
years.

In 1616 a Jurchen leader named Nurhaci declared a state in the forests of the
north-east — the same country this dynasty came out of, in the same language
group, among the descendants of the same people — and called it the Later Jin.
The claim was explicit: descent from this dynasty, and its name.

His son renamed it Qing in 1636, dropped the Jurchen ethnonym in favour of
Manchu, and in 1644 walked through Shanhai pass at the invitation of a Ming
general and took China. It lasted until 1912.

## Why this is a count and not an edge

Because the Qing is not a record on this site. It is one of about fifty backdrop
entries in `reference-set.yaml`, carried for its measurements so that the
percentile columns are computed against something wider than the corpus, with no
page and no chapters.

This corpus's Ming record names it in `ended.by`, which the site permits for
reference-set ids. Edges require records at both ends, so what would otherwise be
`jin → qing`, `claimed legitimacy of`, 1616 is a number in a column instead.

That is the second thing in this pass that the East Asia region cannot draw. The
first is on this corpus's Liao record: the Qara Khitai, founded by a Liao prince
who went west, are a full record here but in a different region, and threads do
not cross regions.

Both absences are the rules working rather than failing. But they are worth
counting: of the six polities in this region, the most interesting relation on
two of them cannot be shown in the picture the region draws.

## The pattern the three records make

Read this corpus's Liao, Jin and Yuan records in order and they are one argument.

A conquest dynasty holds north China by governing two populations under two
systems. It is destroyed by a frontier people it had been extracting from and
managing. That people then holds north China by governing two populations under
two systems. Three iterations — Liao to Jin to Mongol — and then a fourth, in
1616, by a man deliberately invoking the second one.

None of it is visible in any coded field. It is three `ended.by` values and one
successor claim, and the rest is this chapter.
