The Jurchen were forest and river peoples of eastern Manchuria, subjects of the
Liao, whose chieftain Aguda revolted in 1114 and destroyed the Liao empire within
eleven years. The Song, who had been paying the Liao tribute for a century,
allied with the Jurchen to be rid of them, and discovered in 1127 that they had
exchanged a neighbour who wanted silver for one that wanted Kaifeng.

The Jin took northern China and held it for a hundred and nine years, driving the
Song court south of the Huai river. Like the Liao they created a script of their
own, adapted from the Khitan and Chinese ones; unlike the Liao they moved their
capital into China, adopted much of the Chinese administrative apparatus wholesale,
and worried openly and continuously about their own people being absorbed by it.
Emperor Shizong's reign contains a sustained official programme to preserve
Jurchen language, dress and archery, which is evidence of how badly it was going.

They were destroyed by the Mongols between 1211 and 1234, in a campaign that
occupied Chinggis Khan and his successor for over twenty years — considerably
longer than the conquest of Khwarazm, and a reminder that the Islamic world's
catastrophe was one front of several.
