Qasim died in 924 — 1518 — and within twenty-five years the historian who had
described his court was writing that the Kazakhs had ceased to exist.

## The collapse

Contests ensued among the sultans. Qasim's son Mumash succeeded and died in one
of the wars, of shortness of breath. Then Tahir, son of Adik Sultan, took the
khanship, and Tahir was a harsh man who practised much cruelty, so that his people
— about four hundred thousand persons — suddenly deserted him and dispersed. He
was left alone among the Kirghiz and died at last in misery.

About thirty thousand men gathered afterwards in Moghulistan and made Tahir's
brother Bulash their khan. Then, Mirza Haydar says, the wheel of fate made such
strange revolutions that for the last four years not a trace has been visible of
these people.

He gives it twice, once in each of his two accounts, and the second time as an
arithmetic:

> In the year '30, the Kazak numbered a thousand thousand; in the year '44, not a
> vestige of all this host remains on the face of the earth.

## What the figures mean

They are not a census and they are not a massacre. Mirza Haydar is describing a
political fact in demographic language, because on the steppe the two were the
same thing: a horde exists exactly as long as its people choose to camp with its
khan.

Tahir's subjects did not die. They dispersed — to the Moghuls, to the Uzbeks, to
the Nogai, to the Kirghiz — and the name went out of use because there was nobody
left to whom it applied as a political description. The mechanism that had made
the khanate in 1465, when Abu'l-Khayr's people walked over to Kerei and Janibek,
ran in reverse.

## Why it is worth reading carefully

Because the same historian who wrote the obituary also recorded the hospitality
at Jud, and both are true, and they are twenty-five years apart.

A polity whose entire capital is the consent of mobile households can be the
largest power on the steppe in one generation and untraceable in the next, without
losing a battle that anyone thought decisive. Nothing was destroyed. The people
simply stopped being that.

Which is also why the obituary was wrong.
