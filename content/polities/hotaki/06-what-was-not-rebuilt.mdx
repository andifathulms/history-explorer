The Hotaki state lasted twenty-nine years and held a capital for seven of them,
and its afterlife is not a dynasty or a doctrine. It is a hole in a city and two
Afghan states that came out of the same quarrel.

## Isfahan

Abbas I had made Isfahan one of the largest cities on earth — the maidan, the
bridges, the avenue of plane trees, the Armenian suburb across the river with
its own churches and its own silk merchants. Chardin and Tavernier had described
it to Europe as a wonder.

After 1722 it was not that again.

The siege killed a large part of the population and the occupation drove out
much of the rest; the trees that had made it look like a forest had been felled
and sold as bark; the river had been used as a mass grave. When the Afghans left
in 1729 the government did not come back with them — Nadir ruled from Mashhad,
and the capital of Iran afterwards was Shiraz and then Tehran. Isfahan became a
provincial town containing the monuments of an imperial one, which is what it
still is.

That is the largest thing the Hotaks did, and they did it by waiting.

## The two states

The second consequence is the one that has outlasted everything.

Nadir Quli's entire career was built on being the man who reversed 1722. The
army he assembled to expel the Afghans was the instrument he took to Delhi nine
years later, and the empire he made and could not hold came directly out of the
Hotaki occupation. Without Mahmud there is no Nadir.

And on the other side of the same quarrel: the Abdali of Herat, the Ghilzai's
rivals, served Nadir. One of their commanders, Ahmad Khan Abdali, was with him
when he was murdered in 1747, took his treasury and his Afghan contingent east,
and founded the Durrani state at Kandahar. Modern Afghanistan dates itself from
that year.

Both Afghan polities of the eighteenth century, and the Iranian empire that
briefly took Delhi, come out of a dispute between a Safavid frontier province
and the governor sent to discipline it.

## What they did not leave

No administration, because they never built one. No legitimating claim that
anybody afterwards found worth invoking. No literature. No architecture. Their
own dynasty ended in 1738 when Nadir besieged Kandahar for a year, took it, and
destroyed the city.

They are one of the few states of this period that can be said to have left
nothing at all — and to have changed everything, by removing something that had
been there for two hundred and twenty-one years and could not be put back.
