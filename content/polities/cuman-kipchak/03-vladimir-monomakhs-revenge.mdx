Vladimir Monomakh grew up at Pereyaslavl, the border principality his
own father had ruled, and understood the Kipchak danger better than
any prince further north. Across two terms holding that frontier
throne, he built the region's defenses methodically: earthwork lines
and palisades on the Sula and Trubezh manned by scouts, a war-band
recruited from wider sources than any prince before him, and a
renewed practice of hiring the broken remnants of earlier Turkic
peoples - Berendians, Pechenegs, Torks - as auxiliary cavalry who knew
nomad warfare from the inside. His deeper achievement was political:
against Kiev's own weak prince Svyatopolk and his personal rival Oleg
of Chernigov alike, Monomakh pushed relentlessly for a unified
strategy against the common enemy, reviving a princely cooperation
that had not existed since the days of Yaroslav.

## Two campaigns that broke a generation of khans

At the Congress of Lubech, called mainly to settle disputes over
succession, Monomakh maneuvered the assembled princes into an alliance
against the Kipchaks instead. Renewed in 1103 after the crisis of
Vasilko's blinding interrupted it, that alliance produced two
offensive campaigns deep into the steppe. In 1103 the united princes
marched east from the Dnieper rapids and crushed the main Kipchak
force at a place called Suten, killing twenty khans including the
redoubtable Altunopa. In 1111 a second campaign crossed the Sula, Psel
and Vorskla, took towns in the Don basin, and won a decisive victory
at the Salnitsa on 27 March - a battle fought far enough east that
Vladimir's own son Yaropolk led a follow-up raid in 1116 that brought
back an Alan princess as his bride.

## Sixty years of quiet

The results were plain even where the chronicle's own details are
scant. Most Kipchak khans were killed, captured or driven into exile;
Bonyak escaped, though his own brother did not, and neither he nor
Sharukan is heard of again in the record. Ayepak fled to the Volga
Bulgars, who poisoned him. Vladimir made no attempt to hold new
territory - Tmutarakan was never recovered - choosing instead to
consolidate what the Russians already held. It worked. From Bonyak's
own eclipse until the rise of Konchak two generations later, southern
Russia's frontier saw scarcely a single serious violation for the
better part of sixty years.
