Hungary held its own against the Kipchak advance - a mountain barrier,
the province of Transylvania, and fierce Székely and Romanian
frontier communities let King Ladislaus turn back repeated raids at
Cserhalom and Lake Hód, reducing the Kipchaks to occasional raiders
there after a string of decisive defeats. Russia had no comparable
shield. Kiev, Pereyaslavl and Chernigov, its wealthiest cities, sat
directly on the southern frontier, permanently exposed to a people who
had come to regard Kiev itself - which they called Menkerman, "the
great city" - as the center of their own world.

## A monastery raided after Matins

No Kipchak khan matched the terror of Bonyak, called "the Scabby,"
"the godless," "the plunderer." He fought in the Byzantine campaigns,
led expedition after expedition into Kiev's own territory, and in 1096
broke into the Pechersky monastery itself - an intrusion so shocking
that the monk writing the Russian chronicle broke his own detached
tone to record it happening "while we were in our cells resting after
Matins." Bonyak's reputation ran deep enough both ways that Russian
princes who spoke well of him before Monomakh's own son were exiled
for it, and deep enough among his own people that his son, after
Bonyak's death, was killed trying to strike his lance against Kiev's
Golden Gate in imitation of his father's own gesture.

## Allies as often as enemies

Bonyak's value to the Russians was as real as his menace. His own
forces helped Russian princes win a major victory over the Hungarians
on the river San in 1097, one of several campaigns in which Kipchak
horsemen fought beside, not against, the cities they otherwise raided.
Other khans of the period - Tugorkan, Sharukan the Old, Altunopa,
Ayepak - kept up the same steady pressure on the trade caravans that
the wealth of the southern Russian cities depended on, without ever
possessing the organization or resources to conquer Russian territory
outright. What they could do, and very nearly did, was drive Russian
settlement off the whole northern fringe of the steppe for good.
