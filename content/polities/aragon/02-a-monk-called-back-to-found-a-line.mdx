title: A monk called back to found a line

Ramiro II "the Monk" had no ambition for the throne his brother's
strange will had thrust toward him, but Aragon's nobility needed a
king more than they needed his consent. The pope released him from
his religious vows so that he could marry, and the marriage produced
exactly what the kingdom required of it: a single daughter, Petronilla.
With an heir secured, Ramiro arranged her betrothal to Ramon
Berenguer IV, count of Barcelona, abdicated the throne he had never
wanted, and returned to his monastery within three years of leaving
it.

## A union named for the smaller partner

Petronilla's marriage joined the kingdom of Aragon to the county of
Barcelona - and, through it, to the whole of Catalonia, whose own
counts had built an independent, commercially ambitious Mediterranean
power over the previous two centuries while Aragon itself remained a
smaller, landward kingdom on the Ebro. The combined state that emerged
kept the Aragonese royal title, since Aragon alone carried the rank of
a kingdom while Catalonia remained, technically, a county - but
Catalonia's own wealth, population and commercial reach meant it would
go on being the more consequential half of the partnership for as
long as the union lasted.

## A son who traded his mother's name for his father's kingdom

Petronilla's own son, born Ramon Berenguer after his father, changed
his name to the more Aragonese-sounding Alfonso before he came into
his full inheritance - Catalonia in 1162, and the throne of Aragon
proper two years later, once his own mother formally abdicated in his
favour. He was the first ruler to hold both titles in his own right
rather than through a marriage or a regency, and the joint kingdom he
inherited - soon called, collectively, the Crown of Aragon - would
carry both halves of his parents' own inheritance into the
Mediterranean for the next three centuries.
