Peter III inherited a kingdom his own ambitions immediately outgrew.
He forced his younger brother, who held Majorca separately under
their late father's own division of the realm, to recognize himself as
a vassal of Aragon rather than an independent king, and established a
protectorate over Muslim Tunis that brought real commercial advantage
along with a convenient army stationed nearby. His marriage to
Constance, daughter of the deposed Sicilian king Manfred, gave him a
personal claim to an island he had every other reason to want as well.

## Vespers

Sicily itself, by 1282, was ruled by Charles of Anjou, a French prince
the pope had installed after defeating and killing Manfred in the name
of papal authority over the old Hohenstaufen inheritance. Charles's
own tyranny provoked the uprising remembered as the Sicilian Vespers,
in which the island's population turned with terrible violence on his
French garrison and officials. Peter, his army already positioned in
nearby Tunis and his wife's own claim ready to hand, accepted the
Sicilians' invitation and landed the same year. Within months, through
his own campaign and the naval genius of his admiral Roger de Lauria,
he controlled the entire island and, briefly, stretches of the
Italian coast besides.

## Excommunicated for a crown he would not give back

The pope's response was total: excommunication, a declared deposition
of Peter from his own Aragonese throne, and the offer of that throne
to a French prince, Charles of Valois, backed by a crusade proclaimed
against Peter as though he were a heretic rather than a rival Christian
king. A French army duly invaded and overran much of Catalonia before
Roger de Lauria's naval victory and an epidemic in the invaders' own
camp turned the campaign around and drove the French back across the
Pyrenees. Peter died that same year, offering on his deathbed to
return Sicily to the pope after all - a last concession to papal
authority that came only once he could no longer be blamed for having
kept the island in the first place, and one his own son would refuse
to honour regardless.
