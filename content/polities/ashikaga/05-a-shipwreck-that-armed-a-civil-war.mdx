By the sixteenth century the Ashikaga shoguns had become figureheads
in the fullest sense, presiding nominally over a country whose real
power had scattered among competing regional lords. It was into this
fractured landscape, in 1542, that a storm-driven Chinese pirate junk
carrying three shipwrecked Portuguese travellers made landfall on the
small island of Tanegashima, off the southern coast of Kyushu.

## A weapon more interesting than the visitors themselves

The islanders were more impressed by the arquebuses the shipwrecked
Portuguese carried than by the foreigners themselves. The local daimyo
invited one of the survivors, Fernão Mendes Pinto, to visit him, and
within a short time the secret of making gunpowder and firearms had
been taught to Japanese craftsmen directly. Within a few years,
according to native chronicles, firearms had spread into general use
across the country - weapons still called by the name of the island
where they first arrived, centuries afterward.

## A market that drew its own flood of newcomers

The trader who had brought Pinto to Japan cleared a fortune on his own
cargo, and word of the profit available drew hundreds of Portuguese
merchants to Japanese shores in the years that followed. Regional
lords, already locked in contests for territory and advantage that the
distant Ashikaga court could do nothing to arbitrate, competed with
each other to attract these foreign traders - each daimyo hoping the
weapons and wealth they brought would tip his own local balance of
power decisively in his favour.

<Figure id="ak-nanban" />

## Faith arrived where trade had already opened the door

The missionary followed the merchant, and the Jesuit Francis Xavier
landed in Kagoshima within a decade of the first Portuguese shipwreck,
finding his own success in Japan remarkably rapid. Firearms, foreign
traders and a new faith arrived
together, inseparably linked in native memory ever afterward - and none
of it did anything to restore the authority the Ashikaga shoguns had
already lost to the country's own warring regional lords.
