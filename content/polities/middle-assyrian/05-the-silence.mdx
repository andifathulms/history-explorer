Nobody conquered Assyria. That is the difficulty with this record's ending and
the reason its `ended.by` is empty.

What happened, over the decades after Tiglath-Pileser I's death in 1076, is that
Aramaean groups moved across the country between the Assyrian core and the
Euphrates and stayed. There is no battle anybody names, no capital taken, no
king deposed. The provinces west of the Tigris stop appearing in the record; the
Habur is gone; the routes to the Mediterranean are gone. By the death of
Ashur-bel-kala in 1056 Assyria held not much beyond the triangle of Ashur, Nineveh
and Arbela — and then, for something like a hundred and forty years, it stops
writing.

Not entirely: there are king lists, there are eponym lists, there are scraps. But
the archives Postgate works from end, and the royal inscriptions become brief and
few, and the century and a half between this record's close and the Neo-Assyrian
record's opening in 911 is the darkest stretch in this region after the Bronze Age
collapse itself.

The `ended` field reads `fragmentation`, and the word is being asked to cover
something it does not quite fit. The state did not break into pieces. It shrank
back onto its core and went quiet. `gradual absorption` would say Assyria was
absorbed, and it was not — it was still there in 911, with the same god, the same
title, the same city and, when the documents resume, recognisably the same
institutions. `conquest` would require a conqueror. Of the six values available,
`fragmentation` is the least wrong, and this paragraph is the honest version.

## The thing this site cannot draw

Which brings the record back to where its first chapter started.

In 911 Adad-nirari II began the reconquest, and this corpus's Neo-Assyrian record
starts there. Between that page and this one there is no edge. There is no
successor claim either: the count on this record is 0, not because nothing
followed but because what followed was not claiming anybody's legitimacy. It was
the same state.

The same is true of the two Babylons in this region, separated by a thousand
years instead of a hundred and forty. Both pairs need a relation this site does
not have — something meaning *the same polity, later, across an interruption* —
and in its absence the pairs sit in the region unconnected, while a raid that led
nowhere gets a line drawn for it because Mursili I happened to be a different
polity from the one he sacked.

This corpus has learned to state a vocabulary gap on the page rather than quietly
choose the nearest available label, and it has one other outstanding: nine records
now point at the absence of a value for sovereignty delegated by someone who is
not a caliph. This is the second, it is narrower, and it is
specific to a region where the same cities keep producing states. Adding a value
would mean auditing every record in the corpus for pairs that ought to carry it,
which is a decision about the whole site and not about this page.

What this page can do is refuse to fake it. There is no edge from Middle Assyria
to Neo-Assyria, and there should not be one until there is a type that says the
true thing.
