Nobody conquered Assyria. That is the difficulty with this record's ending and
the reason its `ended.by` is empty.

What happened, over the decades after Tiglath-Pileser I's death in 1076, is that
Aramaean groups moved across the country between the Assyrian core and the
Euphrates and stayed. There is no battle anybody names, no capital taken, no
king deposed. The provinces west of the Tigris stop appearing in the record; the
Habur is gone; the routes to the Mediterranean are gone. By the death of
Ashur-bel-kala in 1056 Assyria held not much beyond the triangle of Ashur, Nineveh
and Arbela — and then, for something like a hundred and forty years, it stops
writing.

Not entirely: there are king lists, there are eponym lists, there are scraps. But
the archives Postgate works from end, and the royal inscriptions become brief and
few, and the century and a half between this record's close and the Neo-Assyrian
record's opening in 911 is the darkest stretch in this region after the Bronze Age
collapse itself.

The `ended` field reads `fragmentation`, and the word is being asked to cover
something it does not quite fit. The state did not break into pieces. It shrank
back onto its core and went quiet. `gradual absorption` would say Assyria was
absorbed, and it was not — it was still there in 911, with the same god, the same
title, the same city and, when the documents resume, recognisably the same
institutions. `conquest` would require a conqueror. Of the six values available,
`fragmentation` is the least wrong, and this paragraph is the honest version.

## The thing this site cannot draw

Which brings the record back to where its first chapter started.

In 911 Adad-nirari II began the reconquest, and this corpus's Neo-Assyrian record
starts there. Between that page and this one there is no edge. There is no
successor claim either: the count on this record is 0, not because nothing
followed but because what followed was not claiming anybody's legitimacy. It was
the same state.

The same is true of the two Babylons in this region, separated by a thousand
years instead of a hundred and forty, and of the two Elams. All three pairs
needed a relation this site did not have — something meaning *the same polity,
later, across an interruption* — and while it was missing the pairs sat in the
region unconnected, with a raid that led nowhere getting a line drawn for it
because Mursili I happened to be a different polity from the one he sacked.

## What was done about it

Not an edge, and the reasoning is worth setting out because the obvious fix was
the wrong one.

Every edge in this corpus is an event with a year. A conquest, a secession, a
claim made on a date. This relation has no year, because nothing happened —
there is no morning on which Assyria resumed being Assyria, only a century and a
half during which it was quieter. And an edge is drawn by the region's thread,
which is the one picture on this site whose whole subject is succession. Feeding
it a non-succession is a step toward the single framing mistake this project was
built to avoid.

The other trap was making the relation geographic. Forty-seven pairs in this
corpus share a capital with a gap and no edge between them: Umayyad and Burid at
Damascus, Idrisid and Marinid at Fez, Han and Tang at Chang'an, Seleucid and the
principality of Antioch. Almost none of those are this. And this record and the
Neo-Assyrian one, which are, do not even share a capital — Ashur and Nineveh.
The test is not the map. It is whether the sources treat the two as periods of
one continuous object, which is why the field applies to four pairs and not to
forty-seven.

So it is a field on the record: `resumes`, naming the earlier polity, one
direction, no date, rendered as a sentence in the Succession section and drawn by
nothing. The Neo-Assyrian record carries `resumes: middle-assyrian`. The build
refuses a chain of three, refuses two records claiming the same predecessor,
refuses a pair whose spans overlap, and refuses a pair that already has an edge —
because a pair is either a succession or the same object resuming, and this site
does not assert both.

The other gap this page used to name alongside it, the missing value for
sovereignty delegated by someone who is not a caliph, was closed in the same
pass. `investiture` is in the `legitimation` vocabulary and the nine records that
argued for it carry it.

Two vocabularies changed, and both were changed the way the coding rules require
— argued on the merits from a pattern across many records, not bent to fit a
single inconvenient case.
