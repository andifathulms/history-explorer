Tukulti-Ninurta I reigned thirty-six years and did two things that no Assyrian
king had done. He conquered Babylonia, and he left Ashur.

In about 1225 he took Babylon, deported the Kassite king Kashtiliash IV in chains,
and carried the statue of Marduk north. It is the first time Assyria held
Babylonia and it lasted roughly seven years. The relationship between the two
states that this corpus's Neo-Assyrian record says Sennacherib poisoned in 689 —
sacking the city, demolishing its temples, diverting a canal over the site, and
setting off a chain that ended with his grandsons at war and, eventually, with
Nabopolassar — begins with this campaign. An Assyrian epic was written about it,
and a Babylonian text was written accusing Tukulti-Ninurta of sacrilege.

## Kar-Tukulti-Ninurta

Around 1233 he built a new capital directly across the Tigris from Ashur and moved
into it, naming it after himself: Port Tukulti-Ninurta.

A king of Ashur leaving Ashur is not a change of address. The city and the god have
the same name; the royal title is *iššiak Aššur*, the god's steward, and the
stewardship is of that place. This site's later Assyrian record has the same
manoeuvre repeated by Sargon II at Khorsabad seven hundred years afterwards, and
both new capitals were abandoned within a generation of their builder's death.
Both builders died violently.

In about 1207 Tukulti-Ninurta was besieged in his own new city by a conspiracy led
by his son, and killed.

## What a coded field cannot show

The `succession_rule` on this record reads `nomination`, which is also what the
Neo-Assyrian record reads, coded there from Esarhaddon's succession oath of 672 —
the most elaborate succession instrument in this corpus, sworn by every subject
and vassal of the empire, and comprehensively ignored by the two sons it was
designed to protect.

Coding rule 7 takes the rule and not the outcome, and this site has now made that
argument on several records in this region: the Neo-Babylonian page, where three
of five transitions were coups; the Ur III and Old Babylonian pages, where the
outcome was perfect and there is no rule to code at all; and here, where the rule
is clear and the peak reign ends in patricide.

Four records, four different relationships between the rule and what happened, and
the field says the same word or nothing. That is the field working correctly and
it is also the limit of what a coded vocabulary can carry, which is why the
chapters exist.

## After

The empire came apart within a decade. Babylonia was lost, the west was lost, the
new capital was abandoned, and Assyria did not reach the same extent again for a
century — until Tiglath-Pileser I, who is this record's peak ruler and who reached
the Mediterranean, cut cedars in Lebanon, and hunted elephants in Syria, all of
which he recorded at length.
