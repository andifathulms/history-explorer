Magellan's fleet reached Cebu in April 1521 already carrying word of the
island from a Malay-speaking slave in the captain-general's own service,
who found he could be understood there - a sign, Pigafetta notes, of how
far the same trading world reached. The king who met them, Raja Humabon,
governed a tribute network of his own: Pigafetta lists village after
village on the island by name, each with its own chief, "all of whom
rendered obedience to us, and gave us food and tribute" once Humabon had
struck his own bargain with the newcomers.

## A custom neither side had to explain twice

The bargain itself followed a form the islanders evidently already knew.
Humabon asked, through an interpreter, whether the Spanish captain wished
tribute paid to his emperor; told no, that Magellan wanted only trade and
friendship, the king proposed the exchange of gifts and then blood that
was, he said, customary whenever captains came to that port - each side
drawing blood from the right arm for the other to drink, a pledge neither
side needed the custom explained to them twice. When one of Humabon's
allied chiefs voiced hesitation about firearms, Magellan had a soldier
demonstrate a full suit of armor and had the man told that Spanish weapons
struck only enemies and were, in the captain's own words relayed by
interpreter, "soft toward our friends and harsh toward our enemies" -
theater aimed less at the frightened chief himself than at persuading him
to carry the message back to his king.

## A question about who came after

Before any blood was drawn, Magellan pressed the visiting prince and the
allied king of nearby Mazaua on a genealogical point that mattered more to
a European captain than to his hosts: who would succeed to the rule after
Humabon's death. Told that the king had no sons, only daughters, and that
the eldest daughter's husband was therefore the prince who stood before
him, Magellan used the answer as an opening to argue for the Christian
God and the honoring of parents - the first thread of an evangelizing
campaign that, within the week, would carry the whole island with it.
