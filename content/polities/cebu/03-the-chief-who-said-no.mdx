Not every chief in Humabon's orbit accepted the new alliance. On the
neighboring island of Mactan, the chief Zula sent his son with gifts and
an excuse: he could not deliver the tribute he had promised because a
rival chief on the same island, Lapulapu, refused to obey either Humabon
or the king of Spain, and Zula asked for a single boatload of soldiers to
help him bring Lapulapu to heel. Magellan chose to go himself, with sixty
armed men, over the objections of companions who begged him not to - he
was, Pigafetta writes, "like a good shepherd" and would not abandon
people he had made his own.

## A battle fought in the water

Lapulapu's warriors, more than fifteen hundred strong by Pigafetta's
count, met the landing party on the beach before dawn on 27 April 1521.
Muskets and crossbows fired for half an hour to no effect, the shots
passing through wooden shields without dropping a single defender; when
Magellan ordered houses burned to frighten them, the islanders only
fought harder, killing two Spaniards near the burning buildings. An arrow
struck Magellan in the leg, and he ordered a slow retreat, but most of his
men broke and ran for the boats, leaving six or eight beside him as
warriors converged on the one man they recognized. Wounded again in the
face and then the leg, unable to fully draw his sword, Magellan went down
under spears and cutlasses in water up to his own men's knees - the men
around him retreating, in Pigafetta's account, only once they saw he was
already dead.

## A trophy Humabon's own warriors never touched

Humabon's men, held back in their own boats on Magellan's order before the
fighting began, never joined it. That afternoon the Christian king sent
an offer, with Spanish approval, to ransom the captain's body for as much
merchandise as Lapulapu's people wanted; they refused, saying they meant
to keep him as a memorial rather than surrender him for any price. Eight
Spaniards and four native Christian allies died with Magellan; of the
defenders, by Pigafetta's count, only fifteen fell - a lopsided result
that left the expedition's survivors to choose new commanders and
reckon, for the first time since Cebu had sworn its blood oath, what an
alliance with a foreign captain who could be killed on a nearby beach was
actually worth.
