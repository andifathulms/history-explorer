Within a week of the blood compact, by Pigafetta's count, eight hundred
people on Cebu and some of the neighboring islands had accepted baptism -
counting men, women and children across a population that had, only days
before, met the Spanish as strangers. Humabon was baptized first; his
queen followed at a separate ceremony, arriving with forty attendant
women in a costume Pigafetta describes in detail - black and white cloth,
a palm-leaf hat like a parasol, mouth and nails stained red - and was so
overcome, he writes, that she wept and asked for baptism before the
priest had finished explaining it. She was named Johanna, after the
Spanish emperor's own mother; her daughter, the prince's wife, became
Catherina; the allied queen of Mazaua, Lisabeta.

## The idols burned before the king

Conversion reached beyond ceremony into the island's own religious
buildings. An old man near death was carried out to be healed by prayer
rather than by the customary rites of the local healers; when he
recovered within five days, an idol certain women had hidden in his own
house was burned in front of Humabon and the assembled people, and shrines
along the shore where consecrated meat had been offered were pulled down
by the islanders themselves, who Pigafetta says cried "Castiglia!
Castiglia!" as they did it and vowed to burn every idol they could find,
even inside the king's own house. One village that refused Humabon's
authority and the new alliance alike was burned in turn and marked with a
cross rather than the column the Spanish reserved for Muslim opponents -
a heathen population, in the fleet's own reckoning, easier to bring back
than a Muslim one.

## A little Jesus for the queen's own idols

When the queen asked to keep the small wooden image of the Christ child
she had been shown during her baptism, in place of her own idols, she was
given it outright - a single carved figure that would return to notice
decades later, when a second Spanish expedition found what they took to
be the same image still standing, and reverenced, in a house on the same
island.
