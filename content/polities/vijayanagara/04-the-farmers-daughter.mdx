Harihara II's eldest son Bukka II held the throne only a few years
before his younger brother Deva Raya I was crowned in November 1406.
Within weeks of his own accession, by Firishtah's telling, Deva Raya
was consumed by reports of a farmer's daughter named Pertal, living in
the disputed border town of Mudkal, whose beauty a Brahman go-between
had praised to him without limit. When her family, warned only after
the fact, learned that royal riders were coming to seize her for the
king's household, they fled with her before the raiding party arrived
- who then plundered the countryside on their way home and lost two
thousand men to a local counterattack.

## A war fought over a girl who was never caught

The raid gave the Bahmani sultan Firuz Shah his opening. He marched on
Vijayanagara itself that winter, broke into parts of the city before
being driven back, and settled in for a siege that pinned Deva Raya
inside his own capital for months while Bahmani detachments ravaged
the countryside to the south, took the fortress of Bankapur, and
carried off sixty thousand prisoners. Deva Raya sued for terms Sewell
calls humiliating to the last degree: an immense indemnity, the
permanent cession of Bankapur, and his own daughter's hand in marriage
to the sultan who had just devastated his kingdom - the first time,
by the chronicler's own account, that a Vijayanagara king had married
a daughter outside his own caste. Firuz Shah attended the wedding at
Vijayanagara itself with evident ceremony, though the peace between
the two kings soured almost immediately afterward over a perceived
slight. Pertal herself was never caught in the raid that started the
war; Firuz Shah later had her brought to his own court regardless and
married her to his eldest son.

## A throne passed quietly, for once

Deva Raya I ruled at least into 1412 without further recorded
catastrophe and was succeeded by his son Vira Vijaya, who left the
kingdom to Deva Raya II in turn - a quiet transition, notable mostly
for not producing another crisis, in a dynasty that had spent much of
its first seventy years fighting for its own survival on the Krishna
frontier.
