title: A king who killed his own family

Venkata I, who succeeded his brother Tirumala's own heir in 1586,
reigned at Chandragiri for twenty-eight years and died an old man in
1614 - the longest and, by the account of a Portuguese eyewitness
named Barradas who was present at court, one of the steadiest reigns
the diminished kingdom had known since Talikota. On his deathbed the
old king called for his nephew Chica Raya, offered him the royal ring
twice over the young man's own tearful protests, and had him formally
proclaimed heir before dying three days later at sixty-seven. Three of
his queens burned themselves on his funeral pyre, dressed in their
richest jewels, which they distributed to relatives and Brahmans and
threw to the crowd before mounting the flames.

## A crown taken within days

Chica Raya's reign lasted barely long enough to be noticed. Three of
the old king's own nobles - his army commander, his minister, and a
kinsman named Narpa Raya, each nursing a private grievance against the
new ruler's demands - secretly joined a fourth noble, Jaga Raya, in a
conspiracy to depose him. Admitted to the fortress under the pretense
of paying homage, their combined forces seized its gates within an
hour; Chica Raya, surrounded and abandoned, surrendered on a promise
of safe treatment, and Jaga Raya crowned his own great-nephew king in
his place before the deposed ruler had even left the palace grounds.

## The washerman's laundry

One captain alone, Echama Naique, refused to recognize the new
regime, calling Jaga Raya's puppet "a boy who was the son of no one
knew whom." Unable to reach the imprisoned Chica Raya directly, he
bribed the man who washed the royal family's clothes to smuggle out
the king's twelve-year-old second son hidden among soiled laundry,
past guards warned off by cries of "keep at a distance." A second
rescue attempt, an underground tunnel dug toward the king's own cell,
came within moments of success before a guard's foot broke through
the passage floor by accident and gave it away.

## What Jaga Raya ordered instead

The failed tunnel decided Chica Raya's fate. Jaga Raya sent his own
brother to the prisoner with a choice: kill himself and his family, or
be killed by the messenger's dagger. Chica Raya beheaded his wife, his
youngest son, and his young daughter with his own hand, had his
married elder son kill his own wife in turn, and then fell on his
sword; the son did the same. Only a small daughter was spared the
king's own hand - Jaga Raya's brother killed her himself, so that no
one of the royal blood would survive to challenge the succession he
had just engineered by murder.

## A battle that undid the murder

The killings that were meant to secure the throne cost Jaga Raya the
war for it. Nobles horrified by the massacre defected to Echama
Naique, who met Jaga Raya's larger army in the field, broke it in a
single engagement, and captured the royal insignia along with treasure
Sewell's source valued at two million in precious stones alone. He
proclaimed the rescued twelve-year-old, Chica Raya's own surviving
son, king of the whole empire in Jaga Raya's place - though Jaga Raya
himself escaped to the jungle, rallied fresh support, and kept the
civil war alive for at least two more years, drawing in the rival
Nayaka lords of Madurai and Tanjore on opposite sides until, Barradas
wrote from Chandragiri in 1616, armies numbering close to a million
men between them had gathered on the plains around Tiruchirappalli.
