The throne changed hands violently before Vijayanagara reached its own
highest point. Around 1490 a powerful lord named Narasimha, previously
styled only a minister, had himself declared king, ending the line
descended from Harihara and Bukka and founding a second dynasty. His
successors held power unevenly until 1509, when Krishna Deva Raya came
to the throne — a king a Portuguese eyewitness who saw him in person
would later call the greatest of every Vijayanagara ruler before or
since, in open contradiction of Muslim chroniclers who barely mention
him by name.

## A crushing blow at Raichur

Krishna Deva Raya's reign settled, in his own favor, a long-running
dispute over the Raichur doab, the fertile triangle of land between the
Krishna and Tungabhadra rivers that both Vijayanagara and the Bijapur
sultanate claimed. In 1520 his army handed Bijapur's Ismail Adil Shah a
defeat so complete that even Firishtah, a hostile Muslim historian who
otherwise treats Krishna Deva Raya as barely worth naming, admitted the
Sultan had been dealt a crushing blow. Vijayanagara held Raichur and
the doab it commanded for the rest of Krishna Deva Raya's reign.

## A city as large as Rome

It was in this same period, around 1520, that the Portuguese traveler
Domingo Paes visited the capital and left an eyewitness account later
generations would treat as unmatched by any similar record from the
period. Climbing a hill to survey the city, unable to see it whole from
any single vantage point, Paes wrote that what he could see "seemed to
me as large as Rome, and very beautiful to the sight" — a city of
groves and gardens, water channels running through it, palaces backed
by lakes, and streets given over in turn to every craft and nation
trading in the kingdom's wealth. Nuniz, writing a generation later,
confirmed that it was Krishna Deva Raya himself, not any general acting
in his name, who had led the armies that made this wealth possible — a
king remembered, in the sources that saw him directly, as ruling in
person rather than as a figurehead for others.
