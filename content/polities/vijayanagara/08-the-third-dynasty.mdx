The Muhammadan armies went home within days of finishing their work,
and the man they left behind was Tirumala, now sole regent, holding
King Sadasiva as his prisoner at the fortress of Penukonda. His first
recorded act as ruler of the wreckage was to summon Portuguese horse
traders from Goa, take delivery of a large consignment of animals,
and then dismiss the merchants without paying them - men who, by an
Italian traveller's own account, were left "desperate, and, as it
were, mad with sorrow and grief," unable even to leave the country
safely while robber bands roamed the roads. It was not a promising
start to a reign, and the nobles of the empire drew the obvious
conclusion: one after another, they stopped waiting for orders from
Penukonda and began ruling their own districts as independent men.

## A murder that founded a dynasty

Tirumala went on recognizing Sadasiva as his own sovereign for three
more years - inscriptions dated as late as February 1567 still name
the captive king as overlord - before dispensing with the fiction
altogether. In 1568 he murdered Sadasiva and took the throne outright,
founding what genealogists would later call the third dynasty of
Vijayanagara, its descent from the old royal house real but never
conclusively traced. There was no capital left to defend it: what
remained was a title, a treasury, and a shrinking radius of obedience.

## Everything beyond the horizon

The shrinking did not stop. Bijapur took the fortress of Bankapur in
1573 after an eighteen-month siege of its own already-independent
governor, destroying a temple there and building a mosque on its
foundation; the same Bijapur campaign against Adoni found the place
already held by a former Vijayanagara vassal ruling in his own name.
Three years later a Bijapur attack on Bellamkonda frightened Tirumala's
successors into abandoning Penukonda itself for Chandragiri, deeper
south and further still from the ruined capital across the river.
Golconda, no more scrupulous than its rival, broke a standing
agreement in 1579 to seize Vinukonda and Kondavid along with two
lesser forts, and in 1589 its king founded an entirely new city,
Hyderabad, on ground the old empire's writ had once reached. None of
it was reconquest in any organized sense - each sultanate simply
helped itself to whatever a court at Chandragiri no longer had the
strength to hold.
