What the Zirids had after 1057 was Mahdia — the fortified peninsula on the
Tunisian coast that the Fatimids had built as their own first capital, designed
to be defensible from the land side by people who expected trouble from exactly
that direction.

<Figure id="skifa" />

They lived there for ninety years, and the state they ran from it was a
different kind of thing. There was no province left to tax, so they taxed the
sea: shipping, customs, the grain trade with Sicily, and a fleet that raided
and escorted. Zirid squadrons operated across the central Mediterranean, and
for a time the dynasty was involved in Sicily — where the Muslim emirate the
Aghlabids had founded was breaking into factions, and where one faction invited
in the Normans.

That invitation was the end of them. Norman Sicily under Roger II wanted
the African grain coast, and it took it in pieces: Jerba, then Tripoli, then
through the 1140s the Tunisian ports one after another. In 1148 the Normans
took Sfax, Gabès and Mahdia itself. Al-Hasan, the last Zirid, got out, spent
years as a guest of various Maghribi powers, and died in 1167 under Almohad
protection.

## What actually destroyed them

No dynasty took Ifriqiya from the Zirids. That is the thing worth holding onto,
because it is very unusual.

A migration ruined the tax base. The state retreated to a port and lived on the
sea for ninety years. Then a Norman fleet took the port — and the Sicily those
Normans ruled was the one the Aghlabids had conquered from Kairouan three
centuries earlier, with an administration still working in Arabic.

There is no single agent to name for an ending of that kind, and no year at which
somebody decided to do it. A pastoral migration is not a policy, and the people
who carried it out did not found anything, take a capital or leave a dynasty.
They simply arrived, in numbers, over decades, and pastured over a country that
had been under the plough since Carthage.

## The shape they left

Which is the shape the region kept. Ifriqiya without an
agricultural interior, governed from the coast, with a tribal hinterland that
the state negotiated with rather than administered — that is the country the
Almohads took, and the country the Hafsids inherited from them and ran for three
centuries afterwards.
