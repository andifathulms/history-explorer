title: A port instead of a province

What the Zirids had after 1057 was Mahdia — the fortified peninsula on the
Tunisian coast that this site's Fatimid page records as the Fatimids' own first
capital, built to be defensible from the land side by people who expected
trouble from exactly that direction.

They lived there for ninety years, and the state they ran from it was a
different kind of thing. There was no province left to tax, so they taxed the
sea: shipping, customs, the grain trade with Sicily, and a fleet that raided
and escorted. Zirid squadrons operated across the central Mediterranean, and
for a time the dynasty was involved in Sicily — where the Muslim emirate this
site's Aghlabid page created was breaking into factions, and where one faction
invited in the Normans.

That invitation is the end of this page. Norman Sicily under Roger II wanted
the African grain coast, and it took it in pieces: Jerba, then Tripoli, then
through the 1140s the Tunisian ports one after another. In 1148 the Normans
took Sfax, Gabès and Mahdia itself. Al-Hasan, the last Zirid, got out, spent
years as a guest of various Maghribi powers, and died in 1167 under Almohad
protection.

The record here types the ending `conquest` with no agent named, because the
Kingdom of Sicily is not on this site — which is a gap worth stating rather
than working around, given that the Normans held the African coast for a decade
and that the Sicily they ruled was the one the Aghlabids had made.

Two things to keep from the page.

The Zirid century and a half is the clearest case in this corpus of a polity
being destroyed by something other than a rival state. No dynasty took Ifriqiya
from them. A migration ruined the tax base, they retreated to a port, and a
Norman fleet eventually took the port. `ended.by` is empty and the vocabulary
has no value for the thing that actually did it.

And the shape they left behind is the one the region kept. Ifriqiya without an
agricultural interior, governed from the coast, with a tribal hinterland that
the state negotiated with rather than administered — that is the country the
Almohads took, and the country the Hafsids on this site's page inherited and
ran for three centuries afterwards.
