The family split in 1015, when a cadet branch under Hammad refused subordination
and put the Abbasid caliph's name in its own sermon. That branch held the central
Maghreb for a century and a half, and what it built is the reason to dwell on
it.

Qal'at Bani Hammad was founded in 1008 in the Hodna mountains of what is now
northern Algeria — a fortified capital on a slope at about a thousand metres,
chosen for defensibility rather than for trade or agriculture, in country where
neither is abundant. It was the Hammadid seat for about eighty years before
they moved down to Bougie on the coast, and then it was abandoned; Almohad
forces destroyed what was left of it in the 1150s.

<Figure id="qala2" />

Because it was abandoned rather than built over, it survives. The site holds
the remains of the palaces, the cisterns and water channels, the walls, and a
congregational mosque with a square minaret twenty-five metres high — one of the
oldest standing in Algeria, and in the same tradition as the Kairouan minaret it
was built two centuries after. It is a UNESCO site, and the reason it matters
archaeologically is precisely that nobody kept living in it: a fortified Muslim
city of the eleventh century preserved at the moment it stopped, rather than
edited by eight centuries of continuous occupation.

<Figure id="stucco" />

The split is usually filed as a succession quarrel, and it is better understood
as the ordinary geography of the Maghreb asserting itself.
Ifriqiya faces the sea and lives on its coast and its plain; the central
Maghreb is mountains and steppe and lives on the routes crossing it. A single
dynasty holding both was a Fatimid administrative convenience, and it lasted
forty-three years.

And the two halves ended the same way and for the same reason. Both were driven
out of their inland capitals by the Banu Hilal migration — the Zirids from
Kairouan in 1057, the Hammadids from the Qal'a in the 1090s — and both moved to fortified ports, Mahdia and Bougie, and lived on
shipping for their last century. The migration did not conquer anybody. It
moved two dynasties to the coast and left the interior to the tribes, which is
the map the Almohads and then the Hafsids inherited.
