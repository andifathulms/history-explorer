Diarmait Mac Murchada ruled Leinster as one of Ireland's more powerful
provincial kings, and had, by most accounts, made himself hated among
his own nobles as thoroughly as any of his rank. The event later
chroniclers fixed on as the seed of his eventual ruin was one born of
private passion rather than politics: Tigernán Ua Ruairc of Bréifne
held as his wife a woman named Derbforgaill, and she and Diarmait had
loved each other for some time before either found the chance to act
on it.

## An abduction with the lady's own consent

The opportunity came when Ua Ruairc left his own territory on
business abroad. Derbforgaill sent word to Diarmait that she was
ready to do as he wished if he would only come for her, and he
gathered a following and carried her off into Leinster - by the
chronicle's own telling, willingly on her part, whatever show of
distress she made for appearances. Ua Ruairc, informed of the affair
on his return, was consumed less by the loss of his wife, the account
says, than by the shame of it, and set about gathering every ally he
could find against Diarmait.

## A grudge with a long memory

Nothing about the affair itself immediately cost Diarmait his throne;
years passed before the consequences caught up with him. But the
enmity it created in Ua Ruairc proved durable in a way few political
quarrels of the period were, and when a wider coalition against
Diarmait finally formed in 1166, Ua Ruairc's own old grievance was
central to it - a private wrong from fourteen years earlier furnishing
the occasion, if not quite the sole cause, for driving one of
Ireland's provincial kings into an exile that would change the
country's history far beyond anything either man could have intended.
