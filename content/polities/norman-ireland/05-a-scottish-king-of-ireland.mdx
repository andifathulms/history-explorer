Edward Bruce, brother of Robert the Bruce of Scotland, landed in
Ulster in 1315 at the invitation of Irish lords hoping to use Scotland's
own war against England as cover for a rising against the colony
closer to home. The Earl of Ulster's forces were routed at Connor that
September, and for the next three years Bruce's presence turned
loosely connected local grievances into something closer to a general
war against English rule across much of the island.

## Towns burned from Sligo to Athlone

The fighting spread well beyond Ulster. In Connaught, rival claimants
to the kingship there took Bruce's invasion as their own opportunity,
burning English towns and castles from Sligo to Athlone under his
encouragement, while in Thomond the Anglo-Norman lord Richard de Clare
found the local Irish dynasty he had depended on switching allegiance
to the invaders. No part of the English colony could simply wait the
crisis out; garrisons and settlers across the country found themselves
drawn into a conflict none of them had chosen.

## Faughart

The invasion ended, after three years that left much of the colony
devastated, at the Battle of Faughart near Dundalk in October 1318.
English forces broke the Scottish line in a single rush; Edward Bruce
himself was found dead beneath the body of an English knight named
John Maupas, and the leader who brought his head to the king, John de
Bermingham, was made Earl of Louth for it. The immediate danger ended
with Bruce's death, but the colony that survived him was not the one
that had existed three years before - poorer, more exposed, and
considerably less confident that English rule in Ireland was
permanent.
