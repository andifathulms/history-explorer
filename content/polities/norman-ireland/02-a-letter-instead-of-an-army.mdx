In 1166 the coalition Tigernán Ua Ruairc had spent years assembling -
joined by the high king Rory O'Connor himself - drove Diarmait Mac
Murchada out of Leinster entirely. He crossed first to England, then
on to France, where King Henry II was occupied with other business
of his own. Diarmait had nowhere else obvious to turn: he asked Henry
for help restoring him to his kingdom.

## A grant of permission, not of soldiers

Henry received him courteously and sympathized with his case, but had
no intention of committing an army of his own to an Irish succession
dispute. What he gave instead was a letter, addressed to all his
subjects - English, Norman, Welsh and Scottish alike - declaring that
he had received Diarmait into his grace and permitting any who wished
to help restore him to do so freely, with the king's own blessing.
Diarmait, provided with money from Henry's own treasury besides,
crossed to Bristol to make what he could of the offer.

## Recruiting an army from a royal permission slip

He spent time at Bristol reading the king's letter aloud to whoever
would listen, and promising land, rents, and rich gifts to any who
would come fight for him. For a long while no one of real substance
answered. Only when Richard de Clare, Earl of Strigoil - a man whose
own fortunes in England had faded, and who had every reason to want
what Diarmait was offering - finally came to him did the enterprise
gain the backing it needed. Their agreement was blunt: Strongbow would
help Diarmait with all the force he could muster the following summer,
and in return would receive Diarmait's own daughter in marriage, along
with the whole of Leinster to go with her.
