Nzinga's death in 1663 did not end the war her own family had fought for
nearly a century. It removed the one figure Portugal had never managed to
defeat, and within a decade the remaining Ngola - Dom João Hari, ruling
what was left of the old kingdom from the fortified heights of Pungo
Andongo - found himself facing the same colonial power alone.

## A nine-month siege

Portuguese forces under Luís Lopes de Sequeira laid siege to Pungo Andongo
in 1671. The stronghold held out for months before the position finally
collapsed. Dom João Hari fled to the neighboring territory of Libolo, was
captured there, and was beheaded rather than kept alive - a precaution, by
the Portuguese governor's own reasoning, against the risk that allies might
free him if he were held prisoner instead. A Portuguese fort went up on the
captured heights, and what remained of an independent Ndongo state ended
with it.

## Sixteen princes, and nowhere safe to put them

The dead king was not the only member of the royal line the Portuguese now
held. Roughly sixteen descendants of an earlier Ndongo king, Dom Filipe I,
were taken prisoner in the campaign's aftermath - not enslaved outright,
like most of the war's other captives, but deported across the Atlantic
under guard. They reached Brazil first, then were shipped on to Lisbon by
1673. The reasoning was explicitly political rather than punitive: Portuguese
authorities judged that princes with a hereditary claim to Ndongo were too
dangerous to leave in Angola, where they might rally a rebellion, or even in
Brazil, where the colonial government feared they could make common cause
with the maroon community at Palmares. In Portugal they were dispersed among
convents around the country, held as prisoners while being given a
religious and Portuguese education - a captivity that kept them alive, and
kept them permanently apart from the kingdom their blood still had a claim
to.

Ninety-six years after a Portuguese fleet first arrived at Ndongo's own
coast promising trade, the war that followed ended not with a treaty or a
line of succession but with a captured fortress, a beheaded king, and a
scattering of princes locked into convent cells an ocean away from the
throne they were born to.
