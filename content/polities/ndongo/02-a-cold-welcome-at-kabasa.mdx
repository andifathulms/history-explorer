title: A cold welcome at Kabasa

In 1560, after a long voyage down the coast, the Portuguese explorer Paulo
Dias de Novais landed near the mouth of the Kwanza River and asked to be
presented to the King of Ndongo. He and his party of Jesuits, traders and
Portuguese dignitaries were brought inland to Kabasa, the kingdom's
capital, and presented to Ngola Kiluanje kia Ndambi - the earliest king of
Ndongo any source names, and, though nobody at Kabasa could have known it
that year, the great-grandfather of the woman who would carry the kingdom's
name farther than any of them.

## Trade offered, suspicion returned

Dias de Novais announced that he had come on behalf of the Portuguese
Crown to open trade and offer the benefit of missionary knowledge. Ngola
Kiluanje kia Ndambi was not persuaded by the gifts the mission brought, and
answered coldly: the Portuguese could stay and see the capital, but they
would be watched, and would not be permitted to leave without his own
authorization. During their stay, the visitors took the measure of a
society they found hierarchical and well-organized, skilled in trade,
metalworking, animal husbandry and agriculture - and quietly searched for
the gold and silver deposits the Portuguese Crown coveted for its own mint,
without success.

## A condition attached to leaving

Five years later, the king allowed Dias de Novais to return to Portugal -
but only on condition that he come back at the head of an army, to help
Ndongo fight its own regional rivals. It was a request made in good faith,
treating the Portuguese as a potential ally against Ndongo's own enemies
rather than as a threat in themselves. Ten years after that, in 1575, Dias
de Novais did return at the head of a fleet of armed caravans - not to
honor the alliance he had been asked for, but to conquer the country in the
Portuguese Crown's own name.
