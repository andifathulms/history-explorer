"The greatness of the Moravian Empire ended with his death," Lützow
writes of Svatopluk, who died in 894 leaving two sons, Mojmir and
Svatopluk, whose quarrel over the inheritance did more damage than any
outside enemy had managed in decades. The Bohemian prince Spytihněv
saw his opening in that intestine struggle and used it to shake off
Moravian overlordship entirely, restoring the Přemyslid dynasty's own
independent rule over the whole of Bohemia - the first and largest
piece of Svatopluk's empire to fall away, and one that would never
return to Moravian hands. Seeking his own protection against a Moravia
he had just broken from, Spytihněv turned to the same German power his
predecessors had fought for two generations, appearing with his
brother Vratislav at King Arnulf's own court at Regensburg to submit
to German and ecclesiastical supremacy in exchange for support - a
reversal that also brought Bohemia formally under the Latin liturgy,
though the Slavonic rite Cyril and Methodius had planted continued
alongside it for years after.

## Pressburg

While Germans and Slavs wore each other down in their own recurring
wars, the Magyars - the very people Arnulf had once called in against
Svatopluk - grew steadily stronger on ground both powers had largely
ignored. Germans and Slavs eventually made common cause against this
shared threat, but their combined army was routed at the Battle of
Pressburg, traditionally dated to 907 though the source stresses that
"the exact date cannot be ascertained in the complete absence of
contemporary evidence." In the aftermath the Magyars overran Moravia
outright and held it for more than half a century afterward; only a
small western district survived under Bohemian rule. Lützow, quoting
the Czech historian Palacký, calls the Magyar conquest simply "the
greatest misfortune that has befallen the Slavonic world during
thousands of years" - the empire Rostislav had built to escape German
supremacy, and Svatopluk had carried to Kraków and Magdeburg, ended not
by the Germans at all, but by a people neither ruler had ever
seriously had to reckon with until it was already too late.
