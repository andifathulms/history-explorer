Appointed by the Germans to lead the army sent to crush the Moravian
revolt he had himself, in effect, provoked, Svatopluk deserted his
captors instead, put himself at the head of his own countrymen, and
destroyed the German force in a battle that killed both of the
Austrian margraves commanding it. The victory, in 871, left him
uncontested lord of Moravia - a position he moved quickly to secure
through an alliance with the Bohemian prince Bořivoj, sealed by a
marriage to Bořivoj's own sister, and through renewed war against the
Germans who were certain to come again. When Svatopluk carried that
war directly into Germany the following year, attacking Carlomann on
his own ground, Carlomann's father King Louis judged it wiser to
negotiate, and the resulting Treaty of Forcheim in 874 secured
Svatopluk's own conquests in northern Hungary, even if nominally still
under German supremacy.

## The empire at its widest

Peace with Germany let Methodius return from captivity, and one of his
first acts was very likely the baptism of Bořivoj, the Bohemian prince
whose own wife Ludmila was received into the Church alongside him -
Christianity's decisive entry into Bohemia's own ruling house. Svatopluk
himself used the years of relative peace to expand in every direction,
becoming, in the source's own words, "the chief of a mighty Slavonic
empire" whose exact borders no one can now specify with confidence:
Kraków and the surrounding part of Poland, Silesia, a swath of
northern Germany reaching as far as Magdeburg, and a large part of
northern Bohemia had all, by one account, acknowledged his supremacy.
After Bořivoj's own death Svatopluk went further still, reducing
Bořivoj's sons Spytihněv and Vratislav to local chieftains under his
own authority and ruling Bohemia directly himself - temporarily
displacing the Přemyslid dynasty from the throne of the country it
would otherwise hold without interruption.

## One more German war

Peace with Germany did not last through the reign. Svatopluk fought a
major war against King Arnulf, an illegitimate son of Carlomann,
beginning in 890, and two years later Arnulf brought in an unfamiliar
ally to help him: the Magyars, a people recently arrived in Europe and
at that time still settled somewhere near the lands the sources call
Moldavia. Svatopluk held off these combined attacks for the rest of
his reign, and the empire he had built reached, by his own death, the
furthest extent it would ever hold.
