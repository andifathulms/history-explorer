The Morava valley's earlier history is as obscure as Bohemia's own,
sharing what evidence there is of Avar conquest and, later, a share in
the short-lived realm the Frankish merchant Samo built after freeing
the Slavic tribes from Avar rule in the seventh century. Real
documentation begins only in the ninth century, with a ruler named
Mojmir governing Moravia with what the sources credit as genuine
ability, uniting scattered tribes under a single dominion for the
first time - the "real founder," in Lützow's own words, of a Moravian
empire that would for a time include Bohemia as well.

## A puppet who would not stay one

Mojmir's success drew the attention of the Frankish king Louis "the
German," who sent an army into Moravia in 846, deposed him, and
installed his own nephew Rostislav as ruler in his place - a
straightforward act of imperial engineering, meant to leave Moravia in
Frankish hands through a client prince. The Germans then turned their
army against Bohemia as well, but were defeated there, opening thirty
years of intermittent war between Germany and the Bohemian lands that
Lützow judges of little interest to English readers beyond the fact of
its length. Rostislav proved a poor investment for his Frankish
patrons: rather than governing as their instrument, he spent his own
reign working to free Moravia from exactly the German supremacy that
had put him on the throne, and the mission he would soon send to
Constantinople was the clearest expression of that ambition.
