When the Jin took the Liao capitals, a member of the imperial clan named Yelü
Dashi took what he could gather, went west across the steppe, and founded a state
in Central Asia that outlasted the one he had come from: the Qara Khitai.

It lasted about ninety years, held Transoxiana and the Tarim basin, beat a
Seljuq army at the Qatwan steppe in 1141, and was ruled by Buddhists over a
largely Muslim population, which almost never happens in that direction.

They also kept the dynastic name. Two thousand miles from the nearest
Chinese-speaking administration, ruling Turks and Persians and Muslims who had no
reason to be impressed, the rulers used Chinese reign titles, struck coin in
Chinese form, ran a chancery in Chinese, and went on calling themselves Liao.

## And the other thing that went west

The name Khitai travelled with them, and travelled further than they did. It is
how Central Asia, Persia and eventually Russia referred to China — *Cathay* in
Marco Polo, *Kitai* in Russian today.

A dynasty that ruled the northern part of China for two centuries and never held
the whole of it gave its name, in half the languages of Eurasia, to all of it,
and kept it for six hundred years after the last man who could have objected was
dead.
