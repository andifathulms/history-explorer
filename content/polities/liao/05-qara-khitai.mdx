When the Jin took the Liao capitals, a member of the imperial clan named Yelü
Dashi took what he could gather, went west across the steppe, and founded a state
in Central Asia that this corpus carries as its own record: the Qara Khitai.

It lasted about ninety years, held Transoxiana and the Tarim basin, beat a
Seljuq army at the Qatwan steppe in 1141, and was ruled by Buddhists over a
largely Muslim population — which is one of the few polities in this corpus for
which that is true in that direction. The Gelgel record in Maritime Asia, Hindu
rulers over Muslim Sasak on Lombok, is the other.

## What this site does and does not say about it

The two records are in different regions — this one in East Asia, the Qara Khitai
in Inner Asia — and this site's hard rule is that a thread draws only edges with
both ends inside its own region, because a global thread would assert a sequence
nobody cited.

So the relation exists in the data and is drawn by neither region's picture. That
is the rule working as intended rather than a defect: what the rule protects
against is exactly the temptation to run a line from a Chinese dynasty to a
Central Asian one because both are on the site.

It is also the second time this record's afterlife runs somewhere the schema
handles awkwardly. The name Khitai, from this dynasty, is how Central Asia,
Persia and eventually Russia referred to China — *Cathay* in Marco Polo,
*Kitai* in Russian today. A polity that ruled north China for two centuries and
was never the whole of it gave its name, in half the languages of Eurasia, to all
of it.

There is no influence count for that either. `descendant_scripts` on this record
reads two, for the Khitan large and small scripts, and it is the only column that
registers this dynasty at all.
