The Liao was destroyed by a subject people it had been taxing in hunting birds,
and the sequence took about ten years.

## What the Jurchen were for

The Jurchen lived in the forests and river valleys of eastern Manchuria, north
of the Bohai territory the Liao had annexed in 926. They were not one people;
the Liao distinguished the "civilised" Jurchen who had been resettled inside
the frontier from the "wild" Jurchen who had not, and dealt with the second
group through their own chiefs.

What the Liao wanted from them was tribute in kind: furs, pearls from the
Manchurian rivers, horses, ginseng, and above all gyrfalcons — the *haidongqing*,
the white sea-eagle falcon of the far north-east, which the Liao court used for
hunting swans and which had to be taken from nests on the coast and carried
south alive.

The demands escalated. The accounts describe Liao commissioners going into
Jurchen country each year for falcons and pearls, quartering themselves on the
villages, and behaving in the way tribute collectors behave when there is no
appeal. It is the ordinary grievance of an imperial periphery and it is
usually survivable.

## The dance

The story that everybody tells about the break is a scene at a fishing camp.

The Liao emperor Tianzuo held the annual first-fish feast on the Sungari, at
which the Jurchen chiefs attended and were required in turn to rise and dance
before him. Aguda, chief of the Wanyan, refused.

The emperor's advisers told him to have the man killed. He did not, on the
grounds that it was a small matter.

That anecdote has the shape of a story constructed afterwards, and it is
recorded in the histories of the dynasty Aguda founded, which is exactly the
kind of source that would construct one. What it is doing is real enough: it
identifies the moment at which the tribute relationship stopped being obeyed
and marks that the centre noticed and did nothing.

## Why the centre did nothing

Because it was busy, and because it had stopped being able to tell the
difference between a frontier grievance and a threat.

Tianzuo had come to the throne in 1101 after a succession full of purges. He
hunted, extensively — the accounts of his reign are substantially accounts of
where he was hunting when news arrived. The Northern Administration that
governed the tribal periphery had been drifting for a generation, and the
court's attention was on the Song frontier and on court politics.

The structural problem underneath is the one the two-administration system
never solved. It was designed to govern two populations, the steppe and the
settled, and it worked. The Jurchen were neither: forest hunters and farmers on
the eastern edge, administered as tributaries by a Northern Administration
built for pastoralists, at a distance, through their own chiefs — which meant
through a leader who could turn the whole arrangement into an army the moment
he decided to.

## 1114

Aguda attacked in 1114 with, by the histories' count, about two and a half
thousand men, and won. He took Ningjiang, then beat a much larger Liao force at
the Chu river, and declared himself emperor of a dynasty called Jin — Gold —
in 1115.

The Liao had eleven years left. What follows is the fastest collapse of a major
state in the region's history, and the reason it is so fast is that a
composite empire comes apart along the seams it was assembled on.
