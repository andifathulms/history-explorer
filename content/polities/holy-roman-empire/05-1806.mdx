Napoleon beat the Austrians and Russians at Austerlitz in December 1805. Seven
months later sixteen German princes left the empire and formed the Confederation
of the Rhine under French protection. On 1 August the French envoy at Regensburg
informed the Diet that his master no longer recognised the empire's existence.
Five days later Francis II laid down the imperial crown and released the estates
from their obligations to it.

He had already given himself a replacement. In 1804, watching Napoleon crown
himself emperor of the French, Francis had proclaimed himself hereditary emperor
of Austria — manufacturing a title out of his own hereditary lands so that when
the older one went he would not find himself outranked by a Corsican.

The abdication was calculated to prevent Napoleon from taking the imperial title
himself, which he had considered and which the Confederation of the Rhine had
put within reach. Eight hundred and forty-four years of an institution ended in
a pre-emptive resignation.

## What kind of ending that is

Almost nothing else in European history ends this way.

Nobody conquered it. The French pressure was real and it was indirect: Napoleon
never took Vienna's imperial crown off anybody, and the sixteen princes who
seceded did so voluntarily, having calculated that French protection was worth
more than an imperial court that could no longer protect anything. What killed
the empire was that its members stopped wanting it and its head decided that
abolishing it was cheaper than losing it.

It is also, characteristically, an ending by document. This polity's history had
been made by written settlements — Worms, the Golden Bull, the Reichsreform,
Augsburg, Westphalia — and not one of its five great hinges is a battle. It
finished the way it had run: somebody drafted a text, and the text was the event.

## The last century

The other thing worth saying is that it was not moribund when it went.

The eighteenth-century empire had a supreme court that was still hearing cases, a
Diet sitting permanently at Regensburg, an active body of constitutional law, and
a membership that still used all of it. Small territories were still litigating
against large ones and still winning. What the empire could not do was fight
France, and by 1800 that was the only test anybody was applying.

Wilson's point is that an institution can be perfectly functional at what it
does and be abolished anyway, because the world has started measuring something
else. The empire was designed to stop its members going to war with each other,
and it was destroyed by a war it had no mechanism for.
