For three centuries the imperial election was the empire's central weakness. The
crown was elective in principle and the principle was unwritten: who had a vote,
how many were needed, where the vote happened and whether the pope could confirm
or reject the result were all disputed, and each dispute produced a rival
emperor and a war.

In 1356 Charles IV settled it. Seven electors, named by office — the archbishops
of Mainz, Cologne and Trier, the king of Bohemia, the count palatine of the
Rhine, the duke of Saxony and the margrave of Brandenburg. Election at Frankfurt
within a month of a vacancy. A majority sufficient. Coronation at Aachen. The
electoral territories declared indivisible and passing by primogeniture, so that
the college could not multiply. And no papal role of any kind, which is the
clause the document is really about.

It held for four hundred and fifty years.

## What kind of field this is

This record's `succession_rule` reads `election`, and it is one of a small number
of places in this corpus where the field comes from a surviving constitutional
text rather than from a pattern of what happened.

The others are worth listing because the comparison is the point. The Hittite
record has Telipinu's Edict of about 1500 BC. The Neo-Assyrian record has
Esarhaddon's succession oath of 672. The Hasmonean record has the decree of the
great assembly of 140 BC engraved on bronze. The Venetian record has the ten
alternating rounds of lot and ballot. This one has the Golden Bull.

The Urartian record in the Ancient Near East draws a conclusion from the first
two of those which this record extends. States that write succession rules down
are states that have already been badly hurt by not having one — Telipinu
legislated after a century of murdered kings, Esarhaddon after a civil war caused
by his father's assassination. Charles IV legislated after three centuries of
disputed elections, papal vetoes and civil wars, several of which his own family
had fought.

The Hasmonean record adds a second reason: a dynasty with no lineage writes a
document because it has nothing else. And Venice adds a third, which is the one
this record shares in part — a polity designed by people who had watched the
alternative happen next door.

## The elector's calculation

The Golden Bull did not make the empire strong and was not meant to.

Seven men choosing an emperor will not choose a strong one. What they chose,
repeatedly, was a candidate with enough of his own resources to be useful and not
enough to be dangerous, and they charged for the vote — electoral capitulations,
sworn undertakings extracted from each new emperor before the election, which
accumulated conditions over the centuries in the same way the Venetian doge's
oath of limitations did.

So the empire acquired, by 1519, a written constitution assembled out of a
succession law, a public peace, a supreme court and a set of promises no emperor
could refuse to make. It is a genuinely unusual object: a constitutional monarchy
arrived at not by revolution or theory but by four hundred years of people
extracting concessions at the only moment an emperor could not say no.
