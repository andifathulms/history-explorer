Uthman's death left the office without an agreed method of filling it.

Ali was acclaimed at Medina in the aftermath and immediately inherited the
accusation that he had benefited from a murder he had not prevented. He never
cleared it. He fought and beat a coalition of prominent Companions at the
Battle of the Camel outside Basra, then faced Mu'awiya, Uthman's kinsman and
the governor of Syria for twenty years, at Siffin in 657. Siffin ended not in a
result but in an agreement to arbitrate — and the agreement itself split Ali's
army, since a faction held that submitting God's judgement to human arbitrators
was itself the sin. Those who left became the Kharijites, and one of them
killed Ali at Kufa in 661.

<Figure id="kufa-mosque" />

Ali's son Hasan came to terms with Mu'awiya rather than continue, and the
caliphate passed to the Umayyad house.

Two things end here and it is worth separating them. The office continues —
this is the same caliphate, and Mu'awiya took the same titles. What ends is the
method: after 661 no caliph is chosen by acclamation at Medina, and the
succession becomes dynastic in practice long before anyone defends it in
principle. The institution survived; the people who had made it by acclamation
did not get to make the next one the same way.
