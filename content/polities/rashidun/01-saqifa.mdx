---
title: The succession nobody had planned
drafted_from: madelung-1997
phase: formation
---

Muhammad died at Medina in 632 without a designated successor and without an
institution to produce one. What happened next happened in an afternoon, in a
roofed enclosure belonging to the Banu Sa'ida, while the burial was still being
arranged.

The Medinan Ansar met to choose a leader from among themselves. A small group of
Meccan Emigrants arrived, argued that the community would not accept a leader
from outside the Prophet's own tribe of Quraysh, and secured the acclamation of
Abu Bakr. The argument that carried the room was not scriptural. It was that the
Arabs would obey Quraysh and nobody else.

That improvisation set the shape of the office. The successor — *khalifa* — took
no prophetic authority, only the political and military leadership of the
community, and he took it by acclamation rather than by descent or appointment.
Every later dispute in this chapter of the history, including the one that ends
it, is a dispute about which of those two things should have decided the
question.

Abu Bakr spent almost his entire two years putting down the *ridda*, the
withdrawal of tribes across Arabia who had contracted with Muhammad personally
and considered the contract ended with him. Calling it apostasy was itself a
claim: it asserted that the agreements had been made with a permanent polity
rather than with a man. The tribes lost, and the concentration of armed force
assembled to defeat them was still assembled when the fighting stopped.
