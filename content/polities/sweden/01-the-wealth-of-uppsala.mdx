The first written record of the Swedes comes from a Roman source:
around AD 100, Tacitus's Germania describes the Suiones as a people
whose powerful fleet kept them safe from invasion. Later Roman and
Gothic writers named the Gautar, the Goths of Götaland, alongside
them, and Snorri Sturluson's own thirteenth-century account traced
the early Swedish kings back to a legendary line called the Ynglings,
descendants of the god Frey's own son. What is not legendary is the
temple those kings kept at Uppsala, described around 1070 by Adam of
Bremen: a building covered in gilding, housing statues of Thor, Odin
and Frey, where every ninth year the tribes of Sweden sent embassies
to a great festival at which nine of every kind of animal was
sacrificed and their blood offered to the gods. Bodies of the
sacrificed - among them, on occasion, human victims and even kings
themselves - were hung afterward in a sacred grove beside the temple.
Christian missionaries made little lasting headway against this
religious establishment: Ansgar preached at Birka, the chief Swedish
port on Lake Mälaren, for eighteen months around 830 and again in
853, but the churches he founded did not survive his own death.

## Founders of a foreign kingdom

While Uppsala's own religion held firm at home, Swedish vikings were
building an empire abroad. Swedish settlements existed on Finland's
coasts well before the Christian era, and by the Russian chronicles'
own account, three brothers - Rurik, Askold and Dir - crossed the sea
around 860 to found a kingdom among the Slavic tribes south of Lake
Ladoga, with Rurik ruling from Novgorod. A second Norse-founded
kingdom at Kiev merged with it around 900, and the whole state took
its name, Rus, from the Finnish word for the Swedish oarsmen who had
founded it - the origin of the name Russia itself. This Viking-founded
realm, which the Norsemen called Gardariki, carried on extensive trade
with Constantinople down the Dnieper, whose rapids still bear Swedish
names, and Swedish adventurers serving in the Byzantine emperor's own
Varangian guard carried the name of their homeland as far as the Black
Sea.
