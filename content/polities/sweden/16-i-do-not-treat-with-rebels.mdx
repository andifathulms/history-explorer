By 1786 the nobility Gustavus III had outmanoeuvred in 1772 was
fighting back, and a restive Estates began mutilating or rejecting his
bills outright. He turned instead to a small circle of trusted
loyalists and, seizing on Catherine II's war with Turkey as his
opening, sailed for Finland in the summer of 1788 with a large army,
having told his own Council - falsely - that Russia was about to
invade. His officers, self-described "citizens first and soldiers
afterwards," mutinied rather than fight a war they judged
unconstitutional, joined with Finnish troops, and forced him to
withdraw across the frontier; the ringleaders, meeting at Anjala, drew
up a declaration protesting the war and urging its end. When word
reached Gustavus of a simultaneous Danish invasion aimed at
Gothenburg, he is said to have exclaimed "We are saved" - it gave him
a pretext to leave his mutinous army without appearing to desert it.
Handed the Anjala officers' declaration as he departed, he returned it
unopened: "I do not treat with rebels." Riding two hundred and fifty
miles in forty-eight hours, he reached Gothenburg alone at midnight
and rallied the panicked city to defend itself as Dalecarlian
volunteers streamed in; British diplomatic pressure then persuaded the
Danish army to withdraw without a fight.

## Svensksund

Gustavus used the crisis to break the nobility for good. He had
twenty-one of the Anjala conspirators arrested, personally pushed a
new Act of Union and Security through the Estates by presiding in the
Speaker's own chair and declaring the vote carried over the House of
Nobles' loud opposition, and abolished the Council outright in 1789.
The war itself turned in Sweden's favour: after a near-disaster in the
narrow waters off Viborg, where the fleet escaped encirclement at the
cost of ten ships, Gustavus won the greatest naval victory in Swedish
history at Svensksund in July 1790, destroying or capturing fifty-five
Russian vessels. Peace followed a month later on the prewar
boundaries, with Russia agreeing to stop interfering in Sweden's own
constitution.

## The Opera House

Gustavus spent his final years trying to organise a monarchist
coalition against revolutionary France, allied for the purpose with
the same Catherine II he had just fought. Aristocratic conspirators,
still nursing the grievances of 1772 and 1789, found their opportunity
at a masked ball in the Stockholm Opera House on 16 March 1792, where
Gustavus was shot in the back at close range. He lingered for twelve
days, asked that his killers go unpunished, and died hoping his death
would reconcile Sweden's warring factions. His assassin, Anckarström,
was publicly mutilated and executed; the noblemen who had planned the
killing with him were merely exiled.
