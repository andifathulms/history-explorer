Gustaf Eriksson, born on his family's Uppland estate around 12 May
1496, was educated at the court of the Sture regents his own mother's
family had married into, carried the standard at the Sture victory of
Brännkyrka, and was one of six hostages handed over to King Christian
II of Denmark in 1518 - a betrayal that, as it turned out, saved his
life rather than ending it, since he was still a captive in Jutland
when Christian carried out the Stockholm massacre that killed his own
father. Gustaf escaped his Danish captivity in September 1519, disguised as a
horse-dealer, and made his way to Lübeck before returning to Sweden -
where he was in hiding on his own father's estate the following year
when news reached him that Christian had executed his father in the
Stockholm massacre and put a price on his own head. He set out into
Dalecarlia disguised as a labourer with an axe over his shoulder to
appeal to the peasantry there, and was turned away at first - until
further word of Christian's cruelty and a harsh new tax reached them,
and they sent runners after him to call him back. In
January 1521 the men of the Dales elected him their own lord, and by
that April his growing peasant army had broken a Danish cavalry
charge at Västerås outright, turning a local uprising into a
kingdom-wide revolt.

## Elected at Strängnäs

By 1523 Christian II had fled Denmark itself, driven out by his own
nobility, and Gustaf occupied Danish and Norwegian territory as an ally
of the new Danish king, Frederick I, who had led the revolt against
Christian in his own country. As soon as word of Christian's flight
reached him, Gustaf summoned a national assembly of all the Swedish
estates to Strängnäs. On 6 June 1523 a canon from Västerås addressed
the gathering in Latin, arguing that a king had to be chosen at once
to keep Denmark's own new ruler from claiming the Swedish throne for
himself - and that no one in the country was more worthy of it than
Gustaf Eriksson. The assembly agreed unanimously. Gustaf himself
objected, genuinely rather than for show: he was weary, he told them,
of the burden already resting on his shoulders, and asked them to
relieve him of it and choose one of the older Council nobles instead,
promising to be the first to swear homage to whomever they picked.
The assembly refused to be talked out of its own choice, and Gustaf
yielded, proclaimed King of the Swedes and Goths - the founding
election of a dynasty and, in every practical sense, of an
independent Swedish kingdom no longer bound to Denmark's crown.
