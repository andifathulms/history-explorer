Dalem Waturenggong is this record's `peak` ruler, and the reign is the one every
later Balinese account treats as the golden age — the point the courts of the
nine kingdoms measured themselves against for the next four hundred years.

Gelgel under him held Bali, the eastern tip of Java, Lombok and parts of Sumbawa,
which is the widest this polity ever reached. But the reason the reign is
remembered is not the territory. It is that this is when the Javanese arrived.

## Nirartha

Danghyang Nirartha was a Javanese brahmin priest who came to Bali in
Waturenggong's reign as Majapahit was ending, and the Balinese tradition credits
him with a great deal: the reorganisation of the priesthood, a body of religious
literature, the *padmasana* shrine that now stands in the north-east corner of
every Balinese temple, and the founding of temples along the south and west
coasts — Uluwatu, Tanah Lot, Rambut Siwi — which are the ones a visitor
photographs.

Whether one man did all of that is the kind of question this site does not
adjudicate. What the tradition is describing is a reorganisation of Balinese
religion on Javanese lines during a single reign, by people who had just arrived
from a court that had stopped existing.

## The caste order

The other thing dated to this period is the *wangsa* system: the division of
Balinese society into the three high groups — brahmana, satria, wesia — and the
*jaba* majority outside them, with the ruling houses of the later nine kingdoms
placing themselves in it by descent from named Javanese ancestors who came with
Waturenggong's court.

That is what this record's `successor_claims` count of one is made of, from the
other end. The Majapahit connection is not a sentimental genealogy; it is the
organising principle of who could marry whom, who could hold what office, and
what language a person was addressed in — and it was still doing that work in the
twentieth century.

`legitimation` on this record reads `descent` and `divine-sanction`, which is
what half the records on this site read. Here the first value is a social
structure rather than a claim about a dynasty, and the field cannot tell the
difference.
