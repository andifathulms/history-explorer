This record ends in 1686 with the collapse into nine kingdoms, and the nine have
no pages here. What happened to them belongs on this page because there is
nowhere else for it.

The Dutch took northern Bali in the 1840s and the rest of the island between 1906
and 1908. What they met at Badung in 1906, at Tabanan the same year, and at
Klungkung in 1908 was *puputan*: the court, the household and the retainers
walking out of the palace in white, armed, into rifle fire, until there was
nobody left. At Klungkung the Dewa Agung — the holder of the title this record's
own `successor_claims` names, in unbroken descent from the Gelgel line — died
that way with his court.

Dutch officers wrote accounts of it. The scale is disputed and the fact is not.

## Why it is on this record

Because `successor_claims: 1` on this page points at the house of Klungkung, and
a successor claim that this site counts ought to be followed to wherever it
stops. It stopped in 1908, in front of the palace, and the thing that ended there
had been continuous since Majapahit installed a governing line on Bali in 1343.

Five hundred and sixty-five years, and this site records it as a count of one in
an influence column.

## And what did not end

The other thing this record carries is `religions_carried: 1` for Balinese
Hinduism, and its second and third chapters are about the priesthood, the caste
order and the irrigation associations that the Javanese arrivals reorganised in
the sixteenth century.

None of that was destroyed in 1908. The subak still allocate water. The
*padmasana* is still in the corner of every temple. The wangsa distinctions are
still socially live. Bali is the one place in the archipelago where the
Hindu-Buddhist world this corpus's Kediri, Singhasari, Majapahit and Medang
records describe did not end but changed hands.

This corpus's Majapahit record's last chapter has a section called "What did not
end", which says the literature and the religion moved east. This is the far end
of that sentence, and what it says is that the thing which moved outlived every
polity on both sides of the water — including the one it moved to, twice.
