Gelgel's paramountcy collapsed around 1686 and the court moved to Klungkung. Its
ruler kept the title Dewa Agung and the ritual precedence, and lost the power.

Bali spent the next two hundred years as nine kingdoms — Klungkung, Badung,
Mengwi, Gianyar, Bangli, Karangasem, Buleleng, Tabanan and Jembrana — quarrelling
with each other, and from Karangasem holding Lombok across the strait.

`ended.type` is `fragmentation`, and this is the second fragmentation in this
region. The other is the division of Mataram at Giyanti in 1755, which produced
two courts that both still exist and are both named in that record's
`successor_claims`.

This one produced nine, and `successor_claims` here reads one: the house of
Klungkung, which kept the Dewa Agung title. The other eight argued their standing
by descent from the same line without claiming its precedence, and none of the
nine has a page on this site — which rule 1 does not require.

## The other religion carried

`religions_carried` on this record is one, and the item is Balinese Hinduism. It
is the only non-Muslim religion carried anywhere in the archipelago except its own
parent's — Majapahit's `Hindu-Buddhist Javanese tradition`, and Srivijaya's
Mahayana Buddhism seven centuries earlier.

Balinese kingdoms projected across the strait to Lombok from the seventeenth
century and, from Karangasem, installed Balinese Hindu rule over a Sasak
population that was Muslim. That is the subject of the book this record is coded
from, and it is propagation past a boundary in the sense coding rule 2 asks for.

It is also an inversion. Ten records across the archipelago carry Sunni Islam for
carrying it outward, and this corpus's Pasai record traces them as one
movement running from a throne on the Sumatran coast in 1267 outward to Borneo,
Sulawesi, Maluku and the southern Philippines. Gelgel is the one polity in the
region that pushed the other way across a boundary that movement had already
crossed — Hindu rulers over Muslim subjects, which is the title of the book.

The rulebook asks for the crossing rather than for the direction, and it counts.
