This corpus's Majapahit record ends with a section called "What did not end". It
says that the literature, the music, the theatre and the religion of that court
continued after the polity stopped — that Old Javanese metres were still being
copied in the nineteenth century, that gamelan and wayang continued, and that the
Hindu-Buddhist tradition moved east to Bali, "where a Javanese-derived
aristocracy and a Javanese-derived Hinduism are still there, with the Majapahit
connection claimed in genealogies".

That was prose with nothing to point at. Bali had no page.

It has one now, and the consequence is on the Majapahit record: its
`successor_claims` count goes from one to two, and the second item is this
record. The edge `majapahit → gelgel`, `claimed legitimacy of`, is drawn and
marked contested.

## Two successor claims of opposite kinds

Majapahit's two claimants are worth putting side by side, because between them
they cover most of what rule 1 can mean.

Demak claimed Majapahit by descent — Raden Patah's line traced to Majapahit's own
royal house — and used the claim to justify ruling Java after absorbing what was
left of that court. A political claim by the polity that ended it.

Gelgel claims it by inheritance of everything except the politics: the priests,
the literature, the ritual, the caste order, and genealogies that run the
Balinese ruling houses back to Majapahit's. The Balinese kingdoms never held an
acre of Java and never claimed to.

Both are successor claims and coding rule 1 counts both, because the rule asks
whether a later polity asserted an earlier one's authority and does not ask what
the assertion was for. The count of two on that record is doing more work than a
count of two usually does.

## The turning point typed `catastrophe`

1527 on this record is typed `catastrophe`, and the value is wrong in the
ordinary sense. Nothing was destroyed on Bali in 1527; the arrival of Javanese
priests and aristocrats was the making of this court's golden age under Dalem
Waturenggong.

It is typed that way because the vocabulary has no value for an inheritance, and
because from the point of view of the thing being inherited that is exactly what
it was. The same event on this corpus's Demak record is typed `conversion`, and
on Majapahit's it is `gradual absorption`. Three records, one process, three
values, and none of them wrong.
