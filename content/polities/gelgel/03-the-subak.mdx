`revenue_basis` on this record reads `land-tax`, which is the commonest value in
this corpus and says almost nothing. On Bali it sits on top of something specific
enough to be worth a chapter.

Balinese wet rice is watered by *subak*: associations of the farmers who share a
single irrigation channel, which allocate water in rotation, maintain the works,
settle disputes among their own members, and are organised around a water temple
whose ritual calendar is the scheduling mechanism. A subak is not a village and
does not map onto one — membership follows the water, not the settlement.

They are older than this kingdom. Inscriptions referring to irrigation
associations on Bali run back to the ninth century, three and a half centuries
before Majapahit installed the line this record begins with. They outlasted it
too: subak still allocate water on Bali, and the landscape they produced is on
the UNESCO list.

## What that does to the field

A land tax assessed on rice land in Bali is assessed on land whose productivity
is determined by an institution the state did not create, does not run, and
cannot easily override, because the water schedule is a ritual calendar and the
priest who keeps it is not a royal official.

This site's `revenue_basis` describes where a state's income comes from. It has
no way to say that between the state and the land there was a third body older
than both.

This corpus's Medang record, six hundred years earlier on Java, is coded
`land-tax` for the *sima* system — land whose revenue was permanently assigned to
a religious foundation, recorded on a stone at the site. That comment notes it is
the same instrument, working the same way, as the one this corpus's Pagan record
is coded around, and that Aung-Thwin's argument is that it eventually bankrupted
Pagan.

Three records, one field, three completely different things underneath it: an
endowment that removes land from the crown permanently, a garrison settled on
allotments, and a farmers' association that predates the kingdom. The value is
accurate on all three and informative on none.
