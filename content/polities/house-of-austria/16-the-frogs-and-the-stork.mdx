Matthias Corvinus had every reason to hate Frederick by the mid-1470s:
after being egged into war against Poděbrady and then abandoned
mid-campaign, refused the Bohemian crown he had been promised, watched
Frederick formally invest a rival with it instead, had his own
marriage proposal for the emperor's daughter dismissed outright, and
saw Frederick shelter a rebellious Hungarian archbishop - any one of
which, Coxe notes, would have justified a hostile prince's outrage.
Matthias first survived a war on two fronts of his own, defeating a
Polish-Bohemian coalition and then smashing an Ottoman army supporting
Wallachia's breakaway prince, before declaring formal war on Frederick
and invading Austria outright.

## An emperor humiliated, then humiliated again

The first invasion was swift and total: within a month Matthias had
overrun most of Lower Austria, besieged Vienna itself, and taken every
fortress along the Danube as far as Krems. Frederick fled to Linz and
bought back his own capital for a hundred thousand ducats he then
simply refused to pay once Matthias got tied up in fresh wars
elsewhere. Furious, Matthias made peace with Bohemia instead and
invaded a second time; this campaign took four hard years of siege
warfare rather than one swift month, but ended even more completely,
with Vienna's own capture and the fall of Wiener Neustadt, Frederick's
favorite residence.

Driven from his own hereditary lands, Frederick wandered from city to
city and convent to convent for years, met everywhere with sympathy
and nowhere with real help. He bore the humiliation with a strange,
almost theatrical calm, repeating the maxim that "to forget what
cannot be recovered is supreme felicity," and took his revenge on
Vienna's citizens in words rather than arms - comparing them, for
their long history of turning against him, to the frogs in the fable
who rejected a harmless log as king and got a stork that devoured them
instead. Years of personal appeals finally produced only a token force
under Duke Albert of Saxony, who managed to slow Matthias's advance
through skillful maneuvering but was forced to an armistice without
real reinforcement from either the empire or Frederick himself.
Austria was formally ceded to Matthias's occupation in November 1487,
to be returned only once Frederick paid off his old debts in full - or
reverted outright, the treaty specified, should Matthias die first.

## A stork of Frederick's own

Astrologers had promised Frederick his rival would die within the
year, and for once they were right: Matthias Corvinus died in April
1490 after a prolonged illness, opening the way for Habsburg Austria's
recovery after more than a decade under Hungarian occupation and
reopening, at the same time, an immediate contest for Hungary's own
vacant throne. Frederick and Maximilian put themselves forward as
candidates, citing an old succession compact already agreed with
Matthias, but the Hungarian diet chose a rival claimant instead on 15
July - and Maximilian refused to accept the rejection quietly. Rallying
his father's own Austrian subjects, he drove the occupying Hungarian
garrisons out of Austria in six weeks and pushed on into Hungary
itself, exploiting the usual factional chaos that followed any new
king's accession. He captured Székesfehérvár, the traditional
coronation city of Hungary's own kings, and was preparing to march on
Buda when a mutiny among his own troops forced him to fall back into
Austria.

Neither side could press its advantage further, and the two met at
Bratislava and settled generous terms: the rival king kept the
Hungarian crown and formally renounced any claim to the Austrian
territory Matthias had once conquered, while Maximilian received the
honorary title of King of Hungary, a payment of a hundred thousand
ducats toward his own war costs, and a renewed family compact
entailing the Hungarian succession on Frederick's own line should the
new king die without a son. Between Matthias's death and Maximilian's
own energy and skill, Frederick recovered the Austrian homeland he had
lost for more than a decade, gained financial compensation on top of
it, and was finally relieved of a crisis his own obstinacy and
indolence had done so much to create in the first place.
