---
title: Where it broke first
drafted_from: abun-nasr-1987
phase: contraction
---

The previous chapter leaves the caliphate carrying the converts' grievance in
every province at once. The province where it broke first was not Khurasan, and
it broke four years before the quarrel that became the third civil war.

The Berbers had done most of the conquering in the west. The Maghreb was taken
largely by Berber troops under Arab command, and so was al-Andalus: the army
that crossed the strait in 711 was overwhelmingly Berber. They then converted,
and found themselves administered as a conquered population anyway — taxed at
non-Muslim rates, and levied for slaves as though the conquest were still in
progress. This is the *mawali* problem of the previous chapter, applied to the
people who had carried out the conquest, in the province furthest from anyone
who might be argued with.

The revolt began at Tangier in 740 and took its idiom from Kharijism, which
supplied exactly the doctrine the situation wanted: that the community's
leadership belongs to the worthiest Muslim and not to an Arab lineage. An
Umayyad force of the Arab nobility was destroyed near Tangier that year. A
Syrian army sent to retrieve the situation was destroyed on the Sebou the next.

Its survivors crossed to al-Andalus, and the Syrian regiments settled there
became part of the factional landscape that a fugitive Umayyad exploited fifteen
years later — which is where the Cordoba pages in this section begin.

Umayyad authority was never restored west of Ifriqiya. When the Abbasids took
the caliphate in 750 they inherited an empire that had already lost its western
third, and they never recovered it: the Rustamids from 776 and the Idrisids from
788 were established in the space the revolt cleared, and Ifriqiya itself was
held from 800 by Aghlabid governors on a grant, which this site records as an
edge rather than as rule.

The same grievance produced both endings. In the west it produced secession, and
the rebels wanted out. In the east it produced a revolution, and the rebels
wanted the centre. The next chapter is the east.
