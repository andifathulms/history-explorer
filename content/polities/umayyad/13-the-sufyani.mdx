The Syrians had let it happen. They had abandoned the last Umayyad caliph, whom
they disliked, and had not moved against the Abbasids while moving was still
possible. By the time the extermination of the family woke them up, the
position could not be altered: black had won and white had lost.

They rose anyway, in 750 and 751, Qaysites and Kalbites together — the two
tribal blocs whose quarrel had been tearing the caliphate apart for seventy
years, finally on the same side and too late. At their head was Abu'l Ward, a
distinguished Qaysite of Qinnasrin. They were beaten at Marj al-Akhram, and
Abu'l Ward fell with five hundred men of his own house.

## The man they raised

What is remarkable is whom they raised, because it was not a Marwanid.

The claimant was a descendant not of Marwan and Abd al-Malik, whose branch had
held the caliphate for the last sixty-six years, but of Mu'awiya and Yazid —
the Sufyanids, who had been out of power since 684. He was not generally known
by his own name at all. He was called simply *as-Sufyani*, the Sufyanid, which
is to say that what was being acclaimed was a branch of a family rather than a
man.

He fled to Palmyra with his Kalbites, wandered as a fugitive in the Hijaz, and
was eventually caught and put to death under the second Abbasid caliph.

## And then he grew

His importance did not end with his execution. It increased.

He became the Messiah of the Syrians, the figure whose return they attached
their political hopes to. And then, as the years passed and their opponents
stayed in possession of the field, the expectation curdled: the Sufyani turned
into a figure of the end of days, the one who comes before the Antichrist.

The dynasty, in other words, went on. Not as a claim to the caliphate — that
was finished, and everyone knew it within a decade — but as a name in the
eschatology, a thing Syrians waited for and other Muslims warned about.

The Abbasids called their government the *dawla*, the new era, and it was a
prodigious revolution. But a defeated house that has been driven out of history
does not always stay out of it. Sometimes it moves into prophecy, where it
cannot be beaten again.
