---
title: What they called themselves
drafted_from: crone-hinds-1986
phase: aside

The Abbasid-era tradition is clear about the Umayyads: they were kings, *muluk*,
who held the caliphate as a worldly possession, and religious authority properly
belonged to the scholars who were forming the law at the same time. It is a
tidy account and it was written by the people who replaced them.

Crone and Hinds went looking for what the Umayyads said about themselves in
material that was not transmitted through that filter — court poetry, coin
legends, inscriptions, chancery formulae — and found a different title.
Alongside *khalifat rasul Allah*, successor to the Messenger of God, the early
caliphs are addressed and describe themselves as **khalifat Allah**: deputy of
God, God's caliph. Not the Prophet's successor in an administrative sense but
God's appointee on earth, with authority over the interpretation of His
religion as well as over the army and the tax.

If that is right, the later picture inverts. The Umayyads were not impious
rulers who neglected a religious authority that lay elsewhere. They claimed
that authority, straightforwardly and in public, and lost the argument — after
which the winners wrote a history in which the argument had never been open.
The scholars' monopoly on defining Islam is then not the original arrangement
but the outcome of a fight, and this dynasty is on the losing side of it.

The thesis is contested, and this page does not present it as settled. Critics
argue that *khalifat Allah* was honorific rather than constitutional, that the
poetry is panegyric and reads accordingly, and that Crone and Hinds pressed
thin evidence hard. What is not much disputed is the narrower and still
awkward point: the title is attested, early, in material the Abbasid tradition
did not curate, and the tradition's silence about it is itself something to
explain.

This site records it because the alternative is worse. The `legitimation`
coding on this page has to say on what public ground these caliphs claimed the
right to rule, and answering that from the account written by their successors
would be coding the charge sheet rather than the claim.
