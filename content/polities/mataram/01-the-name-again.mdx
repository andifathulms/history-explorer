There are two Mataram kingdoms on Java, six hundred years and four hundred
kilometres apart, and this site carries both.

The first is on the Kedu and Kewu plains of central Java in the eighth and ninth
centuries, calls itself Mataram in its own inscriptions, builds Borobudur and
Prambanan, and abandons the plain in 929. This site carries it as `medang`,
which is the other name the sources give it, precisely so that this record could
have the one everybody uses for it.

The second is this one: 1587 to 1755, founded by Senapati out of a vassal
territory of Pajang, and named for the district it started in — which is the
same district. Kota Gede, Senapati's capital, is about thirty kilometres from
Prambanan.

The naming is a claim, and the Javanese chronicles composed under this dynasty
make it one. It is not a claim this site counts: coding rule 1 wants an assertion
of a specific earlier polity's authority, and reusing a regional name six
centuries later is not that. The Medang record's single successor claim points at
Kahuripan, which is the polity that actually asserted continuity with it.

## What Senapati took over

Pajang, in 1586, from Hadiwijaya's successor. That record's second chapter is
about what the sequence looks like from the other end.

What matters for this page is the shape Pajang had established eighteen years
earlier and this record inherited without altering: an inland agrarian kingdom in
a river valley, coded `land-tax` and nothing else, holding the coast as a
dependency rather than living on it.

Everything difficult about the next hundred and seventy years comes out of that.
