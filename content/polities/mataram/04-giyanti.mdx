In 1755, after nine years of war, the kingdom was divided in two. Pakubuwana III
kept Surakarta. Mangkubumi took Yogyakarta and the title Hamengkubuwana I. The
Dutch company brokered the treaty and guaranteed both halves.

`ended.type` is `fragmentation`. `ended.by` is empty, because the company is not
a polity this site carries. Fourteen of this region's twenty-two records have an
empty `by`, and this is the ninth of them by date, with Patani, Tidore, Brunei,
Aceh and Sulu still to come.

## The ending that is still there

This site has around two hundred endings and almost all of them ended something.

Both halves of this one are still running. Two courts, two dynasties in unbroken
descent, two palaces, and two divergent traditions of Javanese court music,
dance, dress and language, two hundred and seventy years later. The Yogyakarta
sultan is the governor of a province of Indonesia by hereditary right, which is
the only such arrangement in the republic.

`successor_claims` reads two and the items are those courts. They are the only
successor claims on this site that a reader could go and visit, and they are in
competition: each presents itself as the continuation of this kingdom, and the
divergence in their court traditions is partly the product of two centuries of
each declining to do things the way the other does them.

## What the field cannot show

The obvious thing to want here is an edge, or two of them, from this record to
its halves. There are none, because neither half is a record on this site.

That is a choice rather than an oversight. Surakarta and Yogyakarta after 1755
are courts inside somebody else's empire — first the company's, then the Dutch
state's, then the republic's — with ritual authority, revenue and a great deal of
cultural production, and without the thing every other record here has, which is
the disposal of its own external relations. This site codes polities. A page for
either would have four null institutions and an `ended` block that could not be
written.

So the chain that this corpus's Pajang record traces — Kediri to Singhasari to
second-Kediri to Majapahit to Demak to Pajang to Mataram, seven records and seven
hundred years on one island — stops here. Not because the line stopped, but
because what it turned into is not the kind of thing this site is a list of.
