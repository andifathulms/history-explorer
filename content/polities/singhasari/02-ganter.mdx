The dated event, as against the legend, is 1222 at Ganter, where Ken Arok
defeated Kertajaya of Kediri. Kediri had been the dominant power in eastern Java
for a century and a half. After Ganter it was not, and the centre of Javanese
power moved to Tumapel, which the dynasty renamed Singhasari.

What changed is narrower than the political map. Java in this period is a rice
economy — the Brantas and Solo valleys are among the most productive
wet-rice landscapes in the world, and this corpus's Majapahit record's second
chapter is about what that meant for a state that also wanted the sea. Kediri
had that base and used it inland. Singhasari inherited it and turned outward.

The military coding on this record is `conscript`, and it is the ordinary
Javanese arrangement: the cultivators of the royal domain owed service, and the
army was raised from them through the territorial lords rather than bought. It
is not the client-levy of the straits polities — Srivijaya's *orang laut*, and
after them Melaka's — and the difference is the difference between a state with
a granary and a state with a customs house. Both records in this region carry
one or the other. Singhasari is the first here with the granary.
