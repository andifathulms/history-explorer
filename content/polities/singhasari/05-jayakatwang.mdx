In 1292 Jayakatwang, the ruler of Kediri and a vassal of Kertanegara, revolted
and killed him. Kediri had been the power Singhasari displaced at Ganter
seventy years earlier, and it ended Singhasari by the same route it had lost to
it: one battle, one dead king.

It is a neat piece of symmetry and it should not be over-read. Jayakatwang was
not restoring an old order out of sentiment; he was a subordinate who saw his
overlord's army sail off to Sumatra and took his chance while the capital was
empty.

What follows is the part everyone remembers. Raden Wijaya, Kertanegara's
son-in-law, survived; met the Mongol fleet when it arrived the next year;
persuaded it to help him against Jayakatwang; and then turned on it once
Jayakatwang was dead. From that he founded Majapahit, had Kertanegara
commemorated as the dynasty's founding ancestor, and claimed Singhasari as the
house he was continuing.

So Singhasari's whole legacy is a claim made about it by somebody else. It
conquered a strait it did not administer, insulted an empire it could not
fight, and was ended by a vassal — and then its dead king was made the founding
ancestor of the largest state Java ever produced, by a son-in-law who had
survived in a forest. Seventy years, and the part that lasted was the ancestry.
