In 1292 Jayakatwang, the ruler of Kediri and a vassal of Kertanegara, revolted
and killed him. Kediri had been the power Singhasari displaced at Ganter seventy
years earlier, and it ended Singhasari by the same route it had lost to it: one
battle, one dead king.

`ended.type` on this record is `internal usurpation` and `ended.by` is empty.
The empty list is not a gap — the coding rule is that `by` names polities in
this corpus, and Kediri is not one. What the field cannot say is that the
usurper was also, in every functional sense, an external power that had spent
seventy years as a subordinate.

What follows is the reason this record exists. Raden Wijaya, Kertanegara's
son-in-law, survived; met the Mongol fleet when it arrived the next year;
persuaded it to help him against Jayakatwang; and then turned on it once
Jayakatwang was dead. From that he founded Majapahit, had Kertanegara
commemorated as the dynasty's founding ancestor, and gave this site the edge it
now carries: `singhasari → majapahit`, `claimed legitimacy of`, 1293.

That is the whole of Singhasari's influence coding — one successor claim, no
scripts, no religion carried. It is a small record. What it does is make the
two large records on either side of it say something true about each other.
