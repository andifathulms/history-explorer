In 1275 Kertanegara, the last king of Singhasari, sent an expedition across the
Java Sea to Sumatra. The Javanese sources call it the *Pamalayu* — the campaign
against Melayu, the polity based at Jambi that had been carrying Srivijaya's
position on the strait since the Chola raids of 1025 broke Palembang.

Srivijaya's own power on that strait had been broken by the Chola raid of 1025,
and what survived it was the position rather than the polity: the centre moved
up the coast to Jambi-Melayu, and for two centuries somebody at Jambi collected
what Palembang once had.

In 1275 a Javanese king took it.

That is the moment the archipelago's centre of gravity crosses the Java Sea.
For six hundred years the straits had been run from Sumatra; after the Pamalayu
they were not, and no Sumatran power held that position again until Aceh, three
hundred years later and much further north.

## What it was for

Kertanegara was not trying to govern Sumatra. There is no Javanese
administration there afterwards, no tribute list, no garrison anybody records.

What he was doing is better read against what he did next: he refused Kublai
Khan's envoys, and the Mongol fleet that came in response was already at sea
when he died. A king who takes the far side of the Java Sea and then insults
the largest empire on earth is a king making a case about where authority in
the archipelago now sits.

He was killed by his own vassal two years before the fleet arrived.
