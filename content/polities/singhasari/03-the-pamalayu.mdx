---
title: The Pamalayu
drafted_from: hall-2011
phase: peak
---

In 1275 Kertanegara, the last king of Singhasari, sent an expedition across the
Java Sea to Sumatra. The Javanese sources call it the *Pamalayu* — the campaign
against Melayu, the polity based at Jambi that had been carrying Srivijaya's
position on the strait since the Chola raids of 1025 broke Palembang.

This corpus's Srivijaya record's fifth chapter ends at 1025 and its sixth is
about a polity that was forgotten so completely that its name had to be
recovered by a French scholar in 1918 from Chinese and Arabic notices and a
handful of inscriptions. The Pamalayu is what happened in between and after: the
strait's authority passed to Melayu, and in 1275 a Javanese king took it.

There is now an edge on this site from `srivijaya` to `singhasari`, typed
`conquered by`, at 1275. It replaces an edge that used to run from Srivijaya to
Majapahit, typed `absorbed remnants of`, dated 1293 — which was wrong twice.
Majapahit did not absorb Srivijaya's remnants; Singhasari did, eighteen years
before Majapahit existed. And 1293 is the year Majapahit was founded, not a year
anything passed between the two polities. The edge was drawing a line between
the archipelago's two big names because they were the two big names, which is the
kind of thing a succession graph does when a term is missing.

It is the second edge on this site to be corrected rather than added, and both
corrections were the same shape: a relationship asserted between two records
because both were present, when the actual relationship ran through a third that
was not.
