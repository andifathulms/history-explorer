---
title: A kris and a curse
drafted_from: robson-1995
phase: formation
---

Singhasari lasted seventy years and produced the dynasty that ruled Java for the
next two centuries. It is here for that, and because this corpus's two existing
Maritime Asia records — Srivijaya and Majapahit — were joined by an edge that
asserted something neither of them did. Singhasari is the term that was missing.

The founding story is not a founding story of the kind this site usually
records. The *Pararaton*, the Javanese chronicle that is the main narrative
source for the dynasty, opens with Ken Arok, a man of obscure and possibly
criminal origins in the service of the lord of Tumapel. He commissions a *kris*
— a Javanese dagger — from a smith named Mpu Gandring, demands it before it is
finished, and kills the smith with it when he refuses. The smith, dying, curses
the blade: it will kill seven men, and the first will be Ken Arok himself.

It then does. Ken Arok uses it to kill his lord, takes his lord's wife and his
lord's territory, declares himself king in 1222, and is murdered with the same
weapon by a stepson avenging the man he killed. The stepson is killed by his own
half-brother. The chronicle runs the pattern down four generations.

This site does not code that. `succession_rule` on this record is null and the
comment in the data explains why at some length: what the *Pararaton* describes
is not a rule but a sequence of outcomes, and coding rule 7 asks for the rule
rather than the outcome. But it is worth saying what kind of document the main
source is. The *Pararaton* was compiled long after the events, its early
sections read as legend, and the dynasty it describes is the one from which the
kings of Majapahit claimed descent — which means the chronicle of Singhasari's
murders was preserved by the people with the strongest reason to make its last
king a revered ancestor.
