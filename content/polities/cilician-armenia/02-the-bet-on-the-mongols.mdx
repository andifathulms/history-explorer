title: The bet on the Mongols

In 1247 Hetum I sent his brother to Mongolia. In 1253 he went himself, across
Central Asia to Karakorum, and submitted in person to Möngke.

Every state in this corpus that met the Mongols had the same choice and almost
all of them made it late and under duress. The Khwarazmshah fought and was
destroyed; the Sultanate of Rum fought at Kösedağ and became a tributary
afterwards; the Abbasids refused and Baghdad was sacked. Cilicia submitted
before it was attacked, and got the terms available to those who did:
protection, privileges for the Armenian church, and — this was the point — an
alliance against the Muslim powers to the south.

For fifty years it was the right bet. Armenian contingents rode with Hulegu's
army; the Mongols took Baghdad in 1258 and Aleppo and Damascus in 1260; and it
appeared for a season that the Ayyubid and Mamluk world was finished and
Cilicia would be the western anchor of a Mongol Levant.

Then the Mamluks won at Ayn Jalut in 1260, which this site's Mamluk page
describes, and the arrangement inverted. Cilicia was now a Christian kingdom
that had ridden with the Mongols, sitting on the Mamluk sultanate's northern
frontier, with its ally beyond a desert and increasingly disinclined to cross
it. Baybars came in 1266, beat the Armenian army at Mari, sacked Sis and took
the population of the plain. Further invasions followed in 1275 and at
intervals afterwards; by 1285 the kingdom was paying a million dirhams a year
in tribute.

The last attempt at the original strategy was in 1299, when Hetum II joined
Ghazan — the Ilkhan whose conversion this site's Ilkhanate page describes — for
a victory at Wadi al-Khazandar that could not be held. Mamluk victories in 1303
ended it.

After that the kingdom did what small states do. It appealed to Europe for a
crusade that did not come, accepted a Frankish dynasty, the Lusignans of
Cyprus, whose Catholic reforms split it further, and was raided until nothing
was left. Sis fell in April 1375. Levon V died in Paris, having spent his last
years touring courts asking for an army, and left his title to the kings of
Cyprus.

This corpus contains a good many polities that were destroyed. It contains very
few that chose the winning side early, correctly, and were destroyed anyway
because the winner lost somewhere else.
