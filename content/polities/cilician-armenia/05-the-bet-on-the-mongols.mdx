---
title: The bet on the Mongols
drafted_from: jackson-2017
phase: contraction
---

In 1247 Hetum I sent his brother to Mongolia. In 1253 he went himself, across
Central Asia to Karakorum, and submitted in person to Möngke.

Every state on that frontier faced the same choice and nearly all of them made
it late and under duress. The Khwarazmshah fought and was destroyed. The
Sultanate of Rum fought at Kösedağ and became a tributary afterwards. The
Abbasid caliph refused and Baghdad was sacked. Cilicia submitted before it was
attacked, and got the terms available to those who did:
protection, privileges for the Armenian church, and — this was the point — an
alliance against the Muslim powers to the south.

For fifty years it was the right bet. Armenian contingents rode with Hulegu's
army; the Mongols took Baghdad in 1258 and Aleppo and Damascus in 1260; and it
appeared for a season that the Ayyubid and Mamluk world was finished and
Cilicia would be the western anchor of a Mongol Levant.

Then the Mamluks beat a Mongol force at Ayn Jalut in 1260, and the arrangement
inverted. Cilicia was now a Christian kingdom
that had ridden with the Mongols, sitting on the Mamluk sultanate's northern
frontier, with its ally beyond a desert and increasingly disinclined to cross
it. Baybars came in 1266, beat the Armenian army at Mari, sacked Sis and took
the population of the plain. Further invasions followed in 1275 and at
intervals afterwards; by 1285 the kingdom was paying a million dirhams a year
in tribute.

The last attempt at the original strategy was in 1299, when Hetum II joined the
Ilkhan Ghazan for a victory at Wadi al-Khazandar that could not be held. Mamluk
victories in 1303 ended it — and Ghazan had by then converted to Islam, which
removed the last thing that had made the alliance intelligible to either side.

What the fifty years had bought, in the end, was a reputation. The kingdom was
now identified across the Muslim Levant as the Christian power that had ridden
with the Mongols into Baghdad and Aleppo, and there was no way to put that
down.
