Harsha kept up diplomatic relations with Tang China throughout his later
years. A Brahman envoy he had sent to the imperial court returned in 643
with a Chinese mission bearing a formal reply; that mission stayed in
India for a considerable time before starting home in 645. The following
year a new Chinese embassy was dispatched under Wang Xuance, second in
command of the earlier mission, with a modest escort of thirty horsemen.

## A country without its king

Before the new envoys reached Magadha in 648, Harsha had already died — at
the very end of 647 or the start of 648, the sources leave the exact
moment uncertain — and the removal of what the record calls "his strong
arm" had plunged the country into disorder that a simultaneous famine made
considerably worse. He left no son to inherit what he had spent forty
years assembling.

## The minister who took what was left

Arjuna, one of Harsha's own ministers, seized the vacant throne into this
vacuum — and gave Wang Xuance's arriving mission a hostile reception
entirely out of keeping with the diplomatic relationship it represented.
His men massacred the mission's small escort and plundered its goods. Wang
Xuance and one companion survived only by escaping under cover of night
across the border into Nepal.

## What a defeated ambassador did next

That should have been the end of the story — a diplomatic mission
destroyed by a usurper taking advantage of a dead king's absence, and two
survivors making their way home to report a failure. It was not. What Wang
Xuance did instead, and what it cost the man who had attacked him, belongs
to its own telling: the dynasty Harsha had spent his life building ended,
within months of his death, in an episode no chronicler of early India had
noticed until modern scholars went looking specifically for it.
