title: Ten years as the strongest power in Iran

Uzun Hasan — Tall Hasan — ruled the White Sheep from 1452 and spent about a
decade as the most powerful ruler between Constantinople and Herat, which is a
sentence that surprises most readers because the confederation he led is
usually filed as an interval.

The sequence is short. In 1467 he destroyed the Kara Koyunlu at Chapakchur and
killed Jahan Shah, ending the rival confederation described on the previous
page and taking Azerbaijan and Iraq. In 1469 he defeated and captured Abu Sa'id,
the Timurid ruler of Iran, at Qarabagh, which ended Timurid power west of
Khurasan. Inside three years the White Sheep held eastern Anatolia, Azerbaijan,
Iraq, Fars and much of central Iran, and Tabriz became his capital.

He then did what the successful pastoralists in this corpus do when they have
taken a settled country, and this site has now recorded it for the Seljuks, the
Ghaznavids, the Ilkhanids and the Karakhanids. He hired the Persian
bureaucracy. The four senior civil offices went to Iranian officials, the divan
ran in Persian, and he promulgated a law code — the *qanunname* of Uzun Hasan —
regulating taxation and dues, which was still in use under the Safavids and in
Ottoman eastern Anatolia after his state had gone.

And he looked west, which is the part this corpus can connect. He married a
daughter of the emperor of Trebizond, whose page records the marriage as that
state's last attempt at survival. He negotiated with Venice for firearms and
gunners against the Ottomans, receiving ambassadors and promises for nearly a
decade. He was one of the two or three rulers Mehmed II took seriously.
