Uzun Hasan — Tall Hasan — took the confederation in 1452 after beating his own
brother for it, and inside seventeen years turned the junior of the two sheep
into the most powerful state between Constantinople and Herat. The
transformation happens in three years and it is worth setting out at that speed,
because the speed is the point.

In 1467, at Chapakchur, he destroyed the Kara Koyunlu and killed Jahan Shah, who
had been the strongest ruler in the region for thirty years. In 1468 the Black
Sheep collapsed entirely and he took Azerbaijan, Iraq and Tabriz, which became
his capital. In 1469, at Qarabagh, he defeated and captured Abu Sa'id, the
Timurid ruler of Iran, and had him executed — which ended Timurid power west of
Khurasan for good and left the White Sheep holding Fars, Kerman and most of
central Iran as well.

Three campaigns removed both of the powers that had contained the confederation
for seventy years.

<Figure id="uzun-hasan-coin" /> Woods' framing of the sequence is that it is the moment the
confederation becomes an empire in his sense: the Bayandur clan had been
assembling a following for a century, and what changed was not the following but
that it suddenly had settled provinces underneath it.

The reason the period is filed as an interval is that it is short and it ends in
a defeat. But for about a decade after 1469 the Ak Koyunlu were the principal
Muslim power between the Ottoman frontier and India, they held the trade route
through Tabriz, they claimed suzerainty as far as Hormuz on the Gulf, and every
European power that wanted the Ottomans fought on two fronts wrote to Uzun Hasan
about it.
