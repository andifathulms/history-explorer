title: What gunpowder settled

In August 1473, at Otlukbeli in eastern Anatolia, Mehmed II destroyed the Ak
Koyunlu army. The Ottomans had field artillery and janissaries with handguns;
Uzun Hasan had the finest Turkmen cavalry in the region and the Venetian
firearms he had spent a decade being promised had not come.

Three things follow, and the third is the one this site is interested in.

The frontier settled. Eastern Anatolia was Ottoman from that point in a way it
had not been before, and the boundary of Ottoman power in the east stopped being
a question of which Turkmen confederation was strongest.

The confederation held together for about twenty more years and then did what
confederations do. Uzun Hasan died in 1478, his son Ya'qub ruled competently
until 1490, and after that the White Sheep divided among rival claimants and
fought each other for a decade.

And into that, in 1501, came Isma'il. This site's Safavid page has him taking
Tabriz at fourteen at the head of the Qizilbash — Turkmen tribal followers of a
Sufi order — and declaring Twelver Shi'ism the religion of a Sunni country. The
tribes he led were the same tribes: the human material of the White Sheep and
the Black, from the same pastures, reorganised. He beat the remaining Ak
Koyunlu forces at Nakhchivan in 1501, took Iraq-i Ajam, Fars and Kerman by
1503, Diyarbakır by 1508, and killed the last of the line.

That is the answer to the question the previous page ends on. Two confederations
spent a century unable to make a durable state out of the same tribes, because
what held them together was pasture and a leader and both are perishable. The
Safavids added a religion and an order of loyalty that survived defeat, and got
four hundred years.

Otlukbeli is also, dated against this site's Ottoman page, the first of three:
Otlukbeli in 1473, Chaldiran in 1514 and Ridaniya in 1517. In forty-four years
the same combination of guns and drilled infantry decided who ruled eastern
Anatolia, Iran's western frontier and the Arab lands. The Ak Koyunlu were the
first polity in this corpus to lose to it.
