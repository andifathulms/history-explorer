Uzun Hasan died in 1478. His son Ya'qub ruled competently for twelve years, and
after Ya'qub died in 1490 the White Sheep did what the Black Sheep had done in a
year, only slowly: a decade of cousins fighting each other, with the
confederation splitting into an eastern and a western half and the Turkmen
leaders choosing between claimants.

What was growing inside that decade was the Safavid order at Ardabil, and its
relationship to the Ak Koyunlu is closer than the two pages usually suggest.
Uzun Hasan had married his sister to Shaykh Junayd of the Safaviyya and his
daughter Halima — Alamshah Begum, the child of the Trebizond princess — to
Junayd's son Shaykh Haydar. Isma'il, who took Tabriz in 1501 and founded the
dynasty that made Iran Shi'i, was the son of that marriage. He was Uzun Hasan's
grandson and, through the same line, the great-grandson of an emperor of
Trebizond, and the tribes he led into Tabriz were the same Turkmen his
grandfather had led out of it.

This site does not count that as a successor claim, and the reason is worth
stating because it is the kind of case the rulebook was written for. Coding rule
1 excludes dynastic descent on its own: being descended from a house is not
claiming it, and descent counts only where it is publicly invoked as the basis
of a right to rule. Isma'il's public claim was Safavid and Alid — descent from
the seventh imam, and something considerably stronger than that in the poetry he
wrote — not Bayandur. The genealogy is on this page as prose because that is
where genealogies belong.

The end itself was quick. Isma'il beat Alvand at Sharur in 1501 and took Tabriz;
the eastern half under Sultan Murad held Iraq-i Ajam and Fars for a few years
and lost them by 1503; Diyarbakır, where the confederation had begun, went in
1508, and Murad was killed. A hundred and thirty years, of which about ten were
spent as the strongest power in Iran.

The state Isma'il built kept the law code, the chancery, the painters and the
tribes. What it added was a reason for the tribes to hold together that survived
a defeat, which is the one thing neither sheep ever had.
