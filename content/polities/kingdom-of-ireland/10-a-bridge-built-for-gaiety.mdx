The rising the United Irishmen had organized across much of the
country in 1798 spread furthest and lasted longest in County Wexford,
where rebel forces held the county town for several weeks and won a
real battlefield victory at New Ross on 5 June. Government troops
under General Lake rejected terms of surrender offered on the rebels'
behalf, answering that no conditions could be granted to men still in
arms - a refusal that, whatever it achieved militarily, removed any
incentive for the rebel leadership to restrain what happened next
inside the town they still held.

## Ninety-seven men killed one after another

On 20 June, as government forces closed in, a mob inside Wexford
began dragging prisoners - loyalist civilians and captured militia,
mostly Protestant - out to the town's own bridge, a structure the
chronicler who recorded the killings pointedly called too handsome
and gay in its appearance for what was done there. Groups of ten to
twenty were marched out under guard behind a black flag, and killed
one after another on the bridge itself, mostly by four men at a time
who raised the victim on their pikes while a crowd, largely women by
the same account, watched and cheered each fresh group's arrival.
Ninety-seven men had been killed before a priest, Father Curran,
finally halted the killing - not entirely by persuasion, but aided by
the sudden, accurate news that government troops were already
attacking the rebel position at Vinegar Hill and needed reinforcement.

## A massacre that outlived its own justification

The respite the priest secured proved lasting only because the
military crisis that interrupted the killing did not pass; Vinegar
Hill fell that same day, and the rebellion in Wexford collapsed within
it. What the massacre left behind was harder to contain than the
rising itself - a specific, well-documented atrocity that loyalist
opinion would cite for generations as proof of what Catholic Ireland
in arms actually meant, regardless of how unrepresentative Thomas
Dixon and the mob he led at the bridge were of the rebellion's own
leadership, much of which had spent the previous weeks trying,
without success, to prevent exactly this.
