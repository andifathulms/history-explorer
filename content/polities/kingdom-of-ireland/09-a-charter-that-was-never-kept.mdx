---
title: A charter that was never kept
drafted_from: lecky-1892
phase: peak
---

Limerick held out through the autumn of 1691 even after the Boyne had
already decided the wider war, and when the city finally came to
terms that October, its garrison commander Patrick Sarsfield secured
what looked, on paper, like generous conditions. The treaty's very
first article promised Irish Catholics "such privileges in the
exercise of their religion as are consistent with the laws of
Ireland, or as they did enjoy in the reign of Charles II" - a real
guarantee, signed by the Lords Justices and ratified under the Great
Seal of England, at a time when Charles II's own reign was remembered
as the most tolerant period Irish Catholics had known since the
Reformation.

## A promise that required a parliament willing to keep it

The treaty's civil terms needed parliamentary sanction to take full
legal effect, and the Parliament that convened afterward was not
disposed to grant it. Rather than the promised toleration, the years
that followed produced a new body of law - the Penal Laws - that
barred Catholics from owning land on the same terms as Protestants,
from entering the professions, from bearing arms, and from a long list
of other civil rights, systematically dismantling the position the
Treaty of Limerick had appeared to guarantee. Sarsfield himself did
not stay to see it: under the treaty's military terms he and several
thousand soldiers of the Limerick garrison sailed for France rather
than serve a settlement they had no reason to trust, the first
generation of what became known as the Wild Geese.

## A grievance that outlasted everyone who signed it

The gap between what the Treaty of Limerick actually promised and
what Irish Catholics actually received under the Penal Laws became,
for later generations, one of the central grievances of the whole
period - a broken charter cited by name for a century afterward by
people who had never read its text, because its violation had shaped
the legal position of most of the island's population before any of
them were born.
