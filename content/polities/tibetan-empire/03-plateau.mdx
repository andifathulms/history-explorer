---
title: The third power
drafted_from: beckwith-1987
phase: peak
---

At its height the empire held the Tarim basin oases, the Gansu corridor, much
of what is now Qinghai and the approaches to the Pamirs, and fought both of the
great settled powers of the age at once.

The Abbasid frontier in Central Asia was not simply the far edge of the
caliphate; it was one face of a three-cornered contest, and the Karluk, Türgish
and Uyghur groups in between made and unmade alliances with all three. The
period is usually narrated twice — once from Chang'an, once from Baghdad — and
each version has a frontier that stops where the other begins.

The empire's other creations are a script and a religion. Songtsen Gampo's reign
saw an alphabet devised for Tibetan on an Indic model — a writing system made for
a language that had none. Trisong Detsen made Buddhism the religion of the court.

It broke apart after Langdarma's assassination in 842, and nobody conquered
it.
