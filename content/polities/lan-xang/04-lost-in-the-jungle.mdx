The strategy that had twice saved Wiengchan from Burmese occupation could
not save the king who relied on it. In 1571, campaigning in Cambodia, Jai
Jett'a became separated from his own army in the jungle and was never
found again - no body, no captor, no clear account of what became of him,
only a king who simply stopped appearing in the record. One later
chronicler, unable to resist the irony, called it a fitting judgment on a
man who had once carried off Chiengmai's most sacred images and never
returned them.

## An infant king, and a regency too weak to hold

With no adult heir in the capital, an infant son of Jai Jett'a was
proclaimed king, governed in practice by a regent. It was exactly the kind
of succession a hostile neighbor could exploit, and Burma did not wait
long to try.
