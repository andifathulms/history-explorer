Burma's king demanded that the infant's regent abdicate in favor of one
of Jai Jett'a's own brothers - a man who had spent years as a prisoner in
Burma since an earlier invasion of Wiengchan, and who could therefore be
relied on to rule as a compliant vassal. The regent refused. In 1574 a
Burmese army invaded, captured the guardian who had been protecting the
young king, and placed Jai Jett'a's brother on the Wiengchan throne under
Burmese supervision - the furthest reach Burmese power would ever achieve
over the Lao kingdom, and a striking reversal for a dynasty that only a
few years before had been strong enough to besiege a Siamese border town
on its own initiative.

## Two decades, then a throw of the yoke

Vassalage did not last forever. In 1595, after twenty-one years as a
Burmese tributary, Wiengchan renounced the arrangement and reasserted its
own independence - one of the last of the mainland kingdoms Burma had
subdued in its sixteenth-century expansion to shake the yoke off
entirely. The kingdom that had once survived by vanishing into its own
jungle proved just as capable of waiting out an occupying overlordship as
it had been at waiting out an invading army.
