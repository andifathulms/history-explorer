Jai Jett'a spent most of his reign fighting off Burma rather than fighting
for advantage elsewhere, but one episode shows how far his own ambitions
reached when the opportunity arose. A Siamese governor, out of favor with
the pro-Burmese faction controlling the regency at Ayut'ia, invited Jai
Jett'a to intervene against the border town of P'itsanulok, then held by
the very official who had helped intercept Princess T'ep Krasatri two
years before. Jai Jett'a needed no second invitation. At the end of 1566
he marched a large army to the town and laid siege to it, while a Siamese
prince who marched north ostensibly to relieve the town in fact intended
to hand it over to the Lao king instead. The plot failed - the townspeople
refused the prince admittance - and a Burmese relief force arrived before
Wiengchan's army could take the walls, forcing Jai Jett'a to withdraw.

## A kingdom that kept surviving invasion by disappearing into the jungle

The pattern held throughout his reign: when Burmese armies invaded
Wiengchan itself, in 1563-64 and again in 1569-70, Jai Jett'a did not try
to hold his capital against them. He abandoned it and withdrew into the
jungle with most of his own population, waiting out Burmese armies that
suffered, each time, from famine and disease before they retired. It was
not a glamorous strategy, but it worked twice running - a kingdom too
thinly held to be occupied for long, and too elusive to be permanently
broken by any single campaign.
