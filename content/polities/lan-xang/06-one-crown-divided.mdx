Independence recovered in 1595 did not bring stability. For more
than a century afterward, rival princes contested the Wiangchan
throne with little the neighboring kingdoms troubled to record in
detail - until, by 1699, the dispute had split the kingdom outright.
Prince Chao Ong Wiet had set himself up as king at Wiangchan; his
cousin, Prince Kingkisarat, had meanwhile seized Luang Prabang and
now prepared to reunite the two by force, marching against his rival's
capital.

## A princess offered, an army that never fought

Ong Wiet, facing a cousin's army he could not match alone, appealed
to Siam for help and offered his own daughter's hand as the price of
alliance. Ayutthaya's king sent a relief force at once - but the
campaign never came to battle. Kingkisarat, weighing his own chances
against the combined strength of Wiangchan and its new Siamese ally,
chose negotiation instead: a treaty dividing the old kingdom of Lan
Xang between the two cousins outright, one to hold Luang Prabang and
the other Wiangchan, each an independent throne rather than a single
crown contested between two claimants. The promised princess went on
to Ayutthaya regardless, where she was given in marriage to the
Siamese king's own heir.

## A third throne, already standing apart

By the time Siam next intervened in Lao affairs, in the 1770s, a
third kingdom existed on the middle Mekong as well: Champasak, ruled
by a Prince Chao O as an independent principality in its own right,
far enough downriver that Wood's own account treats its separation
from the old body of Lan Xang as an accomplished fact rather than a
recent event needing explanation, and how or when it broke away is
left unstated. What is certain is that
the single kingdom Fa Ngum had joined by conquest in 1353, and that
Jai Jett'a had governed as one throne two centuries later, had by the
early eighteenth century become three separate courts - Luang
Prabang, Wiangchan and Champasak - each answering for itself, and
each, within a century, a tributary of one or another of its
stronger neighbors rather than of any single Lao crown.
