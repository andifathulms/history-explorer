The Seljuks enter this thread from outside it. Every previous polity here grew
out of an existing administrative structure — a governorship, a guard corps, a
mercenary contract. The Seljuks were Oghuz Turkmen who crossed the Oxus as
pastoralists looking for grazing, with a chief's family that had converted to
Islam a couple of generations earlier and a following that was not, in any
sense the Ghaznavid chancery would recognise, an army.

Mas'ud of Ghazna spent the 1030s trying to manage them as a nomad problem:
grant pasture, punish raids, move them on. It did not work, because the thing
he was negotiating with had no address.

In 1040 at Dandanaqan, in the desert near Merv, a Ghaznavid army that had been
marched around Khurasan without adequate water met the Seljuks and broke.
Tughril Beg was proclaimed ruler of Khurasan.

One battle transferred the richest province in the eastern Islamic world from a
dynasty at the height of its administrative sophistication to a confederation
that had none. This is the sharpest discontinuity in the corpus, and it is
worth noting that it was not the Ghaznavid state that failed — that state went
on functioning in India for another century and a half. It was that a settled
army lost a battle to people it had regarded as a logistical nuisance.
