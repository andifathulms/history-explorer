Under Alp Arslan and then Malik-Shah, from 1063 to 1092, the empire was
administered by one Persian vizier, Nizam al-Mulk, and the peak of the Great
Seljuk state is largely his.

Two of his instruments are the reason the dynasty left anything behind at all.
The *iqta* system assigned the revenue of a district to a commander in
place of pay, which solved the problem of financing a Turkmen cavalry army out
of a settled agrarian economy — and became the standard fiscal-military
arrangement across the Islamic world for the next several centuries. The
Nizamiyya madrasas, endowed at Baghdad, Nishapur and elsewhere, put state
funding behind Sunni jurisprudence and produced a trained, salaried,
institutionally connected class of Sunni scholars. Al-Ghazali taught at the
Baghdad Nizamiyya.

<Figure id="isfahan-jame-mosque" />

That is the difference between holding a faith and carrying one. The Seljuks did
not convert a population; they built the institutions through which
a specific Sunni establishment was propagated and reproduced, in a period when
the Fatimids were doing the same for Isma'ilism from Cairo. The two programmes
were consciously aimed at each other.

He also wrote the *Siyasatnama*, a book of statecraft for Malikshah, in the last
years of a ministry that ended badly.
