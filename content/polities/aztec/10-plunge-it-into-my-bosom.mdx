Cuauhtemoc had kept a fleet of large canoes ready for weeks, intending
to slip across the lake to reeds thick enough to hide in and reach the
mainland once the city's own defence collapsed entirely. When Cortés's
commander Sandoval pushed his brigantines into the quarter where
Cuauhtemoc's own palace stood, the king and his household fled by water
along with the rest of the city's surviving nobility, and the lake
filled with fleeing canoes.

## A pursuit that ended in a single fast ship

Sandoval ordered the brigantines to give chase, with strict instructions
that whoever found the king was to take him alive and treat him with
every courtesy. García Holguín, commanding the fleet's fastest vessel,
recognized Cuauhtemoc's own canoe by its fine carved decoration and
ordered it to stop; when it did not, his men levelled their crossbows
and muskets. Cuauhtemoc called out at once: "Forbid your men to shoot at
me. I am the king of Mexico. I only ask that you not touch my wife, my
children or anything else I have with me - take me alone to Malinche."
Holguín brought him aboard with courtesy, spread mats and cloaks for him
to sit on, and had food set before him.

## A dagger offered, and refused

A dispute broke out between Holguín and Sandoval over who deserved
credit for the capture, settled only when Cortés himself, watching from
the summit of a temple, ordered both to bring the king before him
together. Cuauhtemoc, in tears, told Cortés he had done everything owed
to the defence of his city and his people, that his resources were
exhausted, and that Cortés should draw his own dagger and kill him where
he stood. Cortés refused, telling him through the interpreters that his
courage in defending his capital did him more honour than shame.
Cuauhtemoc was taken prisoner on the thirteenth of August, 1521, at the
hour of vespers - and Bernal Díaz, recalling the moment decades later,
wrote that in the days after the capture the surviving Spanish soldiers
felt strangely deaf, as though a bell that had rung without pause for
ninety-three days had suddenly gone silent.
