Before they had a city of their own, the Mexica served as vassals and
mercenaries to the Colhua lords of Colhuacan, fighting their wars for
them. Sent against the Xochimilcas without weapons the Colhuas thought
fit to lend a despised subject people, the Mexica improvised knives from
obsidian and took captives anyway - and when their own overlords mocked
them for arriving at the victory feast without prisoners to show for it,
the Mexica emptied baskets full of severed ears before the astonished
Colhua ranks, proof of exactly how many enemies they had killed rather
than bothered to bind.

## A sacrifice that frightened their own masters into expelling them

Determined to dedicate an altar to their own god, the Mexica asked the
Colhua lord for something fine to offer on it; he sent them, as an
insult, a filthy dead bird wrapped in a dirty rag. The Mexica said
nothing, but at the dedication itself, before the Colhua king and his
nobles who had come expecting to mock their vassals further, they
produced four Xochimilca prisoners they had secretly kept back from the
earlier campaign and sacrificed them on the spot - the first human
sacrifice, Clavigero notes, known to have been performed in that
country. The horror it caused among the Colhuas was not gratitude for a
show of piety: they expelled the Mexica from their territory outright,
unwilling to keep such a people among them any longer.

## A sign at the end of a long search

Driven out, the Mexica wandered the shores of Lake Texcoco until they
reached a place where a nopal cactus grew from a stone, with an eagle's
foot resting on it - the mark their own tradition held had been promised
by their oracle as the site of their future city. Clavigero is careful to
note that the more elaborate versions of this story, in which the eagle
holds a serpent in its claws, belong to later, embellished retellings
rather than the earliest accounts, and he leaves them aside as fable. What
is agreed is the year: 1325. The Mexica built a shrine to their god
Huitzilopochtli on the spot, consecrating it with the blood of a Colhua
warrior killed in a skirmish, and began raising huts of reed and rush
around it - the beginning of a city that would, within two centuries,
become the largest in the new world.
