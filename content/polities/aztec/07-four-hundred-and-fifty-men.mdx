Hernán Cortés and his party reached the causeway approach to
Tenochtitlan in November 1519, guided and reinforced along the way by
Tlaxcala and other peoples who had their own long-standing grievances
against Mexica tribute demands. Bernal Díaz del Castillo, who marched
with the expedition and set his own account down decades later, recalled
looking out over the crowded canoes and countless people they passed
on the causeway and reckoning that his own company numbered scarcely
four hundred and fifty men - a mere handful, by his own description,
venturing further into a hostile capital than he believed any men in
history had dared before.

## A monarch carried out to meet them

Moctezuma himself came out along the causeway to receive them, borne on
a richly decorated litter under a canopy of green feathers, gold, silver
and precious stones, supported by nobles who walked before him spreading
cotton cloths on the ground so that his feet would never touch bare
earth. No one in his own retinue looked directly at his face except the
four kinsmen who carried him. When word reached Cortés that the monarch
himself was approaching, he dismounted and advanced on foot to meet him.

## An exchange of gifts neither side fully trusted

The two men exchanged courtesies through the interpreter Marina: Cortés
hung a necklace of scented, brightly coloured stones strung on gold wire
around Moctezuma's neck and was prevented by the king's own attendants,
who considered it improper, from embracing him. Moctezuma answered with
appropriate formality of his own, and delegated two of his nephews to
conduct the Spanish party to their quarters before returning to the
city himself. Díaz records the meeting as cordial in every visible
particular - and, in hindsight, as the last moment either side could
plausibly claim the other's intentions were still genuinely unknown.
