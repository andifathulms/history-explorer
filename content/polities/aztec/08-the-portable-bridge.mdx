Cortés's occupation of Tenochtitlan collapsed within a year of his first
meeting with Moctezuma. With the city in open revolt against the
Spanish garrison holding Moctezuma in his own palace, Cortés had the
king address the crowd from the walls in a bid to calm them. Moctezuma
went reluctantly, having by then, in Díaz's account, lost faith in
Cortés's promises entirely. His own people recognized him and briefly
held their fire, but before the ceasefire could hold, he was struck by
an arrow and three sling-stones, wounded in the arm, leg and head.
Carried back to his apartment, he refused all treatment and died of the
wounds within days, mourned, Díaz records, even by Spanish soldiers who
had grown fond of him over months of captivity.

## A bridge fitted to the width of the causeways

With Moctezuma dead and a new king already raised against them, the
Spanish position in the city was untenable, and Cortés ordered a
night retreat along one of the causeways. Knowing the Mexica had cut the
bridges at several points, his men built a portable wooden bridge sized
to span the gaps and carried it with them. It worked for the first
crossing. At the second, in the confusion of a night march under a
thin rain, two horses slipped on the wet planking and went into the
water, overturning the bridge before it could be recovered.

## A canal filled with the drowned

Word of the retreat had already reached the city, and Mexica warriors
poured onto the lake in canoes, cutting off everyone still on the wrong
side of the gap. The canal filled with drowned horses, drowned men and
the baggage they had tried to carry with them, while those still able to
swim were pulled from the water and taken prisoner rather than killed
outright. Hundreds of Spanish soldiers and thousands of their Tlaxcalan
allies died in the retreat later generations would call the Noche
Triste - the sorrowful night - a rout so complete that Cortés's own
survivors reassembled outside the city with barely a fraction of the
force, and the gold, weapons and supplies, they had brought into it.
