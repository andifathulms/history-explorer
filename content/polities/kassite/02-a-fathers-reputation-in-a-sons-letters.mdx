By the fourteenth century the Kassite kings of Babylon were dealing
with Egypt as equals, and the correspondence survives only because
Egypt kept its own copies, filed away at the new capital Akhenaten
built at Amarna. Kurigalzu I, whose own letters have not survived, is
remembered only through the reputation his son left behind: when
Burna-Buriash II wrote to Akhenaten, he invoked his father's own
precedent directly, reminding the Egyptian king what Kurigalzu had
once been owed and complaining when Akhenaten's own gifts of gold
fell short of it. A king who left no letters of his own was still,
a generation later, the standard his son measured Pharaoh against.

## Marriages, physicians and short-changed gold

Burna-Buriash's own letters are a study in a great power's
irritations rather than its crises. He exchanged daughters in
marriage with the Egyptian court, requested physicians be sent to
attend him, and complained repeatedly that consignments of gold sent
from Egypt arrived lighter than promised - grievances a king raises
only with a counterpart he expects to answer him, not a subordinate
he could simply command. The correspondence is domestic in tone and
imperial in implication: two courts treating each other's goodwill as
worth the trouble of managing carefully.

## A boundary agreement is itself an admission

Babylon's other major relationship of the period, with the rising
power of Assyria to the north, tells a quieter story through its own
formalities. Kara-indash, a contemporary of Egypt's Amenhotep III,
concluded a friendly agreement fixing the common boundary with the
Assyrian king Ashur-rim-nishishu; a generation later Burna-Buriash
himself found it necessary to negotiate a similar treaty with a later
Assyrian counterpart. That such agreements needed drawing up at all is
itself evidence of the friction beneath them - two centuries of an
uneasy peace, broken by intermittent wars that would, over time,
generally cost Babylon ground rather than gain her any, even though
neither side was yet strong enough to dominate the other outright.
