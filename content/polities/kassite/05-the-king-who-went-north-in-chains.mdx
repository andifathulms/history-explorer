The uneasy peace with Assyria ended, in the end, the way the treaties
had always implied it might: not with another lost boundary stone, but
with a Kassite king taken prisoner. In about 1225 the Assyrian king
Tukulti-Ninurta I took Babylon outright, deported the reigning
Kashtiliash IV north in chains, and carried off the cult statue of
Marduk with him. It was the first time Assyria had ever held
Babylonia, and it lasted roughly seven years.

## A defeat told from both sides

Assyria wrote an epic about the campaign, and Babylon wrote back: a
text composed on the Babylonian side accused Tukulti-Ninurta of
sacrilege for what he had done to Marduk's own city and temple. The
two accounts do not agree on much beyond the fact of the campaign
itself, but that disagreement is itself the record - a conquest
important enough to both sides that neither one let it pass in
silence, the same way Kashtiliash's own capture would still be
remembered generations later when a Kassite king finally reversed it.

## A captivity nobody in Babylon forgot

Kashtiliash IV himself does not reappear in the record after his
deportation, and no source claims he returned. What his capture began
was not the end of the dynasty - the Kassite line held Babylon's
throne for decades yet - but it was the deepest humiliation the family
had suffered since Gandash first took the city, and it set the terms
for everything the rivalry with Assyria produced afterward: a debt the
dynasty's own later kings would spend a generation trying to collect.
