Gandash took Babylon, but Babylon was not the whole of Babylonia. In
the marshy country at the head of the Persian Gulf, a line of native
kings who had never answered to the new dynasty at all kept a separate
throne going, and for the better part of a century the Kassites ruled
the north of the old kingdom while the Sealand ruled the south of it
as though the conquest further upstream had never happened.

## A rival throne the conquest never reached

The Sealand's own kings are known mostly by name and sequence rather
than by any deed the source can narrate in detail, but their
persistence is itself the point. A newly installed dynasty that could
not make good its own claim to the whole country it named after
itself was not, for several generations, the unchallenged power its
later reputation would suggest. Whatever the Kassite kings controlled
from Babylon, the marshes at the Gulf answered to somebody else.

## Ulam-Buriash finishes what Gandash started

The settlement came only in the time of Kashtiliash's own sons, when
one brother kept the throne at Babylon and the other, Ulam-Buriash,
took an army south and did what a century of Kassite kings before him
had not: he broke the Sealand's own line, folded its territory into
Babylonia proper, and stayed on afterward to reign there in his own
right rather than simply installing a governor. It was not a raid but
an annexation - the first time since Gandash's own conquest that one
king's writ ran from Babylon to the Gulf without interruption, and the
point after which the Kassite dynasty's claim to rule the whole of
Babylonia finally matched the ground it actually held.
