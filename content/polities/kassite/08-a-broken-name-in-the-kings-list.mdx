Sippar's sack did not itself end the dynasty. A further Elamite blow
fell a few years afterward, under whichever obscure successor had
taken the broken throne, and it is this second defeat rather than the
first that the sources treat as the dynasty's actual close.

## A dynasty too obscure at its own end to be named with confidence

The Kings' List itself preserves the name of this very last Kassite
ruler only as a broken word, and he reigned for no more than three
years before the end came. Whether that last name should be restored
as one reading or another is a dispute the source discusses without
resolving - two candidate readings, neither one the source is willing
to prefer over the other.

What is certain is only the shape of the ending itself: a dynasty that
had spent four centuries as a fixture of the Babylonian throne, secure
enough in its own permanence that no one had thought to write down how
it began, went out just as unrecorded - reduced, in the one document
meant to preserve every king's name in order, to a name too damaged to
read. Babylon itself did not go with it. The line that eventually
avenged Sippar and recovered Marduk's statue from Elam belongs to a
different chapter of the city's history, but it is worth noting here
that the throne the last broken-named Kassite lost was not, this time,
lost for good - only the family holding it was.
