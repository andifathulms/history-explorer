Magindanao's first documented clash with Spain came in 1597, when
Datu Bwisan, allied with Silongan, the raja of neighbouring Buayan,
checked an invasion led by the Spanish Marquis Rodriguez and
defeated him at Tampakan. Bwisan was, by the source's own judgment,
the most formidable enemy Spain had yet met in the southern islands
- but his son eclipsed him entirely.

## The strongest sultan Magindanao ever had

Sultan Dipatuan Kudarat, known to the Spanish chroniclers as
"Corralat," ruled with a strength his father had never matched. He
fought Spain bitterly for decades, and his raiding fleets
terrorized the coasts of Luzon and the Visayas and held much of the
southern seas under his own control. In 1636 General Corcuera led a
major Spanish expedition against him; Kudarat's fort, heavily
fortified and armed with a substantial arsenal, held out before it
was finally reduced, and the Spanish carried off eight bronze
cannon, twenty-seven lantaka and a hundred muskets as spoils.

## Peace, then war again

Ten years later, in 1646, Kudarat's position had only grown
stronger, and he chose peace on his own terms rather than continued
war: a treaty of mutual alliance with Spain that secured better
trading conditions and allowed Jesuit missionaries to build a church
in his own capital. The peace lasted thirteen years. When hostilities
resumed in 1659 with a fresh Spanish campaign against Simuay,
Kudarat blocked the Pulangi river at several points and turned the
invasion back a second time - the last great victory of the sultan
who had spent his whole reign proving that Magindanao would be
courted, not conquered.
