No Moro record fixes an exact date for the arrival of Sharif
Kabungsuwan, a descendant of the Prophet by way of Johor, at the
mouth of the Pulangi river - the early Moros dated their own affairs
only by a recurring eight-year cycle, unrelated from one cycle to
the next. Working from the generations recorded between Kabungsuwan
and his great-grandson Bwisan, who is independently known to have
been alive in 1597, the arrival can be placed no more precisely than
around the end of the fifteenth or the start of the sixteenth
century - a range Captain Thomas Forest's own estimate of 1475,
gathered from Magindanao informants in 1776, fits closely enough to
accept.

## People scattered across the archipelago

Kabungsuwan had not travelled alone. A storm scattered the fleet
that left Johor with him, and its survivors made their way to ports
across the region - Bangjar, Tampasuk, Brunei, Sandakan, Sulu,
Malabang and Mindanao among them - seeding Islamic settlement well
beyond the mouth of the Pulangi itself. At Magindanao, Kabungsuwan
married Putri Tunina, the adopted daughter of the local chief
Tabunaway, and it was through their descendants that the ruling
line of the sultanate would trace itself for the next three and a
half centuries.
