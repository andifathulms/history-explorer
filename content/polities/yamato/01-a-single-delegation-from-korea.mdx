Tradition placed Japan's imperial line on the throne from 660 BC, tracing
its descent from the sun goddess through a first emperor, Jimmu, whose
historicity the sources that carry his story cannot themselves confirm.
For twelve centuries afterward the court kept genealogies and reign
lengths that grow steadily less plausible the further back they reach -
one sovereign credited with a hundred-and-one-year reign, a chief
minister said to have served five emperors across three and a half
centuries of life.

## A court already old when its history becomes legible

By the reign of Kimmei in the mid-sixth century, the pattern of
implausibly long reigns had already given way to something closer to
ordinary succession, and the record itself becomes correspondingly more
trustworthy. Kimmei's own reign supplies the moment continental
civilization arrived at court in a form specific enough to date and
describe rather than merely assert.

## One shipment, three disciplines

In 552, a single delegation from Korea brought doctors, diviners,
astronomers and mathematicians to reside at the Japanese court, and
Buddhist missionaries travelled with them. The court that received this
delegation had no writing system of its own, no calendar science, and no
organized medicine beyond what earlier scattered contacts with the
continent had supplied piecemeal. What arrived in 552 came as a single
coherent package - religion, technical knowledge, and the literacy
needed to record both - rather than as another isolated gift of
tailors or horses of the kind earlier annals note in passing.

## The first of three waves

Japanese history would receive two further waves of comparably
transformative foreign contact - from Western Europe in the fifteenth
century, and from the wider world after Commodore Perry's arrival - but
this sixth-century wave from China by way of Korea was, by every measure
of lasting influence, the most powerful of the three. It gave the
Yamato court the tools of a literate, Buddhist, continentally-modelled
state at exactly the moment its own succession was finally settling
into a form later generations could actually verify.
