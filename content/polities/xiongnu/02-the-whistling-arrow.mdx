The Xiongnu confederation was assembled by a man who began by murdering his
father, and the story Sima Qian tells about how he prepared for it is the most
quoted paragraph in the history of the steppe.

## The training

Modu was Touman's heir, and Touman wanted a younger son to succeed instead, so
he sent Modu as a hostage to the Yuezhi and then attacked them, expecting them
to kill him. Modu stole a horse and got home.

Given a command of ten thousand horsemen, he had whistling arrows made, and
ordered that whatever he shot at with a whistling arrow, everyone was to shoot
at too, and anyone who did not would be executed.

He shot a bird. Some men did not shoot and were killed. He shot his own best
horse; some hesitated, and were killed. He shot his own wife; some were too
appalled to shoot, and were killed. He shot his father's best horse, and
everybody shot.

Then he went hunting with his father and shot him with a whistling arrow, and
every man in his guard shot too. He killed his stepmother, his younger brother
and the officials who would not obey him, and made himself chanyu.

Sima Qian is writing a century later and this is a formed literary anecdote, not
a report. But what it is *about* is exact: the problem of converting a tribal
following into an instrument that will do a thing its members find abhorrent, on
a signal, without asking. Every steppe state that worked afterwards solved that
problem somehow.

## The two demands from the east

The story continues into the founding of the empire, and the second half is
better than the first.

The Donghu, the dominant power to the east, tested the new chanyu. They asked
for Touman's famous thousand-li horse. Modu's advisers said refuse; he said one
does not begrudge a neighbour a horse, and sent it. They asked for one of his
consorts. His advisers were furious; he said one does not begrudge a neighbour a
woman, and sent her.

Then the Donghu asked for a strip of abandoned wasteland on the frontier that
neither side used. This time his advisers said it was worthless, give it to them.

Modu said that land is the foundation of the state, executed everyone who had
advised giving it away, mounted, ordered death for anyone who lagged behind, and
destroyed the Donghu in a surprise attack.

## What was assembled

He then broke the Yuezhi and drove them west — the beginning of a migration
that eventually put them in Bactria — subdued the Dingling in the north and the
Kirghiz country, and recovered the Ordos loop that Qin garrisons had taken.

By about 200 BC the confederation ran from Manchuria to the Tarim and from Lake
Baikal to the Great Wall. Its structure, so far as the Chinese sources allow it
to be reconstructed, is a permanent one: a chanyu at the centre, a Wise King of
the Left and a Wise King of the Right holding the two wings, and a descending
order of commands nominally reckoned in ten thousands, with subordinate peoples
attached at every level.

That is a real state apparatus, and it is the first one on the steppe. What it
was for is the subject of the next chapter, and the answer is not raiding.
