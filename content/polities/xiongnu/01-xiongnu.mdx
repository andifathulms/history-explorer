---
title: The first confederation
drafted_from: di-cosmo-2002
phase: formation
---

Modu unified the steppe tribes around 209 BC and immediately did what no steppe
power had done: forced the Han empire into a treaty of equals, sealed with a
princess, silk payments and a frontier settlement. The payments continued for
sixty years.

Di Cosmo's argument, which this page follows, is that this is not a story about
nomads raiding for what they lacked. The Xiongnu state was built by a
confederation that needed the tribute to hold itself together — the chanyu
redistributed Chinese silk and grain to keep subordinate chiefs loyal — and the
Han empire's expansion was as much a cause of steppe centralisation as a response
to it. The two states made each other.

That relationship is the template. Everything on this site's inner-asia and
east-asia pages — the Turks, the Uyghurs, the Khitan, the Jurchen, the Mongols —
repeats some version of it: a steppe confederation whose cohesion depends on
extracting from a settled empire, and a settled empire whose frontier policy
alternates between paying and campaigning. The Xiongnu split into northern and
southern halves and dissolved by the end of the first century, which is also part
of the template.
