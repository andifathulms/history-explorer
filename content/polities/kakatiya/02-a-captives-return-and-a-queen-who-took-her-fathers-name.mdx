Rudradeva's brother Mahadeva succeeded him and kept up the fight
against the Yadavas, but he too died in the field — thrown, tradition
holds, from the back of the war elephant he was fighting from. His
young son Ganapati had already been taken captive by the Yadavas in
the same rout that killed his uncle, and spent his early years a
prisoner at the rival capital of Devagiri while a general named
Recherla Rudra held the Kakatiya kingdom together against invading
neighbors and rebellious chiefs alike, ruling in his absent king's
name.

## A captor's mercy, or a captor's calculation

Ganapati was released not long after, in circumstances the source
cannot pin down with certainty. Yadava inscriptions credit their own
king's compassion for the young captive; it seems just as likely that
the Yadavas had their own reasons — a rising Hoysala threat to their
south made a hostile eastern neighbor a risk not worth the trouble of
keeping. Whatever the cause, Ganapati returned to a throne his father
and Recherla Rudra had barely kept from collapsing, and what followed
was sixty-three years many historians consider the most brilliant
period the Andhra country had yet seen — Ganapati unified nearly every
Telugu-speaking territory under a single crown, by war where he had
to and by diplomacy where he could.

## A daughter who ruled as her father's son

Ganapati had no surviving son, and named his own daughter as his heir
— an unusual choice the kingdom did not fully accept without a fight.
Rudramadevi faced a rebellion from her own half-brothers soon after
taking power, was driven briefly from her capital, recaptured it, and
had the rebels put to death. She ruled for decades afterward under the
name and title Rudradeva Maharaja — her father's own regnal name —
and tradition holds that when a Yadava king came to besiege her
capital, she put on armor and men's dress and fought at the head of
her own army. Whether or not that particular story is literal fact,
the inscriptions of her reign leave no doubt that Kakatiya power, which
had once nearly died with its own founder, was by her time strong
enough to survive open rebellion, foreign invasion, and a queen's own
contested claim to the throne all at once.
