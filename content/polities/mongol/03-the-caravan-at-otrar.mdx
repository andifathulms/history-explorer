The invasion that destroyed the eastern Islamic world began with a commercial
dispute, and the man who provoked it had made himself the only ruler capable of
resisting it.

## The provocation

A Mongol trade caravan was seized at Otrar on the Syr Darya in 1218 and its
merchants put to death by the governor, on the charge of being spies. Chinggis
sent envoys to demand redress. They were executed too.

Ibn al-Athir, the Arab historian writing within a decade of the event and from the
accounts of people fleeing it, does not treat this as an excuse invented afterwards.
He treats it as a blunder, and he apportions the blame precisely — not to the
Mongols, whom he regards as a natural catastrophe, but to the Khwarazmshah.

Three charges. Greed: Muhammad had overrun the Muslim lands and destroyed the
neighbouring kings until he stood alone over all of them, so that when he was
beaten there was nobody left to check the invaders or to protect anyone. Treachery:
his murder of the merchants and the ambassador gave Chinggis the best possible
pretext, and in the process taught the Mongols how weak and undefended Persia was.
And irresolution: at the first reverse he went from boastful defiance to complete
panic, and died about two years later a hunted fugitive on an island in the Caspian.

There is a fourth thread in the account. The caliph al-Nasir at Baghdad, suspecting
his too-powerful vassal of designs on the city, had been working to weaken him by
intrigue — and, Ibn al-Athir hints and al-Maqrizi states outright, encouraged the
Mongols to invade his territory. It was the caliph's own house that they destroyed
thirty-eight years later.

## What Ibn al-Athir wrote

He is the most important witness to the invasion in any language, and he could
barely make himself write it down.

> For some years I continued averse from mentioning this event, deeming it so
> horrible that I shrank from recording it, and ever withdrawing one foot as I
> advanced the other. To whom, indeed, can it be easy to write the announcement of
> the death-blow of Islam and the Muslims, or who is he on whom the remembrance
> thereof can weigh lightly? O would that my mother had not born me, or that I had
> died and become a forgotten thing ere this befell!

His friends urged him to set it down. He hesitated a long time, and concluded at
last that omitting it could serve no useful purpose.

What follows is a claim about scale made by a careful man who knew what he was
comparing it with. The worst calamity on record, he says, was what Nebuchadnezzar
did to the children of Israel — and what was Jerusalem compared to the cities these
destroyed, each of them double its size? Those they massacred in a single city
exceeded all the children of Israel. Antichrist will at least spare those who
follow him; these spared nobody.

## The route, in one paragraph

The most useful passage is the geography, because he wrote it as a single breathless
sentence and the speed is the point.

They came out of the confines of China and attacked the cities of Turkestan,
Kashgar and Balasaghun; thence to Transoxiana, Samarkand and Bukhara; one division
then into Khurasan until it had finished taking and destroying and slaying and
plundering, and on to Ray, Hamadan and the Highlands as far as Iraq; thence into
Azerbaijan and Arran, destroying the towns and killing most of the people, of whom
none escaped but a small remnant — *and all this in less than a year; this is a
thing whereof the like hath not been heard*. Then through the Darband of Shirwan,
past the fortress they could not take, into the country of the Alans and the Lezgi,
and on to the Kipchak, who are the most numerous of the Turks, killing all who
withstood them while the survivors fled to the fords and the mountain tops and
abandoned the country.

Another division, he adds, went to Ghazna and its dependencies and the borders of
India, Sistan and Kirman, and did worse.

Alexander, he notes, is agreed by the historians to have conquered the world — and
took about ten years over it, and did not slaughter, being satisfied that men should
be subject to him.

## Why nothing stopped them

Ibn al-Athir's explanation is logistical and it is correct.

> Moreover they need no commissariat, nor the conveyance of supplies, for they have
> with them sheep, cows, horses, and the like quadrupeds, the flesh of which they
> eat. As for their beasts which they ride, these dig into the earth with their
> hoofs and eat the roots of plants, knowing naught of barley. And so, when they
> alight anywhere, they have need of nothing from without.

An army that carries its own food on the hoof and whose horses forage under snow
does not have to stop, does not have to hold a supply line, and cannot be starved
out of a country. Every state it met was built to fight armies that could.

The rest of his description of them — that they worship the sun at its rising, eat
anything including dogs, and do not recognise marriage, so that a child does not
know its father — is what a horrified man repeats about people he has never seen,
and is worth keeping in the record precisely as that.
