title: Westward, and the numbers problem

The westward campaigns began, on the sources' own account, with a diplomatic
insult: a Mongol trade caravan massacred at Utrar in 1218 by a governor of the
Khwarazmshah, and envoys sent to demand redress executed. Chinggis turned an
army east of the Tien Shan onto Transoxiana in 1219. Within three years Bukhara,
Samarkand, Merv, Nishapur and Herat had been taken, and the Khwarazmian empire —
the largest state in the eastern Islamic world, and the last polity in this
site's Iranian Intermezzo — had ceased to exist.

Later campaigns took Rus' and Hungary under Batu, Iran and Iraq under Hulegu,
and Song China under Kublai. Baghdad fell in 1258.

A caution the sources force. The medieval accounts give casualty figures for
these sieges — 1.7 million at Nishapur, comparable numbers at Merv — that cannot
be reconciled with any plausible population for those cities, and several were
written by authors with an interest in the scale. That the destruction in
Khurasan was severe and that irrigation systems and urban populations took
generations to recover is well attested. The specific numbers are not, and this
site does not print them. Where a figure has no support it is absent here, which
is the same rule that leaves the population axis on this page empty.
