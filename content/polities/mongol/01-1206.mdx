---
title: What was assembled in 1206
drafted_from: morgan-2007
phase: formation
---

Temujin spent twenty years destroying the other confederations of the Mongolian
plateau — Tatars, Kereit, Naiman, Merkit — and was proclaimed Chinggis Khan at a
gathering on the Onon in 1206. The significance is not the conquest. Steppe
confederations had risen and dissolved for a thousand years.

The significance is what he did to the tribes afterwards. He broke them up and
redistributed their fighting men into decimal units — tens, hundreds, thousands,
ten-thousands — commanded by men he appointed, frequently of low birth, who owed
their position to him alone. A soldier could not leave his unit on pain of death.
The tribal aristocracies that had made every previous steppe polity temporary
were dismantled as organising structures and replaced with an army that was
loyal to a person and, after him, to a lineage.

That is the innovation, and it is administrative rather than military. The
Mongol advantages usually listed — horse archery, mobility, feigned retreat —
were shared by every steppe army the settled world had faced. What was not
shared was an instrument that stayed assembled.
