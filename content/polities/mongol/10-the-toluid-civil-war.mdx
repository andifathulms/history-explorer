The Mongol empire stopped being one empire in 1260, in a war between two of
Tolui's sons over a succession, and the fighting settled nothing except that the
thing could not be put back together.

## Why a death abroad was fatal

Möngke died in 1259 on campaign in Sichuan. Under Mongol practice the
succession belonged to a *quriltai*: an assembly of the whole imperial family,
held in Mongolia, which the princes were obliged to attend.

That requirement had already dictated Eurasian history once. In 1241, when
Ögödei died, Batu's armies were in Hungary and had just destroyed the Hungarian
and Polish field forces; the campaign was broken off and the princes rode east
for the assembly, and central Europe was not attacked again. Whether the
succession was the real reason is argued, but it is what the sources say.

In 1259 the same rule produced the opposite result: nobody went.

## Two assemblies

Kublai was in China with the armies. He held his own assembly at Kaiping, among
his own commanders, and was proclaimed. Ariq Böke was at Karakorum, holding the
capital and the homeland, and held the proper kind of assembly with the proper
people, and was proclaimed too.

Four years of civil war followed, and the decisive factor was food. Karakorum
had never been able to feed itself and depended on grain convoys from north
China. Kublai held north China and cut them. Ariq Böke surrendered in 1264 and
was dead within two years.

That is the whole argument of the empire settled by a supply line. The brother
with the farmland beat the brother with the steppe, and the imperial centre
moved permanently out of Mongolia.

## The other war, at the same time

While that was happening, two more Chinggisid princes were fighting each other
in the Caucasus.

Berke of the Golden Horde had converted to Islam. His cousin Hülegü had sacked
Baghdad in 1258 and killed the caliph. Berke is reported to have said that
Hülegü had destroyed all the cities of the Muslims and taken the life of the
caliph without consulting his kinsmen, and that if God willed he would call him
to account for so much innocent blood.

In 1262 they went to war. The Golden Horde allied with the Mamluk sultanate in
Egypt — the state that had beaten Hülegü's army at Ain Jalut two years before —
and the two Mongol houses fought each other along the Terek for decades.

So within four years of Möngke's death: two khans claiming the succession, two
more khans at war with each other, and one of them in formal alliance with the
empire's most successful enemy.

## What was left

Not a partition anybody agreed to. Four states with a common ancestry, of which
the Yuan in China was the largest and richest, the Ilkhanate in Iran recognised
its seniority, and the Golden Horde and the Chagatai ulus effectively did not.

The Great Khan's title survived and stopped meaning anything outside the
territory he actually held. Coins were still struck in his name in places he had
no power over, and envoys still went back and forth. But after 1260 no assembly
of the whole family was ever held again, and the one institution that had made
the empire a single object — the obligation of every prince to ride to Mongolia
and vote — was gone.

An empire that had conquered from the Danube to the Yellow Sea was undone by the
one procedure it had never bothered to write down.
