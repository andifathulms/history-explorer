Chlothar II made his son Dagobert sub-king of Austrasia in 623, and when
father and son nearly came to war three days after Dagobert's own
wedding over how much of that kingdom's territory should actually answer
to him, twelve Frankish lords and the peacemaking bishop Arnulf of Metz
talked them back from the brink rather than let the reunited realm
splinter again so soon. When Chlothar died in 629, Dagobert moved fast:
he mustered his Austrasian followers under arms and sent envoys ahead
into Burgundy and Neustria to secure his own succession before any rival
claim could take hold. Most of both kingdoms' bishops and magnates
submitted before he had even finished the journey from Reims to Soissons;
a half-hearted bid by his own brother Charibert came to little.

## A conqueror who chose to share

Dagobert took the whole of his father's kingdom, Neustria and Burgundy
together with their treasuries, becoming the first king since his
grandfather Chlothar I to rule a genuinely united Frankish realm rather
than merely the largest share of a divided one. He then had second
thoughts about leaving his brother with nothing at all, and granted
Charibert a sub-kingdom between the Loire and the Pyrenees - Toulouse,
Cahors, Agen, Périgueux, Saintes and the country toward Gascony - enough
to support a royal household without threatening Dagobert's own hold on
the rest. Later generations would remember Dagobert's reign as the last
moment a Merovingian king held real, personal command over the whole
Frankish administration rather than leaving it to the mayors of his
palace, and as a period of law, patronage and diplomacy substantial
enough that the kingdom's own bishops and magnates had reason to prefer
his rule to any rival's.

## A death that changed nothing at first, and everything after

Dagobert fell ill with dysentery at his villa near Paris in his sixteenth
year as sole king and was carried to the abbey of Saint-Denis, which his
own patronage had filled with gold and precious ornament. Sensing death
was near, he sent for his trusted adviser Aega and commended to him his
queen Nantechild and his young son Clovis, judging Aega capable of
governing the kingdom well in his name. He died within days and was
buried at Saint-Denis among the riches he had given it. The arrangement
he left behind - a child on the throne, a chosen minister running the
kingdom in his name - would outlive Dagobert by a hundred years, long
after the ministers in question stopped asking any Merovingian king's
leave to do it.
