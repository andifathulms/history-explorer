---
title: A stranger among kinsmen
drafted_from: gregory-brehaut
phase: formation
---

Soissons gave Clovis a former Roman province; it did nothing about the
other Frankish kings who ruled their own peoples alongside him, kinsmen
he had no intention of sharing power with indefinitely. Over the course
of his reign Clovis eliminated them one by one, often through
manipulation rather than open battle. Chararic, a Frankish king who had
refused to help him against Syagrius, was captured with his son, forcibly
tonsured into the clergy, and later killed once Clovis judged the
humiliation insufficient insurance against future rebellion.

## A father turned against his own son

Against Sigebert, the one-legged king of Cologne, Clovis worked through
Sigebert's own son Chloderic, persuading him that his father's death
would leave him free to enjoy the old king's treasure and Clovis's own
friendship. Chloderic had his father murdered while out walking in a
forest, then sent envoys to Clovis to divide up the dead king's gold.
Clovis's own messengers, sent in return to examine the treasure,
struck Chloderic down with an axe while his own arm was in the coffer.
Clovis then addressed the assembled people of Cologne, denying any part
in the murders of either father or son and offering himself instead as
their new king - an offer the crowd, whatever their private suspicions,
accepted by acclamation.

## A ruse played on his own kin

Ragnachar, a Frankish king at Cambrai, and his brother Ricchar met a
similar end after Clovis bribed Ragnachar's own retainers with gilded
bronze passed off as gold to turn against him in battle. Captured and
bound, Ragnachar was brought before Clovis, who rebuked him for the
disgrace of having let himself be taken alive rather than die fighting,
then split his skull with an axe - and did the same to Ricchar for
failing to come to his brother's aid. A third brother, Rignomer, was
killed on Clovis's orders at Le Mans. With every rival king of his own blood
dead, Clovis is said to have gathered his people and lamented, in mock
sorrow, that he had been left "a stranger among strangers" with no
kinsmen left to help him in adversity - a performance Gregory of Tours
states plainly was not grief but a ruse, meant to smoke out any relative
still alive whom Clovis had somehow missed.
