Clovis had married Clotilda, a Christian Burgundian princess, years
before he shared her faith, and her private urging that he abandon the
Frankish gods for her own had gone nowhere. What changed his mind,
according to Gregory of Tours, was a battle against the Alamanni near
Tolbiac in which Clovis's own army found itself losing ground. Hard
pressed, Clovis raised his eyes to heaven and prayed to "Jesus Christ,
whom Clotilda declares to be the son of the living God" for victory,
promising to be baptized in return - admitting, in the same breath, that
his own gods had failed him too often to trust any longer. The Alamanni
broke and fled while he was still speaking.

## A bishop's welcome, and a warning

Clovis returned home converted in practice before he was converted in
rite, and told Clotilda what had happened. Bishop Remigius of Reims
prepared an elaborate baptismal ceremony, and as Clovis stepped toward
the font the bishop is said to have greeted him with a line that stated
plainly what the conversion cost him: "Gently bend your neck, Sigamber;
worship what you burned, and burn what you worshipped." More than three
thousand of Clovis's own warriors were baptized alongside him. The choice
mattered beyond Clovis's own conscience: nearly every other Germanic king
ruling former Roman territory in the west held to Arian Christianity, a
creed the Gallo-Roman population and clergy under their rule regarded as
heretical, while Clovis's own conversion to the same Catholic faith his
new subjects already held gave him a religious legitimacy no other
barbarian king in Gaul or Spain could claim.

## A war fought as much for religion as for land

That legitimacy paid off within a decade. In 507 Clovis marched against
Alaric II, the Arian Visigothic king who ruled most of Aquitaine and
Spain, reportedly declaring that he found it intolerable that heretics
held so much of Gaul. The two armies met near Poitiers; Alaric was killed
in the fighting, and Clovis's own son Theuderic swept east to bring the
cities between the Visigothic and Burgundian borders under Frankish rule
while Clovis himself wintered at Bordeaux and took Alaric's treasury at
Toulouse. The emperor at Constantinople recognized the victory with the
honorary title of consul, which Clovis accepted in a ceremony at Tours
before moving his own court definitively to Paris - by then master of
nearly the whole of Gaul.
