Clovis inherited a Frankish kingdom centred on Tournai in 481, at sixteen,
from his father Childeric. Roman authority in northern Gaul had by then
shrunk to a rump under Syagrius, son of the general Aegidius, who held
Soissons in the old empire's name without any empire left behind him. In
the fifth year of his own reign, Clovis marched against Syagrius,
defeated his army in the field, and pursued him to King Alaric of the
Visigoths' own court at Toulouse when Syagrius fled there for refuge.

Clovis warned Alaric that shielding the fugitive meant war; Alaric,
Gregory of Tours records, was too frightened of Frankish retaliation to
refuse, and handed Syagrius back in chains. Clovis had him quietly
executed once the kingdom that had briefly outlived Rome in Gaul was
fully his own.

## A cup a soldier would not hand back

Clovis's own army, still following the old Frankish gods, plundered
church property freely as it went, and among the loot taken from one
church was a vase of striking size and beauty. When the church's bishop
asked for its return, Clovis promised the messenger he would try to
secure it as his own share once the spoils were divided at Soissons.
There, in front of the assembled warriors, Clovis asked for the vase over
and above his ordinary portion. Most of the men agreed at once - but one
soldier, resenting what looked like a king reaching past the rules of
fair division, swung his battle-axe into the vase and told Clovis he
would get nothing but his rightful lot. Clovis said nothing and handed
the damaged vase to the church's messenger anyway, but he did not forget
the insult.

## A debt collected a year later

At the following year's spring muster, where the whole army paraded its
weapons for inspection, Clovis stopped in front of the same soldier and
criticized the poor condition of his arms, then threw the man's own axe
to the ground. When the soldier bent to retrieve it, Clovis split his
skull with his own axe. "This," he said, "is what you did to the vase at
Soissons." No one in the army mistook the lesson: a king's authority over
his own warriors, not merely over the lands he had just conquered, was
now Clovis's to enforce as he saw fit.
