Pepin of Herstal's own descendants held the mayoralty of the palace
without serious challenge for two more generations. His son Charles
Martel governed without even bothering to fill the throne for several
years after one Merovingian king's death, and won lasting fame turning
back a Muslim army at Poitiers and another at the River Berre near
Narbonne, defending Frankish territory a king in name only could not
have defended himself. When Charles Martel died in 741, his own sons
Carloman and Pepin the Short divided the mayoralty between them, still
governing, for a time, under a Merovingian figurehead the family had
seen fit to install: Childeric III, raised to the throne in 743 after a
gap of several years with no king at all.

## An answer that settled the question in advance

Carloman withdrew to a monastery in Rome not long after, leaving Pepin
sole mayor of the entire Frankish realm - and, in practice, its sole
ruler in everything but title. In 751 Pepin sent an embassy to Pope
Zachary in Rome with a question whose answer he had every reason to
expect: whether it was better for the man who held royal power, or the
man who merely held the royal title, to be called king. Zachary's reply,
delivered before his own death the following year, endorsed exactly the
arrangement Pepin had asked him to endorse.

## The end of a dynasty, disposed of with scissors

Childeric III was deposed, his hair - the mark of Merovingian royal
blood the family had worn unshorn since Clovis's own day - was cut off,
and he was shut away in a monastery for the rest of his life. Pepin was
raised up as king in his place, the first of his own line to hold the
title as well as the power his father and grandfather had exercised
without it. Two and a half centuries after Clovis had eliminated every
rival king of his own blood to make himself sole ruler of the Franks,
the dynasty he founded ended not in battle or conquest but in a
monastery cell, disposed of by the same instrument - a pair of scissors
- his own family had once used to remove rivals from the succession
without shedding royal blood outright.
