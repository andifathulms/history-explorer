Clovis died at Paris in 511, thirty years into his reign, and was buried
in the church of the Apostles he and Clotilda had built together. Frankish
custom treated a king's realm the way it treated any other inheritance:
property to be divided among surviving sons rather than passed whole to
a single heir. Clovis left four - Theuderic, an older son by a
concubine, and Chlodomer, Childebert and Chlothar, his sons by Clotilda -
and the kingdom he had spent thirty years assembling by conquest and
murder was split between them into four separate courts, each with its
own share of cities, revenues and armies.

## Division as an opportunity, not merely a hazard

The four brothers did not treat their inheritance as a fixed settlement.
Chlodomer's own death in battle against the Burgundians left his young
sons in their grandmother Clotilda's care, and their uncles Childebert
and Chlothar wanted the boys' own share of the kingdom for themselves.
Childebert's envoy came to Clotilda carrying scissors and a bare sword,
asking whether she wished the boys shorn - stripped of the long hair that
marked royal blood and put aside from the throne - or killed outright.
Enraged and, by her own later grief, careless with her words, she said
she would rather see them dead than shorn if they were not to reign; the
envoy reported this as her considered wish, and Chlothar stabbed the
elder boy to death on the spot. When the younger begged Childebert for
his life, Childebert wavered and asked to spare him - only for Chlothar
to threaten him with the same fate for breaking their agreement, and
Childebert let the boy go to his brother's knife rather than die himself.
Only a third grandson, hidden by loyal men, escaped to become a monk of
his own choosing rather than a murdered rival. Across the following decades the brothers and
their own heirs fought each other as readily as they fought outside
enemies, campaigning against the Burgundians, the Ostrogoths and the
Thuringians and periodically reannexing each other's territory whenever
a childless death or a battlefield defeat made a share available.

## A single kingdom in name only

By the time Clovis's last surviving son, Chlothar I, briefly reunited the
whole Frankish realm under his own rule shortly before his own death in
561, the pattern had already set for everything that followed: the
kingdom of the Franks would be, in practice, however many separate
kingdoms its current generation of royal heirs numbered, reunited only by
chance, war, or the extinction of rival lines, and never permanently
settled into a single indivisible crown the way the old Roman provinces
it now covered once had been.
