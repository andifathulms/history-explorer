Brunhild outlived every rival of her own generation. Fredegund died in
597, Chilperic and Sigebert decades before her, and Brunhild continued to
govern first through her son Childebert II and then, after his own early
death, through her grandsons - ruling Austrasia and, for a time, Burgundy
as regent for kings too young to rule themselves, and making enemies of
the same Austrasian nobility whose support her regency depended on.

## A grandmother's own great-grandsons turn against her

By 613 that Austrasian nobility had had enough. Led by figures who had
once served her, they abandoned Brunhild's own great-grandsons and
invited Fredegund's son, Chlothar II - by then the sole ruler of
Neustria - to take Austrasia and Burgundy as well. Brunhild's own army
would not fight for her; she and her great-grandsons were betrayed and
handed over. Chlothar had two of the boys killed outright, sparing a
third, Merovech, out of an old attachment as the child's godfather, and
had him quietly placed under a trusted count's protection instead.

## A reckoning for ten kings

Brunhild herself was brought before Chlothar, who charged her to her
face with the deaths of ten Frankish kings and princes across four
decades of rivalry - a tally that included his own grandfather Chilperic
and, by extension if not by her own hand, most of the royal generation
her own feud with Fredegund had consumed. She was tortured for three days,
paraded through the army's ranks on a camel, and finally tied by her
hair, one arm and one leg to the tail of an unbroken horse, which tore
her to pieces at a runaway pace. With her death the rival branches that
had divided the Frankish inheritance since Chlothar I's own sons first
split it reunited under a single king for the first time in more than
two generations - the reunification that would let Chlothar's own son
Dagobert rule, briefly, a Frankish kingdom whole enough to be worth
calling one again.
