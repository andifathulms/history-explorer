By the 560s, the latest division of the Frankish inheritance had settled
into four courts under Chlothar I's own sons, and two of their queens
would dominate everything that followed for the rest of the century. Sigebert, king of
the eastern kingdom later called Austrasia, disdained his brothers'
practice of marrying their own slave women and instead sent to Spain for
Brunhild, a Visigothic princess who converted from Arian to Catholic
Christianity on her marriage. His half-brother Chilperic, ruling the
western kingdom of Neustria and already married in all but name to a
former servant, Fredegund, then asked for Brunhild's own elder sister
Galswintha, promising to set his other women aside for her sake. Within a
short time Chilperic tired of Galswintha's own complaints about his
continued devotion to Fredegund and had her strangled in her bed by a
slave, remarrying Fredegund within days. Brunhild never forgave her
sister's murder, and the rivalry between the two queens' courts hardened
into open war between their husbands' kingdoms.

## A warning a king chose not to hear

Sigebert had the better of the fighting by 575, and after allying with
his other brother Gunthram against Chilperic he found himself invited by
Neustria's own disaffected nobles to take Chilperic's throne as well as
his own. Bishop Germanus of Paris warned him plainly before he set out to
claim it: if he intended no violence against his own brother he would
return alive and victorious, but if his purpose was otherwise he would
die, since scripture itself promised that whoever dug a pit for his
brother would fall into it. Sigebert, Gregory of Tours writes, paid the
warning no heed.

## Raised on a shield, then struck down

At the village of Vitry, Neustria's own army gathered around Sigebert and
raised him on a shield as their king in Chilperic's place. Before the
acclamation could be completed, two of Fredegund's own men, armed with
poisoned double-edged knives and said to have been placed under a charm
for the purpose, approached him on some pretext and stabbed him from
both sides at once. Sigebert cried out and died within moments, his own
triumph over Chilperic complete only in the instant before Fredegund's
own agents ended it. Brunhild, left a widow with a young son too small to
rule alone, would spend the following decades enforcing her own regency
across two kingdoms rather than surrendering the war her husband's death
had not resolved.
