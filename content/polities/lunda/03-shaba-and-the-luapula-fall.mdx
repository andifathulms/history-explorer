Lunda's expansion did not begin, as older scholarship once assumed, with a
single dramatic wave of emigrants pushing outward from the Rund capital in
every direction at once. Later research has shown that the traditions
describing such a wave were themselves later additions, shaped by contact
between the Rund and the Imbangala traders of Angola rather than genuine
memory of the empire's earliest expansion. What actually happened was a
sequence of separate expeditions, spread out over a full century, each
aimed at a different direction and each producing its own distinct
subsidiary kingdom.

## A first thrust toward the salt pans

The earliest expedition, launched before 1700, moved south-east toward the
salt pans of the Lualaba river, near what is now the mining town of
Kolwezi. Around 1700 a further group broke off from this expedition
entirely, moving south under leaders named Musokantanda and Kanongesha to
found a new state among the Mbwela people of what is now Zambia - the
first proof that Lunda's method of absorbing conquered populations as
kin could work as well two hundred miles from the capital as it had at
home.

## A general's title becomes a kingdom

From the same salt-pan base, a further expedition pushed east toward the
copper deposits of Shaba and beyond them to the rich valley of the
Luapula river. A Luba force sent to stop the advance was beaten back, the
mineral-rich chiefs of Shaba - among them one recorded simply as Katanga -
were subjugated, and the existing Shila state on the Luapula was overrun
and absorbed. The Lunda general who led this eastern conquest carried the
title kazembe, ordinarily awarded to any sufficiently accomplished warrior
who had killed a dangerous opponent - and by the second half of the
eighteenth century, the kazembe of the Luapula commanded a kingdom large
and tightly organized enough to answer to nobody in practice but himself.

## An empire held together by force, not by roads

None of this expansion depended on formal administrative reach from the
capital, Mussumba, outward. What held the new eastern territories to the
imperial center was the same mechanism working everywhere else in the
empire - a conquering general absorbed into the Lunda kinship system,
owing nominal obedience to a "father" he had never met, backed by tribute
his own soldiers were prepared to extract by force whenever the
relationship needed reminding.
