Kilij Arslan I, Suleyman's son, had made Nicaea his capital as soon as
the sultanate held the city - close enough to Constantinople to watch
the empire directly, and secure enough behind its Roman walls to serve
as a real seat of government rather than a frontier camp. The First
Crusade, arriving on the plateau in 1097, ended that arrangement
inside a single summer.

## A siege the sultan lost by being elsewhere

Kilij Arslan was away campaigning against the Danishmendids when the
crusading armies of Godfrey of Bouillon, Bohemond and Raymond of
Toulouse invested Nicaea and divided its towers and walls between
their contingents for a coordinated assault. The Byzantine emperor
Alexios I Komnenos, whose own request for Western mercenaries had
set the whole crusade in motion, watched the siege from nearby
Pelekanon rather than join it outright - he judged, correctly, that
an army of that size could not actually take a fortress as strong as
Nicaea by force, and he had no intention of letting the city fall into
Frankish hands regardless of who did the fighting.

## A secret deal struck behind the crusaders' backs

Alexios sent his own trusted agent, Manuel Boutoumites, into the city
under cover of the siege to open secret negotiations with the Turkish
garrison, promising money, titles and safe passage to anyone who
surrendered to the Empire rather than to the Franks. When Kilij Arslan
sent word from the field that the garrison should "act for the future
in whatever way you think best" - his own admission that relief was
not coming - the defenders agreed. Alexios had light boats built,
carried overland on wagons, and launched onto the lake beside Nicaea
under Boutoumites's command, flying more imperial standards than the
force actually warranted to convince the garrison that a full Roman
army had arrived. On the agreed morning, while the crusaders attacked
the land walls in full force believing they were storming the city
themselves, Boutoumites was let in by the lake gate, raised the
imperial banners on the walls, and accepted Nicaea's surrender to
Byzantium before a single crusader set foot inside.

## A capital that moved because a city was lost

The crusaders, furious at discovering the city had already changed
hands, were allowed in only ten at a time to pray at its churches
before being moved on toward Antioch - Alexios's own arrangement,
managed carefully enough that Bohemond and the other leaders swore
their oaths of fealty to him regardless, tempted by further gifts.
For Kilij Arslan the loss was total: his capital, his treasury and his
own wife and sister, still inside the walls, were now in Byzantine
hands, and Nicaea would never again be a Seljuk seat of government.
The sultan and his court relocated to Konya, deep enough in the
Anatolian plateau to be safe from a repeat of the same maneuver - the
move that made an inland Cappadocian town, rather than a city on the
Sea of Marmara, the permanent capital of the Sultanate of Rum for the
rest of its existence.
