title: A branch that outlasted the trunk

After Manzikert in 1071 the Byzantine defence of Anatolia collapsed, and Turkmen
groups moved onto the plateau faster than any authority could organise them. The
sultanate that emerged was founded by Suleyman, son of Qutalmish — a member of a
Seljuk line that had lost a succession war against the main house, and had
therefore been pushed to the furthest frontier available.

That origin shaped everything. Rum was a Seljuk polity that the Great Seljuks did
not control and periodically fought, established in territory taken from an
empire rather than inherited from the caliphate, and governing a population that
was Greek and Armenian Christian for most of its existence. Its rulers took
Persian regnal names out of the *Shahnama* — Kayqubad, Kaykhusraw, Kaykawus —
which is a small thing that says a great deal about what a Turkish dynasty on a
Byzantine plateau thought it was.

It reached its height under Kayqubad I in the 1220s, taking the Black Sea and
Mediterranean ports and turning Anatolia into a transit route between them with
caravanserais a day's march apart. Then a Mongol army destroyed its field force
at Kose Dag in 1243, and the sultanate continued for sixty years as a tributary
of the Ilkhanate, its sultans installed and removed from Tabriz, until the line
was extinguished in 1308.

The Great Seljuk empire had ended in 1194. Its Anatolian offshoot lasted another
hundred and fourteen years, and the Turkish principalities that replaced it
included one, at Sogut, that became the Ottoman empire.
