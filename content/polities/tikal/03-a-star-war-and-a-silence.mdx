Tikal's power endured for generations after the entrada, closely
enough tied to distant courts that its own king, Wak Chan K'awiil,
sponsored the accession of a ruler of Caracol, far to the southeast,
in 553. That patronage collapsed within three years, a sign of a
larger shift already under way as a rival power in the Petén, the
Snake kingdom centered on Calakmul, drew Tikal's former clients
into its own orbit.

## Overrun by a stationary star

Tikal was overrun in a "star war" attack in 562, an assault timed,
in the Maya calendar, to a stationary point in the path of Venus.
The inscription naming the attacker is damaged, but the surrounding
evidence points to the Snake kingdom as the author of the defeat.
Tikal's mastery of the central Petén ended with it, and Wak Chan
K'awiil himself was never heard from again; a badly damaged
monument recording the episode was later found broken at its base
and dumped beside one of the city's main causeways.

## A hundred and thirty years without a stela

What followed is known to scholarship as Tikal's Hiatus: for 130
years, not one dated monument - not even a fragment of one - was
raised anywhere in the city. A ruler called Animal Skull took the
throne at some point after 562, known to us almost entirely from
painted ceramics rather than stone, and there is reason to doubt he
came from the existing royal line at all. The silence did not mean
the city stood empty or its politics stood still, but it marks, as
plainly as any inscription could, how completely the defeat of 562
had broken Tikal's ability, or its will, to commemorate its own
kings in stone.
