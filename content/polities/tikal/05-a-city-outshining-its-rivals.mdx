Jasaw Chan K'awiil I's tomb was already sealed inside Temple I when
his son Yik'in Chan K'awiil took the throne in 734, and it was this
son who brought Tikal's revival to full fruition, building a city
that outshone every rival around it. In the years that followed his
accession he constructed or remodeled most of Tikal's major
causeways, built Temple 6 and the East Market complex, and added
substantially to the Central Acropolis; most of the great palaces
ringing the city center were raised or renewed under his direction
as well.

## The tallest pyramid at Tikal

Yik'in Chan K'awiil matched that record as a builder with an equal
reputation as a warrior. His inaugural monument, raised in 736,
already showed a captive noble from Calakmul, and Calakmul itself saw
a change of rulership at almost exactly this point. His most
celebrated campaigns came in 743 and 744, recorded on carved wooden
lintels inside the sanctuary of Temple 4 - at 212 feet the tallest
pyramid at Tikal and the single largest construction anywhere in the
Maya world during the Late Classic period. In July 743 he attacked a
city allied to the kingdom of El Peru, defeating its ruler and
capturing the effigy of his patron god; just 191 days later he turned
east against Naranjo, seized its capital, and took the Naranjo king
prisoner, shown bound and prostrate on one of his victory monuments.

## Breaking an encirclement a century old

Together the two victories broke a hostile ring around Tikal that
had held for at least a hundred years, since both El Peru and Naranjo
had long stood among the city's most persistent enemies. It was the
clearest possible proof that the recovery Jasaw Chan K'awiil had
begun in 695 was no passing reversal: under his son, Tikal had gone
from a city defending its own survival to one imposing its will on
the kingdoms that had once encircled it.
