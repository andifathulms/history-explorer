title: A correction to another page
drafted_from: wiesehofer-1996

This site's Himyar record, in the Arabian region, describes a South Arabian
kingdom whose inscriptions abandon the old gods around 380 for a single unnamed
one, and whose last kings were explicitly Jewish. Its third chapter says: this
is the only Jewish state in it, and it is one of very few in the period between
antiquity and the twentieth century anywhere.

The first half of that sentence is now wrong, and this chapter is the correction
rather than a quiet edit — the Himyar page has been amended to point here.

The distinction that survives the correction is worth drawing carefully, because
the two cases are not the same thing.

Adiabene is a converted dynasty. A king and a queen mother became Jews in the
first century, their descendants remained Jews for at least two generations, and
the kingdom's public religion beyond the palace is not established. There is no
Adiabenian state Judaism visible in inscriptions, no evidence of a policy, and
no confessional foreign alignment except the family's own involvement in Judaea.

Himyar is a Jewish state in a stronger sense. The royal inscriptions change,
the old gods stop being named, the formulas invoke Rahmanan and refer to Israel
and the people of Israel, the change holds for a century and a half across
several reigns, and by the sixth century the confession is the axis of the
kingdom's foreign policy and the reason two Christian empires destroyed it.

So the amended claim is narrower and still substantial: Himyar is the only
polity in this corpus whose state religion was Judaism, and Adiabene is the only
other one with a Jewish ruling house. Two, in a corpus of a hundred and
seventy-odd polities across three thousand years — which is roughly the
proportion the historical record supports and is worth having both halves of.

There is a general point here about how this site fails. The Himyar sentence was
written when the corpus had no Adiabene page, and it was true of the corpus
rather than of the world — a claim about what this site contained, phrased as a
claim about history. That failure mode is available on every page that compares
itself to the rest of the corpus, and this record has just triggered it. The
answer is not to stop making the comparisons, which are most of what the site is
for, but to correct them in public when a new page breaks one.
