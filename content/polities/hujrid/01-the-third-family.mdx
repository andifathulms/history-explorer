This site's Jafnid record is called Jafnid rather than Ghassanid because Fisher's
book makes a distinction: Ghassan is a confederacy, the Jafnids are the family
Rome actually dealt with, and later Arabic tradition merged them so that a
family with a following became a nation with a royal house.

The book makes that distinction three times. Its subject is the Jafnids, the
Nasrids and the Hujrids — the ruling dynasties later tradition attached to the
confederacies called Ghassan, Lakhm and Kinda. This page is the third of them
and completes an argument the Jafnid page could only half make.

The pattern is the same in all three cases and the empires are different. Rome
paid the Jafnids to hold the Syrian steppe. Sasanian Iran paid the Nasrids at
al-Hira to hold the Euphrates frontier. And Himyar — the South Arabian kingdom
on the record two entries back on this site, at the height of its power in the
fifth century — paid the Hujrids to hold central Arabia.

That third relationship is the one usually left out, because Himyar is usually
left out. A reader who knows the Jafnids and the Nasrids as the Arab clients of
the two great empires has been given a picture with a third empire missing from
it: South Arabia in this period was a substantial state with an interest in the
tribes to its north, and it managed them the way Rome and Iran managed theirs.

The consequences for how these pages are coded are exact. The Jafnid record
carries `tribute` for Roman subsidy and this one carries `tribute` for Himyarite
subsidy. The Jafnid record carries `legitimation: null` because the vocabulary
has no value for a right conferred by a sovereign who is not a caliph, and this
one carries the same null for the same reason. Two client dynasties, two
empires, and the identical shape in the fields.
