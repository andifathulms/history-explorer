title: An empire that chose Manichaeism
drafted_from: golden-1992

The Uyghurs replaced the second Turk khaganate on the Mongolian steppe in 744 and
spent the next century as the Tang dynasty's indispensable and resented ally.
Uyghur cavalry helped put down the An Lushan rebellion; in exchange the khaganate
extracted a silk trade on terms the Chinese sources describe with open
bitterness, exchanging horses for silk at rates Chang'an could not refuse.

The remarkable thing is the religion. Around 762 Bogu Khagan adopted Manichaeism
— a dualist faith of Mesopotamian origin, persecuted in the Roman and Sasanian
empires and by then a minority tradition surviving largely among Sogdian
merchants — and made it the state cult of a steppe empire. It is the only state
in history to have done so. The Sogdian commercial network that ran the Silk Road
was the medium, and the khaganate's literate administration came with it.

The Kyrgyz destroyed the khaganate in 840. The Kyrgyz are not a polity on this
site and are not in its reference set, so the record's `ended.by` field is empty
rather than pointing at an id that resolves to nothing — the same rule that
governs citations applies to identifiers.

Uyghur groups migrating south-west into the Tarim basin afterwards founded the
Qocho kingdom, became settled and largely Buddhist, and supplied the Mongols with
the alphabet and much of the literate administration they used to run their
empire three centuries later.
