The remarkable thing about this khaganate is the religion. Around 762 Bogu Khagan adopted Manichaeism
— a dualist faith of Mesopotamian origin, persecuted in the Roman and Sasanian
empires and by then a minority tradition surviving largely among Sogdian
merchants — and made it the state cult of a steppe empire. It is the only state
in history to have done so. The Sogdian commercial network that ran the Silk Road
was the medium, and the khaganate's literate administration came with it.

<Figure id="ug-electae" />

Why a steppe ruler would do it is worth thinking about rather than filing as an
eccentricity. Manichaeism arrived with the Sogdian merchants who ran the
khaganate's commerce and staffed its chancery, and adopting it bound that class
to the throne. It was a literate religion with a scripture, which gave the court
a clerisy. And it belonged to nobody else's empire: it was not Chinese
Buddhism, not Tang state ritual, not the faith of any neighbour who could claim
authority over a convert.

That is the same calculation the Khazar khagans made on the Volga at almost
exactly the same time, and reached by two steppe states that had no contact with
each other. A power between larger religious empires does not want the religion
of either.

The cost was that it made the khaganate stranger to its subjects than to its
merchants. Manichaeism forbade meat and required a class of Elect who did no
work — an awkward proposition on the steppe — and it never took hold below the
court and the trading towns. When the khaganate fell, the religion went with
it everywhere except among the Uyghurs who had already stopped being nomads.
