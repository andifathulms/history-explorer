title: The alphabet the Mongols borrowed
drafted_from: golden-1992

The khaganate on the Mongolian steppe lasted ninety-six years. What followed it
mattered more.

When the Kyrgyz broke it in 840, Uyghur groups migrated south-west into the Tarim
basin and the Gansu corridor and founded settled kingdoms, principally Qocho.
There they stopped being nomads: irrigated agriculture, walled towns, a
manuscript culture in several religions — Manichaean, then increasingly Buddhist,
with Nestorian Christian communities alongside.

They also became the literate class that every subsequent steppe empire hired.
When Chinggis Khan needed an administration and a script, he took the Uyghur
alphabet — itself descended from Sogdian, and ultimately from Aramaic — and had
Mongolian written in it. Uyghur scribes staffed the Mongol chancery. The script
on this site's Mongol, Golden Horde and Chagatai pages is theirs.

That transmission is not counted on this page, and the reason is worth stating.
Under the coding rules a descendant script requires a source describing a
derivation or a new adaptation attributable to the polity. The adaptation of the
Uyghur alphabet to Mongolian happened two centuries after this khaganate ended,
under the Mongols, and is counted there. The rule is applied even though the
result understates the case, which is the same trade the Carolingian page
describes.
