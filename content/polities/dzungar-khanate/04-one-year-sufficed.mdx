The best short description of what Galdan had built was written by the man who
destroyed him.

## Kangxi's list

> Galdan was a formidable enemy. Samarkand, Bokhara, Pulut, Urghendj, Kashgar,
> Suirmen, Turfan, Khamil, were taken from the Muhammedans, and the capture of more
> than 1,200 towns prove to what a length he had carried his arms. The Khalkhas in
> vain assembled their seven Banners, numbering 100,000 men, to oppose him. One
> year sufficed for their dispersion.

It is an enemy's tally in a private letter, which is the kind of evidence worth
having; a Qing emperor had no reason to inflate the achievements of a rebel he had
just scattered to the winds.

## The Khalkha

The last sentence of it is the one that changed the map of Asia.

In 1688 Galdan crossed into eastern Mongolia — invited, as usual, by one faction of
the people he was about to conquer — and broke the Khalkha. Their seven banners could
not hold him. They abandoned the Orkhon and the Kerulen, the oldest political ground
in the Mongol world, and fled south across the Gobi.

To survive they submitted to the Manchus. That is how the whole of what is now
Mongolia came into the Qing empire: not by conquest, but as refugees asking for
protection from another Mongol.

And it put the Qing frontier and the Dzungar frontier against each other with nothing
in between.

## The state at its height

At its widest the khanate ran from the Irtysh and the Kazakh steppe to the edge of
the Gobi, and from the Siberian forest down over the Tian Shan into the Tarim. It
held the Kokonor Khoshuts as kin, Tibet as a patron and client at once, and the oasis
cities as tributaries.

It fought with firearms. Its ruler corresponded with Moscow and Beijing and Lhasa
simultaneously, complained to Kangxi that only two hundred of his subjects were
allowed to trade with the empire, and received Qing envoys sitting cross-legged on a
mat in the Tatar fashion.

Every element of it was assembled by a man who was not descended from Chinggis Khan
and could not have claimed a khanship on any traditional ground.

## Why it could not last

Because of the two things that made it.

The first was Tibet: a source of legitimacy that belonged to somebody else and could
be transferred. When the Qing eventually took Lhasa, the Dzungars lost the only title
they had.

The second was the Qing themselves. Galdan's own triumph over the Khalkha delivered a
hundred thousand Mongol horsemen to Beijing and abolished the buffer. Every campaign
after 1688 was fought against an empire that could put a hundred and fifty thousand
men across the Gobi, feed them by relay from north China, and do it again next year.

The Dzungars could beat any neighbour. They could not outlast an empire with a tax
base.
