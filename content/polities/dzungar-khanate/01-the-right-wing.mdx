The name is a direction. *Züüngar* — Sungar in the older books — means the left
wing in modern usage and the right wing in the older Mongol military vocabulary
Sanang Setsen uses, where it stands opposite *baraghon gar*. It was an
administrative position in somebody else's army, and it became the name of an
empire.

## Who they were not

The Dzungars were Oirats: western Mongols, of the Choros clan, the people European
writers call Kalmuks. They were not descendants of Chinggis Khan.

That single fact shaped everything. It was why Esen, the greatest Oirat of the
fifteenth century, could hold a Chinese emperor prisoner and still be killed within
three years of calling himself khan. It was why the Dzungar rulers who came after
him had to find their legitimacy somewhere other than in a genealogy — and did,
in Lhasa.

## The Irtysh

Kharakhula led them north of the Altai, where the Chinese called them the northern
Eleuths, and settled them on the Irtysh, subduing the petty princes he found there.
He survived until 1635, when his son Erdeni Baatur Khungtaiji succeeded him.

The Black Irtysh valley put them straight up against two things at once. The
Khalkha Mongols and their Altan Khans were to the east. To the north the Russians
had just broken the khanate of Sibir and had not yet settled what they held.

The Dzungars claimed the Barabinsk Tatars and their neighbours around Tara as
subjects from time immemorial, with a right to collect tribute from them, and went
in to assert it in 1606; a force of Tatars and Cossacks from Tobolsk, Tyumen,
Turinsk and Tara marched against them and failed to drive them off. The next year
some of them rode with the sons of the dispossessed Kuchum on a raid into the
Tobolsk and Tyumen districts. When Tara's deserters went over to them they came
back two hundred strong and wasted the neighbourhood, and told the voivode's envoys
that they knew nothing about any deserters.

## The salt

The most revealing of these early quarrels was about salt.

The people of the Tara district had always taken their salt from the steppes of the
Irtysh, and supplied the whole country from it as a monopoly. In 1610 and 1611 the
Kalmuks claimed the salt lakes and refused the Cossacks permission to work them.
There was a great scarcity; in 1611 a large body of armed Tatars assembled at Tara
determined to march and enforce their right.

That is a state, not a horde: a power asserting exclusive title to a resource on a
defined ground, against a rival state, and making the shortage bite three hundred
miles away.

## The shape of it

What Baatur Khungtaiji left his successors was a confederation of four divisions —
Choros, Dörbet, Khoit and Khoshut — under one over-khan, with the Ili valley and
Dzungaria at its centre, the Irtysh to the north, and the Torghuts who had gone west
to the Volga as relatives who had chosen differently.

Its neighbours were the Russian empire, the Qing empire, the Kazakhs and Tibet. It
is the last time a nomad power on the open steppe faced all of those at once and
held its own for a century.
