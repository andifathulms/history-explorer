Philip V is the dynasty's high point and he spent his reign losing. He held Thessaly
and garrisons in Greece, built a fleet, allied with Hannibal after Cannae, and
fought three wars against Rome; the last of them ended in 197 at Cynoscephalae
in Thessaly, in fog, on broken ground, when the two armies stumbled into each
other over a ridge.

The Macedonian right formed up and drove the Roman left back. The Macedonian
left was still coming up in column when the Roman right went through it, and an
unnamed tribune took twenty maniples out of the pursuit and hit the victorious
right wing in the rear. The phalanx, which cannot turn, was destroyed where it
stood.

## Polybius on why

The comparison of the two systems is one of the set pieces of ancient
historiography and Polybius wrote it as a Greek explaining a defeat to Greeks.
His argument is that the phalanx is invincible frontally on ground that suits it
and that ground that suits it is rare — that a formation sixteen deep with
five-metre pikes needs a level plain with no ditch, no watercourse, no rock and
no gap, and that no commander can guarantee one. The legion, in maniples with a
sword, can fight on anything and can be detached in pieces.

He is describing a technical difference and he is also describing the fate of
the kingdom.
Macedon's army was its adult male population. Rome's was a system that could
absorb Cannae and produce another consular army the following year. Philip lost
Greece, his fleet, his foreign policy and eight hundred talents at
Cynoscephalae, and kept his throne for twenty-nine more years because Rome had
not yet decided what to do with defeated kings.

## Perseus

His son tried again, which is the part that reads as either courage or
miscalculation depending on the historian. The Third Macedonian War ran from 171
to 168 and Perseus won a cavalry engagement early and could not convert it. At
Pydna the phalanx again broke on uneven ground, Aemilius Paullus said afterwards
that he had never seen anything more terrifying than its advance and had never
seen anything collapse faster, and the Macedonian army ceased to exist in about
an hour.

That is the military problem the kingdom could not solve, and it had been
visible for thirty years before anybody had to solve it. The phalanx was
unbeatable on the ground it was designed for, and Macedonia could not choose the
ground.
