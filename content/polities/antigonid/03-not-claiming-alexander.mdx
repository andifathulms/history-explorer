The Antigonids ruled Macedonia from Pella for a hundred and nine years, and did
not claim descent from Alexander, or from Philip II, or from the Argead house at
all.

They had no grounds to. Antigonus One-Eye was a Macedonian noble and one of
Philip's officers; his family's claim is to itself. This record's `legitimation`
field reads `descent` and the descent is from Antigonus and Demetrius, four and
three generations back, and nothing older.

That is worth dwelling on, because the expected thing would have been to invent
something. Every other dynasty on this site that finds itself holding a famous
predecessor's ground reaches for the predecessor: the Sasanians claimed the
Kayanids, the Ottomans took Kayser-i Rûm, Cyrus called himself king of Anshan in
a city he had just taken, and the Mithridatic dynasty on the record beside this
one claimed descent from Darius I *and* from Alexander, neither of which anyone
could check.

The people actually ruling Alexander's capital, with his tomb's contents in
another kingdom and his mother's grave a day's ride away, claimed nothing.

## What the influence counts do with that

This record's `successor_claims` is 0, which is a claim about who claimed *this*
dynasty and is unremarkable — nobody did.

The interesting field is on a different page. This corpus's Macedonian record —
Alexander's own, thirteen years long, filed under Iran before Islam because the
empire it made was the Achaemenid empire — has `successor_claims: null`, and
this record does not change that. The Antigonids are the strongest possible
demonstration of why the field is null rather than zero: they are the polity best
placed in the world to have made the claim, they were in possession of the
ground, and they declined.

A null there says nobody has opened a source and coded the axis. A zero would say
somebody looked and found nothing. Neither is quite what this page establishes,
which is that one specific dynasty that could have claimed did not — and coding
rule 1's unit is the claiming polity, so an absence of a claim by one of them is
prose and not a number.

## Why not

Errington's account suggests the answer is that it would not have helped.

Macedonian kingship was not a mystical office and the Macedonians were not a
population that needed convincing a king was descended from a god. What a
Macedonian king needed was to hold the frontier against the Illyrians, Thracians
and Gauls, to pay and lead the levy, and to not lose. Gonatas got the throne by
destroying a Gallic army and his descendants kept it by governing competently
until Rome arrived. The claim they made was the claim that was true, and it was
sufficient.

Set against the two records entered beside this one it is a striking contrast.
The Attalids, holding a kingdom founded on a stolen treasury, spent a century and
a half arguing in marble that they were the defenders of Greek Asia. The Pontic
dynasty, ruling a corner of Anatolia, claimed two of the largest lineages in
history at once. The dynasty that actually had Alexander's kingdom said it was
the family of Antigonus, and left it there.
