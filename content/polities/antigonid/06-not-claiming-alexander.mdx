The Antigonids ruled Macedonia from Pella for a hundred and nine years, and did
not claim descent from Alexander, or from Philip II, or from the Argead house at
all.

They had no grounds to. Antigonus One-Eye was a Macedonian noble and one of
Philip's officers; his family's claim is to itself. The descent they asserted was
from Antigonus and Demetrius, four and three generations back, and nothing
older.

That is worth dwelling on, because the expected thing would have been to invent
something. A dynasty that finds itself holding a famous predecessor's ground
almost always reaches for the predecessor: the Sasanians claimed the Kayanids,
the Ottomans took Kayser-i Rûm, Cyrus called himself king of Anshan in a city he
had just taken, and the kings of Pontus claimed descent from Darius I *and* from
Alexander, neither of which anyone could check.

The people actually ruling Alexander's capital, with his tomb's contents in
another kingdom and his mother's grave a day's ride away, claimed nothing.

## The silence, and what it is worth

Nobody claimed the Antigonids either, which is unremarkable — a dynasty put down
by Rome and legislated out of existence leaves nothing to claim.

What is remarkable is the other silence. Alexander's own house was claimed by
half the ancient world, and the people best placed on earth to claim it — in
possession of his capital, a day's ride from his mother's grave, ruling the
country he was born in — declined to.

The most plausible reason is that they could not have got away with it in
Macedonia. Everyone there knew who Antigonus had been. A Sasanian in Persia
could claim the Kayanids because the Kayanids were legendary and nobody could
check; an Antigonid in Pella claiming to be an Argead would have been claiming
something his own nobility knew to be false. The dynasties that invent an
ancestry are the ones far enough away from it, in time or in distance, that the
invention is safe. This one was standing in the room.

## Why not

Errington's account suggests the answer is that it would not have helped.

Macedonian kingship was not a mystical office and the Macedonians were not a
population that needed convincing a king was descended from a god. What a
Macedonian king needed was to hold the frontier against the Illyrians, Thracians
and Gauls, to pay and lead the levy, and to not lose. Gonatas got the throne by
destroying a Gallic army and his descendants kept it by governing competently
until Rome arrived. The claim they made was the claim that was true, and it was
sufficient.

Set against the two records entered beside this one it is a striking contrast.
The Attalids, holding a kingdom founded on a stolen treasury, spent a century and
a half arguing in marble that they were the defenders of Greek Asia. The Pontic
dynasty, ruling a corner of Anatolia, claimed two of the largest lineages in
history at once. The dynasty that actually had Alexander's kingdom said it was
the family of Antigonus, and left it there.
