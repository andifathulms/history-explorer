This region is called The Eastern Mediterranean after Alexander and its blurb
describes the countries Alexander took and did not keep. Macedon is neither. It
is the country he started from, and the one thing the Argeads had held before
any of it.

The record is filed here anyway and the reason is worth stating rather than
hiding in a data comment. This kingdom's entire history is the Aegean, the Greek
leagues, the Ptolemaic fleet and the wars with Rome that came through Illyria —
and one of the other two Successor kingdoms, Ptolemaic Egypt, is on this shelf.
Putting Macedon in a region of its own for the sake of a name would be tidier
and less useful. A reader who disagrees is disagreeing with a judgement rather
than with a fact, which is the condition this site tries to leave its judgements
in.

## Forty years of nobody

What happened to Macedon between Alexander's death in 323 and 277 is difficult
to summarise because it is mostly a list. Regents, generals and sons of generals
held it in succession; Cassander killed Alexander's mother, widow and son;
Demetrius the Besieger held it for seven years and lost it; two kings divided it
and one killed the other; and in 279 a Gallic invasion came down through the
Balkans, killed King Ptolemy Keraunos in battle and put his head on a spear.

Then in 277 Antigonus II Gonatas, Demetrius's son, destroyed a Gallic army near
Lysimacheia in the Chersonese and was accepted as king of Macedon on the
strength of it. He held it for thirty-eight years and his family held it for
another seventy.

That is where this record starts, and the founding fact is that the kingship was
won by beating an invasion rather than inherited. The Attalid record entering
this corpus alongside this one has the identical founding fact thirty-nine years
later and eight hundred kilometres away: Attalus I took the royal title after
beating the same people. In the third-century Aegean world, killing Galatians was
how you became a king.

## What it had

A population, and silver.

The population is the thing that separates this kingdom from every other
Hellenistic state and it is why this record's `military_basis` reads `conscript`
alone. The Seleucids and the Ptolemies fought with mercenaries and with military
settlers planted on granted land, because they ruled enormous populations that
were not theirs and could not be armed. Macedon fielded a phalanx of Macedonian
farmers who went home after the campaign. It is a national army in a period that
did not otherwise have one.

The consequence runs through everything that follows. Macedon could field one
army at a time and could not replace it quickly, and it spent a century facing
first the Greek leagues and then a Roman republic that had lost three armies to
Hannibal in as many years and raised more.

The silver is Pangaion and the Strymon country, mined since before Philip II,
along with the timber that every fleet in the Aegean needed and only Macedon had
in quantity. This record's `revenue_basis` reads `land-tax, mining` for that
reason, and the Roman senate's decision after Pydna to close the mines for a
decade rather than work them is the clearest statement anyone made of what they
were worth.
