The Burmese came twice. In 1569 Bayinnaung took the city and Ayutthaya spent
fifteen years as a vassal before Naresuan restored it; the city survived that one
substantially intact.

The second time, in April 1767 after a fourteen-month siege, a Konbaung army took
it, burned it, and carried off what was left of the population. The chronicles,
the law codes, the literature and the palace archive largely went with it, which
is why the history of a state that lasted four hundred years has to be
reconstructed from foreign accounts, from what survived in provincial copies,
and from later recensions made at Bangkok from memory.

`ended.by` is empty. The Konbaung dynasty has no page here.

## Two absences on one page

That is the second empty field on this record caused by a missing neighbour, and
they sit on opposite ends of the story.

`ended.by` cannot name the polity that destroyed this one. And
`successor_claims` names the Chakri dynasty at Bangkok, which rebuilt the city
plan on a new river bend, re-promulgated the law code, recovered what literature
could be recovered and put Ayutthaya's own name at the head of its new capital's
formal title — a claim as explicit as any on this site, made by a polity that
also has no page.

Rule 1 does not require the claimant to have a record here, which is the
position this corpus's Melaka record takes for Johor, so the count stands at one.
`ended.by` has no such latitude, because it is a list of ids.

The state re-formed within a year. Taksin held the country from Thonburi by 1768
and Bangkok was founded in 1782, fifteen years after the sack, which makes this
one of the shortest intervals on this site between a `conquest` and the
appearance of the thing that claims to continue it.
