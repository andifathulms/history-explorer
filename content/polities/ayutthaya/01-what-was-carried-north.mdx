Ayutthaya was founded in 1351 on an island where three rivers meet, about fifty
miles up the Chao Phraya from the Gulf. Eighty years later its army took Angkor,
and what it brought back was not treasure.

It brought the brahmans. The ritual specialists of the Khmer court, the
consecration rites, the Sanskritic royal titulature, the palace language, the
legal formulae — the working apparatus of Angkorian kingship went up the road to
Siam, and the Siamese court used it for four hundred years. The Bangkok dynasty
that followed was still using it in the twentieth century.

This is the only case on this site of a polity acquiring an entire court culture
by capturing the city that had it. The Angkor record carries it as
`successor_claims: 1`, with the item being this kingdom — coding rule 1 counts a
later polity asserting an earlier one's authority and does not ask whether the
two were on speaking terms. The edge `angkor → ayutthaya` is typed `conquered
by`, which is the event, and this page is what the event transferred.

## And the letters

Ayutthaya wrote in the Thai script, which is an adaptation of the Khmer script
made at Sukhothai in the late thirteenth century. So the titles taken from
Angkor in 1431 were written down in letters that had also come from Angkor, a
hundred and fifty years earlier and by a different route, through a Thai kingdom
this site does not carry.

The Angkor record's `descendant_scripts` count is one and it is that script.
Two of that record's three influence counts point at this page.
