The Burmese came twice. In 1569 Bayinnaung took the city and Ayutthaya spent
fifteen years as a vassal before Naresuan restored it; the city survived that one
substantially intact.

The second time, in April 1767 after a fourteen-month siege, a Konbaung army took
it, burned it, and carried off what was left of the population. The chronicles,
the law codes, the literature and the palace archive largely went with it, which
is why the history of a state that lasted four hundred years has to be
reconstructed from foreign accounts, from what survived in provincial copies,
and from later recensions made at Bangkok from memory.

## What replaced it, and how fast

The state re-formed almost immediately. Taksin held most of the country from
Thonburi by 1770, and Bangkok was founded in 1782 — fifteen years after the
sack.

<Figure id="ay-headtree" />

The Chakri dynasty that founded it made the continuity explicit. It rebuilt the
royal precinct on a new river bend to reproduce the old one, re-promulgated the
law code from what could be recovered, had the lost literature composed again by
commission, and put Ayutthaya's own name at the head of its new capital's formal
title, where it still is.

Fifteen years between the destruction of a capital and the founding of its
deliberate replacement is about as short an interval as this kind of thing
takes.
