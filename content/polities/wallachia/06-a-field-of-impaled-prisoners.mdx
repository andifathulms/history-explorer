Vlad's second reign spent its first years maneuvering rather than
fighting outright - a marriage alliance with Matthias Corvinus of
Hungary, a treaty concluded no later than 1460, and a running diplomatic
standoff with the Ottoman Porte over the tribute Wallachia still owed.
The standoff broke in late 1461, when Mehmed II, freshly returned from a
victorious campaign against Trebizond, summoned Vlad to present himself
at the Ottoman court with the overdue payment - and, by at least one
account, sent an escort meant to capture him under cover of the
invitation. Vlad, drawing on lessons learned at Eğrigöz, caught the
Ottoman commander sent to seize him instead, and impaled him.

## An offensive before the real invasion arrived

Rather than wait for retaliation, Vlad crossed the Danube himself, in a
campaign that burned Ottoman towns and fortifications on the southern
bank and, by his own account in a letter to Matthias Corvinus, cost the
Turks more than twenty thousand casualties. It bought time rather than
security. In the spring of 1462, Mehmed II assembled a genuinely large
invasion force - some sixty to eighty thousand soldiers, backed by a
fleet - aimed squarely at ending Wallachian independence rather than
merely punishing its ruler.

## A country that would not stand and fight

Vlad's own strategy after an initial defeat trying to contest the
Danube crossing was to avoid open battle entirely, retreating toward the
mountainous interior while destroying food and water supplies in the
invaders' path and harassing only detachments that strayed from the main
Ottoman force. The campaign's decisive moment came on the night of 17-18
June 1462, when Vlad launched a direct assault on the sultan's own camp,
aiming to kill Mehmed II outright. The attack failed to reach its target,
but the panic it caused, compounded days later when the Ottoman army
found a field of impaled prisoners near the approach to Târgoviște, left
a mark deep enough that, according to later Byzantine sources, Mehmed
himself concluded he could not take a country ruled by a man who
commanded his own subjects and his own cruelty so completely.

## A war nobody quite won, and a throne nobody quite kept

Mehmed withdrew from Wallachia within days of reaching Vlad's own field
of impaled captives, having failed to remove him from power by force -
but force was not the method that finally worked. Barely two months
later, Vlad's own brother Radu, escorted by a small Ottoman detachment
and backed by boyars who judged continued resistance a losing bet without
more reliable Hungarian support than Vlad had actually secured, took the
Wallachian throne without a battle. Vlad fled to Transylvania, and what
Mehmed's army could not accomplish in open war, negotiation and boyar
self-interest accomplished within a season.
