On about forty square kilometres of dry plain beside the Irrawaddy there are
around two thousand standing brick monuments, and the inscriptions and survey
work indicate that something over ten thousand were built. They went up in
roughly two hundred and fifty years, between Anawrahta's accession in 1044 and
the collapse in 1287.

Pagan is the first state to hold the Irrawaddy from the delta to the dry zone.
It is also, on Aung-Thwin's reading, a state that built itself to death.

## Anawrahta and Thaton

The Burmese chronicles give the founding story a clean shape. Anawrahta, having
united the dry zone, asked the Mon king of Thaton in the south for a copy of the
Pali scriptures, was refused, took the city in 1057, and brought back the king,
the monks, the craftsmen, the texts and the script. Theravada Buddhism and
literate administration arrive together, in a cart, in a named year.

Aung-Thwin has spent a career arguing that this did not happen in anything like
that form, and that the Mon contribution to Pagan was substantially constructed
by chroniclers writing centuries later. It is a genuinely contested position
and other specialists do not accept it.

The dispute does not erase 1057 as a hinge, because whatever happened that
year, Pagan writes Old Burmese in a script adapted from Mon afterwards and did
not before. Something came north. The argument is about how much, and about
who wrote the account of it.
