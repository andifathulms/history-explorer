A Yuan army came down from Yunnan. Narathihapate abandoned the capital, and was
killed by one of his own sons. The Irrawaddy broke into pieces held by Shan
chiefs and no single authority held it again for two and a half centuries.

The chronicles call him Tarokpyemin — the king who fled from the Chinese. It is
a verdict, delivered by the people who wrote the history, and this record does
not enter it in a field.

## `fragmentation`, not `conquest`

`ended.type` on this record is `fragmentation` and `ended.by` is empty. The Yuan
record exists on this site, sixteen years old in 1287 and the largest thing in
Asia, and it is deliberately not named here.

The reasoning is Aung-Thwin's and it follows from the previous chapter. On his
account Pagan was already fiscally insolvent when the Mongols arrived: the
taxable land was gone, the crown could not pay for or raise a force
proportionate to what it faced, and the invasion finished a state that had
stopped being able to defend itself rather than defeating one that could. The
Yuan did not annex Burma. They did not garrison it, they did not administer it,
and they left. What replaced Pagan was not Mongol rule but two hundred and fifty
years of nobody.

An `ended.by` of `[yuan]` would say that the Mongol empire took this kingdom,
and no part of what followed looks like that.

It is a contested reading, and putting it in a coded field rather than only in
prose is a real commitment. This site's rule is that where scholarship
disagrees, both readings are recorded and both marked contested — but that rule
governs edges and turning points, and `ended` takes one value. So the value
follows the source the record is drafted from, and this paragraph is here so
that a reader knows a choice was made and what the other option was.

## What the empty `by` is not

This corpus's African records developed a long argument about empty `ended.by`
fields, and it is worth marking the difference. There, nine of twelve are empty
because the polity on the other side has no page: Portugal, Britain, the Funj,
three colonial empires. The field is silent because the corpus is thin.

Here the polity on the other side has a page and is not named anyway. The field
is empty because the source says the ending was not delivered from outside — a
different kind of silence, and one this site does not otherwise have many
examples of.
