Portuguese raids, an Achinese captivity, and decades of Dutch
blockade had all failed to break Kedah outright. The Siamese
invasion of 1821 did what none of them had managed. Sultan Ahmad
Tajuddin Halim Shah, who had begun his reign at Kuala Kedah, was
forced to flee to Province Wellesley as Siamese forces occupied the
country and divided it into four separately administered parts -
Kedah proper, Setul, Perlis and Kubang Pasu, each placed under its
own ruler answering to Siam.

## Six years, a thirtieth of a population

The scale of the devastation that followed is recorded starkly: in
six years, Kedah's population fell from 180,000 to 6,000. The exiled
sultan spent years trying to recover his throne, at one point
sailing from Malacca toward Kedah to launch an attack, only to be
intercepted and taken to Penang by a British gunboat before he could
land. Not until 1843 was he permitted to return, restored to a
diminished Kedah under continuing Siamese protection, reigning from
Kota Kuala Muda because Alor Star, the old capital, had grown over
with forest in his absence.

## An afterword under two protectors

Kubang Pasu was eventually restored to Kedah's own governance, but
Perlis remained a separate state and Setul stayed part of Siamese
territory permanently. The arrangement held until 1909, when Siam
transferred its suzerainty over Kedah to Great Britain, which
administered it thereafter as one of the Unfederated Malay States -
a second protector following the first, neither of them the
independent sultanate that had once bargained with the Dutch East
India Company on something closer to equal terms.
