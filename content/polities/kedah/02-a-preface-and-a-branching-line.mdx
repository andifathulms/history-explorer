Sultan Muzaffar Shah's own line did not pass down a single unbroken
chain. The Kedah Annals record three sons succeeding him in turn -
Muazzam Shah, who reigned from Kota Palas; a Raja Muhamad Shah; and
a Sulaiman Shah who ruled at Langkapuri, the old name for Kedah
itself - before the throne narrowed back to a single line through a
later Muhammad Shah and, by tradition, a Mansur Shah, down to Mahmud
Shah and finally the Sultan Sulaiman Shah whom Aceh would carry into
captivity in 1619.

## A history written in a king's own reign

It was in the reign of Muazzam Shah, the second Muslim ruler of
Kedah, that the Annals themselves claim to have first been composed
- a claim that, if true even of the work's original core, would make
them one of the oldest Malay chronicles of any kind. Later hands
plainly reworked the text more than once: it carries interpolations
from as late as the seventeenth century, and its list of rulers was
extended long after any of these early kings had died.

## A kingdom overshadowed

These sultans reigned through the collapse of the Malay world's old
order. Sultan Mahmud Shah of Malacca, from whom an earlier Kedah
prince had once sought the royal regalia as a mark of Malacca's own
seniority among Malay courts, was driven from his capital by the
Portuguese conquest of 1511 and died in exile two years later.
Malacca's fall left no single Malay power in a position to protect a
smaller court like Kedah's, and by the time a Portuguese account of
1516 described the kingdom, it named Kedah plainly as tributary to
Siam - overshadowed, as it would remain for the following century,
by stronger neighbors on every side.
