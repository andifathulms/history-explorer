Long before it became a Muslim sultanate, Kedah was known to Arab
and Chinese traders alike as a mart for tin, its people Buddhist and
its politics shaped by the great powers around it - first Palembang
and the Chola kings, then the Javanese empire of Majapahit, all of
whom in turn claimed some suzerainty over it. Kedah's own dynastic
annals, the Hikayat Marong Mahawangsa, record seven pre-Muslim
rulers bearing Sanskrit and Siamese titles before the line's eighth
ruler became its first Muhammadan king, taking the style Sultan
Muzaffar Shah.

## A date fixed by two traditions

An Achinese account gives 1474 as the year of that conversion, and
the date is confirmed independently by a story in the Sejarah
Melayu, the Malay Annals, in which a Raja of Kedah travels to
Malacca to request the royal regalia from Sultan Mahmud Shah, the
last Malay ruler of Malacca before its fall to the Portuguese in
1511. Genealogically, Sultan Muzaffar Shah is always reckoned as the
great-great-great-grandfather of the Sultan Sulaiman Shah later
carried into captivity by Aceh in 1619 - a span of generations that
fits the 1474 date far better than the confused chronology of the
Annals themselves, which were repeatedly revised long after their
original composition.
