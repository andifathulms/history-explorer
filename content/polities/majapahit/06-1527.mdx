Majapahit has no fall either. What 1527 dates is the point at which Demak — a
Muslim sultanate on the north Java coast, founded by a family of the port
aristocracy — absorbed what was left of the inland Javanese court. There is no
siege anybody records, no treaty, and no last king whose death ends a line.

## The Islamisation nobody planned

The north Java ports became Muslim over the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries,
merchant community by merchant community, in the ordinary way of the Indian
Ocean — through traders from Gujarat, Bengal and the Arab world who settled,
married and stayed, and through Sufi teachers whom Javanese tradition remembers
as the *wali songo*, the nine saints.

There is no conquest in it. The earliest Muslim gravestones on Java are in the
port towns, and some of them are in Majapahit's own royal cemetery at Trowulan
— Muslims of the court, buried with Javanese dating and Islamic formulae, while
the king was still performing the old ceremonies a few hundred metres away.

What that produced was not a religious war. It was a coast with a different
religion from its interior, in a state whose whole economic structure depended
on the interior's rice reaching the coast's ships.

## Why the traditional date is 1478

Javanese tradition puts the fall of Majapahit at 1478 and attributes it to
Demak and to Islam, in a chronogram — *sirna ilang kertaning bumi*, vanished
and gone the prosperity of the earth — that encodes the year.

Neither part is right. In 1478 the capital was taken by a rival Javanese prince
who was Hindu. The dynasty went on for another fifty years. Demak's absorption
of the remnant is later, and quieter.

The date stuck because a court tradition written under Muslim sultans needed a
year at which the old order ended and the new one began, and because a
chronogram is easy to remember. It is a common enough manoeuvre: Ghana is
supposed to have been destroyed by an Almoravid conquest in 1076 that probably
never happened, and Meroë is still widely credited to Aksum on evidence that
will not carry it. Three kingdoms on three continents, and the same instinct to
date the end of each to whoever turned up next.
