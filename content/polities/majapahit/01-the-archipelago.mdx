title: The other thalassocracy

Majapahit was founded on Java in 1293 by a prince who had used a Mongol
expeditionary force to destroy his rival and then turned on the Mongols and
expelled them, which is a distinction almost nobody else on this site can claim.

Under Hayam Wuruk and his minister Gajah Mada in the fourteenth century it
claimed authority over most of the archipelago — Sumatra, Borneo, the eastern
islands, the Malay peninsula — through a mixture of naval force, marriage,
tribute and the same oath-bound arrangements with coastal chiefs that Srivijaya
had used four centuries earlier. The court poem *Nagarakertagama* lists the
dependencies, and how much of that list was administration and how much was
aspiration is a live scholarly question.

Its measures are empty here for the same reason Srivijaya's are. Power over sea
lanes does not convert into square kilometres of land under state control, and
this site will not compute an area from a map polygon and present it as a figure.
Two of the three polities in this corpus whose reach axis is structurally
inapplicable are in this region, which is itself information: the measures were
built from a literature about territorial empires.

Majapahit declined through the fifteenth century as Islam spread through the port
cities of the north Java coast and the trade shifted to Melaka. There is no
conquest to record.
