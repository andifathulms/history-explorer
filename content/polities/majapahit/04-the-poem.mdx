---
title: A list of dependencies
drafted_from: hall-2011
phase: aside

The *Nagarakertagama*, composed in 1365 by the court poet Prapanca, describes
Hayam Wuruk's realm and lists its dependencies — dozens of places across
Sumatra, Borneo, Sulawesi, the eastern islands and the Malay peninsula.

How to read that list is the central question of Majapahit historiography. It
could be an administrative record of a governed empire; it could be a claim of
overlordship over places that sent occasional tribute; it could be a poet
flattering a king with a catalogue of everywhere his ships had been. The
scholarly consensus sits nearer the middle: real influence over a network of
ports, exercised through tribute, marriage, force where necessary and prestige
where sufficient, and thinner the further from Java it went.

Indonesian nationalism later read it as the first unification of the
archipelago and made it a precedent for the modern state's boundaries — a use
the poem was not written for, six hundred years after it was written, by people
arguing about something else entirely.

The safest thing to say about the list is that it is evidence of reach rather
than of rule, that it was compiled by somebody with an interest in the reach
looking impressive, and that a poet cataloguing everywhere his king's ships had
been is not lying even when he is not describing an administration.
