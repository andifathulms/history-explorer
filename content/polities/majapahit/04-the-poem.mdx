title: A list of dependencies

The *Nagarakertagama*, composed in 1365 by the court poet Prapanca, describes
Hayam Wuruk's realm and lists its dependencies — dozens of places across Sumatra,
Borneo, Sulawesi, the eastern islands and the Malay peninsula.

How to read that list is the central question of Majapahit historiography. It
could be an administrative record of a governed empire; it could be a claim of
overlordship over places that sent occasional tribute; it could be a poet
flattering a king with a catalogue of everywhere his ships had been. The
scholarly consensus sits nearer the middle: real influence over a network of
ports, exercised through tribute, marriage, force where necessary and prestige
where sufficient, and thinner the further from Java it went.

Indonesian nationalism later read it as the first unification of the archipelago
and made it a precedent for the modern state's boundaries, which is a use the
poem was not written for. This site's coding rules exclude modern invocations
from the successor-claim count for exactly this reason, and the count here is
null.

The kingdom declined as Islam spread through the north Java ports and trade
shifted to Melaka, and the transition was gradual enough that this site records
`gradual absorption` with no agent. Its cultural inheritance did not stop:
Javanese court literature, gamelan, wayang and the Hindu-Buddhist tradition that
survives on Bali all run through it.
