title: The Mongols come and go

In 1289 Kublai Khan sent an envoy to Kertanegara of Singhasari on Java
demanding submission. Kertanegara had the man's face branded or his ear cut off
— the accounts differ on which — and sent him back.

The expedition that came in response was substantial: something like a thousand
ships out of Quanzhou, with twenty or thirty thousand men. It belongs with the
two attempts on Japan among the overseas failures of Kublai's reign.

By the time it arrived Kertanegara was dead, killed by a vassal named
Jayakatwang who had taken the throne.

## What Raden Wijaya did

Kertanegara's son-in-law, Raden Wijaya, had survived and was in the forest with
a few followers. He presented himself to the Mongol commanders as the rightful
heir of the ruler they had come to punish, offered submission, and asked for
help against the usurper.

They gave it. The combined force destroyed Jayakatwang and took his capital.
Wijaya then asked leave to go home and fetch the tribute and the princesses he
had promised, was allowed to go with a small escort, killed the escort, turned
on the Mongol army while it was dispersed and celebrating, and drove it back to
the ships.

It sailed in the same year it had arrived. Its commanders were punished on
return.

Majapahit is dated from that: a state founded in the space cleared by an
invasion it then expelled, by a man who had used the largest army in the world
as a temporary ally and not paid for it.

## What it was founded on

Not a new kingdom, formally. Wijaya's claim was descent by marriage from
Kertanegara, and he had his father-in-law commemorated as the dynasty's
founding ancestor.

That claim did a great deal of work. Singhasari had lasted seventy years, held
eastern Java, taken the strait from Sumatra in 1275, and insulted the Mongols;
its last king was dead at a vassal's hands and its capital was in that vassal's
hands too. By presenting himself as Singhasari's heir, Wijaya turned an
opportunistic alliance with a foreign invasion into a restoration — and then
made the restoration permanent by expelling the people who had helped him.

Everything Majapahit later claimed about itself runs back through that. The
dynasty descended from Kertanegara; the archipelago-wide overlordship it
asserted was Kertanegara's Sumatran expedition continued; and the poem that
lists its dependencies is arguing, among other things, that this was always one
line.
