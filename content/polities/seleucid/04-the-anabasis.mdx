Between 212 and 205 Antiochus III marched east to recover the provinces the
dynasty had lost, went as far as India, and came home having recovered almost
none of them. He returned with the title *the Great*, and it was not a joke.

## Bactria

The eastern campaign's decisive moment is a conversation, and Polybius is the
only source for it.

Antiochus had beaten Euthydemus in the field and shut him up in Bactra, and the
siege was going nowhere. So envoys went back and forth, and Euthydemus made an
argument in two parts.

The first part was legal. He was not a rebel: *he was not himself a revolted
subject, but had destroyed the descendants of some who had been such, and so had
obtained the kingdom of Bactria.* Whoever had seceded from the Seleucids, it was
not him — he had killed those people and taken their place, which in the logic of
the period made him a legitimate king rather than a usurper.

The second part was a threat about a third party:

> if he did not yield to this demand, neither of them would be safe: seeing that
> great hordes of Nomads were close at hand, who were a danger to both; and that
> if they admitted them into the country, it would certainly be utterly barbarised.

Two Greek kings fighting each other on the Oxus would open the frontier, and the
steppe would come through it.

## The terms

Antiochus, Polybius says, had been looking for a way out of the controversy for
some time, and accepted at once.

Euthydemus sent his son Demetrius to confirm the treaty. Antiochus received the
young man, judged from his appearance, his conversation and the dignity of his
manners that he was worthy of royal power — and promised him one of his own
daughters, and conceded the royal title to his father.

Then he provisioned his army, took Euthydemus's elephants, and left.

## What he actually got

He crossed the Hindu Kush into India, renewed the old friendship with the Indian
king Sophagasenus, took more elephants until he had a hundred and fifty, took
treasure, and came home through Arachosia, Drangiana and Carmania.

Set that beside what he had gone out to do. Bactria was not recovered; its king
was recognised, given a royal title and offered a Seleucid princess. India was
not recovered; its king was given a treaty. What came back west was a column of
elephants, a quantity of money, and a set of formal acknowledgements that the
Seleucid king was the senior party.

That is what an empire of this kind looked like at its height. Antiochus had
marched from the Mediterranean to the Punjab and back, and the settlement he
imposed was suzerainty rather than administration — the eastern kings kept their
kingdoms and conceded precedence.

## Why it was still worth doing

It bought thirty years. The east stayed quiet, the elephants and the money paid
for the wars in Syria and Asia Minor, and the title *the Great* was earned in the
one way available: by being the first Seleucid since the founder to have stood on
both edges of the inheritance.

And Euthydemus's warning turned out to be exactly right, on a delay. The two
Greek kingdoms did not destroy each other, and the nomads came anyway — sixty
years after this conversation, the Yuezhi crossed the Oxus and the kingdom of
Bactria ended.
