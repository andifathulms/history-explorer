---
title: The largest and most difficult share
drafted_from: chi-3
phase: formation
---

When Alexander's officers stopped ruling on anybody's behalf and called
themselves kings, Seleucus took the part nobody else wanted: Babylonia, and
behind it Iran, and behind that the satrapies reaching to the Hindu Kush.

He had been given Babylon as a satrapy in 321, lost it, and came back in 312
with about eight hundred men, and it is from that return that his dynasty would
afterwards count its years. Over the following decade he took the whole Iranian
plateau and the eastern provinces, marched to the Indus, met Chandragupta
Maurya, and made a bargain with him: the satrapies beyond the Hindu Kush for
five hundred war elephants. He used them at Ipsus in 301 to destroy
Antigonus, and that battle gave him Syria and made his the largest of the
successor kingdoms.

The structural problem was permanent and everything else follows from it. A
Macedonian and Greek ruling class of a few tens of thousands had to govern
Iranian, Mesopotamian, Syrian and Anatolian populations of many millions, across
a territory that took months to cross, with no historic claim to any of it.

Every other power that ever ruled Iran had an answer to the claim question. The
Achaemenids were Persians ruling from Persia. The Parthians and the Sasanians
after them both asserted descent from the Achaemenids. The Seleucids had
nothing: their founder was one of Alexander's officers, the land was somebody
else's, and the dynasty could not point at an ancestral homeland, because its
ancestral homeland was Macedon and Macedon belonged to a rival.

What they did about that is the next chapter.
