---
title: The largest and most difficult share
drafted_from: chi-3
phase: formation
---

When Alexander's officers stopped ruling on anybody's behalf and called
themselves kings, Seleucus took the part nobody else wanted: Babylonia, and
behind it Iran, and behind that the satrapies reaching to the Hindu Kush.

He had been given Babylon as a satrapy in 321, lost it, and came back in 312
with about eight hundred men — the recovery this record dates its era from. Over
the following decade he took the whole Iranian plateau and the eastern
provinces, marched to the Indus, met Chandragupta Maurya, and made the bargain
that is this page's second turning point: the satrapies beyond the Hindu Kush
for five hundred war elephants. He used them at Ipsus in 301 to destroy
Antigonus, and that battle gave him Syria and made his the largest of the
successor kingdoms.

The structural problem was permanent and is the subject of the whole page. A
Macedonian and Greek ruling class of a few tens of thousands had to govern
Iranian, Mesopotamian, Syrian and Anatolian populations of many millions, across
a territory that took months to cross, with no historic claim to any of it.

Every other polity in this corpus's region has an answer to the claim question.
The Achaemenids were Persians ruling from Persia. The Parthians and Sasanians
both asserted descent from them. The Seleucids had nothing: their founder was a
Macedonian officer, the land was somebody else's, and the dynasty could not
point at an ancestral homeland because its ancestral homeland was Macedon and
Macedon belonged to a rival.

What they did about that is the next chapter.
