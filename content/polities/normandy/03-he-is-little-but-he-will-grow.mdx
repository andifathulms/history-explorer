William was born at Falaise, the son of Duke Robert and Herleva, the
daughter of a tanner of that town; his parents had never married, so
he was known for most of his life as William the Bastard. Before
leaving on a pilgrimage to Jerusalem from which he never returned,
Duke Robert persuaded the leading men of Normandy to accept his only
son as their future duke, over the objections that the boy was
barely seven years old. "He is little," Robert is said to have told
them, "but he will grow." Robert died at Nicaea in 1035, on his way
home, and William became duke as a child with no father left to
protect him.

## Rebels, murder, and a fool's warning

The years that followed were violent. Normandy's nobles had never
wanted the tanner's grandson for their lord, and several kinsmen of
Duke Robert believed they had a far better claim to the duchy
themselves. More than one of William's guardians was murdered; the
land fell into confusion, and the king of France seized the
opportunity to take a border castle from his own weakened vassal. The
crisis came to a head in 1047, when the western half of the duchy -
Bayeux and the Cotentin, still more Danish in speech and temper than
the French-speaking east - rose against him under his own cousin Guy
of Burgundy. William was among the rebels at Valognes when they tried
to seize him; only a warning from his own fool in the night let him
escape and ride for his life to Falaise.

## Val-es-Dunes

Unsure he could put the rising down alone, William rode to Poissy and
asked King Henry of France for help. The French king came with an
army, and the combined forces of Henry and the loyal, French-speaking
east of Normandy met the Danish-speaking rebels of the west in
William's first pitched battle, at Val-es-Dunes near Caen. King and
duke both fought hand to hand; Henry himself was unhorsed by a spear
from a Cotentin man, a feat the peninsula's people commemorated
afterward in their own songs, but the rebels broke regardless.
William's victory settled, for good, that Normandy would take its
character from French Rouen rather than Danish Bayeux, and made him
master of his own duchy for the first time - nineteen years before
he would need to master a second one.
