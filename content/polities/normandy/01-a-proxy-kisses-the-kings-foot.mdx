By 911 the Viking leader Rollo had held the ruins of Rouen and the
surrounding country for years, through occupation and reoccupation
that no French king had managed to reverse. King Charles the Simple,
under pressure from his own nobles to make peace with an enemy they
had already tried and failed to dislodge, offered a settlement:
Rollo would receive the lands he already held, formally and for his
heirs forever, in exchange for baptism, an oath of fidelity, and
marriage to the king's own daughter Gisella.

Even the offer of land was contested before it was made. Charles's
first instinct was to grant Rollo nothing more than the wasted,
unstocked country immediately around Rouen - land nobody was
farming, offered as though it cost the crown nothing to give away.
Rollo's own Frankish sponsors, the archbishop Franco chief among
them, judged the offer insulting and pressed the king to be more
generous; the final grant covered the country the Northmen already
held in practice, recognized as theirs by right rather than by
sufferance.

## Not by God

What the negotiations could not soften was the ceremony itself.
Homage to a Frankish king traditionally required the vassal to kiss
the royal foot, and when the moment came, Rollo's refusal was blunt:
ne si by Got - "not so, by God." The Franks insisted on some form of
compliance, and Rollo, unwilling to bend himself, ordered one of his
own men to perform the act as his proxy. The soldier did not kneel so
much as reach down and yank the king's foot up to his own mouth,
pitching Charles backward onto the ground before the assembled court.
It was, by every account that survived to reach Dudo of Saint-Quentin
a century later, the opening note of a relationship between duchy and
crown that neither side would ever quite let go of being an insult.
