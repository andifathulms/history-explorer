title: The bendahara

The `peak` ruler on this record is Mansur Shah, who reigned from 1459 to 1477
and under whom Melaka's authority extended over most of the peninsula and the
Sumatran coast opposite. The *Malay Annals* treat his court as the model against
which everything later is measured, which is what a chronicle written in exile
after the fall does.

The figure the chronicle actually turns on is not the sultan. It is the
*bendahara* — the chief minister, an office held for most of the fifteenth
century by a single family, and the man the *Annals* show choosing between
claimants, restraining rulers, and in one celebrated episode being executed on a
false accusation by a sultan who then regretted it for the rest of his reign.

`succession_rule` on this record is null, and this is why. The sultanate was
hereditary; the decisive intervention in at least three successions was the
bendahara's. Nothing consulted describes that as a rule. Coding it `nomination`
would credit a system to what the sources record as an influence, and coding
rule 7 asks for the rule rather than the outcome — the same reasoning that
leaves this record's neighbour Singhasari null through four generations of
murder.

Two records on this site's archipelago pages, null in the same field for
opposite reasons: one because the sources describe chaos and one because they
describe a man. They are in different regions now — Singhasari on Java, this one
on the strait — and the pair is no better and no worse for it.
