title: A prince out of Palembang
drafted_from: brown-1970

Melaka is named on three other pages on this site before it has one of its own. It is the port whose fall in 1511 dates the arrival of European power in
maritime Asia; it is the polity through which Islam reached the eastern
archipelago; and it is the last of the states this corpus records on that
stretch of water, after Srivijaya and after Singhasari's brief hold on it.

The founder, in the *Sejarah Melayu* — the *Malay Annals*, the court chronicle
composed at Johor a century after the city fell — is a prince of Palembang named
Parameswara, driven out, moving up the peninsula, and stopping at a river mouth
where he saw a mouse-deer turn and face his hunting dogs. He took it as an omen
of a place where the weak stand against the strong, and named the settlement
after the tree he was standing under.

The chronicle then traces his line back through Palembang to the kings of
Srivijaya, and beyond them to Alexander the Great.

This site records the Srivijayan half of that as an edge — `srivijaya → melaka`,
`claimed legitimacy of`, marked contested — and does not record the other half at
all. The line is drawn where the claim stops being a claim about a polity in this
corpus and becomes a genealogy of the kind almost every dynasty in this corpus
produced. Coding rule 1 counts a later polity asserting the authority of an
earlier one. It does not count how far back the assertion goes.
