---
title: Iskandar Shah
drafted_from: reid-1988
phase: peak

Around 1414 the ruler of Melaka married a princess of Pasai in northern Sumatra,
converted, and took the name Iskandar Shah. The date and the identity of the
ruler are both disputed — the turning point on this record is marked contested
for that reason — but the direction is not.

What followed is this record's single entry under `religions_carried`, and it is
the largest claim on the page.

Islam had been present in the archipelago's ports for centuries; Pasai itself
was Muslim before Melaka. What Melaka did was make it the religion of the
region's dominant court, and then export it along the trade network it already
controlled. Melakan merchants carried it. Melakan princesses married into the
ruling houses of ports that then converted. Over the following century Islam
reached Java's north coast, Brunei, Sulu, the Moluccas and the southern
Philippines, and the sequence follows the shipping routes rather than any
frontier.

Coding rule 2 counts propagation beyond a prior confessional boundary, not
adoption. Melaka qualifies about as clearly as any record in this corpus does,
and it does so without a single campaign of conquest — a conversion by commerce,
which is a mechanism this site has recorded elsewhere only in fragments. This
corpus's Kilwa record's coast carried Islam the same way and did not carry it
nearly as far.

`descendant_scripts` is zero, and the near miss is worth naming. Malay was
written here in Jawi — the Arabic script with letters added for sounds Arabic
has no signs for — and Melaka made Jawi the standard written form of Malay
across the archipelago. Adapting a script to a new language is exactly what
coding rule 3 counts, but it counts on the record of the polity whose script was
adapted, and that is Arabic. The same reasoning gives Kilwa a zero for Swahili
in Arabic letters. What Melaka did was spread a script it had not derived, and
this site has no field for that.
