Afonso de Albuquerque took Melaka in August 1511 with about fifteen ships and
twelve hundred men.

He had come for it specifically. Portuguese strategy in Asia was to hold the
points at which the spice route passed through a narrows — Hormuz, Goa, Aden,
Melaka — and Melaka was the one that mattered most, because everything moving
between the Indian Ocean and the South China Sea went through the strait. Taking
it was supposed to give Portugal the trade.

It gave Portugal a customs house.

The trade did not stop and it did not stay. It went to Aceh in northern Sumatra,
to Johor at the tip of the peninsula, to Banten in western Java, to Brunei and to
Patani, of which only Aceh has a page on this site — and the Portuguese spent the
following century holding a fortified city on a strait whose commerce had
rerouted around them. What Melaka had sold was order, and order was the one
thing a garrison could not seize.

`ended.by` is empty. Portugal is not in this corpus, and the second record
here to end that way is the one that predicted this one. The Kilwa record's last
chapter calls it a Portuguese-shaped absence, and says it is going to keep
appearing on this site — at Hormuz, at Goa, on the Ya'ariba record, at Malacca —
until somebody writes the page. This is Malacca, and nobody has. The empty list
is the ordinary rendering rather than a gap: the coding rule is that `by` names
polities in this corpus, and the conqueror here is not one.

The dynasty was not extinguished either. Mahmud Shah withdrew up the peninsula
and his line re-established itself at Johor, which asserted direct continuity and
which commissioned the *Sejarah Melayu* — the chronicle this record's first
chapter draws on for the founding, written by the successor to prove it was one.
That is this record's single `successor_claim`, and coding rule 1 does not
require the claimant to have a page here.
