The *Malay Annals* put the chapter about the destruction of Melaka's chief
minister immediately before the chapter about Albuquerque, and the arrangement
is not an accident.

Sultan Mahmud Shah had a grudge against his bendahara, Sri Maharaja. The
bendahara had a daughter, Tun Fatimah, of famous beauty, and he had not shown
her to the sultan — which in that court was not a private matter but a refusal,
and Mahmud took it as one. The bendahara was also enormously rich, which the
chronicle notes in the same breath.

Then two merchants fell out. Naina Sura Diwana and Raja Mendaliar had a
lawsuit, and it was the bendahara who would hear it. One of them bribed the
bendahara. The other, learning of it, bribed the laksamana instead — not to
influence the case, but to tell the sultan that the bendahara was planning to
seize the throne.

Mahmud had been waiting for exactly this. He ordered the bendahara put to
death, and his brother the Sri Nara 'diraja with him.

## And then he found out

The chronicle does not spare him. Mahmud learned afterwards that the
laksamana's story had been false, was stricken with remorse, and turned on the
men who had told it.

None of which brought back the office. The bendahara had been the thing that
made Melakan succession work for most of a century — the man who chose between
claimants, restrained rulers, and held the court together across reigns. The
family that had supplied him was destroyed on the word of a bribed official in
a commercial dispute, by a sultan settling a private score.

## What was standing when the fleet came

Within a year or two, fifteen Portuguese ships stood off the city.

It is worth resisting the tidy version, in which a decadent court gets what is
coming to it — the Annals are a Johor court's account and have their own
reasons for showing Mahmud badly. But the sequence is what it is. A state whose
entire constitutional ballast sat in one office had removed that office, on a
lie, for a reason that had nothing to do with government, at the exact moment
when the most dangerous thing that had ever happened to it was sailing up the
strait.
