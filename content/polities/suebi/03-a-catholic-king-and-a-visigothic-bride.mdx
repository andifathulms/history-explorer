Rechiarius wasted no time proving he had inherited his father's
ambitions along with his throne. Within months of his accession he
was raiding the Basque lands, and soon afterward he secured a
marriage that placed the Suevi kingdom at the center of the
peninsula's power politics: he took as his wife the daughter of
Theodoric, the Visigothic king who ruled from Gaul. It was, on its
face, an alliance between equals - two Germanic royal houses joining
by marriage rather than by conquest - and Rechiarius treated it as
license for further plundering rather than as a reason for restraint.

## A son-in-law who kept raiding his father-in-law's neighbors

In the years that followed, Rechiarius campaigned with his new
father-in-law's own forces, plundering the region around
Caesaraugusta on his way back from a visit to Theodoric, and later
invaded the province of Tarraco with a large force, dragging captives
back to Galicia. None of this behavior suggested a king who recognized
any limit on Suevi expansion, marriage alliance or not - and it was
Tarraco, imperial territory the Visigoths themselves had an interest
in protecting, that finally forced the relationship to change.

## A father-in-law turned invader

Theodoric entered Spain with a large army, acting, the chronicle says,
"by the will and order of the emperor" Avitus - not as a favor to a
son-in-law but as an instrument of Roman policy against a Suevi
kingdom that had overreached. The alliance that had once linked the
two royal houses by marriage became, within a single campaigning
season, the mechanism of the Suevi kingdom's own near-destruction.
