The two armies met twelve miles from Astorga, at a river the
chronicle calls the Urbicus, on a Friday in early October 456.
Rechiarius met his father-in-law's much larger force with everything
he had, and the battle broke almost as soon as it began. His columns
were slaughtered or captured, and the king himself fled wounded,
barely escaping to the far reaches of Galicia. He did not stay free
for long: within weeks he was captured as a fugitive and brought
before Theodoric, and shortly afterward he was killed. The Suevi
forces that had survived the battle surrendered to the Visigoths in
turn, and a number of them were killed even after giving themselves
up.

## A verdict the chronicle would have to take back

Hydatius records the aftermath in the plainest possible terms: "the
kingdom of the Suevi was destroyed and put to an end." It reads, in
context, like the closing line of the whole story the chronicler had
been telling since 411 - a Germanic kingdom raised by conquest,
undone by the same kind of force that had built it.

## A kingdom that would not stay ended

The verdict did not hold. Within a year, a faction of Suevi who had
survived in the furthest reaches of Galicia elected a new king,
Maldras, and the chronicle's own subsequent entries are full of
Suevi raiding, factional killing among rival claimants, and Gothic
punitive expeditions sent to finish what Theodoric's own campaign had
supposedly already accomplished. Hydatius, writing from inside the
province and watching this unfold in real time, never revises his
earlier judgment in so many words - he simply keeps recording a
kingdom of the Suevi that, by every subsequent entry, plainly still
existed.
