Xiao Daocheng deposed the child emperor Shundi in 479 and took the
throne as Kaoti, founding the Southern Qi in the place of the Song
dynasty he had served. Contemporaries thought well enough of him
beforehand - Boulger quotes one court official's speech declaring
that no one could match Xiao Daocheng "both in merit and for services
rendered," and warning that anyone who thought otherwise should
expect to feel "the weight of my arm and the keenness of my sabre."
As emperor, Kaoti was remembered less for military achievement than
for his patronage of learning, and for one striking, unfulfilled
boast: that if he ruled the country for ten years, he would make gold
as common as earth. History, Boulger notes dryly, preserved no record
that he came close. A prolonged war with the northern Wei kingdom
ended in nothing better than a draw - Kaoti's generals defended the
towns of Chouyang and Juchang successfully, but Kaoti himself never
dared follow the retreating Wei army back into its own territory.

## The most respectable of the Qi

Kaoti's son Xiao Ze, who took the throne as Wuti on his father's
death in 482, presided over what Boulger calls, with evident
qualification, the most respectable reign of the whole dynasty - a
period when it was claimed, with some exaggeration, that China's
division among rival princes had produced rulers who thought only of
their subjects' welfare. The war with Wei flared again under Wuti and
the Qi held their own without ever clearly prevailing, a state of
affairs Boulger attributes less to Qi merit than to circumstance -
underscored by a public proclamation, made during this very reign,
that the dynasty held its throne "not by merit, but by force," and
that a rule built on that foundation could not last. Wuti's own most
substantial act of domestic legislation was reviving an old
prohibition on marriage between families sharing a surname, meant to
address the same falling population in districts still following
older kinship customs. It was a modest, careful reign - and, as the
proclamation itself predicted, the last one the dynasty would get.
