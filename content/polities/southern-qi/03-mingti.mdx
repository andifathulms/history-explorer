Wuti's death opened the same violent pattern that had already doomed
the Song. His infant grandson Xiao Zhao held the throne only briefly
before Xiao Luan, brother of the dynasty's founder, murdered him and
seized power outright, taking the name Mingti.

Mingti's own reign brought, in Boulger's judgment, no real ability to
match the brutality of his rise - the Qi still could not match their
northern rival Wei in either power or policy, and Mingti's last years
saw a revival of the same violent methods that had carried him to the
throne in the first place. His single most consequential act was the
assassination of the general Xiao Yi, the man who had done more than
anyone to defend the state against Wei - an act Boulger identifies
directly as the one that "brought about eventually the fall and
extirpation" of the entire ruling family, since it left Xiao Yi's own
brother, Xiao Yan, with a grievance and the military standing to act
on it. Mingti died in 498 after five years on the throne, leaving that
grievance unresolved.
