The last Mirdasid years are a decline with no battle in them. Mahmud died in
1075; his sons Nasr and Sabiq held Aleppo in turn and fought each other for it,
while Turkmen bands took the countryside and the city's food supply, and the
ahdath and the notables watched their emirs fail to protect either.

In 1080 the Aleppans opened negotiations with somebody else. Sharaf al-Dawla
Muslim ibn Quraysh, the Uqaylid lord of Mosul and the largest Arab power left in
the Jazira, was invited to take the city on the argument that he could keep the
Turkmen out. Sabiq ibn Mahmud gave it up. There was no siege and no massacre,
and the Mirdasid emirate ended in a negotiation between a city and a stranger
about who would defend it.

That ending is why this site codes it `dynastic replacement` rather than
conquest, and it is also the honest verdict on the polity. The Mirdasids held
Aleppo for fifty-five years without ever making themselves necessary to it. They
were a tribal confederation that took a city, drew its revenue, quarrelled over
it in public and never built anything inside the walls that would have made
their removal expensive.

Muslim ibn Quraysh kept Aleppo for five years and was killed by the Seljuks in
1085. The Kilab were still there, still raising horsemen, still selling them.
