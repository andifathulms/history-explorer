Al-Masudi tells one story about the Khazars at length, and it is the story of
how the arrangement broke.

## The bargain at the mouth of the river

Some time after the year 300 of the Hijra, about five hundred Rus ships, each
carrying a hundred men, came down to the channel that connects the Black Sea
with the Caspian.

At that channel the Khazars kept a strongly entrenched post whose whole job was
to stop anyone coming through from either direction. The Rus sent to the king
and offered him a share of whatever they took. He agreed and let them past.

They spent several months on the Caspian, and al-Masudi is plain about what
that meant: they raided the coasts, killed and drowned thousands of Muslims,
and nobody dared put to sea against them, so the very populous shores simply
fortified themselves and waited. Then, glutted and tired of it, the Rus came
back to the river mouth and sent the king his agreed portion of the spoil.

He took it.

Al-Masudi adds, in passing, the detail that explains why the Khazars had to
bargain rather than fight: *this prince possesses no ships, and his subjects are
not accustomed to navigation.* A state on the Volga and the Caspian with no
fleet.

## What the guard did

Then the Larisiyya and the other Muslims of the country heard what had
happened, and went to the king.

> Let us take vengeance on this people that has ravaged the country of our
> brothers, shed their blood and dragged their women and children into slavery.

He could not restrain them. So he did the only thing left: he sent word to the
Rus that they were about to be attacked.

The Muslims came down the river and met them. The Rus were put to the sword or
drowned. And, al-Masudi says, since the news of that disaster they have never
repeated the attempt.

## Read it twice

The first reading is that the Khazar system worked. A confessional guard with a
written exemption acted on it, the king honoured the exemption, warned the party
he had contracted with, and stood back.

The second reading is that the state had stopped being able to decide anything.
Its king took a cut of a raid on his neighbour's subjects, could not prevent his
own army from avenging them, and ended by tipping off the raiders against his
own troops. Every party in that paragraph is doing something reasonable and the
sovereign is not governing.

## The Rus came back

They came back overland. Around 965 to 969 the Rus under Sviatoslav of Kiev
took Sarkel on the Don and then Atil itself, and the khaganate stops appearing
in the sources as a power.

There is no long decline recorded and no great battle described. A state whose
product was a guaranteed crossing, in a corridor where the crossing had become
contested by people with better boats and no interest in guaranteeing anything,
simply stopped being purchased from.

Ibn Hawqal, passing through afterwards, describes the place as wrecked. What
happened to the population is not recorded anywhere reliable, which has left a
vacuum that two centuries of speculation have been poured into.
