For eight years the ruler of Byzantium was, by marriage, the Khazar
khagan's own brother-in-law - and the chronicle that records it in the
most detail never once gives the khagan a name of his own, calling him
only "the Khagan" the way Constantinople recorded every one of them.
It is a fair measure of how this khaganate's early decades survive at
all: in the margins of somebody else's history, about a somebody else
who mattered enough to be dated precisely.

## An emperor with no throne

Justinian II had already lost the Byzantine throne once, mutilated and
exiled to Cherson, when the city's own property-owners turned on him
too and he had to flee again. He crossed to the Khazar khagan's own
territory around 704 and asked for an audience. The khagan received him
with honor and gave him his own sister in marriage - renamed Theodora
for the occasion - and set him up at Phanagoria on the Sea of Azov,
exactly where a deposed emperor with Constantinopolitan enemies could
be watched.

## A price on a guest's head

It worked for a while. Then the reigning Byzantine emperor, Apsimaros,
offered the khagan a substantial reward to have Justinian killed or
handed over - alive if possible, his head if not - and the khagan
agreed, quietly assigning two of his own officials to do it. Word
reached Justinian through one of the khagan's own household slaves,
who told Theodora, who told her husband. Justinian summoned each
assassin separately to his own residence and strangled both with a
cord before either could act, sent his pregnant wife back to her
brother's court for her own safety, and fled by ship - through a
storm his own companions thought would drown them all - to the mouth
of the Danube and the court of the Bulgar ruler Tervel.

## The throne, twice over

Tervel gave Justinian an army; Justinian retook Constantinople within
the year, largely without a battle, and resumed the throne he had lost
a decade before. He then sent ships to bring Theodora and their
newborn son back from Khazaria - so many that a significant number
sank in the crossing, prompting the khagan's own dry rebuke, preserved
in the chronicle word for word: that it would have been simpler to
send two or three ships for his wife than to lose a fleet trying to
take her by force. Theodora and the boy, Tiberius, were duly returned,
crowned, and made to rule alongside Justinian as empress and
co-emperor.

## What the marriage was actually worth

The alliance did not survive Justinian's own second reign. He grew
suspicious enough of Khazar intentions in the Crimea that Byzantine and
Khazar forces later fought each other directly over Cherson, and by
711 the same khaganate that had sheltered him was backing the
rebellion that finally killed him. What the episode actually shows,
independent of which side eventually won, is a Khazar court operating
as a fully equal player in Byzantine dynastic politics eighty years
before al-Masudi ever described its capital - powerful enough to
shelter a deposed emperor, be bribed against him, be out-maneuvered by
him, and still end up related to the throne of Constantinople by blood
through his son.
