---
title: A general who would not survive his defeat
drafted_from: rowe-1948
phase: expansion
---

Seven rulers succeeded Ñancen-pinco in a line the sources give only in
outline, expanding and consolidating the kingdom his conquests had
established until it reached its greatest extent under Minchançaman, the
king who would ultimately face the Inca invasion. Northward, Chimor's own
power ran to the edge of the desert coast, absorbing the wealthy valley
of Lambayeque so thoroughly that it had already had three successive
Chimú governors before the crisis with Cuzco began. In the mountains
behind the coast, Chimor maintained a working alliance with the smaller
but still substantial state of Cajamarca.

## A defeat a general chose not to survive

Southward expansion proved harder. A Chimú general named Querrutumi led
an attack on the powerful valley of Lima, centred on the city of
Maranga, and won three victories in the campaign before finally being
defeated. Rather than return home in failure, he killed himself at a
hill between Chimor and Chicama. Chimú cultural influence, archaeology
suggests, still reached much further south than Minchançaman's own
political control ever did - traces of Chimú pottery appear as far
down the coast as Nazca, well beyond any valley the kings of Chimor
ever actually governed.

## A kingdom racing another kingdom it had not yet met

Chimor was not the only Andean kingdom taking shape in this period;
Chincha, Chucuito and, in the highlands to the south, Cuzco itself were
all consolidating around the same time. It is a reasonable guess, though
no source states it outright, that Minchançaman's own rapid expansion
in his final years was driven by news of Pachacuti's rise after the
defeat of the Chanka around 1438 - two rising powers each trying to
reach rough parity with the other before the day arrived when their
own borders would finally meet.
