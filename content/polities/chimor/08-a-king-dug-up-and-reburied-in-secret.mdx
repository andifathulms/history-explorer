Taycanamo's line outlived the Inca conquest of Chimor by generations
and outlived the Spanish conquest of the Inca by generations more.
Chimú lords baptized under Christian names continued to govern
fragments of the old kingdom into the colonial period, among them a
cacique known as Don Martin - a man who, whatever forms of Christianity
he adopted to satisfy his own new masters, kept the old religion alive
in private, as it seems many of his own subjects did as well.

## A body that would not stay in a Christian grave

When Don Martin died, he was buried according to Spanish custom in the
church of Santa Ana at Trujillo - but his rest there lasted only a
single night. His own people dug him up in secret and carried him away
to be buried again by the rites of his ancestors, and the Spanish
authorities were never able to discover where he had actually been
laid to rest.

## A line still being counted a century after the conquest

Six Christian caciques followed Don Martin in turn, down to 1604, when
a descendant named Don Antonio Chayguar was still carrying the ancient
line forward - the same year the Spanish chronicle that preserves
Taycanamo's own story was itself being written. The written record ends
there, but nothing suggests Don Antonio was the last of his own house;
it remains entirely possible that families descended from the kings of
Chimor still lived in the valley of Trujillo long after any written
account thought to keep counting them, the kingdom itself gone but the
line that had once ruled it never quite extinguished.
