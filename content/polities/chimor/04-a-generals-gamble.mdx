The war that would end Chimor's independence began by accident. Around
1461, the Inca ruler Pachacuti sent his own half-brother, General Capac
Yupanqui, on what was meant to be a limited raiding and reconnaissance
expedition into central Peru, with orders to turn back at a river called
the Yanamayo. Near that boundary, a contingent of Chanka troops
serving under him deserted after a jealous quarrel and fled northeast
into the mountains; Capac Yupanqui's own pursuit carried him across the
forbidden river before the deserters finally escaped for good.

## A choice between disgrace and a bigger gamble

Returning to Cuzco to report meant facing charges for letting the
deserters go and for crossing the river without leave. Capac Yupanqui,
weighing his own poor options, chose instead to press on: the wealthy
state of Cajamarca lay within reach to the north, and a great victory
there might redeem an otherwise disastrous expedition. He turned his
army north.

## Two empires drawn into a war neither wanted yet

Cajamarca's own king, Cusmanco, appealed to his ally Minchançaman for
help against the sudden threat, and the Chimú king sent a detachment
under a prince of his own house to support him - very possibly because
he had already read the Inca advance as the opening move of a conflict
he expected sooner or later. The fighting at Cajamarca was fierce, and
Chimor's own soldiers fought well in it, but Capac Yupanqui's raiders
won in the end: Cusmanco was killed, Cajamarca taken, and a Chimú
prince's own attempt to prevent exactly this outcome had instead
delivered his king's kingdom into direct confrontation with Cuzco.
