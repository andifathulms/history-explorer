What Mahmud had asked for was a sentence. No tribute, no garrison, no
territory, no change of administration. The *khutba* is where a polity states
publicly whose authority it holds under. Tahir had omitted the caliph's name at
Merv in 822; the Saffarids and the Samanids spent two decades competing over
whose name Baghdad would authorise. It is the cheapest possible demand and the
most total one, and it was conceded.

The army decided that the concession was the offence. On Wednesday 20 March
1017, at Hazarasp, the Khwarazmian forces turned against their own sovereign
under the chief chamberlain Alptigin of Bukhara — no relation to the Alptigin
who had walked off with Ghazna fifty-five years earlier, though the repetition
of the name is worth a moment. The vizier and several of Ma'mun's adherents were
killed and the rest ran. The Khwarazmshah shut himself in his castle; the rebels
set fire to it and killed him while it burned. They put his seventeen-year-old
nephew on the throne and ran the country themselves, plundering the rich and
settling private scores, with a vizier of their own choosing.

Mahmud then had what he needed, which was not a khutba but a pretext: the
murder of his sister's husband. The Ghaznavid army came north, beat the
Khwarazmian forces, took Gurganj, enslaved a large part of the population,
executed the commanders, and annexed the delta. The nephew lasted a few months.

And the scholars were taken to Ghazna. Al-Biruni went, and spent the rest of
his life in Ghaznavid service, and wrote the book about India there. Abu Sahl
al-Masihi died on the road. Ibn Sina refused, left before the army arrived, and
spent the next two decades moving between the courts of western Iran — Rayy,
Hamadan, and finally Isfahan under the Kakuyids — writing the *Canon* and the
*Book of Healing* while working as a vizier and, more than once, in hiding.

That is what an annexation looks like at the scale of individuals. The campaigns
and the square miles are the part that gets counted; the part that mattered most
about Khwarazm in 1017 was six or seven men and where each of them went next.
