In 1014 Mahmud of Ghazna sent word to his brother-in-law that his own name
should be pronounced in the Friday sermon at Gurganj.

Nothing else was asked for. No tribute, no garrison, no territory, no change of
administration. The *khutba* is where a polity states publicly whose authority
it holds under, and this site has recorded the same act twice already in the
other direction: Tahir omitting the caliph's name at Merv in 822, and the
Saffarids and Samanids competing for whose name Baghdad would authorise. It is
the cheapest possible demand and the most total one.

Ma'mun II temporised. He was married to Mahmud's sister, he could see the size
of the Ghaznavid army, and he appears to have intended to agree in some form
that would not look like agreement. His own commanders and notables would not
have it. In March 1017 the army, under a commander named Alptigin — not the
Alptigin of this site's Ghaznavid page, who had taken Ghazna fifty-five years
earlier, but the coincidence is worth noticing in a corpus where the same
sentence keeps recurring — killed him.

Mahmud then had what he needed, which was not a khutba but a pretext: the
murder of his sister's husband. The Ghaznavid army came north, beat the
Khwarazmian forces, took Gurganj, enslaved a large part of the population,
executed the commanders, and annexed the delta. The nephew who had been raised
to the throne after the murder lasted a few months.

And the scholars were taken to Ghazna. Al-Biruni went, and spent the rest of
his life in Ghaznavid service, and wrote the book about India there. Abu Sahl
al-Masihi died on the road. Ibn Sina refused, left before the army arrived, and
spent the next two decades moving between the courts of western Iran — Rayy,
Hamadan, and finally Isfahan under the Kakuyids — writing the *Canon* and the
*Book of Healing* while working as a vizier and, more than once, in hiding.

That is what an annexation looks like at the scale of individuals, and it is
the reason this twenty-two-year polity has a page. The Ghaznavid page counts
the Punjab, the reach figure and the campaigns. This is the other thing Mahmud
acquired in 1017, and the site can only record it in prose.
