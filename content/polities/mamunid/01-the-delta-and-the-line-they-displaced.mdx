---
title: The delta, and the line they displaced
drafted_from: chi-4
phase: formation
---

Khwarazm is a delta. The Oxus, having run fifteen hundred miles from the
Pamirs, spreads out before it reaches the Aral Sea into a fan of irrigated land
surrounded on every side by desert — a green island a couple of hundred
kilometres across with nothing around it for a week's travel in any direction.

That geography produced two things everything else here depends on. It made the
province
rich for its size, because irrigated alluvium is the most productive land there
is and Khwarazm had been farming it with a maintained canal system since
antiquity. And it made the province separate: distinct enough to have kept its
own Iranian language, its own script, its own calendar and its own dynasty
through the Achaemenids, the Sasanians and three centuries of Islam.

That dynasty was the Afrighids, and they had held the title Khwarazmshah since
about the fourth century. The Ma'munids displaced them in 995 — a rival line
from Gurganj on the western side of the delta, which took the eastern capital
at Kath and with it the title.

Two things follow.

The first is the title. *Khwarazmshah* is not an Islamic office. It predates
Islam by six hundred years, it was not in anybody's gift, and the Ma'munids took
it because holding it was what made a ruler of the delta legitimate to the
people of the delta. Seventy years later the same title would be handed out by a
Seljuk sultan to a household slave, which is what had become of it.

The second is what the displacement destroyed. The last Afrighid was
overthrown in 995, and al-Biruni — who was born in the old capital and was
about twenty at the time — wrote afterwards that the conqueror had the
Khwarazmian written records destroyed and the scholars who knew the old script
killed or dispersed. He says this in a book about calendars, in the course of
explaining why he cannot reconstruct the Khwarazmian one properly. Whether the
destruction was as complete as he says is not checkable. What is on the record
is that the man best placed to know said the evidence had been deliberately
removed, and that he was describing the founding of the dynasty that then
employed him.
