---
title: Fifteen years of an empire
drafted_from: hovannisian-1997
phase: peak
---

Tigranes II came to the Armenian throne in 95 BC, having spent his youth as a
hostage of the Parthians and having bought his release with seventy valleys. He
then spent about twenty-five years assembling the largest state ever governed
from the Armenian highland.

The circumstances made it possible: the Seleucid empire was in terminal collapse
and Parthia was in a succession crisis, so the two powers that ordinarily
bounded Armenia both stopped functioning at once. Tigranes took Sophene, then
Media Atropatene, then Mesopotamia, then Syria — where the Antiochenes invited
him in, and where he ruled for fourteen years as the successor to the Seleucids
— and reached the Mediterranean. He took the title king of kings, which is the
Iranian imperial style and which this corpus records Palmyra's Odaenathus
claiming three centuries later for the same reason.

He built a capital, Tigranocerta, and populated it the way Hellenistic
sovereigns did: by deporting the inhabitants of Greek cities into it.

Then Rome arrived. Tigranes had sheltered his father-in-law Mithridates of
Pontus, which gave Lucullus a reason; in 69 BC the Roman army destroyed the
Armenian field force outside Tigranocerta, and the deported Greeks opened the
gates. Pompey finished it in 66. Tigranes kept Armenia and gave up everything
else, and was left as a Roman client for the rest of his life.

The empire lasted about fifteen years. What is worth taking from it is not its
size but what the size was made of: it existed because two neighbouring empires
were simultaneously incapacitated, and it ended when one of them was replaced by
a third that was not. Armenia's position on the map is the constant, and every
subsequent page of this record and of the Bagratid one is about managing it.
