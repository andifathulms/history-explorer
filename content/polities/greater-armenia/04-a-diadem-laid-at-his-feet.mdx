The Rhandeia arrangement held for exactly as long as both empires found
it convenient, and it stopped being convenient for Rome the moment
Trajan decided he wanted Armenia outright rather than as a jointly
managed client. In 113 the Parthian king Osroes deposed the reigning
Armenian king and replaced him, on his own authority alone, with
Parthamasiris - a direct breach of the treaty's requirement that Rome
crown whoever Parthia nominated. Trajan did not answer the letters
Parthamasiris sent asking for recognition. He marched instead.

## A king who came expecting his crown back

Trajan advanced through Armenia in 114, taking towns without a fight
and accepting the submission of local rulers, until he reached Elegeia,
where Parthamasiris came to meet him in person - confident, by his own
later account, that he would receive the kingdom the same way Tiridates
had received it from Nero at Rhandeia fifty years before. Seated on a
tribunal in the Roman camp, Trajan received him. Parthamasiris saluted
him, took the diadem from his own head, and laid it at Trajan's feet -
then stood in silence, waiting to have it handed back.

## A shout that terrified a king

The watching Roman soldiers, seeing a descendant of Arsaces and nephew
of the Parthian king standing bare-headed before their emperor like a
captive, broke into a spontaneous acclamation hailing Trajan imperator,
as though for a victory - what Roman writers afterward called a
"crownless and bloodless victory." Parthamasiris, certain the shout
signaled his own execution, panicked and tried to flee before finding
himself hemmed in on every side. Trajan had him brought into his tent
privately, then - deliberately, so that no rumor of a secret deal could
circulate - had him brought back out and made him state his case
publicly before the whole army. Parthamasiris spoke bluntly: he had not
been defeated or captured, he said, but had come voluntarily, expecting
to be treated exactly as Tiridates had been. Trajan's answer was final.
He would surrender Armenia to no one; it now belonged to Rome and would
have a Roman governor. Parthamasiris was free to leave for wherever he
pleased - under a Roman cavalry escort that ensured he could contact
no one and raise no rebellion on the way. The Armenian nobles who had
come with him were simply kept, as Trajan's own new subjects.

## A kingdom that briefly stopped existing at all

For the first and only time since Rhandeia, Armenia ceased to be a
kingdom altogether. Trajan organized it as a Roman province, took the
surrender or submission of the surrounding petty kings, captured
Nisibis and Batnae further south, and was voted the title Parthicus by
the Senate for it - though contemporaries noted he took more pride in
being called Optimus, "the best," a title about his character rather
than his conquests. The arrangement did not outlive him. Trajan's
successor Hadrian, judging the eastern provinces overextended,
abandoned direct rule of Armenia almost immediately after taking power
and restored it to client-kingdom status - a reversion to something
close to the Rhandeia model, though never again quite as stable as the
fifty years that had preceded Parthamasiris's ill-fated journey to
Elegeia.
