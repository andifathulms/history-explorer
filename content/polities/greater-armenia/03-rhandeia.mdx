title: A throne disposed of by treaty

For the century after Pompey, Armenia was the standing quarrel between Rome and
Parthia: each installed kings, each removed the other's, and the fighting was
periodic and inconclusive. In AD 63 they settled it at Rhandeia, and the terms
are what this chapter is about.

The king of Armenia would be a prince of the Arsacid house — that is, a Parthian
royal — nominated at Ctesiphon. He would receive his crown from Rome. Tiridates
travelled to Italy to be crowned by Nero in person, in a ceremony Roman writers
describe at length and evidently enjoyed.

It worked. There was no serious Roman-Parthian war over Armenia for fifty years,
and the arrangement in modified forms governed the kingdom's constitutional
position for three centuries.

It also produced the emptiest field on this record. `succession_rule` is null,
and the comment on the record says why: the vocabulary has values for
primogeniture, tanistry, appanage, nomination, election, acclamation and
factional, all of which describe an arrangement internal to a polity, and this
throne was the subject of a written agreement between two other powers about how
it would be filled.

This corpus has now met that shape eight times, from the Jafnid phylarchs
holding Roman rank to the Timurids ruling beside a Chinggisid puppet to the
sharifs of Mecca appointed from Istanbul, and it has always been in the
`legitimation` field. This is the first time it has surfaced in the succession
field with a treaty behind it, and it is the most formalised version the site
has recorded: two empires, in writing, agreeing who chooses a third country's
king and who crowns him.

The Armenian nobility went on being Armenian throughout, which is the other half
of the story and the reason the arrangement was survivable. A king nominated in
Ctesiphon and crowned in Rome still had to govern through nakharar houses that
had been there before either empire and would be there after.
