The Lombards came into Italy in 568, two years after this corpus's Ostrogothic
record ends and fifteen years after Justinian's eighteen-year war had finished
wrecking the peninsula. There was no field army to stop them. They took the Po
valley within a few years, and Pavia after a three-year siege.

Alboin was murdered in 572 by his own men, reportedly at his wife's instigation
and reportedly after he had made her drink from a cup fashioned out of her
father's skull. His successor Cleph was murdered in 574.

And then the Lombards did not replace him.

## Ten years of dukes

For about a decade some thirty-five dukes governed their own territories with no
king at all, and the kingship was only restored when a Frankish invasion and a
Byzantine subsidy made a single command necessary — the dukes endowing the new
king Authari with half their own estates to pay for it.

That is this record's first turning point and it fixes the kingdom's permanent
shape. The crown was strong when there was an external threat and weak when there
was not. Spoleto and Benevento, the two southern duchies, were effectively
independent for the whole two hundred years and occasionally allied against the
king. No Lombard king ever held all of Italy, and several held considerably less
than the dukes did.

## `acclamation`, which this site rarely codes

This record's `succession_rule` field reads `acclamation` and it is one of very
few places in the corpus where that value appears.

Lombard kings were raised by the assembly of the free warriors and the dukes. It
was not hereditary in law and frequently was not in practice: dynasties lasted
two or three reigns, and the throne moved between families repeatedly. What the
kingless decade demonstrates is that the body doing the raising could decline to
raise anybody — which makes the acclamation constitutive rather than ceremonial,
and is the test this site applies before coding it.

`legitimation` reads `election, descent`. The first draft of this record had
`acclamation` in that field too and the build refused it: `acclamation` is in the
succession vocabulary and not in the legitimation one, and the two lists are
deliberately different. In the legitimation field the value for a right conferred
by a body is `election`, which is what this site's Visigothic record carries for
election by the magnates in council and its Holy Roman record for the electoral
college.

That makes five records in the Latin West carrying an elective value in one field
or the other, out of eight. The observation was first made when this region and
Rome and Byzantium were a single region at six of eight, and it has now survived
a split and the addition of four post-Roman kingdoms.
