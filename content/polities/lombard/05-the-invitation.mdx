Aistulf did what Liutprand had drawn back from. In 751 he took Ravenna, ended
the Byzantine exarchate, and removed the last imperial power from northern
Italy.

It looks like the moment the kingdom finally wins the peninsula. It is the
moment it loses it. The papacy had spent two centuries with an imperial
protector at Ravenna and a Lombard army on its northern side; with Ravenna gone
there was no protector, and Pope Stephen went looking for another one on the far
side of the Alps.

## What the Franks thought of it

Einhard, writing about Charlemagne's Italian war two generations later, pauses
to note how unpopular the first one had been.

Pepin had undertaken the Lombard war at Stephen's request, and he had undertaken
it *under great difficulties*: certain leading Franks, the men he habitually took
counsel from, opposed the plan so vehemently that they declared openly they
would leave the king and go home.

That is worth sitting with. The alliance that ended the Lombard kingdom, gave
the papacy a territorial state, and set up everything that followed in Italy was
not obvious to the people asked to fight it. It was a war on behalf of a
churchman in another country, across the highest mountains in Europe, against a
kingdom that had done the Franks no injury. Pepin went anyway.

## The terms

The first war was short. Einhard says Pepin besieged Aistulf for a few days in
Pavia and compelled him to give hostages, to restore to the Romans the cities
and castles he had taken, and to swear that he would not attempt to seize them
again.

Every clause of that is a description of the trap the kingdom was now in. The
Lombards had spent two hundred years unable to take the whole of Italy because
an empire held part of it. They had finally removed the empire, and discovered
that what replaced it was a bishop with the power to summon an army from Gaul —
and that the cities they had taken were now, by treaty and on oath, somebody
else's.

Aistulf broke the oath, as everyone involved had presumably expected. Pepin came
back and imposed the terms again. The territory went to the pope, which is where
the papal state comes from, and the Lombard kingdom was left holding northern
Italy on sufferance from a power it could not reach.

Desiderius, who took the crown in 756, spent eighteen years trying to work his
way out of that position by diplomacy — including marrying a daughter to the
Frankish king. Einhard mentions the marriage twice and never explains why it
ended. It lasted about a year, and after it ended there was nothing left between
Pavia and the Alps.
