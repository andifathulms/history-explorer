Aistulf took Ravenna in 751, ending the Byzantine exarchate and, with it, any
imperial power in northern Italy. It looks like the moment the kingdom finally
wins the peninsula and it is the moment it loses it.

The papacy had spent two centuries with an imperial protector in Ravenna and a
Lombard army to its north. With Ravenna gone there was no protector, so the
pope looked for another one — and what happened next made the Carolingians.
Pepin, who had just deposed the last Merovingian and needed a churchman's
opinion about whether he could, crossed the Alps twice on the papacy's behalf
and gave the pope the territories that became the papal state.

Charlemagne finished it. In 773 he besieged Pavia for nine months, took King
Desiderius, sent him to a monastery, and added *rex Langobardorum* to his own
titles.

## A change of family, not a conquest

The kingdom was not dissolved in 774, and the distinction is real rather than
pedantic.

Charlemagne became king of the Lombards and ruled as one. The *regnum
Langobardorum* continued as an administrative entity. The duchies continued.
Lombard law continued to be issued and pleaded, and Lombard counts and gastalds
went on doing what they had always done. What changed was the family holding
the crown.

Set that beside the two other post-Roman kingdoms of Italy and Spain and the
three endings are genuinely different things. After 553 there was no Gothic
army, no Gothic kingdom and no Gothic law: the Ostrogoths were conquered out of
existence. In 711 a Visigothic kingdom ended and something else entirely began
in its place. In 774 a Lombard kingdom carried on under new management for
another sixty years.

## Benevento

One footnote, because the ending is less clean than the date suggests.

The southern duchy of Benevento was never taken. Its duke styled himself prince
after 774, and it survived as an independent Lombard state until the Normans
took it in the eleventh century — three hundred years after the kingdom had
ended, still issuing Lombard law and still calling itself Lombard.

So the northern half of Lombard Italy became Carolingian in a single season,
and the southern half went on being Lombard for longer than the kingdom itself
had lasted.
