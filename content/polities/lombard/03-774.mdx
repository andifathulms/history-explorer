Aistulf took Ravenna in 751, ending the Byzantine exarchate and, with it, any
imperial power in northern Italy. It looks like the moment the kingdom finally
wins the peninsula and it is the moment it loses it.

The papacy had spent two centuries with an imperial protector in Ravenna and a
Lombard army to its north. With Ravenna gone there was no protector, so the pope
looked for another one — and this corpus's Carolingian record's first chapter is
about what happened next: Pepin, who had just deposed the last Merovingian and
needed a churchman's opinion about whether he could, crossed the Alps twice on
the papacy's behalf and gave the pope the territories that became the papal
state.

Charlemagne finished it. In 773 he besieged Pavia for nine months, took King
Desiderius, sent him to a monastery, and added *rex Langobardorum* to his own
titles.

## `dynastic replacement`, not `conquest`

This record's `ended.type` is `dynastic replacement` and the distinction is real
rather than pedantic.

The kingdom was not dissolved. Charlemagne became king of the Lombards and ruled
as one: the *regnum Langobardorum* continued as an administrative entity, the
duchies continued, Lombard law continued to be issued and pleaded, and Lombard
counts and gastalds went on doing what they had done. What changed was the
family holding the crown.

Compare the Ostrogothic record two entries earlier in this region, where
`conquest` is coded because after 553 there was no Gothic army, no Gothic
kingdom and no Gothic law. Compare the Visigothic record, where 711 ends a
kingdom and starts a different kind of state entirely. This corpus codes three
post-Roman kingdoms ending in three different ways, and the differences are the
argument for having all three.

## The edge, and the region

`lombard -> carolingian`, type `conquered by`, dated 774, is the second internal
edge in the Latin West and one of only two — the other being `carolingian ->
holy-roman-empire` in 962.

That is thin for eight records and it is the honest picture. The other five
post-Roman and maritime records here point outward: the Visigoths at the Umayyad
caliphate, the Vandals and Ostrogoths at Byzantium, Venice at Byzantium, and
Genoa at nothing at all. This region is not a sequence. It is a set of states
assembled out of the same wreckage, mostly ended by powers outside it.

## Benevento

One footnote, because the ending is less clean than the date suggests.

The southern duchy of Benevento was never taken. Its duke styled himself prince
after 774 and it survived as an independent Lombard state until the Normans took
it in the eleventh century — three hundred years after the kingdom this record
covers had ended, still issuing Lombard law and calling itself Lombard.

This corpus does not carry it, and a reader looking for what became of Lombard
Italy will find the northern half in this site's Carolingian record and the
southern half nowhere. It is the largest thing missing from this page.
