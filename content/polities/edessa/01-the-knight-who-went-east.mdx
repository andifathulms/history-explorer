title: The knight who went east

In the autumn of 1097 the armies of the First Crusade were grinding south
through Anatolia towards Antioch, and at Marash one of the leaders left them.
Baldwin of Boulogne, younger brother of Godfrey of Bouillon, took a small force
east instead — away from Jerusalem, away from the campaign, across the Euphrates
into a country of Armenian towns that no crusading vow had mentioned.

He was invited. The Armenian lords of the region had been living between the
Seljuk amirs of Mosul and Aleppo for a generation and were looking for a
military patron, and T'oros, the ruler of Edessa, went furthest: in February
1098 he adopted Baldwin as his son and heir in a public ceremony. Within weeks
T'oros had been dragged out of the citadel and killed by his own townspeople,
and Baldwin was in possession.

Whether Baldwin arranged the killing is disputed, and this record does not
decide it. The turning point is marked `contested: true` on that basis, which is
what the flag is for.

What the episode is not in dispute about is chronology. Edessa was taken in
March 1098. Antioch did not fall until June. Jerusalem was not stormed until
July 1099. **The first Crusader state was founded before the crusade reached its
objective, a hundred and eighty kilometres in the wrong direction, by a man who
never went to Jerusalem with the army at all.**

He went later, and as king. Godfrey died in 1100 and Baldwin rode south to take
the crown as Baldwin I, handing Edessa to his cousin Baldwin of Bourcq — who in
1118 did exactly the same thing and became Baldwin II. The two men who built the
kingdom of Jerusalem into a state both learned the work here first.

That relationship is in this paragraph and not in the edge list. It is a fact
about personnel and not about succession: nothing passed from this county to
that kingdom except two men, and the vocabulary on this site has no type for
that, which is correct. An edge is a claim about causation between polities.
Two shared rulers are a claim about careers.
