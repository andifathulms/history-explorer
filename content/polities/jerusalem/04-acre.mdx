For a century after 1191 the kingdom of Jerusalem was a coastal strip governed
from Acre, and it did not much resemble the thing it was named after.

Its money was trade. The Italian communes — Venetian, Genoese, Pisan — held
quarters in the ports with their own courts, consuls, weights and fleets, and
the customs of Acre were worth more to the crown than the agricultural revenue
of what territory remained, which is why this record codes `trade-toll` beside
`land-tax`. It also meant the kingdom contained three sovereign commercial
powers whose wars with each other it could not stop: the War of Saint Sabas in
the 1250s was fought inside Acre between Venice and Genoa, with the military
orders taking sides, while the Mamluks were consolidating Egypt.

Its kings were increasingly absent. The crown passed to the Hohenstaufen and
then, by the Haute Cour's own judgement, to the Lusignans of Cyprus, and by the
last decades the king of Jerusalem was normally to be found on Cyprus. This is
where the `succession_rule` gap on this page comes from and why it is left null
rather than filled: the crown was hereditary, it went four times through women
to their husbands, and every one of those transfers had to be accepted by an
assembly of barons who argued the law of it in detail. No value in this site's
closed vocabulary describes that, and choosing the nearest would be a definition
bent to fit an inconvenient case, which the coding rulebook forbids.

Jerusalem itself was held again from 1229 to 1244 — obtained by Frederick II
from al-Kamil by treaty, undefended and demilitarised, in a settlement so
unwelcome to both sides' clergy that the emperor crowned himself in an
excommunicate's silence and the city fell to Khwarazmian mercenaries fifteen
years later without a real siege. The court never moved back.

The end came in 1291. Al-Ashraf Khalil brought siege artillery from Damascus and
Baalbek and took Acre in six weeks; Tyre, Sidon, Beirut and Tortosa were
abandoned within weeks, most without being attacked. The edge from this record
runs to the Mamluk page and it is the only succession edge this kingdom has,
which is the accurate shape of it: founded by an expedition from outside this
corpus entirely, inherited by nobody, ended by the polity next door.

The title outlived the state by six hundred years, carried by the Lusignan kings
of Cyprus and afterwards claimed by the houses of Savoy, Naples, Lorraine and
Habsburg — down to the twentieth century, long after anyone believed it meant a
country. This site records no `successor_claims` count for it. That is not
because the claims are unreal but because the coding rules require a rule
applied uniformly, and this corpus has not yet fixed one for a title that
becomes heraldic. The count is null, and the gap renders, which is the honest
state of the question.
