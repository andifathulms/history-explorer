This site's `military_basis` coding for the kingdom is `land-grant` and
`standing-professional` together, and the second value appears here for reasons
that have no parallel elsewhere in the corpus.

The first half is ordinary. Fiefs were held of the crown, knights owed service,
and the kingdom's Latin manpower was permanently and dangerously small — a few
hundred knights and a few thousand sergeants for a frontier of five hundred
kilometres.

The second half is the Templars and the Hospitallers. They began as a guard for
pilgrims and a hospital, and within fifty years they were something the medieval
world had not previously produced: permanently embodied professional forces,
recruited by vocation rather than by tenure, holding the great castles, and
funded by estates spread across the whole of Latin Europe. They were the only
troops in the kingdom who were always there, always trained and never went home
when a campaigning season ended.

They also did not answer to the king. Both orders were exempt, reporting to the
pope, and their masters sat in the Haute Cour as powers rather than as vassals.
By the thirteenth century the crown could not fight without them and could not
order them, and the record is full of the consequences — orders taking opposite
sides in the kingdom's succession disputes, and in one case the Templars and
Hospitallers supporting rival claimants in a civil war.

There is a mechanism this corpus keeps recording, most often under
`slave-soldier`: a state builds a fighting force outside the ordinary structure
of obligation because the ordinary structure cannot supply enough men, and the
force becomes an independent political actor. Samarra, Ghazna, Cairo. The
Frankish version arrived at it from a completely different direction, with free
volunteers under religious vows instead of purchased slaves, and produced the
same problem. That the two roads meet is the sort of thing the coded fields on
this site exist to make visible, and it is why `standing-professional` is on
this page rather than a fifth value invented for the case.
