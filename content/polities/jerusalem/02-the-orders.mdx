---
title: An army the king could not command
drafted_from: barber-2012
phase: expansion
---

The kingdom fought with two armies that had nothing in common except the enemy,
and the second of them was an institution nobody had built before.

The first half is ordinary. Fiefs were held of the crown, knights owed service,
and the kingdom's Latin manpower was permanently and dangerously small — a few
hundred knights and a few thousand sergeants for a frontier of five hundred
kilometres.

The second half is the Templars and the Hospitallers. They began as a guard for
pilgrims and a hospital, and within fifty years they were something the
medieval world had not previously produced: permanently embodied professional
forces, recruited by vocation rather than by tenure, holding the great castles,
and funded by estates spread across the whole of Latin Europe. They were the
only troops in the kingdom who were always there, always trained and never went
home when a campaigning season ended.

They also did not answer to the king. Both orders were exempt, reporting to the
pope, and their masters sat in the Haute Cour as powers rather than as vassals.
By the thirteenth century the crown could not fight without them and could not
order them, and the record is full of the consequences — orders taking opposite
sides in the kingdom's succession disputes, and in one case the Templars and
Hospitallers supporting rival claimants in a civil war.

It is a mechanism the Islamic states around them knew intimately, having
arrived at it from the opposite direction. A state that cannot raise enough
soldiers through the ordinary structure of obligation builds a force outside
it; the force turns out to be the only reliable army it has; and within a
generation the force is an independent political actor its nominal masters
cannot overrule. Samarra did it with purchased Turkish slaves, Ghazna and Cairo
after it.

The Franks got there with free volunteers under religious vows, which is about
as different a starting point as could be arranged, and ended in the same
place.
