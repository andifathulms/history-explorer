Jerusalem was stormed on 15 July 1099 and the question of what to do with it
was open for a week. The city had been taken by an army with no head of state
in it, under a papal legate who had died at Antioch, on behalf of a movement
whose declared purpose had been reached and which had no constitutional theory
for what came next.

The compromise was Godfrey of Bouillon, and the interest of it is the title. He
refused to be crowned king in the city where Christ had worn a crown of thorns
and took instead the style Advocate of the Holy Sepulchre — a term borrowed
from the lay protectors of European monasteries, which made the state's
principal a guardian of the church rather than a monarch over a territory. He
died within a year. His brother Baldwin, who had spent that year making himself
count of Edessa and had no such scruples, came south and was crowned king at
Bethlehem on Christmas Day 1100.

The kingdom's claim rested on two separate assertions made together: that it
had taken the country by force, and that the force had been sanctioned. The
Franks went on making both, continuously, for two centuries.

It is worth noticing that the states on the other side of the frontier were
arguing from the same premise in an incompatible vocabulary. The caliphate a
few hundred kilometres away was an office held to be divinely constituted, and
every ruler in Syria who mattered held his province by a grant from it. Two
systems of sanctified authority were being asserted over the same ground in the
same decades, and neither had any language in which to recognise the other.

What the kingdom actually consisted of took another twenty-five years to
assemble. Jerusalem in 1099 was a city and a road to the coast. The ports came
one at a time, mostly with Italian fleets that were paid for their help in
quarters and trading privileges inside the towns they helped capture: Haifa,
Arsuf, Caesarea, Acre in 1104, Tripoli, Beirut and Sidon by 1110, Tyre in 1124,
Ascalon not until 1153. Those grants are the origin of the Venetian, Genoese
and Pisan quarters that later ran the kingdom's economy and answered to nobody
in it.
