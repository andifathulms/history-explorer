Jerusalem was stormed on 15 July 1099 and the question of what to do with it was
open for a week. The city had been taken by an army with no head of state in it,
under a papal legate who had died at Antioch, on behalf of a movement whose
declared purpose had been reached and which had no constitutional theory for
what came next.

The compromise was Godfrey of Bouillon, and the interest of it is the title. He
refused to be crowned king in the city where Christ had worn a crown of thorns
and took instead the style Advocate of the Holy Sepulchre — a term borrowed from
the lay protectors of European monasteries, which made the state's principal a
guardian of the church rather than a monarch over a territory. He died within a
year. His brother Baldwin, who had spent that year making himself count of
Edessa and had no such scruples, came south and was crowned king at Bethlehem on
Christmas Day 1100.

This record's `legitimation` carries `conquest` and `divine-sanction` together,
and the pairing is exact rather than decorative. The kingdom's claim was that it
had taken the country by force and that the force had been sanctioned — those
are two separate assertions and the Franks made both, continuously, for two
centuries. It is worth setting beside the other polities on this site coded
`divine-sanction`, and beside the Caliphates region next door, whose entire
membership rule is a claim on an office held to be divinely constituted. The
same argument was being conducted from both sides of the same country in the
same decades in incompatible vocabularies.

What the kingdom actually consisted of took another twenty-five years to
assemble. Jerusalem in 1099 was a city and a road to the coast. The ports came
one at a time, mostly with Italian fleets that were paid for their help in
quarters and trading privileges inside the towns they helped capture: Haifa,
Arsuf, Caesarea, Acre in 1104, Tripoli, Beirut and Sidon by 1110, Tyre in 1124,
Ascalon not until 1153. Those grants are the origin of the Venetian, Genoese and
Pisan quarters that later ran the kingdom's economy and answered to nobody in
it.
