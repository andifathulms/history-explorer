John Sigismund spent much of his adult reign pressing an unwinnable
claim to the wider Hungarian crown his mother had renounced on his
behalf as a child, provoking repeated wars with the Habsburgs that
Transylvania's own limited resources could not sustain indefinitely.
When a conspiracy against him failed and one of his own confidants was
revealed to have sold himself to the Austrians, he finally agreed to a
settlement that redefined what his throne actually was.

## Trading a crown for a patrimony

By treaty, John Sigismund renounced the title of king outright and
accepted instead the style of prince, holding Transylvania as his own
hereditary possession rather than as a rump claim on a larger kingdom.
The agreement even specified what would happen if the Ottoman sultan
objected to arrangements he had no part in making, and what should
happen if John Sigismund died without a male heir - the states of
Transylvania would elect a successor answerable to Austria instead.
Within this single settlement, Transylvania stopped being a wounded
fragment of Hungary and became a principality in its own right.

## An election that startled all of Europe

When John Sigismund died shortly afterward, the Transylvanian diet chose
as his successor Stephen Báthory - a choice whose consequences reached
far beyond the principality itself. Báthory's reputation for eloquence
and military service proved strong enough that, within a few years, the
Polish nobility elected him their own king as well, after their previous
elected monarch, Henry of Valois, fled home in the night on hearing of
his brother's death in France. Báthory governed Transylvania from Poland
through his brother Christopher, appointed as his lieutenant - the same
principality that had begun as an infant's consolation prize now
supplying a king to one of the largest realms in Europe.

## A precedent that outlived any one prince

What Báthory's double elevation demonstrated was that Transylvania's own
princely office, however small the territory attached to it, carried
real standing in its own right - recognised, negotiated with, and
courted by powers far larger than itself. No later prince would match
Báthory's own reach into Poland, but every one of them governed a state
whose international standing that single election had helped establish.
