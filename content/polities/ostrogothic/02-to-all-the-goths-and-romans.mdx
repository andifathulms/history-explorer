The Ostrogothic kingdom in Italy is unusually well documented for a barbarian
successor state, and the reason is that the man who ran its chancery published
his own correspondence.

Cassiodorus served the Gothic kings for something over thirty years, rising to
praetorian prefect, and the *Variae* are the letters he drafted in their names —
appointments, judgements, instructions to officials, replies to petitions,
diplomatic correspondence. He collected and issued them at the end of his career,
and his stated motive is worth keeping in view: to demonstrate that his influence
had been exerted throughout on the side of law and order and just government,
directed equally at the repression of Teutonic barbarism and the punishment of
Roman venality.

That sentence is the kingdom's programme, and it is also a defence brief.

## What the letters are about

Not war. The striking thing about a collection of state papers from a Gothic
kingdom is how little of it is military.

There are letters about the price of grain, the drainage of marshes, the repair
of aqueducts, the licensing of a monopoly, the pension of a retired charioteer,
the relief of famine-stricken citizens. There are appointments to Roman offices
with their full traditional titulature. There are long digressions on natural
history and moral philosophy, which Hodgkin doubts were in the originals and
which are entirely characteristic of the author.

It is the paperwork of a functioning late-Roman provincial administration,
because that is what it was. The Goths took over the machine and kept the staff.

## The salutation

The formula that matters most is a form of address.

Theoderic's edicts and circulars are directed *universis Gothis et Romanis* — to
all the Goths and Romans. Two peoples, named separately, addressed together, in
one document, by one ruler.

That is the constitutional settlement in three words. The Goths were the army
and held their land by military service; the Romans held the civil offices, the
law and the church; each kept its own law and its own religion, Arian and
Nicene, with the king above both and belonging formally to neither jurisdiction.
Nobody was assimilated and nobody was expelled.

Theoderic ruled Italy for thirty-three years on that basis and it worked. The
senate met. The consuls were appointed and the emperor at Constantinople
confirmed them. The grain dole continued, the games continued, the buildings
were repaired, and the letters show the government arguing about municipal water
supply rather than about survival.

## The limit

It was a settlement that depended on a man rather than on an institution, and
Cassiodorus's own collection shows what happened to it. The last letters he
wrote were for a different king, and they are addressed differently.
