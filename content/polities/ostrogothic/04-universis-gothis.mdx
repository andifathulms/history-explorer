In the summer of 536 the Gothic army, encamped in the field, deposed the king
they had and raised a veteran officer named Witigis on a shield among drawn
swords, in the manner of their ancestors. Theodahad was put to death on the new
king's order. Witigis, being of obscure family, married Theoderic's
granddaughter to improve his title.

Cassiodorus wrote the accession letter, and it is one of the best things he ever
composed. It is also the moment the Ostrogothic experiment ends, and the
evidence is in the first two words.

## The address

Theoderic had written *universis Gothis et Romanis*. To all the Goths and
Romans.

Witigis's letter is addressed *universis Gothis*.

## What he says

The text is a boast about how he was chosen, and it reads as a deliberate
repudiation of everything the previous forty years had been.

He was raised, he says, by his Gothic ancestors' custom, on a shield, among
drawn swords. *You should know that I was elected not in the narrow confines of
a bedchamber, but in wide open fields; nor was I sought out amid the delicate
conversation of flatterers, but to the sound of blaring trumpets* — so that the
Gothic people, stirred by that din, might find themselves a martial king.

Every clause is aimed at something. The bedchamber is the palace at Ravenna. The
flatterers are the Roman civil service. The trumpets and the shield are the
Gothic army asserting that it, and not the chancery, decides who rules.

The man who drafted it was the head of that civil service, writing in the voice
of the movement that had displaced him.

## What had gone wrong

The settlement had one structural weakness and it was visible from the start:
the Goths were the army and the Romans were the administration, and the two had
different interests as soon as there was a war.

Theoderic's successors could not manage both constituencies. Amalasuintha, his
daughter, governed as regent for a child and was accused by the Gothic nobility
of raising him as a Roman; when she was murdered, Justinian had the pretext for
an invasion, exactly as Gelimer had furnished him one in Africa two years
before.

By the time Witigis was raised on the shield, Belisarius had taken Sicily and
was in southern Italy. There were five surviving letters written for Witigis and
four of them are about negotiating peace with Justinian.

## The end of the correspondence

Cassiodorus retired from public life around 538 or 539, at the point where the
government he had served for thirty years was becoming a war it could not win.

Belisarius entered Ravenna in 540 with his secretary Procopius in his train. The
praetorian prefect was no longer there; he had gone home to Bruttii. Procopius
wrote a history of the Gothic war in eight books and never once mentions
Cassiodorus's name — an omission Hodgkin, who translated both, calls the thing
most difficult to comprehend in the whole business.
