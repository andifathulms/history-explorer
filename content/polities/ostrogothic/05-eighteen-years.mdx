Theodoric died in 526 with no son. He had designated his grandson Athalaric,
aged ten, under the regency of his daughter Amalasuintha, who had been educated
at Ravenna in Greek and Latin and who governed capably and was disliked for it by
the Gothic nobility. Athalaric died at eighteen. Amalasuintha took a cousin,
Theodahad, as co-ruler, and he had her imprisoned on an island and strangled in
her bath within a year.

Justinian had been waiting for a pretext and took that one. Theodoric had
nominated his successor, which is as much of a rule as the kingdom had, and it
failed at the first attempt: nine years from his death to a Byzantine army in
Sicily.

## What the war did

Belisarius took Sicily in 535 and Rome in 536. The Goths besieged him in Rome for
a year. He took Ravenna in 540 by pretending to accept an offer of the western
crown and then arresting the man who offered it. Justinian recalled him, the
Goths elected Totila, and Totila spent ten years winning most of Italy back —
taking Rome twice, once emptying it of its population entirely, and offering
terms to a senate that had run out of reasons to prefer either side.

Narses finished it at Taginae in 552 and at Mons Lactarius the year after. Teia
was killed. After 553 there was no Gothic army.

Rome changed hands five times in eighteen years. The aqueducts were cut. The
senate, which had met continuously for something over a thousand years and had
gone on meeting under Odoacer and Theodoric, is not heard of again after the
560s. The city of Rome had held over half a million people when Constantine
founded his rival capital and was under fifty thousand by the end of the sixth
century, and this war is where most of that difference happened.

## What arrived fifteen years later

The Lombard record two entries along in this region.

Italy in 568 had no field army, a ruined administration, a halved population and
an imperial government at Ravenna with no capacity to defend the north. The
Lombards took the Po valley in a few years and were never dislodged, and the
peninsula stayed politically divided for the following thirteen hundred years.

That is the shape of the whole episode. Sixty years of a kingdom that left
nothing measurable behind, followed by an empire reconquering a province in
order to have it back, spending eighteen years doing it, and handing what was
left to the next people who came through the Alps.

## Two kingdoms, one campaign

Justinian took Africa from the Vandals in a season and Italy from the Goths in
eighteen years, and the two conquests are usually filed separately. They were one
policy: the recovery of the western Mediterranean by an eastern emperor who
believed the western provinces had never legitimately ceased to be Roman.

The difference in cost is the whole lesson. Africa had a regime with no
constituency outside its own people and the cities opened. Italy had a
settlement that a substantial part of the Roman population had been willing to
live under, and the war to remove it took a generation and destroyed the thing
being recovered.
