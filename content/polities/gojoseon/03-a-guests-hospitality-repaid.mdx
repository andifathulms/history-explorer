---
title: A guest's hospitality repaid
drafted_from: hulbert-1905

Wiman arrived in Joseon as a fugitive, one of many refugees fleeing
after his own commander's failed bid for independence from Han China
collapsed. King Gi-jun, the reigning descendant of Gija's own line,
took him in without suspicion, granting him a hundred li of land along
the northern frontier in exchange for service as a border guard against
exactly the kind of threat Wiman himself represented.

## A warning that was really an invasion

Wiman spent his years on the frontier building his own following from
Chinese refugees and local tribesmen loyal to him personally rather
than to the king who had sheltered him. When he judged his own strength
sufficient, he sent word to Gi-jun that a vast Chinese army was
advancing in four columns and that he, Wiman, must rush his own forces
to the capital to serve as the king's bodyguard. By the time Gi-jun
understood the deception, Wiman's army was already at the gates.
Gi-jun fled downriver by boat with what treasure he could load in time,
the last representative of Gija's line abandoning the capital his
ancestor had ruled for close to a millennium.

## A kingdom built on the same skill that stole it

Wiman proved as capable a ruler as he had been a schemer, holding
together a coalition of Chinese refugees and native tribes through the
same combination of firmness and calculated generosity that had won him
Gi-jun's trust in the first place. Under his own rule the kingdom
reached its greatest extent - stretching into Liao-tung, encompassing
the whole of northern and eastern Korea, and reaching south to the Han
River - bringing surrounding tribal chieftains to his court and using
them, in turn, to conduct diplomacy with China itself.

## A strength that could not be passed down

What Wiman's kingdom depended on was never institutional; it was the
personal judgment of one man who knew exactly when to tighten his grip
on the tribes around him and when to loosen it. That judgment did not
survive him. His grandson U-gu inherited every ambition his grandfather
had shown and none of the tact - and the kingdom's fortunes would prove,
within two generations of its founding by treachery, to rest on
foundations no sturdier than the men who happened to be sitting on the
throne.
