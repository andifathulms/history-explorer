The war began in 66 and Titus took Jerusalem in the summer of 70. The Temple
this record's second chapter is about — begun by Herod about 20 BC, finished
around AD 64 — burned.

Agrippa II, the last of this dynasty to hold territory, spent the war on the
Roman side. He had tried to prevent it: Josephus gives him a long speech to the
crowd in Jerusalem in 66 arguing that Rome could not be beaten, listing the
nations that had tried, and it is a set piece rather than a transcript but the
position is certainly his. When it failed he sent two thousand cavalry to the
Roman army, was wounded at the siege of Gamla, and was with Titus at Jerusalem.
His sister Berenice became Titus's mistress and nearly his wife.

He kept his northern territories for another twenty-two years and died about 92,
and Rome took them.

## The shape of the record

A dynasty founded by a Roman vote to make Judaea governable spent its last act
watching Rome destroy the thing it had built, from the winning side, and was then
quietly wound up.

The coded fields do not show any of that, and it is worth saying so directly on
the page rather than implying it. `ended.type` reads `gradual absorption` and
`by` reads Rome, which is accurate and describes an administrative process.
Influence: zero, zero, zero. Reach: no cited figure. What the fields do carry is
the arrangement — `investiture`, `nomination`, `mercenary` and `land-grant`,
`monopoly` and `mining` — and read together those six values describe a client
kingship precisely and describe the destruction of Jerusalem not at all.

That is the correct division of labour on this site and not a defect in it. The
coded fields answer a fixed set of questions so that a hundred and sixty records
can be compared on the same terms, and the chapters carry what is particular. A
field that could hold AD 70 would be a field that could hold anything.

## Two records, one pair

This region now has seven polities and one internal edge, and both ends of it
are on this page and the one before it.

The pairing is closer than a succession. The Hasmonean record's single influence
count is Judaism carried into Idumaea by John Hyrcanus in 112 BC, and the reason
that count exists is the reason this record exists: Herod's family were Jews
because of that campaign, and the charge his enemies pressed for fifty years was
that being a Jew that way did not count. A dynasty's one entry on the influence
axis produced the dynasty that destroyed it, seventy-five years later.

The two records also make opposite uses of the same vocabulary. The Hasmoneans
had no lineage and wrote a constitution; Herod had no lineage and got a Roman
vote. `election` and `investiture`, in the same field, in the same city, a
century apart — two answers to the identical problem of ruling Judaea without
being descended from anyone entitled to.

Neither answer survived contact with the empire next door, and the Temple
outlasted both dynasties by six years.
