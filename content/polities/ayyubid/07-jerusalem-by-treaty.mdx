---
title: Jerusalem, given back
drafted_from: humphreys-1977

The Fifth Crusade went to Egypt rather than Palestine, on the correct reasoning
that Egypt was where the money and the army were. It took Damietta in 1219 after
an eighteen-month siege. Al-Kamil offered, twice, to hand over Jerusalem and
most of the kingdom's old territory in exchange for withdrawal; the papal legate
Pelagius refused, both times, and in 1221 marched on Cairo at the season when the
Nile rises. Al-Kamil opened the dykes. The Crusade was cut off in the flood and
surrendered on terms of withdrawal.

Eight years later he gave Jerusalem away for nothing very much. Frederick II
arrived in 1228 excommunicated, with a small force and no intention of
campaigning; al-Kamil was in the middle of a quarrel with his brother
al-Mu'azzam of Damascus, wanted a Frankish emperor as a counterweight, and
signed the Treaty of Jaffa in February 1229. It gave the Franks Jerusalem,
Bethlehem, Nazareth and a corridor to the coast for ten years. Muslims kept the
Haram al-Sharif, its administration and its worship. Both men were denounced by
their own clergy — Frederick by the pope, al-Kamil by preachers from Aleppo to
Baghdad, and the sermons after the news reached Damascus are among the most
bitter things in thirteenth-century Arabic writing.

Two things follow for this site. The first is that the treaty is unintelligible
without the appanage structure: al-Mu'azzam had died in 1227 and Jerusalem was
in territory al-Kamil was trying to take from his nephew, so the city he was
giving away was a bargaining chip in a family quarrel rather than a national
possession. The second is that it worked. Al-Kamil got Damascus, held the
confederation together until his death in 1238, and the ten years ran out with
no crusade against him.

Jerusalem returned to Muslim control in 1244, taken not by an Ayyubid army but
by Khwarazmian cavalry — refugees from the Mongol destruction of their own
empire, hired by al-Kamil's son, who sacked the city on their way through. The
same year they and the Egyptian army destroyed the Frankish field army at La
Forbie. It was the last Ayyubid victory of that scale, and it was won by men
the Mongols had dispossessed, six years before the Mongols arrived themselves.
