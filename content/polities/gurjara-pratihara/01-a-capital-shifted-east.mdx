The Gurjaras who built the Pratihara dynasty were, in Smith's own
account, foreign immigrants who entered Rajputana alongside or soon
after the White Huns, settling in large numbers around Mount Abu and
eventually founding a kingdom whose capital, Bhillamala (modern
Bhinmal), lay some fifty miles to its northwest. Like other such
groups the Gurjaras were absorbed into Hindu society through
Hinduization, their ruling families accepted as Kshatriyas and
Rajputs; the Pratiharas themselves - Parihars, in the vernacular -
were one of four clans a well-known legend traced to a purifying fire
at Mount Abu, alongside the Paramaras, Chahamanas and Chaulukyas.
Smith reads the myth as a folk memory of exactly this process:
foreign newcomers ritually cleansed of their outsider status and
admitted into the Hindu caste order as high-ranking warriors.

## A dethroned raja, a new capital

Panchala, the old kingdom centered on Kanauj, had passed through a
chain of weak rulers after Harsha's death in 647 - Yasovarman deposed
by a Kashmiri king, Vajrayudha deposed in turn, Indrayudha dethroned
around 810 by Dharmapala of Bengal, who installed a client named
Chakrayudha on the throne rather than ruling the city directly.
Chakrayudha's own tenure lasted only a few years. Around 816 - the
date rests on a dated Gwalior inscription - Nagabhata, the ambitious
ruler of the Gurjara-Pratihara kingdom at Bhillamala, deposed him and
shifted his own capital east to Kanauj, which Smith credits with
becoming "again for a considerable time the premier city of Northern
India." The move set the Pratiharas on a collision course with the
same eastern and southern powers that had already fought over the
city once: Dharmapala's own Pala kingdom in Bengal and Bihar, and the
Rashtrakuta dynasty of the Deccan, whose king Govinda III claimed an
early victory over Nagabhata's own forces in the opening years of the
contest.

<Figure id="gp-map" />

Little else is recorded of Nagabhata's own reign at Kanauj, and even
less of his son Ramabhadra, who held the throne only briefly before
his own son inherited a capital three rival powers were now prepared
to fight over for the better part of two centuries.
