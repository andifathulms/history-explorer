The An Lushan rebellion destroyed the tax registers. A state that assessed
households by head and by allotted land, and could no longer say who lived where
or held what, had no way to collect.

What kept the Tang alive for a hundred and fifty years afterwards was salt.

From 758 the state bought salt at the production sites, resold it to licensed
merchants at a large markup, and let them distribute it. It required no
registers, no census and no control of the countryside — only control of a few
hundred coastal pans and inland wells. By the 780s that one indirect tax was
providing more than half of central government revenue.

<Figure id="tg-saltseal" />

The Han had monopolised the same commodity in the same country nine hundred
years earlier, and had held a court debate in 81 BC over whether a state should
be in that business at all. The Tang did not reopen the argument. It reopened
the monopoly, for the reason above: it was the only tax a government that had
lost its records could still assess.

## Two-tax

The other half of the answer is the reform of 780. Yang Yan abandoned assessment
by head and by allotment and taxed households twice a year on what they actually
held, in cash.

<Figure id="tg-kaiyuan" />

Very few fiscal reforms anywhere outlive the dynasty that makes them. This one
outlived the Tang by seven hundred years: in outline it is how China was taxed
until the sixteenth century, when the Ming Single Whip consolidated the
descendants of Yang Yan's two taxes into one payment in silver.

## And Huang Chao

The man who finished the dynasty was both of the things this fiscal system
produced: a failed examination candidate and a salt smuggler.

Huang Chao's rising crossed the empire twice, sacked Guangzhou, and took both
capitals. A monopoly high enough to fund a state is high enough to make smuggling
worth an army; and an examination system that had become the only respectable
road to office produced, every three years, a cohort of literate men who had been
told the empire was open to talent and had then been failed.

The dynasty survived him by twenty-three years and was destroyed by the general
who put him down.
