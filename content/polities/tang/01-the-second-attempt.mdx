The Tang is the second dynasty in Chinese history to inherit a unified empire
from a short-lived predecessor that had built it and then destroyed itself
doing so. The first was the Han, taking over from the Qin. The parallel was
obvious to everyone at the time and was discussed openly at court.

## What the Sui had done

The Sui reunified China in 589 after three and a half centuries of division, and
in thirty years built the physical apparatus the Tang then ran on: the Grand
Canal linking the Yangtze rice country to the northern capitals, granaries on a
scale that has to be excavated to be believed, a rebuilt Chang'an, the
equal-field land system, and a revived examination.

Then Yang Guang invaded Korea three times, lost armies estimated in the
hundreds of thousands, exhausted the corvée, and was murdered by his own guard
in 618 while the empire came apart in revolt.

## The advantage of being second

Li Yuan was a Sui general and a relative of the imperial house who took Chang'an
in 617, and his family took the throne the following year. The Tang inherited
the canal, the granaries, the land system, the law code and the capital, and
inherited them intact — the Sui had been destroyed by its own exertions rather
than by an invasion, so the machinery was still there.

They also inherited the lesson, and stated it. The verdict on the Sui was the
verdict on the Qin: an empire lost through overreach and the exhaustion of the
people. Taizong's court took that as a working constitution, and the *Zhenguan
zhengyao* — the record of his policy discussions, compiled afterwards — is
substantially a book about a ruler being contradicted by his officials and
accepting it.

How much of that is genuine and how much is a later idealisation is argued
about. The relevant point is that it became the model. Every Chinese emperor
afterwards was measured against a portrait of a founder who listened.

## Where Taizong came from

He had also killed two of his brothers at the Xuanwu Gate in 626 and forced his
father to abdicate.

Both things are in the official record, which is itself informative: Taizong is
known to have read and intervened in the court diaries covering the episode,
which every historian since has noted, and the episode is still there. What he
appears to have secured was not suppression but framing — that his brothers had
been plotting first.

The dynasty that produced the most admired model of consultative rule in
Chinese history was founded by fratricide in a palace gateway, and both facts
were kept.

## The shape of what they had

By the 640s the Tang held the north China plain, the Yangtze, the south, and a
frontier that ran out into country nobody had governed from Chang'an since the
Han.

The armies were *fubing* militia: farmer-soldiers holding land under the
equal-field allotment, serving in rotation, providing their own kit and going
home. It cost almost nothing and was designed so that no commander ever held a
permanent army.

That system, and what happened when it stopped working, is the political history
of the dynasty.
