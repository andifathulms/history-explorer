By the time Sjarif Kasim II became Siak's twelfth sultan, Dutch
colonial administration had already stripped the throne of most of
its real governing power, leaving the sultan a traditional and
religious figurehead rather than a ruler in any working sense. What
he did with the authority he still had was direct the sultanate's own
treasury toward education rather than toward maintaining the trappings
of a court the Dutch had already hollowed out.

## Schools built on the sultanate's own money

In 1915 Sjarif Kasim II helped establish a Hollandsch-Inlandsche
School at Siak, though its curriculum and staff answered to Dutch
colonial oversight and its doors, by colonial policy, opened only to
European children and the local nobility. Dissatisfied that a
Dutch-run school taught his own people so little of their own religion
or nationality, he founded Madrasah Taufiq Alhasjimiah in 1917, a
school for boys built and staffed independent of colonial control,
its teachers recruited from West Sumatra. He did not stop with boys'
education: in 1927 his consort, Tengku Agung Sultanah Lathifah,
founded Sulthanah Lathifah School to give Siak's girls a formal
education of their own, and in 1933 the sultan renewed that effort
with Madrassatoen Nisa', a girls' religious school built opposite the
Asserayah Palace and staffed by teachers drawn from the same Diniyah
Putri circle at Padang Panjang that was, at the same moment, driving
the modernization of Islamic education across West Sumatra generally.

It was the same instinct that would define the rest of his reign:
authority he could no longer exercise as an independent ruler, he
still put to direct use for the people who remained, in every sense
that mattered to him, his own subjects.
