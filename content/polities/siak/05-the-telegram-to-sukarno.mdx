---
title: The telegram to Sukarno

When word reached Sultan Sjarif Kasim II that Indonesia had declared
its independence in 1945, he did not wait to see how the new
republic's fortunes would turn before deciding where Siak stood. He
sent a telegram directly to President Sukarno, pledging the young
republic his support - moral, political and material - and followed
the pledge with an act no negotiated settlement required of him: he
turned over the whole of the Siak sultanate's own treasury to the new
Indonesian state. The gesture closed out, at his own initiative rather
than under outside compulsion, the same line Raja Kecil had opened by
converting an unenforceable claim to one throne into the founding of
another, more than two centuries before. Siak's own historians would
later argue, at a 1970 national seminar, that the sultanate's role in
the Republic's own founding deserved wider recognition than it had
received - a case made on the strength of exactly this: a ruler who,
offered the chance to bargain for his own family's future, chose
instead to hand a kingdom to a nation being born.
