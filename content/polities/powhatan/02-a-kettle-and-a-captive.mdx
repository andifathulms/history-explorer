Trust between the confederacy and the colony never survived long
after Smith himself left Virginia in 1609. Word reached Powhatan -
by way of English deserters living among his people - that the
colony's leadership meant to surprise him and seize his stores of
corn, and rather than wait to find out if the warning was true,
Powhatan abandoned Werowocomoco entirely, carrying away his
provisions and building a new residence the English would have to
find all over again. When an English party did arrive at his old
capital with exactly that plan in mind, they found empty houses and a
population too hostile to risk lingering among.

Years of raids, ransoms and running skirmishes followed, with neither
side able to force a lasting settlement. It was Captain Samuel
Argall who finally found the opening the colony needed - not against
Powhatan himself, but against his daughter. Learning that Pocahontas
was staying at Patawomeke, Argall enlisted an old acquaintance named
Japazaws to lure her aboard his ship with the promise of a copper
kettle, a bribe Japazaws found irresistible enough to stage-manage his
own wife's tears to make the invitation convincing. Once Pocahontas
was aboard and the kettle delivered, Argall told her plainly, in front
of her weeping hosts, that she would not see her father again until
her captivity had bought peace between his people and the colony.

## A father who would not come in person

Powhatan's reply took three months. When it came, it carried seven
returned Englishmen, ineffective muskets by way of restitution, and a
promise of corn and lasting friendship in exchange for his daughter -
an offer the colony judged insufficient and answered by keeping
Pocahontas regardless. Silence followed for months more, until Sir
Thomas Dale sailed up Powhatan's own river with a hundred and fifty
armed men and the captive herself, met by four hundred of Powhatan's
warriors at a town called Matchot who dared the English to come
ashore and then, having made their show of defiance, simply asked to
confer instead. Powhatan did not come to negotiate his own daughter's
ransom in person at any point in the standoff - it was two of his
sons, sent to see their sister, who opened the path to the peace that
would settle the matter for good.
