When the English colonists who founded Jamestown in 1607 began
exploring the rivers around their new settlement, they found a
political order already old and already vast: some thirty tribes of
the Virginia Tidewater, each under its own werowance, all of them
answering to a single paramount ruler the English called Powhatan.
His own court stood at Werowocomoco on the York river, where visitors
found him seated before a fire on a bedstead-like throne, wrapped in a
robe of raccoon skins with all the tails still hanging from it, a
young woman on either hand and two long rows of men and painted women
lining the house behind him. No European power had built this
confederacy or given Powhatan his authority over it. The English had
arrived, without quite grasping it at first, in the middle of another
empire's own established order.

<Figure id="po-smith" />

John Smith learned exactly how established it was in December 1607,
when a Powhatan hunting party captured him while he explored the
Chickahominy river and marched him from town to town before finally
bringing him to Werowocomoco itself. More than two hundred of
Powhatan's court stood watching him as though he were a monster. After
a long consultation, two great stones were brought before the ruler,
and as many hands as could reach him dragged Smith to lay his head
upon them, clubs raised to beat out his brains.

## A daughter's intervention

By Smith's own account, it was Powhatan's daughter Pocahontas -
described as the ruler's dearest child, no more than ten or twelve
years old at the time - who stopped the execution: when no pleading
could move the assembly, she took Smith's head in her arms and laid
her own upon it, and Powhatan relented, content instead to have Smith
make hatchets for him and beads for her. Whatever the full truth
behind the moment, it marked the start of a relationship the
confederacy and the colony would spend the next fifteen years unable
to settle into anything permanent - alternating between trade,
war, and, eventually, a peace bought with a second and far more
lasting captivity.
