The prologue, by the colonists' own account, was the death of a
warrior called Nemattanow - Jack of the Feather to the English, so
named for the plumage he wore into battle, and considered by his own
people nearly immortal in combat. Accused of murdering a settler
named Morgan, Nemattanow was shot by two of the dead man's servants
when he returned wearing Morgan's cap; dying, he asked only that his
killers not reveal he had been shot, and that they bury him among the
English. Opechancanough, by the colonists' account, grieved openly
and threatened revenge - and then, within a fortnight, sent back word
so full of professed love and peace that the English governor
believed the country more settled than at any time since the
colony's founding.

It was calculated deception. For weeks Opechancanough's people
guided English travellers through the woods with every appearance of
friendship, borrowed English boats, and, as late as the evening before
the attack, sat down to breakfast at English tables carrying deer,
turkeys and fish to sell. On the morning of March 22, 1622, at
plantations scattered the length of the James river, Powhatan's
warriors turned on their hosts with the settlers' own tools, killing
without regard to age or sex before anyone could recognize the blow
that struck them down. Three hundred and forty-seven English men,
women and children died that single morning - roughly a quarter of
the colony's population - and the attackers did not stop at the
killing, mutilating bodies afterward and carrying pieces away as
trophies.

## No ending in sight

What the massacre opened, Smith's own account does not live to close.
The war that followed ran on past the point where his narrative,
compiled and published in 1624, simply stops giving new events -
Opechancanough still very much alive, still the confederacy's
effective ruler in all but name, and still, so far as this source
records, unanswered for what his warriors had done. The peace a
marriage had bought in 1614 was gone in a single coordinated morning
eight years later, and nothing in this account suggests either side
believed it could be rebuilt the same way twice.
