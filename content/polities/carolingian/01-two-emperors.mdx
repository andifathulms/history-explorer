title: Two emperors, and an embassy

On Christmas Day 800 the pope crowned Charlemagne emperor in Rome. There had not
been an emperor in the west since 476, and there was already one in the east, who
regarded the coronation as a usurpation — the more so because the eastern throne
was at that moment occupied by a woman, Irene, which the Frankish position held
to mean it was vacant.

The empire itself was Frankish expansion given a Roman title: Gaul, most of
Germany after thirty years of Saxon wars, northern Italy, and a march across the
Pyrenees. It was administered through counts and royal envoys rather than a
bureaucracy, and its most durable product was not political but cultural — a
programme of manuscript copying and script reform in the monasteries that
preserved the bulk of the Latin literature that survives, in a new hand,
Carolingian minuscule, that is the ancestor of the lower case in this sentence.

Its relevance to this site is an exchange. Charlemagne and Harun al-Rashid sent
embassies to each other, and Harun sent an elephant named Abul-Abbas, which
walked from Baghdad to Aachen and lived there for eight years. Both rulers had a
reason: Harun's rival was the Umayyad emirate at Cordoba, Charlemagne's was
Byzantium, and each was the other's enemy's enemy. It is the clearest single
illustration that the polities on this site were one connected political world
and knew it.

The empire was divided among Louis the Pious's sons at Verdun in 843 and had
effectively dissolved by 888. This site types that `fragmentation`: inheritance
practice, not defeat.
