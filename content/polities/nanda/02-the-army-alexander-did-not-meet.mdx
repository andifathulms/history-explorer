In 326 BC, on the banks of the Hyphasis — the modern Beas, the furthest
point east Alexander's army would ever reach — the Macedonian advance
stopped for good. The immediate cause was exhaustion: years from home, in
monsoon rain, Alexander's own veterans refused to go further. But the
intelligence that reached the camp about what lay ahead did nothing to
change their minds.

## What the Greeks were told

A native chieftain, reported by the Greek sources as Bhagala or Phegelas,
told Alexander of a kingdom on the Ganges ruled by a monarch the Greeks
transliterated as Xandrames or Agrammes — a name that can only be a
garbled rendering of one of the Nanda kings, since the capital of the
people in question, the Prasii, was unmistakably Pataliputra. The
information was confirmed independently by Porus, whom Alexander had
already defeated and had every reason to trust on this point rather than
resent it.

The army this king could field was reported at twenty thousand cavalry,
two hundred thousand infantry, two thousand chariots, and between three
and four thousand elephants — a force on a scale nothing Alexander had
fought in Persia or the Punjab approached. The reigning king, the same
report added, was widely hated for his wickedness and his base origin: the
son of a barber who had seduced the previous queen, engineered the old
king's death, and murdered his way to the throne under cover of a false
guardianship. It is, in every particular but the names, the same story the
Puranas tell about Mahapadma Nanda.

## A battle that never happened

Whether the army's size alone decided anything is impossible to know; the
mutiny in Alexander's own ranks had been building before the report
arrived. But the two facts landed together, and Alexander turned back
without ever testing whether the numbers he had been given were real.

Magadha's reputation for overwhelming force was thereby fixed in the Greek
record by a battle that was never fought — the largest army west of the
Ganges never learning firsthand whether the largest army east of it could
actually have stopped them, and choosing, on secondhand report alone, not
to find out.
