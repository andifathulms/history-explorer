---
title: The empire Rome could not finish
drafted_from: chi-3
phase: peak
---

The Arsacids began as a nomadic group that took the Seleucid satrapy of Parthia
around 247 BC and spent the next century working west, taking Media and then
Mesopotamia. They held Iran for four hundred and seventy years, which is longer
than the Sasanians who replaced them and longer than any other polity in this
site's Iranian regions.

They are also the worst documented. The Parthians left very little writing of
their own; almost everything comes from Greek and Roman sources describing an
enemy, and from coins. That imbalance produced a long scholarly habit of treating
them as a loosely organised interlude between two real Iranian empires, which
their four and a half centuries and their record against Rome do not support.

That record: Crassus destroyed at Carrhae in 53 BC with seven legions lost, Mark
Antony's invasion beaten in 36 BC, and a frontier on the Euphrates that Rome
crossed repeatedly and never held. Trajan reached the Persian Gulf in 116 and the
gains were abandoned within two years.

The empire was governed loosely — great houses with their own armies, vassal
kingdoms, Greek cities keeping their institutions — and that decentralisation is
both why it lasted and why Ardashir, a vassal in Fars, could kill Artabanus IV in
224 and take it.
