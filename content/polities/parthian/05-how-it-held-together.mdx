There was no Parthian bureaucracy in the Achaemenid or Sasanian sense. There was
a king of kings, over an assortment of things.

Great noble houses — the Suren, the Karen, the Mihran — holding hereditary
territories and hereditary offices with their own armed followings. Vassal
kingdoms in Persis, Elymais, Characene, Adiabene, Media Atropatene and Armenia,
each with its own dynasty and its own coinage. Greek cities keeping their
assemblies and their civic institutions. Nomadic groups on the eastern frontier
under their own chiefs.

The army was the sum of what those parties supplied, and the most famous Parthian
victory in history was won by a force raised and commanded by the head of one
noble house: Surena at Carrhae, with cataphracts, horse archers and a baggage
train of arrows. The king had him executed shortly afterwards, which tells you
exactly what the relationship was.

## What that costs, and what it buys

An army that can make a ruler will eventually make one it prefers. The Parthian
version of that is chronic rather than acute. Arsacid civil wars are more or less
continuous from the first century BC, with rival kings coining simultaneously and
the great houses backing candidates on their own account. Rome repeatedly
sponsored claimants, and the frontier wars are frequently interventions in a
succession rather than campaigns of conquest.

It is also why the empire lasted four hundred and seventy years.

A state that is a coalition cannot be destroyed by defeating its king, because
the coalition survives him. Rome demonstrated this three times, by taking
Ctesiphon and finding that nothing followed. Trajan in 116, Lucius Verus's
generals in 165, Septimius Severus in 198: three sacks of the capital, and no
conquest.

The end came from inside the structure, which is the only place it could have
come from. Ardashir was a sub-king in Persis, one of the vassal dynasties, and in
224 he killed Artabanus IV in battle and took the empire. The coalition did not
fail; it changed which of its members was in charge of it.
