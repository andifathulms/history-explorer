The decree of 1860 left southern Borneo with a war and no sultanate to
formally end it in the name of. What followed was the unwinding of a
resistance rather than the transfer of an office.

## The two claimants' separate ends

Antasari, who had opened the war with the attack on the Pengaron mine, went
on leading Banjarese resistance from the interior after the abolition
decree, refusing Dutch offers of accommodation, until his death from
smallpox in October 1862. Hidayatullah, whose overruled claim to the throne
had been the dispute's original legal grievance, continued the fight after
Antasari's death before being captured by Dutch forces; he was exiled to
Java's north coast for the rest of his life rather than executed, which the
colonial administration judged the safer way to dispose of a man whose
testamentary claim to the throne it had itself once acknowledged existed.

Scattered resistance in Banjarese and neighbouring Dayak territory continued
in diminishing form for years after both leaders were gone, without ever
again coalescing around a single claimant or a plausible route back to a
restored sultanate.

## What was actually being fought for by the end

By the war's later years, the fighting had outgrown its original dynastic
question. What remained contested was not whether Tamjidillah or
Hidayatullah should have been sultan — that question the Dutch decree had
already made irrelevant to both men's actual prospects — but whether Dutch
administration would extend fully into the interior of southern Borneo at
all. It did, eventually, and the pepper and diamond country the earlier
chapters describe became a colonial residency rather than a client
sultanate's own territory.

## What the sultanate left behind

Antasari is remembered in independent Indonesia as a national hero, and the
war that broke his sultanate's own formal existence is remembered as one of
the region's principal episodes of resistance to Dutch colonial expansion —
a reputation the sultanate's own two centuries of pepper contracts and
diamond gifts never earned it in its own lifetime. It took the state's
ending, rather than anything it did while it existed, to make it a name
people outside southern Borneo would keep.
