In 1586 Senapati, the ruler of Mataram — a vassal territory Hadiwijaya had
granted to his own foster-father — took Pajang. `ended.by` names Mataram and the
edge is drawn.

It is the third Javanese succession in sixty years to run that way: a subordinate
displacing an overlord who had himself displaced one. Jaka Tingkir was Demak's
man before he was Demak's end; Senapati was Pajang's before he was Pajang's. And
it is the last of them for a long time, because what Senapati founded held Java
until 1755.

## What this page completes

With this record and Mataram's, Java's sequence on this site now runs unbroken
for seven hundred years:

Kediri, from 1042 — Singhasari, from 1222 — second-Kediri, for twelve months —
Majapahit, from 1293 — Demak, from 1475 — Pajang, from 1568 — Mataram, from 1587
to the division of 1755.

Seven records joined by six succession edges and one `resumes`, each ending
named by the next in its own `ended.by`, with no gap between any of them wider
than the fourteen years between Demak's collapse in 1554 and Hadiwijaya's
recognition at Pajang. There is a seventh edge inside the run — `singhasari →
majapahit`, `claimed legitimacy of`, for Raden Wijaya presenting himself as
Kertanegara's heir — which vaults over the twelve months of second-Kediri rather
than joining consecutive records.

Nothing else on this site is continuous like that. The Iranian and Anatolian
regions have longer threads with more records in them, and they run through
conquests by outsiders who arrived from elsewhere; this one is seven polities on
one island, each of which was inside the previous one before it replaced it.

There is one absence at the head of it and this site is explicit about it. The
Medang record's last chapter declines to draw an edge from eighth-century central
Java to Kediri, because what Kediri came out of is Kahuripan, which has no page
— and drawing the edge anyway would be the mistake the archipelago's records
already had to correct once. So the chain starts in 1042 rather than 732, and the reason is
written down rather than invisible.
