This record exists because another one asked for it.

The Demak record's last chapter, written a pass ago, ends by saying that its
`ended.by` was empty because Pajang had no page, that Pajang was the smallest
identifiable hole left in the archipelago, and that writing it would close one field
and would carry an unusual double claim. That is what this page is.

Demak's `ended.by` now reads `[pajang]`. The edge `demak → pajang` is drawn,
typed `overthrew`, at 1554.

## The double claim

Jaka Tingkir — Hadiwijaya, once he had the throne — won the civil war that
followed Sultan Trenggana's death, killed Arya Penangsang, and moved the centre
of Javanese power inland to Pajang in the middle Solo valley.

He then argued his right to it from two directions at once. He claimed descent
from Trenggana, whose dynasty he had just finished off. And he claimed descent
from Brawijaya, the last king of Majapahit, whose kingdom Trenggana's dynasty
had absorbed thirty years earlier.

This record's `legitimation` field carries `descent` for both, and Demak's
carries the identical pair for its own founder — Raden Patah's line traced to
Majapahit's royal house, which is the Majapahit record's single successor claim.

Two consecutive Javanese polities argued their standing by descent from the two
things the previous one had destroyed, and each of them was itself destroyed by
somebody making the same argument about it.

## Inland

The one substantive thing in this record's coded fields is `revenue_basis`:
`land-tax`, and nothing else.

Demak was a north-coast port with a fleet, coded `land-tax` and `trade-toll`,
which is how it could sell rice to the archipelago and send ships against
Portuguese Melaka. Pajang is up the Solo valley with no coast at all. Mataram
after it is coded the same way.

The centre of Javanese power went inland in 1568 and did not come back to the
sea. Everything that follows on this site's Java pages — Sultan Agung's armies
starving outside Batavia because the coast was somebody else's, the Dutch company
buying its way into Javanese successions from the ports — follows from a
kingdom that lasted eighteen years choosing a river valley.
