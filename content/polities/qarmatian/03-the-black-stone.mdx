---
title: Twenty-two years in al-Hasa
drafted_from: daftary-2007
phase: peak
---

In 930 Abu Tahir al-Jannabi took an army into Mecca during the pilgrimage. His
men killed pilgrims in the sanctuary, threw bodies into the well of Zamzam,
and prised the Black Stone out of the corner of the Ka'ba and carried it back
to eastern Arabia. It stayed there for twenty-two years. The Abbasids
eventually paid an enormous sum to get it back, and it was returned in 951.

It is easy to file this as banditry and the sources hostile to the Qarmatians
were happy to. It was an argument. The Isma'ili expectation was that the mahdi
would abrogate the outward law when he came, and the attack asserted that the
age of the law was ending: that the pilgrimage was now void and the stone an
object rather than a sacrament. This site's Nizari page has the same doctrine
arriving at the same conclusion two and a half centuries later, when Hasan II
declared the Resurrection at Alamut and had the fast broken in the courtyard.

The difference is that Alamut did it to its own community and Mecca was done to
everybody. The cost fell hardest on the Isma'ilis who had nothing to do with
it: the Fatimids in Cairo, building a caliphate on the same doctrinal family,
spent the following century being associated with the sack of the holy city by
opponents who did not care to distinguish the branches. Cairo repudiated it and
the repudiation was not much believed.

The stone went back in 951, after the Abbasids had paid an enormous sum for it,
and the sources report that it was returned wrapped in a sack with a note. What
the raid had established was that the Qarmatians could do anything they liked to
the pilgrimage and nobody could stop them — which was true, and turned out to be
worth less than it sounds. The next chapter is about the state that could do
that, and the one after it about the year the argument collapsed.
