title: Eighty days

In 931 Abu Tahir handed the state to somebody else. A young Persian had arrived
at al-Hasa, and Abu Tahir was persuaded — the sources differ on by whom and on
what evidence — that this was the awaited one: Muhammad ibn Isma'il returned,
the mahdi whose expected coming was the entire basis of Qarmatian authority.

The young man behaved as the doctrine said the mahdi would. He abolished the
law, since the law was the outward form that the mahdi's coming would supersede.
He cursed the prophets, including Muhammad. He ordered the execution of
notables, including members of Abu Tahir's own family. After about eighty days
Abu Tahir concluded he had been deceived, and had him put to death.

Nothing else in this corpus is quite so completely self-inflicted. A polity
whose sole claim was that it knew who the mahdi was and could recognise him had
publicly, spectacularly, failed to — in front of its own community and in front
of the entire Isma'ili world it had spent thirty years telling that the Fatimid
caliph was an impostor. The prestige never came back. Qarmatian doctrine
afterwards is described as having quietly returned to expecting the mahdi in the
future, which is a difficult position for people who have just executed one.

The decline afterwards is unremarkable and took a hundred and forty years. Abu
Tahir died in 944. The council governed. Abbasid and Buyid campaigns from the
970s pushed the state back to eastern Arabia; a Qarmatian attempt on Fatimid
Syria failed; Bahrain island broke away in 1058, and by 1077 the Uyunids —
local, Sunni, and backed by the Seljuks — had taken al-Hasa.

This page carries no reach figure and no population, and the ending is typed
`gradual absorption` with no agent named, because none of the sources read here
gives one. What the record has is a hundred and seventy years, a coherent and
genuinely radical argument about property and law, the only occasion on which
the Ka'ba was dismantled in part and carried into the desert, and eighty days in
931 that undid all of it.
