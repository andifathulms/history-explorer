The end was quick once Syria was consolidated. Abd al-Malik dealt with the
Qaysi opposition in the north, then took Iraq: Mus'ab ibn al-Zubayr, his
brother's governor there, was defeated and killed at Maskin in 691. The
following year al-Hajjaj brought an army to Mecca and besieged it for months.
Ibn al-Zubayr's supporters drifted away and two of his sons took the amnesty
offered. He did not, and was killed fighting near the sanctuary in 692, aged
about seventy.

Al-Hajjaj then did something worth noticing. Ibn al-Zubayr had rebuilt the
Ka'ba after the fire of 683 in an altered form, on the argument that he was
restoring the shape the Prophet had wanted. Al-Hajjaj pulled the alteration
down and put the building back the way it had been. A physical object was
returned to its previous state because the political question above it had been
settled, which is as literal a statement of what the victory meant as the
period offers.

The historiographical point is the reason this page exists. For nine years this
polity was acknowledged by more of the Islamic world than the Umayyads were,
struck coin in its own name from Egypt to Khurasan, and held the two holy
cities. It is nonetheless called the second fitna, and its head is called a
rebel or a counter-caliph, because the other side won and the account that
reached us was written under the dynasty that beat him and then under the
dynasty that beat them. This site's Umayyad page carries Hawting's warning
about exactly that filter operating one step further along.

The judgement historians do make is narrower and more interesting than
legitimacy. Ibn al-Zubayr sponsored no doctrine and no programme — unlike the
Alids, and unlike the Kharijites who were fighting their own war at the same
time — and Wellhausen's verdict that the struggle turned around him nominally
while he took little part in it has stuck. A claim to the caliphate on
procedural grounds, held by a man of impeccable standing who offered nothing
else, turned out to be a thing many people would recognise and few would fight
for.
