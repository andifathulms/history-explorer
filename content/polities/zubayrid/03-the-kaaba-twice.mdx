---
title: The Ka'ba, twice
drafted_from: hawting-2000
phase: aside
---

The building at the centre of this polity was demolished and rebuilt twice in
thirteen years, both times for political reasons, and the second rebuilding
undid the first. It is the clearest object lesson in this corpus about what a
victory is.

In 683 the Umayyad army besieging Mecca bombarded the city, and the Ka'ba
caught fire and was badly damaged — the covering burned and the structure
cracked. The siege lifted when Yazid died. Ibn al-Zubayr then pulled the
remains down and rebuilt it, and he did not rebuild it as it had been. On the
argument, attributed to a report from the Prophet's wife A'isha, that Quraysh
had run short of funds when they last rebuilt it and had therefore left part of
the original foundation outside the walls, he extended the building to enclose
the semicircular area on its north-west side, and he put in two doors at
ground level so that people could walk through.

Al-Hajjaj took Mecca in 692, killed him, and wrote to Abd al-Malik for
instructions. The answer was to put it back. The extension was demolished, the
enclosed ground was excluded again, one door was blocked and the other raised
above the ground where it had been. The building has been in that shape since.

Two things are worth saying about it and this site says both.

The first is what it demonstrates. Ibn al-Zubayr's rebuilding was an act of
authority of the most fundamental kind available in that religion — deciding
the form of the house — and it lasted nine years, exactly as long as his
caliphate. The reversal was not vandalism and was not, on the Umayyad account,
even a rejection: al-Hajjaj's stated reason was that the building should be as
the Prophet had known it and that a rebel's alteration had no standing. A
physical object was returned to its previous state because the political
question above it had been settled, and it stayed that way.

The second is about the record. Almost everything here comes from Abbasid-era
compilers with reasons to think poorly of both parties, and the A'isha report
that justifies the extension is exactly the kind of tradition whose date is
impossible to establish. This site's Rashidun page has a chapter on that
problem in general. Here it has a specific shape: we can see the building, and
we cannot independently check the argument either man made about it.
