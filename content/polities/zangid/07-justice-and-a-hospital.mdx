title: A hospital, and a room to be complained in

The part of Nur al-Din's programme that outlasted him was not the campaigning.
It was domestic, and it was built in the same years.

He built madrasas across Syria in numbers — Damascus and Aleppo especially —
endowing a Sunni religious establishment with the instrument Nizam al-Mulk had
invented for the Seljuks a century earlier. He built Sufi lodges. He restored
the walls and the water supply of Damascus.

Two of the foundations are worth naming individually.

The **Bimaristan al-Nuri** at Damascus, endowed in 1154, was a hospital and a
teaching hospital: wards, a pharmacy, salaried physicians, and instruction. It
went on functioning as a hospital and a medical school into the nineteenth
century, seven hundred years, and the doctors attached to it wrote a
substantial medical literature. Ibn al-Nafis, who described the pulmonary
circulation of the blood three centuries before Europe did, trained there.

The **Dar al-Adl** — the house of justice — was something new. Nur al-Din built
the first one at Damascus around 1163: a hall in which the ruler himself sat,
in public, on fixed days, to hear grievances brought by his subjects against
his own officials and soldiers, with the jurists present. The institution
spread; the Ayyubids and then the Mamluks built their own. What it was for is
legible. A Turkish military ruler with no lineage, governing an Arab
population, asking it to pay for a long war, made his answerability the public
face of his government.

It is the standing problem of every Turkish dynasty in the Arab world, and it
was answered in a number of ways — the Marinids with madrasas, the Almohads
with a doctrine, the Fatimids with a mission. Nur al-Din built a room in which
he could be complained to.

His reputation followed accordingly. The chroniclers of the next century,
writing under the Ayyubids and Mamluks, made him the model just ruler — pious,
austere, accessible — against whom later sultans were measured. Saladin, who
took his state, spent a good deal of effort being seen to continue it.
