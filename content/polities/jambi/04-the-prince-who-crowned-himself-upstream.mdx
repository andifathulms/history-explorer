The prosperity that let Jambi sack Johor Lama in 1673 did not survive
the following decade. By the 1680s Jambi had lost its position as the
main pepper port on Sumatra's east coast, worn down by the clash with
Johor and by upheaval inside its own court; the English closed their
trading post there in 1679, and tensions between the upstream (Ulu)
and downstream (Ilir) halves of the kingdom, already strained by trade
disputes, debt and smuggling, were pulling the sultanate apart from
within even as its outward reach shrank.

## A sultan taken to Batavia twice over

The Dutch East India Company, largely passive through Jambi's earlier
prosperity, turned active the moment the court's own leadership grew
unstable. In 1688 the sultan was arrested when he came to the Dutch
trading post to answer an invitation, and was shipped off to Batavia.
Worse followed in 1690: the head of the Dutch trading office at Muara
Kumpeh was killed in an act the Company blamed on the sultan's own
household, and the Dutch responded by arresting the sultan himself,
deporting him first to Batavia and then into exile at Banda - and, in
the same stroke, passing over the rightful heir, Pangeran Ratu, in
favor of installing the exiled sultan's own son as the new Dutch-backed
ruler instead.

## Two courts on the same river

Pangeran Ratu did not accept the substitution. He withdrew upriver to
Muara Tebo and had himself crowned there under the title Sultan Sri
Maharaja Batu, drawing support from Minangkabau migrants already
settled in the interior and winning recognition from the Minangkabau
king at Pagaruyung himself - a rival court answering to a different
patron than the Dutch-backed sultanate that continued at the old
downstream capital, Tanah Pilih. Eight years later, in 1698, the
sultanate's own resentment of Dutch interference erupted again: Sybrant
Swart, the head of the Company's trading office at Muara Kumpeh, was
killed along with his staff - a second act of violence against the
Company's own local presence within a decade, and a clear sign that
neither Jambi court, upstream or down, had made its peace with the
arrangement the Dutch had imposed.
