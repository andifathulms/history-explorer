Sultan Thaha Syaifuddin took the throne in 1855 and moved immediately
to repudiate what his own father had signed. He refused to recognize
the 1833 and 1835 agreements, declined to meet a Dutch delegation sent
to deliver a letter from the Governor-General, and in 1857 sent his
own crown prince to read a letter to the Ottoman representative in
Singapore - asking the Sultan of Turkey, recognized across much of the
Islamic world as its Caliph, to declare a formal ban on any outside
power's interference in Jambi's internal affairs. It was a direct
appeal past the Dutch entirely, to the one authority Thaha judged
might actually outrank them.

## Deposed by declaration

The Dutch answered with an ultimatum Thaha ignored, and on 25
September 1858 they announced they no longer recognized his rule at
all, treating him as removed from the throne by their own decree
rather than by any Jambi institution. An attack on Jambi followed that
November; Thaha, his crown prince and several other court officials
withdrew to Muara Tembesi to organize resistance from there, while the
Dutch installed his own uncle, Peramudjaja, as a replacement sultan
under the title Ahmad Nazaruddin - one more agreement extracted from
the new court, reaffirming everything the old one had signed and
adding formal recognition that Jambi and its conquered territories now
counted as part of the Dutch East Indies outright.

## A crown in exile that would not disappear

Exile cost Thaha the throne but not his following. He kept the loyalty
of much of the aristocracy and ordinary population from Muara Tembesi
for decades, and his supporters kept up sporadic armed raids on Dutch
posts throughout the following years, fighting with little more than
daggers, spears and axes against a Dutch army equipped with rifles and
cannon - a mismatch that cost the resistance dearly in every direct
engagement but never quite extinguished it. In 1889 Thaha's own
brother, Pangeran Dipa Negara, built a fortified base at Muara Tabir,
and a cousin, Prince Kusin, built another at Muara Sekanis; from both
strongholds they collected tolls from passing merchants and declared
that no European would be permitted to set foot in the territory they
held. By early 1903, worn down and increasingly isolated, Thaha's own
son surrendered the territory still under his family's authority to
the Dutch outright, handing over the crown prince's own royal regalia
to the Resident in person. Around the same time a separate prince of
the family, Thaha's own son-in-law Prabu Negara, delivered the sacred
kris Siginjai - the heirloom that had marked the legitimacy of Jambi's
rulers since the sixteenth century - into Dutch hands as well.
