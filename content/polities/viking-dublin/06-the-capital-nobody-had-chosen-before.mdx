No Irish record calls the place where Dublin stands a city, or even a
place of any real importance, before the ninth century. The annals
know a harbour, the Dubhlinn, and a river crossing, the Ath Cliath,
but no dun or rath at either site was ever the seat of an Irish king or
the centre of an Irish territory. The kings of Ireland sat at Tara; the
kings of Leinster, the province Dublin sits in, ruled from Naas and
from Ferns. Whatever Dublin became, it did not become it as an Irish
capital first.

## What Henry found when he took it

Henry II made Dublin the seat of his own Irish lordship not because he
judged it the natural capital of the island he claimed, and not
because its harbour beat Wexford's or Waterford's - the ports through
which England's own traffic with Ireland actually ran at the time. He
took it because it was the principal city of the Ostmen he had just
conquered, the largest and richest prize among the Norse and
Hiberno-Norse territories Strongbow's war had put within his reach. He
left Strongbow in possession of the Leinster lands his marriage had
brought him, but claimed by right of conquest the Ostmen cities
outright - Dublin, Wexford, Waterford and Limerick - and carved four
royal manors for the crown itself out of the countryside that had
belonged to Dublin's own Norse kings.

Dublin was the seat of Ostman sovereignty before Henry arrived. Once
he took it, it became the capital of his own Irish dominion instead -
and as that dominion grew to cover the island, the city grew with it
into the capital of Ireland entire.

## A kingdom, then a name on a map

None of the Norse dynasty's own political weight survived into this
new arrangement. Hasculf's failed attempt to retake the city with a
hired berserker was the kingdom's last independent act, and no Ostman
ruler held it in his own right again. What outlasted the dynasty was only the place itself, and the accident
of which city a foreign king's war had happened to hand him - a Norse
trading settlement's own choice of harbour, made three centuries
earlier for reasons that had nothing to do with Ireland's internal
geography, and never displaced again as the government of Ireland
grew up around it.
