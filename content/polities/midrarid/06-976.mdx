The last Midrarid was removed in 976 and nobody recorded much about it.

By then the dynasty had been restored and deposed several times, had changed
sect, and had spent most of a century as the client of whichever caliphate was
currently paying for the Maghreb. The removal was not a conquest of Sijilmasa —
Sijilmasa went on, richer than most places in Africa, for another five hundred
years. It was the point at which the family that had founded the town stopped
being the people who held it.

## An ending with nothing in it

There is no siege, no last stand, and no agreement among the sources about which
Midrarid was the last one. That is unusual even by the standards of small
dynasties, and it says something precise about what this state had become.

A dynasty that matters produces a contested ending: somebody writes down who took
it and from whom, because the succession is worth arguing about. Sijilmasa's
chroniclers were interested in the town, the mint and the caravans, and by the
970s the ruling family was an administrative detail of an asset that Cairo and
Córdoba were fighting over.

## What outlasted it

Everything except the dynasty and the doctrine.

The mint went on striking. The caravans went on assembling in the autumn and
leaving for the south. The customs house went on collecting. The Zanata held the
town, then the Almoravids took it in 1055 and used its gold to conquer Morocco
and Spain, then the Almohads, then the Marinids.

Four centuries after the Midrarids, Ibn Battuta walked into a handsome city with
the best dates in the world and lodged with a scholar whose brother lived in
China. Nothing in his account suggests he had any idea whose town it had
originally been.
