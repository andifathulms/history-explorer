---
title: The prisoner in the oasis
drafted_from: halm-1996
phase: contraction
---

Around 905 a man arrived in Sijilmasa with his young son and settled quietly as
a merchant. He was Abd Allah, the head of the Isma'ili mission — the hidden imam whose
agents were at that moment converting the Kutama Berbers of the Kabylie in his
name. He had come west from Syria ahead of Abbasid agents and chosen the far end
of the world to wait in.

The Midrarid ruler eventually worked out who his guest was and imprisoned him.
The reasons the sources give are the ordinary ones: pressure from Baghdad, and
the ruler's own reluctance to be holding a man claiming to be the mahdi.

In 909 the Kutama army finished the Aghlabids at Raqqada and then marched west
across the Maghreb — a very long way, for one prisoner — took Sijilmasa, and
released him. Abd Allah was proclaimed caliph and mahdi, and the Fatimid caliphate dates from
that moment. The dynasty that would rule Egypt for two centuries and found Cairo
collected its founder from a cell in this oasis.

The town was left with a Fatimid governor and then, repeatedly, without one.
Sijilmasa was too far from Ifriqiya to hold and too valuable to abandon, so the
tenth century runs through a sequence of Midrarid restorations, Fatimid
reconquests, a ruler who converted to Sunnism and declared for the Umayyads of
Cordoba, and the proxy war between Cairo and Córdoba that
consumed the far Maghreb for most of the century. The dynasty was finally removed in
976, and the town passed between Zanata clients until the Almoravids took it in 1055 — which
gave a desert movement a bullion supply before it had a country.

Nobody can say with confidence which Midrarid was the last, because the sequence
of restorations is disputed in the sources.

What is worth keeping is the disproportion. A walled oasis town of no military
weight sat on the pipe through which West African gold reached the Mediterranean
for two hundred years, minted it — and happened to be holding the founder of the
Fatimid caliphate in a cell when his army came to collect him.
