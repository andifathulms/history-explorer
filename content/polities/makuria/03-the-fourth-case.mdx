The Makurian throne passed to the king's sister's son. It is in the Arabic
accounts, in the Nubian material, and in the way the royal women are titled — the
king's mother and the king's sister hold formal position and appear in the wall
paintings at Faras alongside the kings.

This record's `succession_rule` now reads `matrilineal`. It was null when this
chapter was written, and the chapter is left as it was written, because what it
records is the argument that changed the vocabulary.

## Why it was empty

The seven values this corpus allowed were primogeniture, tanistry, appanage,
nomination, election, acclamation and factional. None of them described descent
traced through the female line to a specific person.

`tanistry` is the closest and is about choosing a successor from a defined pool
of kin, which is a rule about selection. This is a rule about descent. Coding
rule 7 takes the rule in force and the vocabulary could not hold this one, so
the field was empty and the chapter was the answer — the same treatment the
Ghana record gave the same problem one pass earlier.

## Where the count stands

The Ghana record's chapter on this closed with a paragraph saying the note was
there so that a fourth case had somewhere to arrive. Here it is, one pass later.

**Ghana**, before 1068, where al-Bakri states the rule outright and gives the
reason he was given — a man can be certain of his sister's child and not of his
wife's.

**Mali**, in 1352, where Ibn Battuta records the practice with disapproval as a
general rule of inheritance and the royal succession shows the same mixed shape.

**Elam**, in the third and second millennia BC, noted on this corpus's Ur III
record's last chapter: the throne passing from brother to brother and then to a
sister's son, in a period six hundred years before the earliest Elamite record
this site carries.

**Makuria**, from the seventh century to the fourteenth.

Four records. Three regions — the Ancient Near East, Africa's Niger and Africa's
Nile. Something over two and a half thousand years between the earliest and the
latest. No contact between any of them, and no possibility of transmission
between the Elamite case and the other three.

## No proposal, at the time

This site has changed a closed vocabulary once, the paragraph here said.
`investiture` was added to the `legitimation` list after nine records in four
regions had each recorded, on the page, that the list could not say the thing
that mattered most about them. The threshold was not a number chosen in advance;
it was the point at which the gap had stopped looking like an awkward case and
started looking like a category.

Four is not nine, it went on, and what had changed with this record was narrower:
matrilineal succession was no longer a curiosity about one page but a documented
constitutional arrangement, attested independently on three continents, that the
corpus could describe and could not code.

It ended: *a fifth case would make the argument on the merits that the rulebook
requires. This paragraph is where it would start.*

## And the fifth arrived

Two passes later, from the direction this chapter had guessed. Asante, on the
Akan goldfields, where the Asantehene succeeded through the matriclan, the queen
mother nominated from the eligible matrilineage and a council of chiefs confirmed
or refused — documented in institutional detail by Wilks rather than reported by
a foreign visitor.

That record's second chapter set out the argument, the value was added, and this
field, the Ghana field and the Asante field were all filled in the same pass.

The chapter above is left standing. A site that quietly replaced the reasoning
with the answer would be a worse record of how it arrived at anything.

## One thing the null does not mean

It does not mean the succession was unstable.

That is worth separating out, because most of the null `succession_rule` fields
in this corpus are null for the opposite reason — the Second Bulgarian record has
twenty transfers in two hundred and eleven years and no stated rule, and the Mali
record's field is empty because nobody wrote one down.

Makuria's rule was known, followed, and produced a kingdom that lasted seven
hundred years on a frontier with the Islamic world. The field is empty because
the site cannot write the answer down, not because the kingdom could not.
