In 1276 a Mamluk expedition beat the Nubian army, took Dongola, and installed a
king who agreed to pay tribute. That is the end of the baqt, which had been an
exchange between two parties, and the beginning of something else.

This corpus's Mamluk record disposes of it in a clause. Baybars, it says, made
the Nubian kingdoms tributary and took the Red Sea ports — one item in a list of
how that state engineered its inputs, alongside the alliance with the Golden
Horde and the negotiation with Byzantium over the straits.

This is the page of what it was done to.

## Ninety years

What followed was not a conquest and was worse.

Mamluk expeditions came repeatedly, installing and deposing kings; Makurian
claimants went to Cairo to be backed; the succession this record's third chapter
describes was fought over by candidates with Egyptian sponsors. In 1317 the
audience hall of the royal palace at Dongola was converted into a mosque and an
inscription was set up recording it — the building and the inscription both
survive — and there was a Muslim on the Makurian throne.

Underneath that, two slower things. Arab groups had been moving into the Nile
valley and the grasslands beside it for generations, marrying into local
families; and because Makurian descent ran through the mother, the sons of Arab
fathers and Nubian mothers were Nubian heirs. The matrilineal rule that this
site cannot code is one of the mechanisms by which the kingdom's ruling class
became Muslim without anybody conquering it.

The church went the other way for a reason of its own. Nubian bishops were
consecrated in Alexandria, and the Coptic patriarchate under Mamluk rule was
under pressure and increasingly unable to supply them. This corpus's Aksum
record says the Alexandrian connection is what kept Ethiopian Christianity alive
for a thousand years. This is the same supply line, seen at the point where it
failed.

In 1365 the court left Dongola for Gebel Adda, far to the north, and this record
ends there.

## `gradual absorption`, and what it is doing

The field says `gradual absorption` with `by` naming the Mamluk record, and both
halves need the qualification this corpus's African endings keep needing.

Nobody annexed Makuria. There is no year of conquest, no treaty of cession, and
Egypt never administered the middle Nile. What there is is ninety years of
intervention by a neighbour that mostly wanted the frontier quiet, a demographic
change that ran underneath it, and a church that ran out of bishops.

`conquest` would assert a campaign that ended it and there was not one.
`fragmentation` would be closer to what the territory did and would leave the
Mamluks out, and leaving them out would be worse: the interventions are the
proximate cause and this site's Mamluk record already claims them in a clause.

So the type is the least wrong of six, `by` names the power that did the
intervening, and this chapter carries what the field cannot.

## Alodia, and Kush

Two things upstream that this corpus still does not carry.

Alodia, the third Christian Nubian kingdom, beyond the confluence of the two
Niles, lasted until about 1504 — a hundred and forty years past this record's
end — and fell to a coalition of Arabs and the Funj. And Kush, which ruled Egypt
as its twenty-fifth dynasty in the eighth century BC and then held Meroë for a
thousand years upstream of everything on this page, writing in a script nobody
can read.

This site's Egyptian records point at Kush four times and its Ancient Near East
region's own recommendation list has carried it for a while. One stretch of one
river, three kingdoms deep in absences, and a region blurb that says three
records for a continent is a fact about this project rather than about Africa.

It says seven now.
