Makuria absorbed Nobatia in the seventh century and became the only kingdom on
five hundred kilometres of river. What let it be a kingdom rather than a string
of villages was a machine.

The *saqia* — the ox-driven waterwheel, lifting Nile water into channels above
the flood line — arrives in Nubia in this period and is what makes the middle
Nile support a kingdom rather than a string of villages. A narrow floodplain
between two deserts becomes farmable above the reach of the annual inundation,
and Welsby's excavation record shows settlement expanding onto exactly the ground
the saqia makes usable.

The second value is the river itself: everything moving between Egypt and the
African interior passed through, and the baqt guaranteed merchants free passage
in both directions, which is a customs regime written into a peace treaty.

## What that buys

A narrow floodplain between two deserts is a set of separate oases. A floodplain
with irrigation above the flood line is continuous farmland, and continuous
farmland supports a tax base, a court, a cathedral and an army.

The other half of the revenue was the river itself. Everything moving between
Egypt and the African interior went through, and the baqt guaranteed merchants
free passage in both directions — a customs regime written into a peace treaty,
which is a rare and very sensible thing to have done.

So the kingdom's economy was a waterwheel and a toll gate, and the treaty that
kept the frontier quiet is what made the second of them worth anything.
