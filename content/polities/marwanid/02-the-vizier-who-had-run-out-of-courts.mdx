title: The vizier who had run out of courts

The way a small Kurdish emirate in Diyar Bakr turned into a state was not by
taking ground. It was by hiring people the great powers had finished with, and
the clearest case is the man who died in office at Mayyafariqin in October 1027.

## What he was

Ibn Khallikan opens the life of al-Wazir al-Maghribi with a genealogy that runs
back through Sasanian kings to Bahram Gur, and then quietly notes that people
disagree about whether the family was even from the Maghrib.

His father's note on the flyleaf of one of his books survives, and it is the note
of a man who knows what he has produced. The boy was born in June 981. Before he
was fourteen he had by heart the Qur'an, a number of grammatical and philological
textbooks without their commentaries, and about fifteen thousand verses of the
old Arabic poets; he wrote poetry, wrote prose, and surpassed his contemporaries
in penmanship, in casting nativities and in algebra. Before he was seventeen he
had abridged a standard work on lexicography, reorganised its chapters, and begun
turning the whole thing into verse — a number of sheets in a single night.

*And all this,* the father writes, *before he had completed his fourteenth year.*

## What happened to him

In June 1010 the Fatimid caliph al-Hakim had his father, his uncle and two of his
brothers put to death.

He fled to Ramla and attached himself to the Jarrahid chiefs of Tayy who had
seized the town, and set about turning them against Egypt. Then he went to the
Hijaz and worked on the governor of Mecca, holding out the prospect of
overthrowing al-Hakim and taking Egypt. The intrigue got far enough that the
sharif of Mecca was brought to Ramla and proclaimed caliph under the title
al-Rashid, and it alarmed al-Hakim seriously enough that he bought the Jarrahids
off with a large sum of money.

The scheme collapsed. The sharif fled home. Al-Maghribi went to Iraq to be out of
reach — where the Abbasid caliph al-Qadir, hearing that he had arrived and
assuming he had come to plot against the Abbasids too, ordered him expelled from
the caliphate's territory.

He was sheltered anyway by the vizier Fakhr al-Mulk, who kept him in his suite and
protected him until Fakhr al-Mulk was himself executed. He then talked his way
back into the caliph's tolerance, went to Mosul, and was taken on by Qirwash the
Uqaylid as secretary of state. From there he intrigued for the vizierate of the
Buyid Musharraf al-Dawla at Baghdad and did not stop until the sitting vizier had
been arrested and he had the post.

By the mid-1020s he had worked for or against the Fatimids, the Abbasids, the
Uqaylids and the Buyids, and had run out of courts.

## Diyar Bakr

So he went to Ahmad ibn Marwan, and was his vizier until he died.

That is the transaction the emirate turned on. Nasr al-Dawla acquired, for a
frontier emirate of no great size, an administrator who had run a Buyid
government at Baghdad, had been a Fatimid insider before that, and had personally
attempted to make a new caliph. Ibn Khallikan notes elsewhere that Nasr al-Dawla
had two viziers who had served caliphs in that capacity — this one, and Ibn Jahir,
who left Mayyafariqin to become vizier at Baghdad.

A court that can attract that kind of man twice is not a chieftaincy. It is a
place where a serious career could be continued, and the emirate's growth in the
eleventh century is largely a matter of having been the place people went when
the larger courts became fatal.

## The tomb

He died at Mayyafariqin in October 1027, forty-five years old, in office.

His will directed that his body be carried to Kufa and buried near the shrine of
Ali. On the tomb, by his own instruction, they cut verses he had written:

> I had long travelled in the path of error and ignorance; it was time for me to
> arrive at my journey's end. I have repented of all my sins, and this last part
> of my conduct may perhaps efface the former. After five and forty years, I had
> hoped for a longer respite, did I not know that my creditor is generous.

A man who had spent his adult life trying to bring down the dynasty that killed
his family, and who found employment at last in the Kurdish highlands, arranged
to be buried in Iraq with an apology on the stone.
