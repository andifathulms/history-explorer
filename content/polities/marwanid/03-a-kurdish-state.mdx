The corpus is short of Kurdish polities. The Ayyubids are Kurdish by origin and
this site's Ayyubid page says so, but Saladin's house was Arabized within a
generation, ruled in Arabic, and is remembered as an Egyptian and Syrian
dynasty rather than a Kurdish one. The Marwanids are the other entry, and they
are the one that stayed what it was.

Ripper's monograph — the only book-length study of the dynasty, and in German,
which is worth stating rather than hiding — makes a claim about the emirate that
goes beyond its hundred years. The Marwanid century is when the westward
movement of Kurdish population into Diyar Bakr intensified under a Kurdish
ruler, and it is a substantial part of why the upper Tigris is Kurdish country
now rather than Armenian or Arab or Syriac. A polity that lasted from 983 to
1085 is still visible in a map of who lives where.

That is a kind of consequence this site has no field for. `descendant_scripts`,
`religions_carried`, `successor_claims` — the three influence counts — would all
be null or zero here, and they would be right. The Marwanids carried no
religion outward, produced no script, and nobody claimed to succeed them.
Demography is not one of the three, and it is the largest thing they did.

The coding rulebook's answer is the correct one and should be stated rather than
worked around: the counts measure what they measure, and a polity whose main
effect is not among them shows three nulls and a chapter. Adding a fourth count
for population movement would require a rule that could be applied to sixty
polities uniformly, and there is no such rule available — every migration in the
corpus is attested differently, most of them are contested, and a count would be
an opinion wearing a number. That is exactly what `content/coding-rules.md`
exists to prevent.
