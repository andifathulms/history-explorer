---
title: The counterweight goes
drafted_from: kennedy-2004
phase: contraction
---

Nasr al-Dawla's whole method required that there be several great powers within
reach and none of them dominant. From the 1050s that stopped being the case.

Tughril Beg's Seljuks took Baghdad in 1055 and made the Abbasid caliph their
client, which merged two of the four powers the Marwanids had been balancing
into one. Turkmen bands were already in the Jazira and Armenia, grazing and
raiding, attached to the sultanate loosely or not at all. Nasr al-Dawla
acknowledged the sultan, which was the correct move and was also the end of the
game he had been playing: an emirate whose product is its own convenient
neutrality has nothing to sell once there is only one buyer.

He died in 1061, at about seventy, having ruled for half a century. His sons and
grandsons held Diyar Bakr for another twenty-four years and are hard to see in
the sources, which is itself informative.

Ten years after his death, at Manzikert — a day's ride north of Marwanid
territory — Alp Arslan destroyed a Byzantine army and captured its emperor. The
Marwanids were not in that battle and this site records no turning point for it
on this page. What it did to them was remove the last counterweight. Byzantium
had been the power on the other side of the Marwanid position for a hundred
years, and after 1071 it was not on that side of anything.
