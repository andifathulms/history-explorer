Yuknoom Ch'een II, remembered today as Yuknoom the Great, took the
throne in 636 and ruled for fifty years, presiding over the dynasty's
golden age. Calakmul's output of carved stelae, and much of the
monumental palace architecture that still dominates the city's core,
dates substantially to his reign; as many as eighteen stelae were his
own commissions.

## The star war of 657

His long tenure turned on a running struggle with Tikal. In 650 he
campaigned into the Petexbatun region, backing the founder of a
rival Tikal dynasty at Dos Pilas against the reigning king, Nuun Ujol
Chaak. He followed this in 657 by attacking Tikal directly in a
"star war," bringing Nuun Ujol Chaak under his supervision, at least
for a time. Calakmul intervened again in 677 to inflict a second
defeat on the same king, and a combined Calakmul-Dos Pilas force is
likely to have delivered the decisive blow that finally broke him in
679.

## Overking of the Petén

Beyond Tikal, Yuknoom the Great's authority as an "overking" reached
across a wide network of client courts. He oversaw the installation
of a ruler at El Perú, sealed by marriage to a Calakmul princess; he
kept the small centre of La Corona so firmly under his control that
its own heir resided at Calakmul; he installed kings at Cancuen
across three generations, and travelled personally to Oxwitz in 662
to help a local ruler mark a calendar anniversary, coordinating the
visit with a campaign by an allied city against one of its
neighbours the same year. He died an octogenarian early in 686,
Calakmul still the dominant power of the central lowlands - though,
as later events would show, his one lasting failure was that Tikal
itself had never been fully subdued.
