title: An island of roots

Crowned at Saigon under his dead father's name, the young king held the
city only long enough to fortify it before disaster returned. Warned that
a large army was marching against him and that resistance was hopeless,
he gathered his family and a handful of loyal followers, took to the
river, and put to sea for a small uninhabited island in the Gulf of Siam
called Pulo Wai. There he was joined, in twos and threes, by some twelve
hundred subjects still able to carry arms - and there, according to
Bishop Adran's own letter describing the meeting, the king's soldiers
survived on roots dug out of the ground.

## A war fought for a foreign king

Rather than wait passively for the rebels to find his island refuge, the
king sailed on to Siam and offered his services, and his roughly thousand
armed followers, to the Siamese king, then at war with Burma. Using
tactics learned years before from his French tutor - avoiding pitched
battle, striking from commanding positions, harassing the enemy's march
rather than meeting it head-on - he forced the Burmese to sue for peace on
his terms and returned to the Siamese capital loaded with gold, silver and
royal gratitude.

## A refusal, a plot, and a night escape

Gratitude did not last. The Siamese king, rebuffed when he proposed to
take the exiled king's sister as a concubine and then, more brazenly, to
share his own throne with her, grew resentful of a guest he had begun to
consider above his own station - and Siamese generals, jealous in their
own right, are said to have plotted against the exile's life. Warned in
time, the king and his family cut their way out of the Siamese capital by
night with sword in hand, seized enough vessels in the harbor to carry
some fifteen hundred followers, and sailed back to Pulo Wai, which he now
fortified with guns taken from the captured ships - secure, for the
moment, against either the Siamese king he had just abandoned or the
rebels who still held his own country.
