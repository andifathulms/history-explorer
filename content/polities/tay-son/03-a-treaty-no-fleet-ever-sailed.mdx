The heir the Tay Son brothers had hunted did not stay caught. A French
missionary bishop known as Adran, who had tutored the murdered king's
young son and heir, smuggled the boy's father, mother, wife, infant son
and one sister out of the capital by night; for months the fugitive royal
family hid in the branches of a banyan tree, fed at the risk of his own
life by a Christian priest named Paul, until the search parties gave up.
The survivors made their way to Saigon, where the people crowned the
young heir under his late father's name. A purchased fleet's surprise raid
on the rebel navy at Qui Nhon failed when the lead French ship ran
aground and its Portuguese allies fled to Macao, but it was enough to draw
the eldest brother's attention south - and enough to keep the boy-king's
cause alive.

## A prince at Versailles

Adran did not stop at raids. Judging the cause hopeless without outside
help, he carried the young prince first to Pondicherry and then to Paris,
arriving in 1787. There, in the space of a few months, he negotiated a
treaty between Louis XVI and the exiled King of Cochinchina, signed at
Versailles - an alliance promising twenty French ships of war, five
European regiments and two colonial ones, a million dollars in specie and
military stores, all in exchange for the cession in perpetuity of the bay
of Turon and its surrounding territory, French consuls along the whole
coast, timber rights in the forests, and a Cochinchinese pledge to raise
sixty thousand men in Louis's defense if ever the new French territory
were attacked. Adran himself was made Bishop of Cochinchina and appointed
Ambassador Plenipotentiary to the court he had once fled.

## Undone by a jealous mistress

The expedition never sailed. Reaching Pondicherry in 1789 to organize the
promised ships and troops, Adran gave offense to Madame de Vienne, mistress
of the French governor's aide-de-camp, by refusing to pay her the social
call custom expected. She took her revenge by working on the governor's
own vanity until he delayed the armament pending further orders from
Versailles - and before those orders could arrive, revolution broke out in
France and ended the matter for good. Refusing to give up his original
plan regardless, Adran gathered what volunteer officers he could and
sailed on toward Cochinchina anyway, determined to find the exiled king
himself and see the restoration through without an army France had
promised and never sent.
