---
title: A throne two empires agreed about
drafted_from: toumanoff-1963
phase: expansion
---

Iberia's position is Armenia's problem one range further north and with less
depth to trade. Rome reached the Caucasus under Pompey in the 60s BC; Parthia
and then the Sasanians were on the other side of it; and the passes through the
mountains — the Darial gorge in the centre, the Derbend defile on the Caspian
that this site carries its own record of — were what both empires actually cared
about, because through them came the steppe.

So Iberia was worth having as a client and not worth conquering, and both
empires arrived at the same conclusion repeatedly.

The Peace of Nisibis in 298, which ended a Roman-Persian war the Sasanians had
lost, put Iberia in the Roman sphere and gave Rome the right to invest its
kings. It is precisely the arrangement this corpus's Greater Armenia record
carries from Rhandeia in AD 63, running the other way — and it is why both of
those records leave `succession_rule` empty. A throne disposed of by treaty
between two other powers is not primogeniture, tanistry, appanage, nomination,
election, acclamation or factional. It is the eighth and ninth appearances in
this corpus of an idea the vocabulary does not have.

What the kings did with the position was what the Shirvanshahs on the
neighbouring record did seven centuries later: submit early, pay, be useful at
the passes, and avoid deciding. The Iberian crown passed between Roman and
Persian orientation several times without the country being destroyed, which for
a small kingdom between two empires over four hundred years is a considerable
administrative achievement and reads in the sources as vacillation.

Then in the 330s a decision was made that could not be reversed.
