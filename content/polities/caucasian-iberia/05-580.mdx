---
title: Four centuries without a king
drafted_from: suny-1994
phase: end
---

The Sasanians abolished the Iberian monarchy around 580 and governed through a
marzban. It is the last of the three Caucasian crowns to go, after Armenia in
428 and Albania in 510.

What replaced it in 588 is the arrangement that mattered: a *presiding prince*,
an *erismtavari*, chosen from among the noble houses and recognised by whichever
empire was currently paramount. Iberia had presiding princes for three hundred
years, under Sasanian, then Byzantine, then Arab suzerainty, and the office
worked because it made explicit what had always been the case — that the country
was governed by its nobility and that the sovereign at the top was a matter for
negotiation with outsiders.

The Arabs took Tbilisi in the 640s and it became an emirate under a Muslim amir,
which it remained until 1122. The presiding princes governed the rest.

In the ninth century a branch of the Bagratids established itself in
Tao-Klarjeti in the south-west, in the marches between the Byzantine and Arab
worlds, built up a principality with monasteries and fortresses, and in 888
Adarnase IV took the title king of the Kartvelians. That is the restoration of a
Georgian monarchy, three hundred and eight years after this record ends, and
this corpus's Georgia record begins a hundred and twenty years after that when
his descendant inherited Abkhazia as well.

There is no edge from this record to that one, and the reason is the same one
the Greater Armenia record gives. Four centuries separate them; what is
continuous across the gap is the church, the alphabet, the language and the
noble houses, and none of those is a polity. A dynasty that appears in the
marches in the ninth century and takes an old title is making a claim about
succession, and this corpus's rules are explicit that a claim of that kind is
counted where a source records it being publicly asserted — which is a different
question from whether one state became another.

What this record leaves is a country that had been Christian for two hundred and
fifty years when its crown was removed and was still Christian four hundred
years later when it got another one, governed throughout by the same families in
the same valleys.
