The Sasanians abolished the Iberian monarchy around 580 and governed through a
marzban. It is the last of the three Caucasian crowns to go, after Armenia in
428 and Albania in 510.

What replaced it in 588 is the arrangement that mattered: a presiding prince, an
*erismtavari*, chosen from among the noble houses and recognised by whichever
empire was currently paramount. Iberia had presiding princes for three hundred
years, under Sasanian, then Byzantine, then Arab suzerainty.

The office worked because it made explicit what had always been true. The
country was governed by its nobility. The sovereign at the top was a matter to
be negotiated with outsiders, and had been since 298. Removing the king changed
who signed the negotiation and changed nothing else.

It is the same finding all three Caucasian kingdoms produce. An empire that takes a crown here has taken the one
part of the machinery it could reach. The houses hold their own districts, their
own fortresses and their own cavalry; they were there before the crown and they
are there after it; and the country goes on being the country, in the same
valleys, run by the same families, worshipping in the same church.

What the Sasanians actually achieved in 580 was to stop paying for a king.
