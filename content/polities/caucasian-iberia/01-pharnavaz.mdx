The Greeks and Romans called eastern Georgia Iberia, and they also called Spain
Iberia, and there is no connection between the two names that anybody has
established. This corpus has pages on al-Andalus, the Almohads and the Nasrids
that use the other one, which is why this record's id disambiguates and why the
first line of the page has to.

The Georgians call it Kartli, and their own historiographical tradition — the
*Kartlis Tskhovreba*, assembled centuries later out of older material — gives
the kingdom a founder: Pharnavaz I, around 302 BC, who is said to have thrown
off Macedonian control after Alexander's death, established the cult of the god
Armazi at Mtskheta, and organised the country into districts under *eristavi*.

Almost none of that is verifiable and the traditional foundation date is a
construction. What the archaeology and the classical sources support is a
kingdom in the Kura valley from at least the second century BC, with a capital
at Mtskheta where the Aragvi joins the Kura, ruled by a dynasty and a nobility,
and positioned — like Armenia to its south and Albania to its east — on the
route through the Caucasus and between two empires.

The range at the start of this record's span is that gap between tradition and
evidence, and it is written into the field rather than resolved.

The structure is the one the three Caucasian records share. The *eristavi* were
provincial lords, the *aznauri* the noble class below them, and both held their
positions hereditarily and supplied the king's forces. Toumanoff's book is about
that system across all three countries, and this site codes `client-levy` on all
three from it. A king in this arrangement presides; he does not command.
