Razadarit came to the throne of Pegu in 1385 at the age of sixteen,
and within it he already had an enemy at home: his own uncle
Laukpya, lord of Myaungmya, wrote to the king of Ava offering to
hold Pegu as his vassal if the northerners would help oust his
nephew before the boy could secure his own throne - "march against
Razadarit before he is firm upon his throne," the letter urged. "When
we have conquered him, do thou keep the heart-wood and leave me the
bark." That betrayal opened a war between Upper Burma and the
Talaing kingdom of Pegu that ran, on and off, for a generation.

## Forty years of ambush and stratagem

The war was fought less by pitched battle than by raid, siege and
elaborate trickery. Commanders deserted to the enemy only to reveal
their true loyalty at the critical hour; besieged officers forged
letters announcing relief armies that did not exist; a Talaing agent
named Emuntaya, trapped inside a besieged town, smuggled himself out
past a double watch by disguising himself as a corpse laid out on a
funeral raft, complete with wailing mourners, and slipped away once
the raft had drifted out of sight. Razadarit captured Myaungmya and
its rebel lord in 1390 and cleared Burmese forces from the frontier
garrison of Myanaung in 1391, defeating an attempt to retake it so
thoroughly that his own men returned the Burmese king's own captured
golden barge along with a letter praising fair dealing between
enemies. He fought more than one rival commander in single combat
himself; one Burmese king never dared accept his challenge, and
another who did turned his elephant and fled within minutes.

## Piety, and death in a hunting accident

When news reached Pegu in 1422 that his great rival King Minhkaung
of Ava had died, Razadarit's own queens urged him to seize the
moment and raid the dead king's palace. He refused. "My sweet enemy
is dead," he told them. "I will fight no more but spend my declining
years in piety." He did not have long to spend them: the following
year, while snaring elephants with his own hands at the age of
fifty-four, he was caught in his own rope and injured badly enough
to die on the journey home. His own chronicle, devoted to him alone,
closes with the judgment that this "Lion King, so wise, so generous,
so mighty in word and deed, could overcome all his enemies, but he
too at the last must bow before King Death."

## Sons, a sword for a stolen ring, and a childless king

None of Razadarit's own successors matched his reign. His son
Binnyadammayaza was poisoned by his own brother within three years;
that brother, Binnyaran, was succeeded in turn by Binnyawaru, a king
so devoted to strict justice that he reportedly had a cat executed
for catching and eating a mouse, and an officer's servant hacked in
two for stealing a pedlar's ring rather than accept a bribe to
overlook it. After a further reign that raised the Shwedagon pagoda's
own height, palace massacres left no male descendant of Razadarit
alive at all - and it was in that vacancy, by general consent of the
court, that the throne passed to his daughter Shinsawbu in 1453.
