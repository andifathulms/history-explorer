Wareru began his career as a Shan pedlar from Donwun, in the
Thaton district of Lower Burma, who took service in the elephant
stables of the chief of Sukhotai in Siam and rose to Captain of the
Guard. He did not stay a guardsman for long: he eloped with the
chief's own daughter, gathered a handful of kindred spirits from the
Guard, and set himself up back at his native village.

## A wedding feast and a murder

His next step was bolder still. He arranged for his own beautiful
sister to bathe where Aleimma, the Burmese governor of Martaban,
would be sure to see her, and when Aleimma duly asked for her hand,
Wareru staged a wedding feast in apparent gratitude - then murdered
his new brother-in-law at it and seized his governorship. By 1281 he
was lord of Martaban.

## A duel on elephant-back

The crumbling Pagan kingdom gave Wareru his opening to expand. He
made common cause with Tarabya, the rebel governor of Pegu, each man
marrying the other's daughter as a pledge of alliance, and together
they drove out the last Burmese governors from the country south of
Prome and Toungoo by 1287. Tarabya then tried to ambush his own
ally, and failed; Wareru, calling on the spirits of earth and air to
witness his innocence and pouring a libation from a golden bowl,
mounted his elephant and defeated Tarabya in single combat, sparing
his life at the monks' request. Tarabya plotted again, was betrayed
by his own wife - Wareru's own daughter - and was executed despite
her attempt to shield him with her own body. Wareru became sole
prince of a Lower Burma kingdom that would last until 1539, and
within a decade even received formal recognition from the imperial
court of China.
