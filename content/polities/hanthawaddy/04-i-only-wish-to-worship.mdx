Dhammazedi died at eighty and was succeeded by his own son, Binnyaran,
who reigned from 1492 to 1526 and was remembered afterward for his
gentleness - though his very first act as king was to enforce the
kingdom's own custom of the Massacre of the Kinsmen, putting every
one of his royal relatives to death to remove any rival claim to the
throne. In 1501 he marched upstream with a large armed force; when
the lord of Prome moved to check him, fearing invasion, Binnyaran
answered plainly: "I could conquer both you and Ava but I do not
wish. I only wish to worship before the Shwezigon at Pagan." He
completed his pilgrimage and returned home without a fight.

## A king who preferred the hunt to the throne

His own son, Takayutpi, inherited the crown in 1526 and proved a far
weaker ruler. The kingdom's own astrologers held that he could have
reigned to the age of eighty-five had he followed their prescriptions
- but, by the judgment later chroniclers passed on him, "he never
looked at a book, he gave himself up to sport in the woods with
elephants and horses, he searched for shellfish and crabs, he was
like one witless." It was this same Takayutpi, the last king of
Wareru's own line, who would lose Pegu itself to Tabinshwehti's
forged letter within little more than a decade of taking the throne.
