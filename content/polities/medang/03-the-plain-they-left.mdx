Around 929 Mpu Sindok moved the court from central Java to the Brantas valley
in the east. Central Java was never again the seat of a Javanese kingdom.

Nothing explains it. There is no defeat, no invasion, no succession crisis; the
inscriptions simply stop being issued on one plain and start being issued four
hundred kilometres away. Merapi erupting, an epidemic, a Srivijayan raid, and a
deliberate move toward the rice surplus and the trade of the Brantas have all
been proposed, and not one of them is attested.

What the move left behind is the point. The largest concentration of eighth-
and ninth-century monumental architecture in Southeast Asia stands on a plain
that a state walked away from, and it stood there through everything that
followed — Kediri, Singhasari, Majapahit, Demak, the sultanates, the Dutch —
without anybody ruling from it again. Borobudur was under volcanic ash and
vegetation when Raffles's men were shown it in 1814.

## Where it went instead

The move east is where the rest of Javanese history starts.

Every kingdom that follows sits on the Brantas or its plains: Kediri from 1042,
Singhasari from 1222, Majapahit from 1293, and the sultanates after them. Seven
hundred years of Javanese states, all of them downstream of a decision taken in
929 for reasons nobody wrote down.

It also put the Javanese court on top of the granary. Melaka, with no farmland
of its own, bought most of its rice from Java; Demak on the north coast sold
it. The surplus that fed the trade of the whole strait came off the plains Mpu
Sindok moved onto.
