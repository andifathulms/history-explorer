Borobudur was mentioned once on this site before this record existed — in
passing, on the Srivijaya page, as a thing that happened somewhere else.
Prambanan was not mentioned at all.

They are four hundred metres apart in significance and about twenty kilometres
apart on the ground, on the same volcanic plain in central Java, built within
roughly a century of each other. Borobudur is a Mahayana Buddhist stupa-mountain
of some two million blocks with five kilometres of narrative relief around its
galleries. Prambanan is a Shaivite complex whose central tower stands
forty-seven metres and whose reliefs carry the Ramayana. Between and around them
are Kalasan, Sewu, Plaosan, Mendut, Sambisari and forty or so others.

The polity that built all of it is this one, and this site did not have it.

## Buddhist and Hindu, twenty kilometres apart

The obvious question about that plain is how one small kingdom produced the two
monuments at once, and the older answer was that it did not: that there were two
dynasties, the Buddhist Sailendras and the Shaivite line of Sanjaya, ruling in
rivalry or in alternation.

That reading has been argued down over the last half-century toward something
messier — a single ruling group with both patronages inside it, intermarrying,
in which the labels are about what a particular king endowed rather than about
which house he belonged to. This record does not adjudicate it. What it does is
not code around it: `legitimation` reads `descent` and `divine-sanction`, and
the god is deliberately not named, because the second value has never on this
site meant a specific god and would have to here.

`religions_carried` reads zero, which on the record of Borobudur is going to look
like an error and is the same call this corpus's Pagan record makes on the record
of ten thousand temples. Coding rule 2 counts propagation past a prior boundary,
not adoption and not scale of building. Java received Shaivism and Mahayana
Buddhism through the same channels everything else Indic arrived by, and then
built more of it than anywhere outside India.
