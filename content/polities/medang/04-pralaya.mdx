In 1016 a vassal named in the inscriptions as Wurawari attacked Dharmawangsa's
court during the wedding of the king's daughter and killed him. The Javanese
word for what followed is *pralaya*, the dissolution at the end of a world age.

Airlangga, the king's son-in-law and then sixteen years old, took refuge among
hermits on Mount Vanagiri.

## Twenty years to get it back

He stayed on the mountain four years. Then notables and brahmans came to plead
with him to take the royal power as his father-in-law's successor, and in 1019
he was crowned — over a territory that did not reach beyond a strip of the
north coast between Surabaya and Pasuruhan.

He waited another decade before trying to recover the rest, and the delay was
probably prudence: Srivijaya, which may have had a hand in 1016, was itself
wrecked by the Chola raid of 1025.

The reconquest ran from 1028 to 1035, against a country divided among
competitors, and the inscriptions record it in their own register. A residence
burned in 1031. In 1032 he rid the country of a woman "endowed with a
formidable power, similar to a rakshasi", and devastated the southern region,
"which he burned with his tongue like a fiery serpent". The prince of Wengker,
beaten and fleeing "abandoning his wife, children, treasures, and royal
vehicles", was seized by his own troops and died in 1035.

In 1037, "having placed his feet on the head of his enemies", Airlangga took
his place on the throne of lions. He moved his residence east, to Kahuripan,
whose site has still not been identified.

His court produced the *Arjunawiwaha*, one of the great Old Javanese poems, in
which Arjuna withdraws as an ascetic, resists temptation, and is given a divine
weapon — written for a king who had spent four years on a mountain and twenty
getting his kingdom back.

## And then he divided it

In 1042 Airlangga split Kahuripan between his two sons.

The Javanese account gives the reason as a father's even-handedness. What it
produced was two kingdoms, Janggala and Panjalu, which fought each other;
Panjalu won, took the name of its capital's district, and became Kediri.

So the line from Medang to Kediri is not a line. It runs through a kingdom
assembled from nothing by a teenager, held for twenty years, and then
deliberately cut in half — and only one of the halves survived to matter.
Dharmawangsa's kingdom ended in 1016 with a wedding and a massacre, and what
followed was not its continuation but its reconstruction by somebody else,
under another name, before being taken apart again on purpose.
