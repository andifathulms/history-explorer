In 1016 a vassal named in the inscriptions as Wurawari attacked Dharmawangsa's
court during the wedding of the king's daughter and killed him. The Javanese word
for what followed is *pralaya*, the dissolution at the end of a world age.

Airlangga, a young relative who escaped into the forest with a few retainers,
spent about twenty years rebuilding. The kingdom he assembled was called
Kahuripan, and in 1042 he divided it between his sons — and one of the halves is
this corpus's Kediri record.

So there is a clean-looking line from this page to the next Javanese one, and
this site is not drawing it.

## Why not

`successor_claims` here reads one, and the item is Kahuripan under Airlangga.
Rule 1 does not require a claimant to have a page, and Airlangga's inscriptions
argue his continuity with Dharmawangsa's line explicitly, so the count is honest.

An edge `medang → kediri` would be a different assertion. It would say that
Kediri came out of Medang, when what Kediri came out of is Kahuripan — a
kingdom this site does not carry, assembled over two decades by a man who
started with nothing, and then split in two.

This region has already made that mistake once and had to correct it. There used
to be an edge here reading `srivijaya → majapahit`, `absorbed remnants of`, 1293.
It was wrong twice: Singhasari absorbed Srivijaya's remnants, in 1275, eighteen
years before Majapahit existed. The Singhasari record's third chapter says what
went wrong — an edge drawn between two records because both were present, when
the actual relationship ran through a third that was not.

Kahuripan is that third. The difference is that this time the missing record is
known to be missing before the edge is drawn, so the edge is not drawn.

## What it would take

Kahuripan is a small page and a real one: twenty years of reconstruction, the
*Arjunawiwaha* written at its court, and a division in 1042 that produced two
kingdoms of which this site carries one. Writing it would give this region two
more edges — `medang → kahuripan` and `kahuripan → kediri`, the second of them
`partitioned from`, which is a type this region does not yet use anywhere.

That is now the second smallest identifiable hole in Maritime Asia, after the
Pajang record that would close Demak's `ended.by`. Both are on the list and
neither is on this page's account of what happened.
