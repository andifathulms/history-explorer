In February 1570 the Portuguese captain at Ternate invited Sultan Hairun into
the fort to talk, and had him stabbed as he was leaving.

Fifty-eight years of alliance ended in that room. Whatever the arrangement of
1512 had been — and it had been an alliance, entered into by Ternate, on
Ternate's initiative — nothing about it could be argued for afterwards.

Hairun's son Baabullah besieged the fort. It took five years. In July 1575 the
garrison surrendered and left the island, and Ternate under Baabullah went on to
extend its authority further than it ever had before or would again: across
Halmahera, the Sula islands, into eastern Sulawesi, out along the Papuan coast.

## Three records

This site carries a hundred and ninety polities. Three of them threw a European
power out by force and kept their own capital afterwards.

The Ya'ariba record took Muscat from the Portuguese in 1650 and then spent forty
years raiding Portuguese Asia from it. The Ethiopian record destroyed an Italian
army at Adwa in 1896, and its chapter on that battle calls it the only occasion
in the partition of Africa when the partition was refused. This one is the
earliest of the three by seventy-five years, and the smallest party to it by an
enormous margin: an island of forty-odd square kilometres, against the empire
that had taken Melaka and Hormuz and Goa.

It is worth stating plainly what happened next, because the shape of this record
does not reward the victory. Ternate won in 1575 and was a Dutch company's vassal
by 1683. The two facts are not in tension. What arrived in the seventeenth
century was a different kind of thing from what Baabullah expelled, and the last
chapter of this record is about what that difference was.
