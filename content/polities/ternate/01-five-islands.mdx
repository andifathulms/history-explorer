Until Europeans transplanted it in the eighteenth century, the clove tree grew
on five small islands off the western coast of Halmahera and nowhere else on
earth. Ternate, Tidore, Moti, Makian and Bacan: the whole of them together are
smaller than a mid-sized European city.

Cloves reached Rome. They reached Han China, where court officials are described
holding them in the mouth before addressing the emperor. They are in the Cairo
Geniza and in Venetian ledgers and in the price lists of every long-distance
market between the two, and for most of that history nobody at either end of the
chain knew where they came from. The commodity travelled through five or six
intermediaries, each of whom had an interest in the origin staying vague.

This is why Ternate is on this site, and it is the reason the record's
`revenue_basis` reads the way it does. Nine other polities in this corpus carry
`monopoly`, and in every one of them the value sits beside a land tax or a trade
toll, because a monopoly is normally a thing a state adds to a revenue base it
already has. Ternate has no land tax to add it to. There is not enough flat
ground on a volcanic island of that size to build a fiscal system on, and there
did not need to be.

## What the sultanate is dated from

The traditions list *kolano* — kings of Ternate — running back into the
thirteenth century. This record does not begin there. It begins in 1486 with
Zainal Abidin, the first ruler to take the title sultan and to govern by Islamic
law, because that is the point from which the polity is documented rather than
remembered.

The site has made this choice before and in both directions. It is the same
judgement that starts the Aksum record where the inscriptions do rather than
where the king-lists do, and the opposite of the judgement on the Melakan record
one region over, which takes a chronicle's founding story as its start date
because the chronicle is the only source there is. What separates them is
whether anything independent survives from the earlier period. At Ternate a good
deal does — the cloves were reaching China long before 1486 — but it says
nothing about who was governing the island.
