Until Europeans transplanted it in the eighteenth century, the clove tree grew
on five small islands off the western coast of Halmahera and nowhere else on
earth. Ternate, Tidore, Moti, Makian and Bacan: the whole of them together are
smaller than a mid-sized European city.

Cloves reached Rome. They reached Han China, where court officials are
described holding them in the mouth before addressing the emperor. They are in
the Cairo Geniza and in Venetian ledgers and in the price lists of every
long-distance market between the two, and for most of that history nobody at
either end of the chain knew where they came from. The commodity travelled
through five or six intermediaries, each of whom had an interest in the origin
staying vague.

A monopoly is normally something a state adds to a revenue base it already has
— a land tax, a customs house, a hinterland that pays. Ternate had none of that
to add it to. There is not enough flat ground on a volcanic island of that size
to build a fiscal system on, and there did not need to be.

The tree was the fiscal system.

## What the sultanate is dated from

The traditions list *kolano* — kings of Ternate — running back into the
thirteenth century. The sultanate proper is dated from 1486 and Zainal Abidin,
the first ruler to take the title sultan and to govern by Islamic law, because
that is the point from which the polity is documented rather than remembered.

The distinction that matters is whether anything independent survives from
before. At Ternate a great deal does — the cloves were reaching China centuries
before 1486 — but none of it says a word about who was governing the island.
The commodity is documented and the polity is not, which is an unusual way
round and the whole difficulty of writing about these islands.
