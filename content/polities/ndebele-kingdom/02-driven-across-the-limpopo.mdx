By the mid-1830s Moselekatse, as the Betshuana called him, had built
his kingdom's centre at Mosega, in the Marico district, from which his
regiments ranged out to plunder the tribes of the highveld with almost
no resistance - until a new kind of enemy began crossing the Vaal
river from the Cape Colony. Voortrekker families, emigrating north in
search of land beyond colonial rule, brought horses and firearms that
Moselekatse's spearmen had never had to face. In October 1836 a
Matabele raiding party, sent after a hunting party led by Stephanus
Erasmus had strayed into territory Moselekatse considered his own,
massacred two families outright and then turned on a larger group of
emigrants encamped at Vechtkop.

The Vechtkop lager held. Forty men and boys with firearms, drawn up
behind fifty wagons lashed into a circle, broke a Matabele division
several thousand strong under Moselekatse's favourite captain Kalipi -
losing only one man killed inside the laager, against a third of the
attacking force left on the ground before the Matabele withdrew. It
was the first check Moselekatse's kingdom had suffered since its
founding, and it cost the emigrants nearly all their cattle - but it
proved, for the first time, that his regiments could be stopped.

## Nine days on the Marikwa

The Voortrekkers did not let the lesson end there. In November 1837 a
combined commando of one hundred and thirty-five farmers under
Hendrik Potgieter and Pieter Uys found Moselekatse's forces on the
Marikwa river, about fifty miles north of Mosega, and spent nine days
riding up in line to fire a volley and falling back before the
Matabele could close with spears at all - tactics that made the
fighting, in one participant's words, barely deserving the name of
warfare, and that nonetheless broke Moselekatse's army so thoroughly
that his kraals stood abandoned when the farmers finally found them.

Potgieter proclaimed the whole territory Moselekatse had occupied and
now abandoned forfeited to the emigrants - the greater part of what
would become the South African Republic, half of the future Orange
Free State, and all of southern Bechuanaland besides. Moselekatse
did not stay to contest the proclamation. He led his people north
across the Limpopo, through disaster - an entire vanguard division was
lured onto an island in the Zambezi and abandoned there by its own
paddlers, and every man drowned, starved or was killed - and did not
halt again until he was safely beyond the Matopo hills, ready to begin
building a kingdom for the second time.
