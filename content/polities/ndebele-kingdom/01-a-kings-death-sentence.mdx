Umsilikazi's father had once been an independent chief in his own
right, until the threat of annihilation drove him to surrender his
people into Tshaka's Zulu tribe rather than be destroyed by it. His
son grew up inside that tribe instead, rising on ability alone to
command a large and important division of Tshaka's army - tall,
well-formed, close-shorn but for the ring that marked a man of rank,
and by every account a favourite of the king he served.

Favour did not survive one act of disrespect. Sent to exterminate a
tribe and seize its cattle, Umsilikazi kept back part of the booty
rather than forward the whole of it to Tshaka. The king, enraged that
a trusted officer would treat him this way, sent an army with orders
to kill Umsilikazi and every one of his followers. Warned in time,
Umsilikazi crossed the mountains north of Zululand with the soldiers
under his own command before the punitive force could reach him -
turning a single Zulu regiment, in the space of one royal grudge, into
an independent nation with nowhere to go but forward.

## A desert between himself and his old king

What followed was deliberate devastation on a scale the tribes north
of Zululand had never faced. Umsilikazi's men, trained to Zulu
discipline and armed with the same short stabbing spear that had made
Tshaka's own regiments unstoppable, killed everyone who could not
escape them, sparing only the most attractive girls and young men fit
to be absorbed as porters and, eventually, soldiers of his own. His
object, plainly stated by those who observed him, was to put a great
desert between Tshaka and himself - and by the time he judged the
distance sufficient and halted to build military kraals on the Zulu
pattern, the country he had crossed was, in the words of one
contemporary account, left with "literally no human beings" in it at
all. From that emptied ground, Umsilikazi began sending regiments out
in every direction to gather more.
