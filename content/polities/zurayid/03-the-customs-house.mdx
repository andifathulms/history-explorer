Aden is a harbour inside a dead volcano on the southern corner of Arabia, and
its importance is a fact about wind rather than about Yemen. The monsoon
divides the Indian Ocean run from the Red Sea run, and ships coming up from
Gujarat and Malabar could not simply carry on to Egypt. Cargo was transferred.
The place where it was transferred took a percentage.

The Zurayid state was that percentage, and nothing else. Every other power in
Yemen had land under it: the dynasties of the coastal plain lived off irrigated
grain, the highland states off terraces, and the Rasulids later off both
together. The Zurayids had no agricultural hinterland worth the name. They had a
gate.

The consequence is that a dynasty with almost no territory could outbid its
neighbours for troops and could matter to powers a very long way off. Merchants
from Egypt and the Gulf and the Indian coast used the port; the Jewish trading
network whose letters ended up in the Cairo geniza ran through it; and the
customs of Aden are the reason the Ayyubids came down to Yemen at all.

How much ground they held is the wrong question to ask about them, and it would
stay the wrong question if somebody produced a good figure for it. The area of
Zurayid authority is not the measure of the Zurayid state. The port was the
polity, and everyone dealing with them knew it.
