Aden is a harbour inside a dead volcano on the southern corner of Arabia, and
its importance is a fact about wind rather than about Yemen. The monsoon
divides the Indian Ocean run from the Red Sea run, and ships coming up from
Gujarat and Malabar could not simply carry on to Egypt. Cargo was transferred.
The place where it was transferred took a percentage.

The Zurayid state was that percentage. This site codes its `revenue_basis` as
`trade-toll` and nothing else, which makes it the only polity in this region
recorded that way — the Ziyadids and the Najahids on the coastal plain lived off
irrigated grain, the Sulayhids and the Zaydis off highland terraces, and the
Rasulids later off both. The Zurayids had no agricultural hinterland worth the
name. They had a gate.

The consequence is that a dynasty with almost no territory could outbid its
neighbours for troops and could matter to powers a very long way off. Merchants
from Egypt and the Gulf and the Indian coast used the port; the Jewish trading
network whose letters ended up in the Cairo geniza ran through it; and the
customs of Aden are the reason the Ayyubids came down to Yemen at all.

This record carries no `reach_km2`. The field is null and would be null even if
a source printed a figure worth having, in the sense that matters: the extent of
Zurayid authority is not the measure of the Zurayid state. That is true of
several polities on this site and is honest here in a way it is not always
honest elsewhere, because the port was the polity and everyone involved knew it.
