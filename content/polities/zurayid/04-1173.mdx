In 1173 Saladin's elder brother Turanshah came down into Yemen with an Ayyubid
army and took the country in a single season: Zabid from the Mahdids on the
coastal plain, the highlands from what remained of the Sulayhid successors, and
Aden from the Zurayids.

The Zurayid end was not much of a fight. The dynasty's last years had gone the
way the Ziyadids' had gone two centuries earlier and on the same coast — the
government in the hands of the sultans' viziers, the sultans themselves
indistinct in the record — and this page's `last` ruler is accordingly null. An
army arriving from Egypt against a port held by a household with a garrison had
an obvious result.

What ended in 1173 was larger than a dynasty. For ninety years the customs of the
richest transfer harbour in the Indian Ocean had belonged to whoever physically
held Aden, and had funded nobody but him. From that year they funded a state
governed from the Yemeni highlands. The Ayyubids took them; the Rasulids, who
were the Ayyubids' deputies and stayed on when the family went home, built the
wealthiest polity Yemen ever had on them, and the Rasulid record two pages over
is largely an account of what Aden's revenue could buy.

That is the succession this page is here to make visible. Nothing passed from
the Zurayids to the Rasulids — no claim, no institution, no edge in this corpus
joins them, and the Rasulids would not have recognised any connection. What
passed was the port, and the port is why both of them existed.
