In 1212 a combined army of Castile, Aragon and Navarre, with a papal crusade
proclamation behind it, destroyed the Almohad field army at Las Navas de Tolosa
in the Sierra Morena.

The defeat did not end the caliphate immediately — that took another fifty
years — but it ended the Almohad ability to hold al-Andalus. Cordoba fell to
Castile in 1236, Valencia in 1238, Seville in 1248. By the 1260s Muslim rule in
Iberia was reduced to the emirate of Granada, which survived as a tributary until
1492 and is not on this site.

The wider Almohad collapse was doctrinal as much as military. The whole structure
rested on Ibn Tumart's status as the infallible mahdi, and a caliphate founded on
a claim of that kind cannot easily survive an obvious divine judgement against
it. The later Almohad caliphs quietly abandoned the mahdist doctrine, which
removed the argument for their own authority, and the Maghreb divided into three
successor states — Hafsid, Zayyanid and Marinid — none of which is in this
corpus.

The empire is the only polity in this site's islamic-west region with a cited
reach figure, and the ranking that results reads oddly against its neighbours:
the Almohads appear substantial and the Aghlabids, Idrisids and Rustamids appear
empty, when the real difference is that Taagepera tabulated one of them.
