Abd al-Mu'min took Marrakesh in 1147 and spent the next sixteen years doing
something no ruler in the Islamic west had done before or would do again: he
held the whole of it. Morocco, the central Maghreb, Ifriqiya, and al-Andalus,
under one man, from one capital.

Ibn Khaldun's account of how the last of that was got is worth following,
because the sequence in it is the sequence of the whole reign — the caliph
setting out for one war and being turned by news of another.

## Mahdia

In 1158 he came to Salé meaning to cross into Spain and fight the Christians;
his son had just been beaten under the walls of Seville and a number of Almohad
shaykhs and doctors had been killed there. Then word came that the kingdom of
Ifriqiya had collapsed and that Christians were holding Mahdia. Sicily had
taken the Tunisian coast a decade earlier from the last of the Zirids.

He turned the army round at Salé, appointed the shaykh Abu Hafs his lieutenant
in the Maghreb and a governor at Fez, and marched east very fast. He besieged
the Sicilians in Mahdia and made them capitulate, and took Sfax, Tripoli and
the rest of the coastal towns after it. While the siege was still going on he
had sent his son against Gabès, which had fallen to an Arab family; the young
man took Gabès, then Gafsa from one clan, Zaghouan from another, Sicca Veneria
from a third, Tobourba from a man who had helped himself to it, Laribus from
the Arabs. Ifriqiya had come apart into a dozen private holdings, and he
collected them one at a time.

Setting out for Marrakesh again in 1161, he learned the Arabs of Ifriqiya had
gone back into revolt, and sent a detachment back to Kairouan to kill their
chief.

## And then Spain

He was still settling Ifriqiya when he heard that Ibn Mardanish, who ruled
independently at Murcia, had besieged Jaén and forced its governor to
acknowledge him, had surprised Carmona, and had destroyed an Almohad army
outside Cordoba and killed its general.

So he wrote to his governors in Spain announcing the triumph of his arms in
Ifriqiya and his intention to come, landed at Gibraltar, gathered the Andalusi
population and the Almohad troops he had already sent over, sent the whole army
against the Christians, and went home to Marrakesh. Granada was lost by
surprise in the night and retaken. Carmona was taken. His two sons beat Ibn
Mardanish and his Christian auxiliaries in the plain outside Granada so badly
that Ibn Mardanish went straight back to his own territory.

Then the caliph set out for one more holy war, reached Salé, and died there.

## What it cost to be that big

Read forward from a map of 1163 and the Almohad empire looks like a settled
fact. Read the campaigns and it is a man with one army going wherever the last
courier came from, and an empire whose furthest provinces were held by the
credible threat that he would arrive personally. Ifriqiya went back into revolt
before he was out of it. Al-Andalus needed the caliph in person twice in five
years. No later Almohad could be in four places, and the empire lasted about as
long as the reputation of the man who could.
