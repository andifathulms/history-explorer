In the winter of 1523 the strongest ruler on the western steppe marched down and
took Astrakhan, and within weeks he was dead and the city had its own khan back.
The episode is the clearest single demonstration of what the khanate was for.

## The march

Mehmed Giray of the Crimea had spent years playing Moscow and Poland against each
other, and Herberstein — who was in Moscow trying to negotiate a peace between
them — watched him do it. Each court bribed him to attack the other; he took the
presents from both and deluded both with vain hopes.

Strengthened by that trade, and allied with Mamai, one of the princes of the
Nogai, he brought an army out of the Crimea in January and fell on Astrakhan. The
khan abandoned the city and fled. Mehmed Giray besieged it, took it, and then did
the thing that killed him: he quartered his army inside the walls and stayed there
as conqueror.

## The advice

Agish, Mamai's brother, was appalled. He rebuked Mamai for having lent his forces
to so powerful a neighbour, and warned him that a man of Mehmed Giray's temper
might well turn on them both next — expel them, kill them, or make slaves of them.

Mamai took the point and sent word for Agish to come with every man he could
raise. Then he sent a message to the khan in Astrakhan, full of concern for his
army: he should not corrupt his soldiers and ruin their discipline by keeping them
housed, but leave the city and live in the open field, *according to the custom of
the Tartars*.

Mehmed Giray agreed. He brought his troops out and encamped in the open country.
Agish arrived and joined his brother. A short time later the two of them fell on
the camp while the khan was at dinner with the son of Sultan Batir, entirely
unsuspecting, and killed him. Most of his army was destroyed and the rest
scattered. The Nogai pursued the wreck beyond the Don and into the Crimea itself,
and got as far as laying siege to Perekop before giving it up and going home.

## What it did

Herberstein's summary is exact: the king of Astrakhan, by the agency of these
princes, regained his kingdom — and the Crimea never recovered the position it had
held under Mehmed Giray.

Astrakhan had been conquered and restored inside a single campaigning season, and
had done nothing whatever to bring either about. It survived because the Nogai
would not tolerate a Crimean delta, and the Crimea would not tolerate a Nogai one,
and each was strong enough to stop the other. That balance was the khanate's
entire defence, and it held for another thirty years, until a fourth party arrived
with boats.
