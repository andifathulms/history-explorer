The second Russian expedition came down the Volga in the summer of 1556 and found
the city empty.

## No siege

There was no defence to make. Dervish Ali had taken what force he had and gone,
and the townspeople had scattered into the delta; the Russians occupied Hajji
Tarkhan without a fight and this time installed nobody. The khanate was annexed,
the Chinggisid line in the delta ended, and a garrison of musketeers was set to
building an earth-and-timber fort on an island in the river a short way below the
old town.

Dervish Ali made for the Crimea and disappears from the record. The Nogai mirza
Ismail, who had murdered his own brother Yusuf and written to the tsar that his
enemy was dead and the people had elected him by acclamation, had supported the
Russians throughout and was rewarded with the friendship of a monarch who
nonetheless declined to let a Tatar prince address him as brother.

## What had actually been conquered

Not much of a state. Astrakhan had never controlled more than its town, its
islands and the fishing and salt grounds around them; its army had never been able
to face any neighbour; its dynasty had been installed and removed by outsiders for
ninety years.

What Moscow had conquered was the Caspian end of the Volga, and with it the
entire river — the first time since the Khazars that the whole of it had belonged
to one power, and the moment at which a forest principality became an empire with
a frontier on Asia. The Persian trade, the Bukharan caravan road and the fisheries
came with it. So did an open steppe frontier and a hundred and fifty years of war
on it.

## The title

Ivan IV's style thereafter ran tsar of all the Russias, of Kazan and of Astrakhan,
and the two khanates stayed in the Russian imperial title until 1917. Conquering
a Chinggisid throne and then wearing it was not incidental. It was the argument:
the successor of the khans, by right of having beaten them, in a realm where that
had been the basis of legitimacy for three hundred years.
