Mahmud Badaruddin II died in exile at Ternate in 1852, more than three
decades after the war that had ended his rule and thirty years after the
Dutch had formally dissolved the sultanate itself. He never returned to the
Musi river, and no restoration was ever attempted in his name or his
descendants'.

## A resistance remembered longer than the state it defended

Independent Indonesia would eventually recognise Badaruddin II as a national
hero, in the same pattern seen elsewhere on this coast: rulers whose armed
resistance to Dutch expansion outlived, in national memory, the two
centuries of ordinary trade and diplomacy that preceded it. The sultanate's long relationship with Jambi, its Bangka tin
contracts, and its place in the routine commerce of the Dutch East India
Company generated the bulk of what survives in the documentary record; the
two years of war at the very end of its existence generated most of what
the sultanate is now actually remembered for.

## The river went on being a river

Palembang itself continued as a city and, eventually, as a major
administrative and commercial centre of Dutch and then Indonesian South
Sumatra, its position at the mouth of the Musi doing the same geographic
work under colonial and national administration that it had done for the
sultanate and, at a distance of centuries neither state had any working
memory of, for Srivijaya before it. Modern Palembang draws on that older,
pre-Islamic history as a source of civic identity today in a way the
sultanate's own rulers, described at the start of this account, never did
or needed to.

## What that sequence adds up to

Three states, on the same stretch of river, separated from each other by
centuries and by every marker — religion, ruling house, relationship to the
outside world — that would ordinarily distinguish one polity from a wholly
unrelated one. Srivijaya, Palembang's own sultanate, and the modern city
that claims descent from both without being continuous with either: the
river outlasted all three arrangements, and each one used it for exactly as
long as it could.
