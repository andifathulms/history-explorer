Ibrahim Pasha demolished Diriyah in 1818, deported the ruling families to Egypt
and beheaded the imam in Istanbul. In 1824 Turki ibn Abdullah, a cousin of the
executed imam who had avoided the deportation, took Riyadh and reconstituted the
state.

Six years is not long. The reasons it was possible are the reasons central
Arabia had been left alone for a thousand years and are worth stating plainly,
because they govern everything on this page. An Egyptian army could reach the
Najd and could not afford to stay in it: garrisons had to be supplied across
eight hundred kilometres of desert from the Hejaz, at a cost out of all
proportion to a province that produced dates and camels. Muhammad Ali had what
he had been commissioned to get, which was the holy cities. The interior was
handed back to whoever could hold it.

And the doctrine had not been deported. The scholars of the Najd, the Al
al-Shaykh among them, went on teaching what they had taught, and the compact of
1744 did not need renegotiating — it needed a Saudi amir to be the other party
to it again.

Turki made Riyadh the capital rather than rebuilding Diriyah, which was rubble
and under a prohibition, and Riyadh has been the capital of every Saudi state
since. He ruled ten years and was assassinated by a cousin in 1834.

What the second state was, and how it differed from the first, is visible in one
field on this record. The Diriyah page carries `plunder` in its revenue basis
because it expanded by raiding and the raiding paid for the expansion. This page
carries `land-tax` alone.
