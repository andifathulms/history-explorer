---
title: The man who could not be khan
drafted_from: manz-1989
phase: formation
---

Timur was born around 1336 into the Barlas, a minor Turkicised Mongol clan in
the western Chagatai lands south of Samarkand. He was not a descendant of
Chinggis Khan, and under the political rules of post-Mongol Central Asia that
meant he could never legitimately hold the title of khan. He never claimed it.

He ruled for thirty-five years as *amir*. He married a Chinggisid princess in
order to style himself *gurkan*, son-in-law of the house. He kept a puppet khan
of the correct descent at his court for the entire period, issued documents in
that khan's name, and when one died he installed another.

Manz's argument, and the reason this page opens on a technicality, is that the
constraint explains the career. A ruler who cannot command by right has to be
followed for some other reason, and the reasons available were plunder,
success and personal obligation — all three of which are consumed by use and
have to be renewed. Timur's following was not an army of a state; it was an
army that was the state, assembled out of Chaghatay tribal contingents whose
leaders had to be kept satisfied and out of personal *nökers* who owed him
directly and could be used against the tribal amirs.

That is why the campaigns never stopped. There was no year of Timur's rule in
which he was not preparing, conducting or returning from one, and the standard
explanation — restless ambition, the will to conquer — describes the behaviour
without accounting for it. A confederation held together by success cannot
afford a quiet decade, and Timur appears to have understood this better than
anyone writing about him since.
