This corpus's Ternate record ends with a chapter about *extirpatie* — the Dutch
company's programme of cutting and burning clove trees everywhere except the
islands it chose, carried out by Ternatan fleets against Ternate's own
dependencies, on the company's orders. It calls it a polity whose entire revenue
was one crop being made to destroy that crop wherever it could not be watched.

Tidore's `revenue_basis` reads `monopoly` and `tribute`, the same pair, and the
same thing was done to it.

The difference is what the eastern network meant.

## Two revenues, one destroyed

The clove monopoly was the value that could be taken away, and it was. What could
not be taken as easily was the tribute — the massoi bark, the plumes, the shell
and the sago coming west along a chain of dependencies running six hundred miles
into New Guinea, through waters the company had no interest in patrolling and no
capacity to.

That is a large part of why this record's span runs a hundred and twenty-two years
past Ternate's. A polity reduced to one commodity that the company controlled had
nothing left when the commodity went. A polity with a second network in
territory nobody else wanted had somewhere to be.

It is also where Nuku's fleet came from, seventeen years of it, which is the
subject of the last chapter.

## The pattern this makes with two other records

Three records in this corpus were reduced by a European power taking away the one
thing they lived on, and the differences between them are instructive.

This corpus's Aceh and Banten records both held crown monopolies on pepper, and
both were broken the same way — by a company that could buy upcountry from the
growers without calling at the capital, after which the monopoly was a claim
rather than a mechanism.

Ternate's clove monopoly was broken the opposite way: not by competition but by
destruction, because cloves grew in a small enough area that eliminating the
supply was cheaper than controlling the trade.

Tidore had the same monopoly destroyed and did not stop, because a second revenue
existed in a direction nobody was looking. That is not resilience in any planned
sense. It is an accident of where the islands are.
