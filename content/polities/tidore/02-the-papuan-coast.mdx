Ternate's half of Maluku ran west and south: Halmahera, the Sula islands, the
eastern arm of Sulawesi. Tidore's ran east, and it ran a very long way.

The Raja Ampat — four island groups off the western tip of New Guinea, whose
rulers held titles from Tidore. The Onin and Kowiai coasts of what is now Papua.
Seram Laut and the islands used as exchange points for the trade beyond them.

What came back along that network was not cloves. It was massoi bark, tortoise
shell, bird of paradise plumes, ambergris, sago, and slaves — and the plumes in
particular went from western New Guinea through Tidore into a trade that reached
China and, eventually, European hat-makers.

## The hongi

The instrument was the *hongi*: a fleet of kora-kora, the long oared war craft
this record and Ternate's both carry as `client-levy`, sailing east in season to
collect tribute, enforce the titles, and take what was owed from anybody who had
not sent it.

Both sultanates ran hongi expeditions. Under the Dutch company the institution
was turned to a different purpose — the clove extirpation voyages this corpus's
Ternate record's last chapter describes, in which Malukan fleets destroyed the
crop on Malukan islands on the company's schedule — but the fleets and the
obligation long predate the company, and this is what they were for.

## Why the religion count is separate

This record's `religions_carried` is one, and so is Ternate's, and the objection
that they double-count a single spread of Islam is answered in this record's last
chapter.

This chapter is the evidence for the answer. The territories are not the same
territories. Ternate's count is for Halmahera and the Sulas; this one is for the
Raja Ampat and the western New Guinea coasts, which became Muslim through
Tidore's titles and Tidore's fleets, and which are the eastern limit of Islam's
spread in the archipelago before the modern period.

Two adjacent islands with two networks running in opposite directions, and a
schema that would have to treat them as one polity to make the objection stick.
