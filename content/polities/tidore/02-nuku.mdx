Tidore outlasted Ternate by a hundred and twenty-two years, and the reason has a
name.

Nuku had been fighting the Dutch from the sea since 1780, with a fleet drawn from
the Papuan coasts and the Raja Ampat islands — Tidore's own dependencies, the
eastern end of its half of the dualism — and with British help when it suited
both of them. In 1797 he took Tidore itself. He held the sultanate as an
independent power until he died in 1805.

Seventeen years of insurgency, then eight years of a state.

## A fourth kind of win

This site has kept a short list. Three records threw a European power out and
kept their capital: the Ya'ariba took Muscat from the Portuguese in 1650, the
Ethiopian record destroyed an Italian army at Adwa in 1896, and Ternate's
five-year siege ended with the Portuguese garrison leaving the island in 1575.

This is a fourth and it is a different shape. Tidore had already been absorbed —
company treaties, a resident, a sultan installed and deposed at the company's
convenience — and Nuku took it back. Not an expulsion but a recovery, by a
polity that on the evidence of its own coded fields had stopped being one.

`ended.type` on this record is `gradual absorption` with the year 1805, and that
is honest about the whole arc rather than about its best moment. What Nuku
recovered did not survive him. But it is why this record's span runs to 1805 and
Ternate's stops at 1683.

## Two counts for one movement

`religions_carried` reads one, and so does Ternate's, and the obvious objection
is that this double-counts a single spread of Islam through the north Moluccas.

It does not, and the reason is the subject of this record's first chapter.
Ternate's count is for Halmahera, the Sula islands and the eastern arm of
Sulawesi. Tidore's is for its own dependencies — the Raja Ampat and the coasts of
western New Guinea, which became Muslim through Tidore, and which are the eastern
limit of Islam's spread in the archipelago before the modern period.

Adjacent islands counting separately for adjacent territories is exactly what the
dualism means. Coding rule 2 asks for propagation past a boundary, not for credit
for a movement, and if the two records had been treated as one the count would
have been measuring a European's idea of Maluku rather than Maluku's.
