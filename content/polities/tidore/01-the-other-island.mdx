This corpus's Ternate record has a chapter called "A record with no thread",
about a polity whose defining relationship could not be expressed on this site at
all. Tidore is visible from Ternate; the channel is about two kilometres wide;
they grew the same crop, spoke closely related languages, had courts of the same
form, and spent five hundred years in opposition. And the schema had nothing —
no edge, because they are contemporaries; no claim in either direction; no
influence count.

Tidore has a page now, and that chapter's argument is unchanged. The two records
sit beside each other in this region with no edge between them and there will
never be one — which is why the region they are in carries `thread: false`.

What the second page does is make the argument checkable. It was possible to
read the Ternate chapter as special pleading — a record explaining away an empty
field. Now both records exist and the fields are empty on both, symmetrically,
for the same reason, and the reader can see the shape of what is missing rather
than being told about it.

## What the dualism was

Andaya's account of Maluku is built on the claim that the opposition was the
organising structure of the region rather than a rivalry between two states that
happened to be adjacent: Maluku understood itself as paired and complementary
halves with two centres, and every island, district and dependency attached
itself to one side or the other.

The clearest evidence for it is what happened when Europeans arrived. Ternate
took the Portuguese in 1512 and Tidore took the Spanish in 1521, and neither
chose by preference — each chose the one the other had not. Two European empires
spent a century in the north Moluccas as the hired halves of a local quarrel that
predated both of them by centuries.

And then, when both Europeans were gone, it kept working. Spain withdrew in
1663; by then Ternate was a Dutch company vassal in all but name, and Tidore
negotiated its own terms and got better ones — precisely because the company
wanted a counterweight rather than a single dependent. The structure outlived the
outsiders it had recruited.

## Where this record starts

1521, the arrival of the Victoria — the last surviving ship of Magellan's
expedition, which reached Maluku eight months after Magellan was killed in the
Philippines and loaded cloves here.

The sultanate is older. Its king list, like Ternate's, is not securely datable,
and this record starts where the documentation does rather than where the
tradition does — the same judgement the Ternate record makes about its own
kolano, and the same one this corpus's Medang record makes about eighth-century
Java.
