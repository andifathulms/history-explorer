Jamshid's brother Ibrahim had fled the disfigured king's cruelty to
Vijayanagara, where the regent Ramraj gave him an estate for his
support - and where, according to Firishta, he killed two men in a
sword duel provoked by a rival officer's insult over that very grant.
When Jamshid died leaving an infant heir, Golconda's own courtiers
squabbled openly over the boy-king until the minister Mustafa Khan
wrote to Ibrahim at Vijayanagara inviting him home; by the time Ibrahim
reached the frontier, six thousand horsemen had already rallied to
him, and he took the throne at Golconda without opposition the day
after his arrival.

## An ally who never stayed allied

Ibrahim's reign became a study in switching sides. He joined Husain
Nizam Shah of Ahmadnagar against Bijapur in 1558, then abandoned the
siege of Kulliani and decamped for home rather than let his own ally
grow too powerful; he joined the combined Bijapur-Vijayanagara army
against Ahmadnagar not long after, then broke camp without warning
once the immediate campaign was won. He sealed a fresh alliance with
Ahmadnagar in 1564 by marrying Husain Nizam Shah's own daughter,
Bibi Jamali, at the siege lines outside Kulliani itself - only for
Bijapur, Vijayanagara and their allies to force both bridegroom and
new father-in-law into retreat within the same campaign.

## A glorious campaign, and a son who paid for ambition

In 1565 Ibrahim joined the general league of Deccan sultans against
Ramraj, the Vijayanagara regent whose own estate had sheltered him in
exile - a coalition that killed Ramraj in battle and occupied his
kingdom's territory, the campaign Firishta calls simply "glorious."
The alliances that had made it possible did not survive contact with
peace: within a few years Ibrahim was fleeing his own former ally
Ahmadnagar after a botched attempt at secret diplomacy with Bijapur
was discovered and exposed, losing a hundred and fifty elephants in
the retreat. His own son Abdul Qadir, denied permission to harass the
pursuing enemy, fell under suspicion of plotting to seize the throne
for himself; Ibrahim had him confined and then poisoned. Ibrahim Qutb
Shah died suddenly in 1581, after thirty-two years on the throne he
had once fled as a young man in fear of his brother.
