There are more published administrative tablets from this hundred and eight
years than from any other comparable stretch of the ancient world. The number
usually given for the Ur III corpus is somewhere well over a hundred thousand,
and it is still rising, and none of it is literature. It is receipts.

A dead sheep is recorded, with the date, the animal's condition, the official
responsible for it and the official who received the carcass. A day's labour by
a named woman in a milling establishment is recorded, with her ration. Reed
bundles, fish, silver, barley, hides, the movement of oxen between provinces —
all of it entered, all of it dated to the day by a system in which every year
has an official name, the names are listed in order on tablets kept for the
purpose, and the sequence has no gaps.

## What that buys, and what it does not

It buys the `revenue_basis` field on this record, which reads `land-tax,
tribute`, and the first value is not the generic thing that phrase usually means
in this corpus. The Ur III state ran a system called the *bala*: each province
delivered into a central pool during its assigned months of the year and drew
from that pool during the others. It is a redistributive rotation rather than a
straightforward tax, and it is reconstructed entirely from the delivery records
themselves — nobody in antiquity described it. Beside it, the *gun mada*, a levy
in cattle and sheep owed by the militarised zone on the eastern frontier.

That is the earliest tax system this corpus can describe in operational detail,
by more than a thousand years, and the next one it can describe at this
resolution is the Neo-Babylonian, at the other end of this region and of this
site's Mesopotamian sequence.

What the receipts do not buy is a number in this site's principal measure.
`reach_km2` on this record is null. Taagepera's tabulations supply the extent of
Akkad, of Mitanni, of Hatti, of Egypt's New Kingdom, of both Assyrias and of
Neo-Babylon, and they do not carry this
dynasty. So the state whose provincial deliveries can be counted by the sheep has
no figure in the column the rankings sort on, and a reader arriving at this
record from `/rankings/` will find it in the same condition as a polity nobody
has opened a source for.

That is worth saying plainly rather than burying in a data comment. Hard rule 3
says a null renders as "no cited figure" and never as a zero, and it is right to,
but the effect here is still that the axis this site measures on and the axis this
record is remarkable on are different axes. Documentation is not size.

## Shulgi

The reign that built the apparatus is the second and longest, forty-seven years.
Standardised weights and measures, a single state calendar imposed across the
provinces, a reformed accounting system with a standard year of three hundred and
sixty days for the bookkeeping, the scribal schools, a road system with staging
posts, a reorganisation of the army, and the conversion of the eastern frontier
into a belt of garrisoned provinces.

None of that is a turning point on this record, because coding rule 4.2 excludes
reigns and the reforms are spread across decades of one. What is dated is the
thing that came with them: in his twenty-first year Shulgi was made a god.
