Bodawpaya opened his reign in 1782 the way Konbaung succession
disputes usually got settled for good: a clean sweep of every
possible rival, their followers, servants and children, remembered
in the chronicles as the Massacre of the Kinsmen. Singu's queens and
concubines, who had spent his reign laughing in forest glades while
he fished and prayed, were burned alive holding their own children.
Months later Bodawpaya discovered his own brother plotting against
him with the help of Mahathihathura, the veteran general who had
commanded the Guard against Maung Maung's coup and won some of the
dynasty's greatest victories - a betrayal that convinced the king
never again to trust anyone, kinsman or otherwise, for the rest of
his life; from that point on he changed his sleeping quarters every
night. The entire faction, general included, was exterminated. A
minor pretender who scaled the palace wall with two hundred badly
armed followers at the end of the same year met the same fate, along
with the village that had supposedly backed him - burned to the
ground, its people killed down to the infants, its orchards cut down
and its fields ploughed under.

## A count of the whole kingdom

Once secure, Bodawpaya turned to administration on a scale no
Konbaung king had attempted: a revenue inquest in 1784, repeated in
1803, that had village headmen across the country depose the
boundaries of their own jurisdictions, what they grew, what they
paid, and how many people lived there - a survey giving the crown a
clearer picture of its own resources than it had held since 1638,
when a Restored Toungoo king had last attempted anything similar.
Ordinary people, unsurprisingly, read a nationwide inquest the same
way the English had once read the Domesday Book: not as good
government but as the prelude to fresh taxes. Bodawpaya decreed at
the same inquest that legal claims to land should not lapse merely
because a king or dynasty changed - a rule as often ignored as
followed, since Burma's institutions kept getting reset by exactly
the kind of violent succession that had just put him on the throne.

## A conquest that reached the British frontier

The one conquest of Bodawpaya's reign that outlasted everything else
he did was Arakan, annexed in 1785 after the small, war-weakened
coastal kingdom offered little resistance. It convinced Bodawpaya he
was destined to conquer the world - he spoke openly of annexing both
India and China - and the following year he launched a four-pronged
invasion of Siam that collapsed from poor coordination among its own
commanders rather than Siamese resistance. Arakan mattered more than
either ambition. It gave Bodawpaya's kingdom, for the first time, a
land border with territory the English East India Company actually
governed in Bengal - and Arakanese refugees fleeing north across that
same border, along with Burmese troops who crossed it chasing them,
would spend the next four decades turning an administrative
afterthought into the spark for a war neither side, in 1785, had any
reason yet to expect.
