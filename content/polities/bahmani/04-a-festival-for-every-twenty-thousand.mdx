Ahmad Shah spent his early reign rebuilding the army for a reckoning
with Vijayanagara, still resented for its invasions under his brother.
He marched south with forty thousand horse and camped across the
Tungabhadra from the Raja's own army for forty days, hoping to draw
the Hindus into a set battle on ground of his own choosing; when they
would not cross, his officers forded the river by night instead and
fell on the Raja's camp at dawn. The Raja himself, found half-dressed
fleeing into a stand of sugar cane, was seized by soldiers who mistook
him for a villager and made him carry a bundle of cane before them -
and so escaped unrecognized in the chaos that followed, reaching his
own officers again only by early afternoon.

## An occupation without mercy

What followed was not the punitive raid-and-tribute pattern of earlier
campaigns. Ahmad Shah overran the open country and, by Firishta's own
account, "put to death men, women, and children, without mercy" -
breaking with the understanding his brother and Muhammad Shah before
him had kept with Vijayanagara's kings, and halting for three days of
celebration each time the toll of the dead reached twenty thousand. He
tore down temples and destroyed Brahmin colleges as he went. A band of
five thousand Hindus, driven to desperation by the destruction of
their own shrines, swore an oath to kill the king in revenge and set
spies to track his movements, though Firishta's account does not
record them succeeding.

## A saint's tomb, and a captured city's namesake

Returning north from this campaign, Ahmad Shah stopped to hunt near
the old fortress town of Bidar and chose the spot to found a new
capital, Ahmadabad Bidar, on the ancient site. He was, in his own
lifetime, revered by his subjects as a wali - a saint - for his
devotion to the Sufi teacher Geesu Daraz, whom he showered with
grants of land near Gulbarga and whose shrine Firishta says became a
place of pilgrimage across the Deccan; Firishta calls him, by his own
count, only the sixth king in Indian history to carry that title.
