Taj ud-Din Firuz Shah, who came to the throne in 1397, was by
Firishta's own account the Bahmani dynasty's most accomplished king -
fought twenty-four campaigns, took the fortress of Bankapur, and
brought much of Telangana under Bahmani rule for the first time. He
was also the first Deccan king to wear a jeweled crown in the Persian
style, and Firishta records him consulting his own religious scholars
on how far Islamic law would let him indulge an appetite for women
that outran the four wives the law permitted - a private vanity that
sat alongside the same energy he brought to war and administration.

## A war begun over a stolen bride, and a marriage that ended it

Early in his reign a botched attempt to seize a farmer's daughter from
the border town of Mudkal for his own household gave the Raja of
Vijayanagara grounds to raid Bahmani territory in return. Firuz Shah
answered by besieging Vijayanagara itself for months, ravaging the
country around it and reducing Bankapur, until the Raja bought peace
with an immense indemnity, the permanent loss of that fortress, and
his own daughter's hand in marriage - a match Firishta notes was
contrary to Hindu custom, which married daughters only within caste.
The peace did not hold; war broke out again within Firuz Shah's own
reign, severe enough at one point that Firishta says the survival of
the Bahmani kingdom itself was in question, before the sultan's
brother Ahmad finally broke the Hindu army in the field.

## A son passed over, a brother crowned

Firuz Shah's mind and health failed him in his final years, and his
brother Ahmad Khan, governing as Khan Khanan, moved to claim the
throne over the sultan's own son Hasan Khan. The two armies met
outside the capital; when a false report of Firuz Shah's death in the
fighting swung the soldiers to Ahmad's banner, Firuz retreated into
the citadel and, on recovering from a fainting fit brought on by the
alarm, judged the contest already lost. He called his son before him,
told him that a throne rested on the loyalty of the army and nobility
rather than on birthright, and opened the gates. Ahmad ascended the
throne as Ahmad Shah ten days before Firuz Shah's own death; Firishta
records, without endorsing it, a rumor current in his own time that
Ahmad's nephew had the dying king strangled rather than let nature
take its course.
