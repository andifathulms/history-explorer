Struensee's enemies multiplied as fast as his reforms alienated
Danish opinion: a nobility that resented a German adventurer who had
made German the language of government, a public scandalized by his
moral reforms, and a court where he was noted, more than once during
riots, to lack any real personal courage of his own. A powerful
secret conspiracy took shape around the Queen Dowager, Juliane
Marie, and struck in early 1772. Struensee was arrested in his own
bedroom and chained to a prison wall, prosecuted for usurping royal
authority, for lèse-majesté, and for injury to the king's honour. He
denied everything until he learned the queen herself had also been
arrested, at which point he confessed the nature of their
relationship in full.

## A sentence out of an older age

Struensee and his ally Enevold Brandt, convicted of personal
violence toward the king, were sentenced to a mode of execution that
belonged to a much older century than the one that had produced
Struensee's own Enlightenment reforms: on 28 April 1772, his right
hand was cut off first, then his head, after which the head was set
on a pole and his body drawn and quartered. Caroline Matilda, not yet
twenty-one, was imprisoned at Kronborg Castle with her infant
daughter; she had tried to shield Struensee, but once confronted with
his own confession admitted the truth herself. An extraordinary court
of thirty-five members sentenced her to divorce from the king on 6
April, and only her brother George III's direct intervention - he
insisted she be treated as an English princess and sent a British
warship to Elsinore - spared her lifelong imprisonment in a Danish
fortress. She was allowed to leave for Hanover, but alone, without
her daughter, and lived out her remaining years at Celle, beloved by
its townspeople, while plans hatched with British and Danish
sympathizers to restore her to power in Copenhagen came to nothing.
She died of smallpox on 10 May 1775, not yet twenty-four - a queen
whose entire public life, from her triumphal entry into Copenhagen at
fifteen to her death in exile, spanned less than a decade.
