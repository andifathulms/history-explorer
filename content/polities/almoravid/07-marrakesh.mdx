The Almoravid state was destroyed by an argument about the nature of God, and
the argument took about twenty-five years to arrive at the gate of the city
that had been built to house it.

Ibn Tumart's charge was not that the Almoravids ruled badly. It was that their
attachment to the letter of Maliki law and their suspicion of speculative
theology had led them to read the anthropomorphic language of scripture
literally, and so to describe God as having a body — which made them not lax
Muslims but unbelievers, against whom war was lawful. Dynasties end by
conquest, by fragmentation, by revolt and by the failure of a line. This one
ended because somebody won a disputation about the divine attributes and then
raised the mountains on the strength of it.

## The last two

After Tashfin's death at Oran his son Ibrahim was proclaimed at Marrakesh. Ibn
Khaldun disposes of him in a sentence: the weakness and incapacity of this
prince brought about his deposition. In his place they put his uncle Ishaq,
son of Ali and grandson of Yusuf ibn Tashfin — a boy, raised to a throne with
an Almohad army finishing the last of the Maghreb outside.

When the army arrived, Ishaq came out to present himself before Abd al-Mu'min
with his household and his favourites around him. The Almohads killed all of
them in front of the conqueror.

Abd al-Mu'min crossed into Spain, and death closed on the Lamtuna from every
side, and their commanders went down with them. It is the end of a family and
of the tribal confederation that had carried it, in a city the family had
founded seventy years earlier on empty ground because it had nowhere else to
be.

## The one that got away

A band of fugitives got out to the eastern islands — Mallorca, Menorca and
Ibiza. They did not stay there quietly. Ibn Khaldun, writing two centuries
later, still counts what they built afterwards in Ifriqiya as the Almoravid
monarchy, which means the dynasty outlived its own destruction by eighty
years.
