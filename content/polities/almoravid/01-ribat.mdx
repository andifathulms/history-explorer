The Almoravids began among the Sanhaja Berber confederations of the western
Sahara — veiled, camel-borne, and controlling the northern end of the gold routes
out of the Sudan. A scholar named Abd Allah ibn Yasin was invited to teach them
Maliki law in the 1040s and built a disciplined religious-military movement out
of the result. The name is usually derived from *ribat*, the frontier
fortress-monastery, though the etymology is disputed.

Under Yusuf ibn Tashfin they took Morocco, founded Marrakesh, and were then
invited across the strait. The Cordoban caliphate had collapsed in 1031 into the
*taifa* kingdoms — a few dozen small states, several of them paying tribute to
Christian rulers to be left alone — and after Alfonso VI took Toledo in 1085 the
taifa kings asked for help.

Yusuf beat Alfonso at Sagrajas in 1086, went home, came back, and then deposed
the taifa kings one after another on the grounds that rulers who paid tribute to
Christians and taxed Muslims illegally had forfeited their authority. He had a
legal opinion from the jurists of Cordoba and one from Baghdad to that effect.
Al-Andalus was governed from Marrakesh for the next sixty years.
