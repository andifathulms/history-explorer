title: The gold road
drafted_from: bennison-2016

The Almoravids began as a Saharan movement sitting on the northern end of the
trans-Saharan gold routes, and the gold is why they could pay for the rest.

West African gold from Bambuk and Bure moved north by camel to Sijilmasa and
Awdaghust and from there into the Mediterranean economy. Almoravid control of
both termini — they took Awdaghust in 1054 and Sijilmasa in 1055, before they
took Morocco — gave them a bullion supply that funded an army and produced a gold
dinar of such reliability that it circulated in Christian Spain and was imitated
there.

The southern end of the same system was the Sudanic empires — Ghana while the
Almoravids were rising, Mali afterwards — and Mansa Musa's pilgrimage two and a
half centuries later is the same gold moving up the same routes. The fiscal
history of the Maghreb and the political history of the western Sudan are one
subject, and nothing in either of them can be weighed without the other.

The dynasty's relationship with the scholars is worth a line. The Almoravids
enforced Maliki law strictly, deferred to its jurists, and were denounced by the
Almohads for exactly that — Ibn Tumart's charge was that a rigid attachment to
legal texts, at the expense of theology, had produced rulers who described God in
bodily terms. That is the doctrinal argument that ended them, and dynasties do
not often fall to an objection of that kind.
