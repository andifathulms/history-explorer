Ibn Khaldun gives the Almoravid remnant its own heading, and the heading is the
argument: *History of the empire founded at Gabès and Tripoli by Ibn Ghaniya,
last representative of the Almoravid monarchy.* A dynasty that lost its capital
in 1147 is still being written up as a going concern in Ifriqiya two
generations later.

## Named after a woman

The family begins with a quarrel. Ali ibn Yusuf al-Massufi was one of the
bravest and most influential chiefs of his tribe and stood high at the court of
Yusuf ibn Tashfin, until he killed a Lamtuna chief in a dispute and had to run
for the desert, leaving a war between the Massufa and the Lamtuna behind him.
Some years later Yusuf paid the blood price out of his own treasury, which
freed the man to come home, and then married him to a kinswoman of his own
called Ghaniya, discharging a promise he had made to her father.

Their sons were brought up in the palace. Ali ibn Yusuf, the next ruler, gave
one of them western al-Andalus at Cordoba and the other the Balearic islands.
Shortly after that the empire fell down. The Banu Ghaniya still had the
islands, and were known then and afterwards by the name of the woman the
marriage had been made with rather than by the name of any of the men.

## Thirty-two ships

Three of them ruled Mallorca in turn, and Ibn Khaldun records without deciding
between them two accounts of how the third got it: that his brother died, or
that he had his father and his brother both assassinated. When he died in the
1180s he left eight sons, and the eldest at once submitted to the Almohad
caliph. His brothers thought otherwise. When the caliph's agent arrived at
Mallorca to test how sincere the submission was, they deposed their eldest
brother, installed the next one, stopped the agent leaving, and — on hearing that the Almohad caliph had been
killed in battle and his son Ya'qub had succeeded him — threw him into the
bottom of a prison.

Then Ali ibn Ishaq fitted out thirty-two ships, took three of his brothers, and
sailed for Africa. They walked into Bougie in May 1185 without a fight, because
nobody there had imagined they were coming. The Almohad prince commanding the
city was away on an excursion. They arrested another Almohad prince who
happened to be passing through on his way from Ifriqiya, and emptied the houses
of the governing family.

What followed was twenty years of the most destructive campaigning in the
Maghreb: Algiers, Miliana, the Qal'a of the Banu Hammad stormed in three days,
Constantine besieged, and the whole thing financed by distributing the plunder
to whatever Arab tribes would ride with them, who arrived, Ibn Khaldun says,
with their tents, their flocks and their families. He is not neutral about
them. He was a Hafsid official and the Hafsids inherited the wreckage.

## What it means to end

The Almohads eventually put them down. But the dynasty that had been finished
at Marrakesh in 1147 went on taking cities until the 1230s, from a base of
three islands, under a name inherited through a marriage arranged to settle a
homicide — and it was still being fought when the empire that destroyed it had
itself begun to come apart.

An ending is a date somebody chose. It is worth asking, each time, what it is
the date of.
