This chapter was called "A value the vocabulary does not have", and for a long
time this page's `legitimation` field was null. The field now reads
`investiture`. What follows is the argument that was made here, kept, and then
what happened to it — because the argument is the more useful half and deleting
it would leave the site looking as though the value had always been obvious.

## The case

The Jafnids' public ground was an imperial grant. Al-Harith was phylarch and
patrician because Justinian made him so, the rank was announced and displayed,
and the family's standing among the Arabs of the frontier rested visibly on
Constantinople's recognition. That is exactly the structure this corpus records
dozens of times over as `caliphal-investiture`: a ruler whose right to rule is
asserted on the ground that a universal sovereign conferred it.

The vocabulary had a value for investiture by a caliph and none for investiture
by an emperor. The rulebook explains why `caliphal-investiture` was separated
out — it is the characteristic move of half this corpus, and collapsing it into
a general category would hide the pattern the site exists to show — which is a
good argument that produced a gap the moment the corpus reached a polity outside
that half.

The available values did not close it. `titulature` means taking a
*predecessor's* title as one's own ground, which is Palmyra taking king of kings
and the Ottomans taking Kayser-i Rûm; a title conferred by a living sovereign who
could and did revoke it is the opposite arrangement. `descent` was available to
the Jafnids and is not what they led with. `conquest` misdescribes a family that
held its position by grant and lost it by decree. `divine-sanction` is simply
false.

So the field stayed null and this chapter carried the answer, which is the
outcome the coding rules prescribe: coding this `titulature` because it was the
nearest familiar label is precisely what standing requirement 4 in part three
forbids, and it forbids it in these words — when a polity outside the corpus's
centre of gravity does not fit, the answer is null or a vocabulary change argued
on the merits, never the nearest familiar label.

The last paragraph used to end: the merits of the change are arguable and this
site has not made it, because doing so would mean revisiting every polity that
held an office from a sovereign it did not claim to be, and that is a decision
about the whole data model rather than about this page.

## What happened

Eight more records hit the same wall over the following months, in five regions
and across fifteen hundred years. A Hujrid phylarchate held from Himyar. Timur
ruling thirty-five years beside a Chinggisid puppet because he was barred by
descent from the title himself, and Hasan Buzurg doing the same with Ilkhans a
generation earlier. Karim Khan Zand refusing the title of shah and governing as
deputy for a Safavid infant. A Shaddadid branch holding Ani on a Seljuk diploma.
The sharifs of Mecca, qualified by descent from the Prophet and appointed by
whoever held Egypt. The kings of Caucasian Iberia, whom Rome acquired the right
to invest by treaty in 298, and the kings of Greater Armenia, nominated by
Parthia and crowned by Rome under the settlement of AD 63.

Nine records is a pattern rather than an awkward case, and the change was made:
`investiture` was added as the genus and `caliphal-investiture` kept as the
species, on the rule that where the conferring authority is a caliph you use the
specific value and otherwise the general one.

## The objection that held it up

Worth recording, because it was the reason for the delay and it was wrong.

The objection was that a single `investiture` cannot distinguish a Roman client
kingship from a Mongol yarligh from an Ottoman berat from a treaty between two
empires, and that a value which flattens all of those says almost nothing.

It cannot distinguish them, and neither can any other value on the list.
`descent` does not name the ancestor. `conquest` does not name the conquered.
`divine-sanction` does not name the god, and covers the mandate of heaven, an
Egyptian king who *is* Horus, and a claim to be the mahdi. Not naming the
counterparty is how the entire vocabulary works. `caliphal-investiture` is the
anomaly for being specific, and an anomaly is not the standard a new value has to
clear.

Which leaves the specific value looking harder to justify than the general one,
and it is kept anyway, for the reason the rulebook already gives: half this
corpus made that particular move, and a site whose subject includes the Abbasid
delegation of authority should be able to see it in a field rather than only in
prose.

## What did not change

The audit that added the value also went the other way twice, and both are worth
naming.

The Danishmendid record had only `conquest`, and the sources have Emir Gazi sent
the insignia of a sultan from Baghdad — a diploma, a standard and a robe. That is
`caliphal-investiture`, the old value, missed. It has been added.

The Hotaki record still reads `conquest`. Its comment used to say the vocabulary
had a value for authorisation by a caliph and none for authorisation by a jurist,
and coding rule 8 now names a jurist among the authorities `investiture` covers.
The field did not change, because rule 8's test is publicity and the ruling
Mirwais is reported to have obtained at Mecca cannot be shown to have existed. A
vocabulary gap closing is not a licence to fill the fields that pointed at it.
