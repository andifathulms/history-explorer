---
title: A value the vocabulary does not have
drafted_from: fisher-2011
phase: aside
---

This page's `legitimation` field is null, and the reason is not that nothing is
known. It is that the answer is known and the vocabulary cannot say it.

The Jafnids' public ground was an imperial grant. Al-Harith was phylarch and
patrician because Justinian made him so, the rank was announced and displayed,
and the family's standing among the Arabs of the frontier rested visibly on
Constantinople's recognition. That is exactly the structure this corpus records
forty times over as `caliphal-investiture`: a ruler whose right to rule is
asserted on the ground that a universal sovereign conferred it.

The vocabulary has a value for investiture by a caliph and none for investiture
by an emperor. The rulebook explains why `caliphal-investiture` was separated
out — it is the characteristic move of half this corpus, and collapsing it into
a general category would hide the pattern the site exists to show — which is a
good argument that has produced a gap the moment the corpus reached a polity
outside that half.

The available values do not close it. `titulature` means taking a
*predecessor's* title as one's own ground, which is Palmyra taking king of
kings and the Ottomans taking Kayser-i Rûm; a title conferred by a living
sovereign who could and did revoke it is the opposite arrangement. `descent`
was available to the Jafnids and is not what they led with. `conquest`
misdescribes a family that held its position by grant and lost it by decree.
`divine-sanction` is simply false.

So the field is null and this chapter carries the answer, which is the outcome
the coding rules prescribe: a value without a source that addresses the question
is not enterable, and neither is a value the vocabulary does not contain. The
alternative — coding this `titulature` because it is the nearest familiar label
— is precisely what standing requirement 4 in part three forbids, and it
forbids it in these words: when a polity outside the corpus's centre of gravity
does not fit, the answer is null or a vocabulary change argued on the merits,
never the nearest familiar label.

The merits of the change are arguable and this site has not made it. Doing so
would mean revisiting every polity in the corpus that held an office from a
sovereign it did not claim to be, and that is a decision about the whole data
model rather than about this page.
