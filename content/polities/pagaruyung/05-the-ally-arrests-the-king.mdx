In February 1821 the reigning king, Sultan Alam Bagagarsyah, together with a
number of adat chiefs from the surrounding districts, signed an agreement
ceding Pagaruyung, Suruaso, Sungai Tarab and neighbouring territory to the
Dutch East Indies government. In exchange the Dutch undertook to supply
troops against the Padris.

It was the last significant act the Pagaruyung kingship performed as an
independent party to anything, and it was an act of surrender dressed as an
alliance. The kingship no longer had the means to defend its own capital or
compel the customary order it claimed to arbitrate; what it still had was the
one asset it had always traded in — recognised legitimacy — and it spent
that asset on Dutch soldiers.

## What twelve years bought

The alliance did what it was supposed to do in the narrow sense: Dutch and
allied forces campaigned against the Padri strongholds for the following
decade and a half, in a war that would not fully end until the fall of
Bonjol years later. What it did not do was restore the kingship to anything
resembling its former standing. The court at Pagaruyung existed now inside a
colonial administrative structure, its authority no longer independent of
the power it had invited in.

On 2 May 1833, Sultan Alam Bagagarsyah was arrested by Dutch troops at
Batusangkar on suspicion of conspiring against the government he had allied
himself with twelve years earlier. He was exiled to Batavia and never
returned to the highlands. He died there in 1849, sixteen years into an
exile that outlasted his kingdom by that same span.

## What ended, precisely

Nobody was installed in his place. The office of Raja Alam, and the wider
structure of the Kings of the Three Seats, did not pass to an heir or a rival
house; it simply stopped being occupied by anyone the Dutch administration
answered to. Dutch rule over the highlands, already substantially in place
since 1821, continued without requiring a Pagaruyung king to legitimate it.

A kingship that had lasted nearly five centuries by supplying legitimacy to
other people's authority ended when the power it had supplied its own
legitimacy to decided it no longer needed the office at all.
