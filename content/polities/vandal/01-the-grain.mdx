The Vandals crossed the Rhine in 406, moved through Gaul into Spain, and in 429
crossed to Africa — the whole group, with the Alans who had joined them, in a
crossing the sources put at eighty thousand people and modern accounts treat with
caution. Ten years later Geiseric took Carthage.

That is the moment this record begins, because that is when a group moving
through other people's provinces becomes a state holding the richest one.

## What Carthage was

This corpus's Roman record has a chapter on the annona: something in the order of
two hundred thousand tonnes of grain a year, shipped from Egypt and North Africa
to feed the city of Rome, in a state-contracted fleet, distributed free to a
registered list of citizens.

Egypt had gone east with the division of 395. Africa went here, in 439, along
with the shipyards and the fleet.

The western empire lost its food supply and a very large share of its remaining
tax revenue in a single afternoon, and never recovered either. Everything in the
next thirty-seven years of the western empire's existence — this site's Roman
record ends in 395 and the western administration limped on to 476 — happens to a
government that cannot feed its own capital or pay its own army.

## A raiding economy, coded

This record's `revenue_basis` reads `land-tax, plunder`, and the second value is
coded in coding rule 6's strict sense, which asks for a source that treats
campaign proceeds as structural income rather than as windfall.

Merrills and Miles do exactly that. Geiseric's fleet raided the Mediterranean
annually for decades, and the raiding produced not just movable wealth but
tribute agreements, ransom payments, hostages and, in 474, a treaty by which the
eastern empire recognised the kingdom and paid for peace. It is a foreign policy
conducted by fleet, and the fleet was the state's principal revenue instrument
for most of the reign.

The other half of the field is the Roman land tax, on the provinces that had not
been distributed to Vandal families, collected through the surviving provincial
administration in the same way the Ostrogothic record beside this one collected
it in Italy.

## The difference from Italy

Both records code `military_basis: land-grant` and the difference in how is the
whole reason one kingdom lasted ninety-five years and the other sixty.

In Italy the Goths largely took a share of the *tax* on land, which left the
Roman landowning aristocracy in possession and gave it a reason to cooperate. In
Africa the Vandals took the *land* — the *sortes Vandalorum*, carved out of the
estates of the African senatorial class in the richest province, whose owners
were dispossessed, exiled or reduced.

That produced a permanently hostile aristocracy and, when the confessional
quarrel in the third chapter was added to it, a province with no reason at all to
fight for its rulers. The last chapter is what that looked like in 534.
