The Vandals are the only one of the peoples who came into the western empire
who solved the problem the others did not, which was money, and they solved it
by taking the richest province in the west and then building a navy.

Procopius, who came to Africa with the army that destroyed them, opens his
account with the century before he arrived. A Germanic people crossed into
Africa in 429 under Geiseric, took the Roman provinces one at a time, and in 439
took Carthage — the second city of the western empire and the port through which
the grain that fed Rome was shipped.

Everything afterwards follows from the harbour.

## What a fleet bought

An enemy who has ships and is not on your border is a different kind of problem
from one who is.

The Vandals raided Sicily, Sardinia, Corsica, the Balearics and the coasts of
Italy and Greece at will, and the empires could not reach them. In 455 a Vandal
fleet came up the Tiber and took Rome itself, and held the city for a fortnight —
methodically, without a massacre, and removing everything portable, including the
gilded roof tiles of the temple of Jupiter and the treasures Titus had brought
back from Jerusalem.

Then in 468 the eastern and western empires combined for the largest amphibious
operation of the century, a reported hundred thousand men and a thousand ships,
to finish the Vandal kingdom for good. Geiseric asked for a five-day truce,
used it, filled ships with combustible material, and sent them downwind into the
anchored Roman fleet at night. The expedition was destroyed. It reportedly cost
the eastern treasury the equivalent of several years of revenue and the western
empire the last credibility it had.

The Vandals were then left alone for sixty-five years.

## The other half of it

What made the fleet possible was that the Vandals had taken a working province
rather than a devastated one.

Africa Proconsularis was the granary and it was intact. Its estates, its olive
presses, its export trade and its tax registers survived the conquest, because
what the conquerors wanted was to own them. Geiseric expropriated the Roman
landowners of the best districts and settled his own people on the estates as
proprietors — which is a change of ownership rather than a change of economy, and
the pottery and the oil kept going out.

That is the whole Vandal position: a small conquering population living on the
revenues of a rich province, with a fleet paid for out of it, in a place two
empires could not easily reach.

It worked for a hundred years, and then a single expedition of about fifteen
thousand men ended it in a season.
