British anxiety over Russian influence in Tibet, more than any
Tibetan provocation, produced the next real crisis for the Ganden
Phodrang's own government. A mission under Colonel Younghusband,
sent in 1903 to negotiate trade and frontier questions, found Tibetan
officials unwilling to talk and grew, over the following months, into
a full military expedition - fighting its way from the Chumbi Valley
to Gyantse and on to Lhasa itself against Tibetan soldiers armed with
antiquated matchlocks and swords against modern rifles. The Dalai
Lama and his entourage fled north to Urga in Mongolia rather than
receive the British in his own capital, leaving his ministers to sign
the Lhasa Convention of 7 September 1904 in his absence.

## A deposition nobody in Lhasa accepted

Beijing tried to use the crisis to reassert its own authority
directly, issuing a proclamation deposing the Thirteenth Dalai Lama
outright. The Tibetans' answer was immediate and unambiguous: they
smeared the proclamation with dirt and, throughout the years of His
Holiness's exile - first in Mongolia, then in China itself, where he
was received by the Qing court - continued referring every important
question of state to him in absentia, exactly as though the
deposition had never been proclaimed at all. He did not see his own
capital again until the very end of 1909, more than five years after
he had fled it.

## A homecoming that lasted weeks

The Dalai Lama's return to Lhasa proved brief. Chinese troops under
the aggressive general Chao Erh-feng had already been subjugating
eastern Tibet by force in his absence, and in February 1910 some two
thousand Chinese soldiers advanced on Lhasa itself; an advance party
entered the city, fired on the population, and moved to seize the
Tibetan ministers outright. Fearing execution or permanent
imprisonment for the man they had just welcomed home, the Dalai Lama
and his government fled the capital again in the middle of the night
- this time south toward India rather than north toward Mongolia,
with the Ganden Phodrang's own seals of office carried out alongside
them.
