In 1521 the begs of Kazan expelled the khan Moscow had given them and sent for a
Giray from the Crimea. It was the most consequential decision the khanate ever
took, and it converted a managed client into a member of a hostile alliance.

## Why it happened

Because the alternative had become intolerable and because an alternative had
become available.

Thirty-four years of Muscovite appointment had produced khans who were selected
for obedience, and the Kazan aristocracy had watched three of them removed by
Moscow when they showed any. The city's merchants had also seen Moscow attempt to
divert the Volga fair to Nizhny Novgorod — a direct attack on the khanate's
revenue, aimed at the one thing everybody in Kazan agreed about.

At the same time the Crimea under Mehmed Giray had become the strongest of the
successor states, had destroyed the rump of the Golden Horde, and was collecting
gifts from both Moscow and Poland. A Giray on the throne of Kazan meant Ottoman
protection at one remove and a patron whose interests pointed away from the
Volga rather than at it.

So they took one: Sahib Giray, brother of the Crimean khan, and after him his
nephew Safa Giray.

## What it produced

A war that ran, with interruptions, for thirty-one years.

In the same year the Crimean and Kazan armies attacked Muscovy together and
reached the outskirts of Moscow; the grand prince left the city; the Crimeans
withdrew with an enormous number of captives and a document acknowledging tribute
which Moscow subsequently disavowed. That campaign is the high point of the
Jochid states in Russia, and Kazan's part in it is the reason the khanate has a
peak at all.

It is also the moment Kazan's destruction became a Russian strategic objective
rather than a dynastic convenience. A khanate that could be managed was worth
managing. A khanate that could open a second front for the Crimea was worth
taking.

## The two parties

For the next generation the khanate had a Crimean party and a Moscow party and
the throne alternated between them, sometimes in the same year.

The pattern is exhausting to follow and the exhaustion is the point: khans
installed, expelled, recalled and murdered; Muscovite armies in the field in
1524, 1530, 1545, 1548, 1550; Crimean reinforcements arriving and leaving; the
Nogai hordes selling their support to whichever side was paying. Safa Giray was
put on the throne three separate times.

What no Russian campaign could do before 1551 was hold anything. The armies
arrived in summer, could not be supplied at the walls, and went home.

## The subject peoples

The other thing the thirty years did was detach the khanate from its own
hinterland.

The Mari, Chuvash, Udmurt and Mordvin peoples paid the khanate's tribute and
filled its levies, and had no dynastic loyalty to the Girays whatever. As the
wars went on and the Russian frontier came closer, Moscow began taking their
oaths directly — offering the same tribute burden under a different collector,
with the argument that the collector was winning.

By 1551 most of the right bank had gone over. The khanate that faced the final
siege was a city and a Tatar aristocracy, without the forest levies that had
supplied two-thirds of its army in Herberstein's description.
