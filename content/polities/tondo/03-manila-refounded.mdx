Legazpi himself had not been present for the burning; he learned of it in
Panay, where he had spent the intervening months settling the affairs of
Cebu and the other islands his expedition had already subdued. In 1571
he sailed north to Manila Bay in person, and this time found a peaceful
reception waiting for him - the chiefs of the ruined town and its
neighbors reaffirming the friendship and submission they had already
offered Goiti the year before, this time without the ambush.

## A name kept, a river's other bank left standing

On the nineteenth of May, 1571 - the feast day of Saint Potentiana -
Legazpi founded a Spanish city on the exact ground where Maynila had
stood, choosing the site for its defensibility and its position at the
center of the islands rather than for comfort, since the narrow spit of
land between river and sea left it hot and cramped. He kept the town's
own name, transliterated into Spanish as Manila, and made it the seat of
colonial government for the whole archipelago. Across the river, the
allied town the Spanish had called Candola continued to stand under its
own chief, absorbed into the new order as a native settlement rather than
razed alongside its neighbor - the second half of the river-mouth
partnership that had first met Goiti's ships, still there when the
Spanish city rose on the other bank.

Seeing what had happened at Manila, Morga records, the natives of the
neighboring islands of the bay came in peacefully to make their own
submission and to provision Legazpi's camp, sparing the rest of the
region the fate of Soliman's town - a peace bought, in the end, by the
example of the one that had already burned.
