---
title: The chamberlain who did not need the title
drafted_from: kennedy-1996
phase: peak
---

Ibn Abi Amir, called al-Mansur, was a lawyer's son from a minor Arab family who
entered the palace administration as a clerk and by 981 was governing
al-Andalus. He never claimed the caliphate. He did not need to: he had the
caliph.

The method is worth setting out because this corpus keeps producing it. He
attached himself to the household of Hisham II's mother, took the chamberlainship
— the *hajib*, the office controlling access to the caliph — and then used
control of access as the whole of his authority. Hisham was kept in Madinat
al-Zahra, of age, uncontested and unseen, for the rest of al-Mansur's life. In
978 al-Mansur built himself a rival palace-city, Madinat al-Zahira, and moved the
government into it, so that the administration met in his house and not the
caliph's. Coins were struck and the Friday sermon preached in Hisham's name
throughout.

He held it by campaigning. The sources credit him with something over fifty
expeditions against the Christian north in twenty-five years, of which the raid
that reached Santiago de Compostela in 997 and carried its church bells back to
Cordoba is the one everybody remembers. These were not conquests — almost none
of the ground was held — and they were not meant to be. They kept an army in the
field, produced plunder to pay it, and manufactured the one thing a usurper
without a claim could offer: a continuous demonstration that the arrangement
worked.

The army was the problem underneath. To campaign every year he recruited heavily
from Berber contingents brought over from the Maghreb, who were loyal to him
personally and had no standing in Cordoban society at all. That is the same
instrument Abd al-Rahman III had used for the same reason, applied harder, by a
man whose position rested on nothing else.

He died in 1002 on the way back from a campaign. His son held the arrangement
for six years. His second son claimed the succession to the caliphate itself in
1008, which was the one thing the fiction could not survive, and the next
chapter but one is what happened.
