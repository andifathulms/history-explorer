---
title: What al-Hakam left
drafted_from: kennedy-1996
phase: peak
---

Al-Hakam II ruled for fifteen years from 961 and is remembered for what he
collected rather than what he took. He extended the Great Mosque of Cordoba into
the form most of its later fame rests on, and he ran an acquisition programme
for the library — agents buying and copying in Baghdad, Cairo and Kairouan —
that made Cordoba a place eastern scholars had reason to write to. The volume
counts the sources give are large and this site does not repeat them as figures,
because they are not the kind of number it is willing to print.

The more useful thing about his reign is structural, and it is the setup for
everything after it.

The state his father built was personal in a specific way. The administration
worked out of the palace, the army was salaried and professional, and both
answered to the caliph rather than to any constituency that could have replaced
him. That is an efficient arrangement and an unusually capturable one: a
machine with a single point of control is a machine that changes hands
completely when the point of control does.

In 976 al-Hakam died and left Hisham II, who was about eleven. Nothing had gone
wrong. The treasury was full, the frontier was quiet, the army was intact, and
al-Andalus was the strongest state in the western Mediterranean. Within a few
years the chamberlain was running all of it, and the caliph was a formality in a
palace. The next chapter dates that takeover and follows it to the end.

This chapter is tagged *peak*, and the tag is uncomfortable rather than wrong,
which is the argument. The vocabulary has *contraction* for a polity that is
losing things, and this one lost nothing for another thirty years: the treasury
stayed full, the frontier stayed quiet, the army stayed intact, and the raiding
went further into Christian territory than it ever had. What happened between
976 and 1009 was a state at full strength being operated by somebody other than
its owner, and the arc this site draws chapters along has no word for that. It
is filed at the peak because that is where the measurements put it, and the
next chapter is the reason a reader should not take the filing as reassurance.
