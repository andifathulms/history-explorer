---
title: What survived was the reputation
drafted_from: maqqari-gayangos
phase: afterlife
---

Three things did not survive the twenty years of civil war that followed the
coup of 1009, and one did.

Not a caliphate. In 1031 the office was simply abolished by the notables of a
city that had had enough of it, and al-Andalus broke into the *taifa*
kingdoms — Seville, Zaragoza, Toledo, Granada and a couple of dozen others,
each a fragment of the state ruling itself.

Not the palaces. Madinat al-Zahra had taken twelve years to build, and lasted
about seventy years from foundation to ruin.

What survived was the reputation. The Andalusi historians whose accounts of
this are preserved were writing under the taifa kings and after them, and what
comes through the quotations is a settled conviction that something enormous
had been lost, and a precise, almost forensic interest in how few people it had
taken to lose it.
