title: The Cocom bring in mercenaries

Mayapan's joint government of resident lords was not, in its earlier
years, dominated by any single family. That changed around 1380, when a
poorly documented revolt within the city appears to mark the point at
which one lineage, the Cocom, established itself as the leading house
among the noble families sharing the capital - a family who traced their
own descent, by their own later claim, to Quetzalcoatl himself, and who
would hold the office of halach uinic, supreme lord, for roughly the
next sixty years.

## Foreign soldiers to hold a Maya city

To secure a dominance the peninsula's other noble houses had not
consented to, the Cocom ruler brought outside muscle into the equation:
Mexican mercenary troops, drawn from garrisons the distant Mexica kept
at Tabasco and Xicalango on the Gulf coast, took up residence in
Mayapan itself. Maya tradition remembered this starkly - the Cocom
governor, one account states plainly, coveted riches and arranged with
the garrison troops to hand the city over to them, bringing Mexican
soldiers into a Maya capital and, with their backing, oppressing the
common people and making many of them slaves.

## A lesson the rest of Yucatán learned the hard way

The presence of Mexican garrison troops in the city did not go
unanswered. Yucatecans living under this new arrangement, the same
tradition records, learned the Mexican use of arms directly from the
soldiers stationed among them - the bow, the lance, the axe, and shields
reinforced with salt and cotton - until they had mastered these weapons
well enough that they no longer feared or deferred to the men who had
brought them. The Cocom family's own bid for security through foreign
arms taught the rest of the peninsula exactly the skills it would later
need to end that family's rule outright.
