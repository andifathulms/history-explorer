Alexander landed in Asia in 334 with something over thirty thousand men and no
fleet worth the name, against an empire with the resources of the Near East and
control of the sea.

He won at the Granicus in 334 against a satrapal army, at Issus in 333 against
Darius III in ground too narrow for Persian numbers to tell, and at Gaugamela in
331 in open country Darius had chosen and levelled. The third is the one that
mattered. The Persian army was larger, had chariots and elephants and the better
cavalry, and was outmanoeuvred and broken; Darius left the field, as he had at
Issus.

After Gaugamela there was no second army. Babylon surrendered and Alexander
entered it as Cyrus had, sacrificing to Marduk. Susa surrendered with the
treasury in it. Persepolis was taken, its treasury — the accumulated reserve of
two centuries — removed, and the palace burned, in an act the sources variously
present as a drunken accident, a calculated announcement that the Persian
wars were avenged, and a deliberate destruction of the dynasty's ceremonial
centre. It was probably the second.

Darius III fled east and was arrested, and then killed, by Bessus, his own
satrap of Bactria, in the summer of 330. Alexander found the body, sent it to
Persepolis for burial in the royal tombs, and hunted Bessus for two years.

This record's `ended` field types the year 330 and names the Macedonians, and the
last detail is the one worth keeping. The empire was not ended by a foreign
army defeating a king. It was ended when the king's own governor decided he was
finished and killed him — which is how a great many polities on this site end,
and is the reason Alexander's first act on taking the body was to bury it as a
king. He was not avenging Darius. He was establishing that the office continued
and that he now held it.
