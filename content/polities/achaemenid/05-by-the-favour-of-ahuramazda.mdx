---
title: By the favour of Ahuramazda
drafted_from: briant-2002
phase: peak
---

Every Achaemenid royal inscription says the same thing before it says anything
else. A great god is Ahuramazda, who created this earth, who created that sky,
who created man, who created happiness for man, who made Darius king. By the
favour of Ahuramazda I am king.

That formula is this record's `legitimation` field, and it is `divine-sanction`
in the rulebook's exact sense: the right comes from above rather than from a
predecessor, a lineage or an army. The second value, `descent`, is the other
half, and its prominence is a symptom. Darius spends the opening of the Behistun
inscription establishing that he is an Achaemenid, eight kings in his line before
him, precisely because his claim to it was the weakest thing about him: he came
to the throne in 522 by killing the man on it, and his account of why that was
lawful runs to several hundred lines on a cliff face in three languages.

Behistun is worth a moment as an object. It is cut sixty metres up a rock over
the road from Babylon to Ecbatana, in Old Persian, Elamite and Babylonian, with
a relief of the king facing nine bound rebels. Nobody could read it from the
road. It was made to be true rather than to be read, and copies were circulated
in Aramaic — one was found at Elephantine in Egypt, two thousand kilometres
away, which is how we know the text was distributed as policy.

Whether the Achaemenids were Zoroastrians is a long argument this site will not
settle. They worshipped Ahuramazda, used a calendar with Zoroastrian month
names, and had magi at court; they also did not mention Zoroaster, and the royal
inscriptions differ in important ways from the Avesta. The safe statement is that
the dynasty's religion is continuous with what later became Zoroastrianism and
is not identical to it.

What is not in doubt is the political use of the arrangement. A king who rules
by the favour of one god who created the earth is not the god of one people, and
the empire's practice of restoring local cults follows from the theology rather
than contradicting it. Marduk chose Cyrus; Ahuramazda made Darius king; the
Judaeans were sent home to rebuild their temple. Those are not three
inconsistent policies. They are one policy stated in three vocabularies.
