When Jumong's Buyeo-born son Yu-ri arrived at the Goguryeo court bearing
the broken half of a sword token and was proclaimed heir, two younger
sons of Jumong's more recent wife read the succession correctly and fled
south rather than wait to learn what an established heir does with rival
half-brothers. Pi-ryu and On-jo climbed Sam-gak San together, the
mountain behind what is now Seoul, and surveyed the country before
splitting up: Pi-ryu chose the coast, On-jo chose to press further
south.

## One settlement fails, one succeeds

Pi-ryu settled at Mi-ch'u-hol, near modern Incheon, and found the land
sterile and the climate unhealthy; his settlement never took hold, and
he is recorded to have died "of chagrin at his own failure" not long
after rejoining his brother. On-jo settled inland at Eui-rye-sŭng, in
what is now Ch'ung-ch'ŭng Province, where the king of Ma-han granted him
a generous tract of land as a guest rather than a rival. On this grant
On-jo raised a small kingdom he first named South Pu-yŭ, after the
homeland his own family had already left once.

## A name of uncertain origin

The source records three competing explanations for how the kingdom
came to be called Baekje rather than South Pu-yŭ: that a hundred
followers made up On-jo's entire original party; that it was first
called Sip-je and renamed once Pi-ryu's people swelled its numbers; or
that Baekje was already the name of a Ma-han district before On-jo ever
arrived, and simply stuck to the kingdom that grew there. Whichever is
true, the people who lived under the name seem not to have used it
themselves.

## An early kingdom, not yet a threat

On-jo's small state spent its first years defending itself rather than
expanding: in 10 BC the Mal-gal tribes besieged his capital and were
driven back only after heavy fighting, after which On-jo built the
fortresses of Ma-su-sŭng and Ch'il-chung-sŭng against further raids.
Nothing in these early years marked the kingdom as anything but one more
small state living at the sufferance of its neighbors, least of all the
Ma-han that had granted it land in the first place.
