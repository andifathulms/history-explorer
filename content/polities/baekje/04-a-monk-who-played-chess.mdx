A century after Baekje moved its own capital in triumph onto ground it
had taken from Goguryeo, the same rivalry reversed itself completely -
and it began with a chess game rather than an army.

## A councillor sent to empty a treasury

Goguryeo sent a monk named To-rim into Baekje's capital pretending to
seek asylum. The Baekje king, finding him an excellent chess player,
took him into his own confidence as a trusted advisor - a position
To-rim used to convince the king that his palaces, walls, tombs and
public buildings all badly needed expensive repair, and to press him
into hauling an enormous stone monolith to the capital at ruinous
public expense. With the treasury drained exactly as he had planned,
To-rim slipped back across the border and reported that Baekje was now
too poor to defend itself.

## A capital surrounded before it knew there was a war

Goguryeo struck immediately, guided to the weakest approaches by a
Baekje fugitive from justice named Kŭl-lu. The capital was surrounded
almost before its own court understood a war had begun. Goguryeo's
army burned the suburbs first, then forced its way in and set the
palace itself alight. The king fled out the west gate with only ten
attendants, but Kŭl-lu caught up with him on the road. According to
the account, the king begged for his life on his knees; Kŭl-lu spat in
his face three times, bound him, and sent him to a fortress where he
was killed. Goguryeo's army withdrew north with eight thousand
captives.

## A prince who rebuilt a kingdom from its ashes

Crown Prince Mun-ju had been in Silla seeking help when the disaster
struck, and returned with ten thousand borrowed troops to find his
capital in ashes, his father dead, and the survivors mourning their own
families dragged off into captivity. He took control immediately,
moved Baekje's capital south to Ung-jin - modern Kongju - and
withdrew what remained of Baekje's population from the Han River
territory the kingdom had held in triumph for exactly a century. The
frontier Baekje's king had once crossed to build a new capital in 371
was the same frontier that erased the old one in 475: the kingdom that
had humiliated Goguryeo at the walls of P'yongyang had, within four
generations, lost everything that victory had won.
