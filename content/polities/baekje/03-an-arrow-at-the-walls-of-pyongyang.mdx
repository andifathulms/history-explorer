title: An arrow at the walls of P'yongyang

Goguryeo, checked in the north by a more dangerous neighbor, turned
south against Baekje around 360 and threw up a fort at Ch'i-yang, near
Baekje's own capital, garrisoned with twenty thousand men who set about
systematically plundering the countryside. Baekje's Crown Prince led a
sudden counterattack that broke the garrison outright, leaving five
thousand Goguryeo dead on the field when the survivors withdrew.

## A defensive victory becomes an invasion

Baekje followed up by building breastworks along the Han River against
further raids, but the victory had also exposed something to Baekje's
own leadership: Goguryeo was weaker than its reputation. An army of
thirty thousand crossed into Goguryeo territory and met no serious
resistance the entire way north, arriving beneath the walls of
P'yongyang itself. During the assault on the city an arrow mortally
wounded Goguryeo's own king. The attempt to storm the walls failed, and
Baekje's army withdrew in good order rather than press a siege it could
not sustain — but it withdrew having done something no Baekje army had
done before or would do again.

## A capital moved in triumph

"Elated over so many evidences of his growing power," Baekje's king
moved his own capital north across the Han River into what had been
Goguryeo's own territory, settling in the area of present-day Seoul. The
source records disagreement among traditions over the precise site —
Puk-han, Nam P'yŭng-yang, or a point just outside Seoul's east gate —
but agrees on the direction: Baekje's government had shifted its center
of gravity to the very frontier it had just fought across.

## A victory the people did not share

Success at the top did not translate into contentment below it. Whether
from the weight of military exactions or from fear of the reprisal
Goguryeo's wounded pride would eventually bring, over a thousand people
fled Baekje for asylum in Silla during this same period, settling in six
villages Silla's own king set aside for them and refusing Baekje's
demand for their return. The kingdom that had just humiliated Goguryeo
at its own capital could not fully hold its own people at home.
