Washukanni has never been found.

The capital of one of the great powers of the Late Bronze Age is named dozens
of times in Hittite, Assyrian and Egyptian documents, and nobody knows where it
was. Tell Fekheriye on the Khabur headwaters has been the leading candidate for
most of a century and remains unconfirmed. With no capital there is no palace,
and with no palace there is no archive.

## Everything known, known from elsewhere

The political narrative comes from Hittite treaties and Assyrian royal
inscriptions — from the two states that destroyed Mitanni, written to explain
why they were right to. The diplomacy comes from a rubbish deposit in Egypt.
The social and legal material comes from Nuzi and Alalakh, provincial towns
that happen to have been excavated, and describes how a Mitanni province worked
rather than how Mitanni did. The horse-training manual survives at Hattusa, in
Hittite.

The shape of what can be said follows exactly from that.

How the army was raised is answerable, because provincial land documents record
chariot warriors holding estates. What the state lived on is not, because no
Mitanni tax record exists. How the succession was supposed to work is not,
because the only succession described in any detail is the one the Hittites
broke. On what public ground the kings claimed to rule is not, because no
Mitanni royal inscription survives to make the claim.

## The temptation

It would be easy to fill those in. A Late Bronze Age kingdom of this size
probably taxed its provinces, probably passed the throne from father to son,
and its kings probably claimed a god's favour, because that is what its
neighbours did and what the period looks like.

Every one of those guesses might be right. None of them is attested for Mitanni
by anything anyone has read, and a plausible answer that nobody wrote down is
worth less than an admitted blank — because the blank is honest about the state
of the evidence, and the guess quietly upgrades a modern inference into an
ancient fact. Mitanni is the clearest case for that discipline in the whole
period: an empire that corresponded with pharaohs as an equal, and whose own
voice survives in almost nothing.
