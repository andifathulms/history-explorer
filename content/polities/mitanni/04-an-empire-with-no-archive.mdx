title: An empire with no archive
drafted_from: van-de-mieroop-2015

This page has one of its four institutions coded and three null. Plenty of pages
in this corpus have none coded at all, and on most of those the gaps are a
backlog — nobody has yet opened a source that addresses the question. Here they
are not.

Washukanni has never been found. The capital of one of the great powers of the
Late Bronze Age is named dozens of times in Hittite, Assyrian and Egyptian
documents and nobody knows where it was; Tell Fekheriye on the Khabur headwaters
has been the leading candidate for most of a century and remains unconfirmed.
With no capital there is no palace, and with no palace there is no archive.

Everything on this page therefore comes from somewhere else. The political
narrative comes from Hittite treaties and Assyrian royal inscriptions — from the
two states that destroyed it, written to explain why they were right to. The
diplomacy comes from a rubbish deposit in Egypt. The social and legal material
comes from Nuzi and Alalakh, provincial towns that happen to have been excavated,
and describes how a Mitanni province worked rather than how Mitanni did. The
horse manual survives in Hattusa.

So this site can say how the army was raised, because provincial land documents
record chariot warriors holding estates. It cannot say what the state lived on,
because no Mitanni tax record exists. It cannot say how the succession was
supposed to work, because the only succession described in detail is the one the
Hittites broke. It cannot say on what public ground the kings claimed to rule,
because no Mitanni royal inscription survives to make the claim.

Coding rule 9 says gaps on a page make an honest page, and that the failure to
watch for is the opposite one — that these vocabularies are small enough that
any polity can be given four plausible values from general knowledge in about a
minute, producing a corpus that is uniform, complete and entirely unsourced.
Mitanni is the case that makes the rule concrete. It would be very easy to code
this page `tribute`, `primogeniture` and `divine-sanction`, because those are
what a Late Bronze Age kingdom probably had. Nothing consulted says so, and the
page says nothing instead.
