title: Taken apart twice
drafted_from: van-de-mieroop-2015

Suppiluliuma I of Hatti spent his reign dismantling Mitanni, and he did it the
efficient way: he took the Syrian vassals first, one at a time, until the
kingdom's western half had changed sides, and only then went for the centre. The
Egyptian alliance did not help, because Egypt was in the middle of the Amarna
period and was not answering.

Tushratta was murdered by one of his own sons. A rival claimant, Artatama II,
was backed by Assyria; Suppiluliuma backed another son, Shattiwaza, married him
to a Hittite princess, and installed him over a rump kingdom west of the
Euphrates as a vassal. The treaty recording the arrangement is the fullest
surviving document the kingdom produced, and it was written by the people who
had just finished it.

The second ending took another eighty years. Assyria, which Mitanni had ruled
for a century, took the eastern territories under Adad-nirari I and completed the
work under Shalmaneser I around 1260, deporting populations and administering
the country as a province called Hanigalbat. Whatever was left of the Mitanni
state ceased to be one.

This site records the ending as `conquest` with an empty `by`, and the empty
field is deliberate. The conquerors were the Middle Assyrian kings; the Assyrian
entry in this corpus is the Neo-Assyrian Empire, which begins in 911 BC, three
and a half centuries later. They are continuous as a place and a tradition and
they are not the same polity, and putting `neo-assyrian` in that field to avoid
an empty one would assert a succession across three hundred and fifty years that
nobody claimed.

What Mitanni left is the country it had held, which stayed Hurrian in population
long after it stopped being Hurrian in government, and a hole in the record where
its own account of itself should be.
