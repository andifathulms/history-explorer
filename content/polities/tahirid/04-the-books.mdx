title: The books Abdallah is said to have burned
drafted_from: iranica-taherids

Abdallah ibn Tahir is reported to have ordered Persian books destroyed. The
story attaches particularly to a romance called *Vamiq u Adhra* and to
Zoroastrian material, and the reasoning attributed to him is that the Qur'an
and the traditions of the Prophet are in Arabic and nothing else was needed.

The anecdote is late and this site does not vouch for the incident. What it
usefully marks is a real difference, and the difference is the reason this
chapter exists.

The Tahirids were a Persian family from Khurasan who were culturally as Arab as
they could make themselves. They claimed connections to the old Arab nobility,
patronised Arabic poetry, corresponded and administered in Arabic, and — on the
evidence of what they paid for — regarded the Persian past as something to have
emerged from rather than to cultivate. That was not eccentricity. It was the
correct posture for a family whose entire position depended on being the
caliph's most reliable servants, in a province the caliphate was nervous about,
during a century when Iranian revolt was a live category and Babak was holding
out in Azerbaijan.

Set that against what happens sixty miles of history later. This site's Samanid
page has a court at Bukhara commissioning Rudaki, paying for a Persian version
of Tabari, and funding the beginning of the *Shahnama* — the Iranian national
epic, in Persian, in verse. The Saffarids in between are credited with the first
Persian court poetry, on the argument, reported of Ya'qub, that he could not
understand what was being recited at him in Arabic.

Three successive holders of the same province, then: one that suppressed
Persian letters or is said to have, one that started them because its founder
was not educated in Arabic, and one that turned them into a literature. The
thread this region draws is usually about how power passed. This is the same
sequence read for what each holder thought it was, and the answers are not
continuous at all.
