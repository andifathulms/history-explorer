---
title: A reward for a civil war
drafted_from: iranica-taherids
phase: formation
---

Tahir ibn al-Husayn won al-Ma'mun's civil war. He commanded the Khurasani army
that took Baghdad in 813 and killed the caliph al-Amin, and the man he had
fought for became caliph on the strength of it.

The reward, eventually, was Khurasan: in 821 Tahir was made its governor. He
held it for about a year before dying, and the manner of the death is the part
worth keeping. In 822 he omitted the caliph's name from the Friday sermon at
Merv. That is not a gesture. The khutba is where a polity states publicly whose
authority it holds under, and dropping a name from it is a formal act with a
formal meaning. He was dead within days, and no source can tell us whether the
two facts are connected.

What happened next is the thing that makes the Tahirids the right place to
start this thread. Al-Ma'mun did not reclaim the province. He appointed Tahir's
son. Then that son's brother. Then that brother's son. The governorship stayed
in the family for fifty years while remaining, formally and in every document,
a governorship.

The Tahirids are the demonstration that a title and a power can come apart
without either of them changing.
