---
title: The family that also policed Baghdad
drafted_from: chi-4
phase: peak
---

The previous chapters describe a family governing Khurasan, two thousand
kilometres from the capital, with increasing independence and unfailing
punctuality about the tax. That is half of what the Tahirids were doing.

The other half was in Baghdad. From 820 a branch of the same family held the
*shurta* — the security command of the capital: the policing, the prisons, the
garrison, the maintenance of order in the largest city west of China. Ishaq ibn
Ibrahim held it for over twenty-five years. His successors held it, with the
Khurasan governorship running in parallel in the hands of cousins, until 891.

Set the two together and the arrangement is remarkable. One family
simultaneously governed the empire's richest eastern province hereditarily and
commanded the police of the caliph's own city, and did both for seventy years,
and was never accused of disloyalty by anyone whose opinion mattered. This is
not the usual shape of provincial autonomy in this corpus. The Tulunids stopped
remitting; the Saffarids took a province and asked afterwards; the Buyids
occupied the capital and kept the caliph as an asset. The Tahirids held both
ends and behaved.

Then the caliphate broke in the middle. When the guard at Samarra started
raising and destroying caliphs — the anarchy this site's Abbasid page dates to
861 — the man holding Baghdad was Muhammad ibn Abdallah ibn Tahir, and he
turned out to be the only figure in Iraq with a city, a garrison and no stake
in the Turkish factions. In 865 he sheltered the caliph al-Musta'in in Baghdad
and defended it against the Samarra army through a siege lasting most of a
year. Then he negotiated: al-Musta'in abdicated, al-Mu'tazz was recognised, and
Muhammad kept his position.

That is a governor arbitrating a caliphal succession, and it is the high-water
mark of what a Tahirid could do. It is also the last of it. The Khurasan branch
lost Nishapur eight years later to a man with no appointment at all, and the
Baghdad branch was replaced in 891 by a commander of the caliph's own.
