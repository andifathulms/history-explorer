Abdallah ibn Tahir governed from 828 to 845 and is the reason the dynasty is
remembered as more than a caretaker.

He moved the seat from Merv to Nishapur, which shifted the centre of Khurasan
westward and made Nishapur the city it would be for the next three centuries —
the place the Saffarids would have to take, and then the Samanids, and then the
Seljuks. He is associated with a body of work on the regulation of irrigation
rights, the *Kitab al-quni*, compiled by jurists at his direction: qanat water
in Khurasan is a scarce resource whose division is the difference between a
functioning province and a set of feuds, and codifying it is the least
glamorous and most durable thing a government there can do.

<Figure id="qanat" />

He also remitted taxes to Baghdad reliably, which is why Baghdad kept
reappointing his family.

That is the Tahirid settlement in one sentence: they were extremely good
governors, and they were paid in the right to go on being governors
hereditarily. It was a stable arrangement and it did not survive contact with
someone who did not want a governorship.
