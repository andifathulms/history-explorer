Andrea Doria was an admiral with a fleet under contract, first to the pope, then
to France. In 1528, unpaid and unhappy, he took the fleet and the city out of
French service, put both under the protection of Charles V, and rewrote the
constitution.

The new arrangement had a doge elected for two years and ineligible immediately
afterwards, a Great and a Lesser Council, and — the decisive part — the old
clans dissolved and their members redistributed into twenty-eight artificial
*alberghi*, each an association of families required to take a common surname.
The factions were abolished by administrative fiat and reassembled in shapes that
cut across the old alignments.

It held for two hundred and sixty-nine years, which is longer than anything else
Genoa tried.

## What it cost

Independence, more or less, and the record says so plainly rather than treating
1528 as a restoration.

Genoa after Doria was in the Spanish system: the fleet served the Habsburgs, the
republic followed Spanish foreign policy, and Genoese banking houses became the
principal financiers of the Spanish crown. The silver from Potosí and Zacatecas
was landed at Seville and disbursed across Europe through Genoese bills of
exchange, and Spanish armies in Flanders were paid with money Genoese houses
advanced against fleets that had not yet arrived.

It made a period historians call the Genoese century, from roughly 1557 to 1627,
in which the city was probably the richest place in Europe per head and had
almost no political weight of its own. Then Spain defaulted, repeatedly, and the
houses that had lent to it did not recover.

## `founder` is null and `peak` held no office

This record's `rulers` block is the strangest on the site and the data comment
says why.

There is no founder because the commune was a sworn association with elected
consuls. And the man entered as `peak` never held the dogeship, refused it when
offered, and directed the republic for thirty years as a private citizen with a
fleet under contract to a foreign king. The site's schema asks for three rulers
because nearly every polity in this corpus had them, and this one is a
demonstration that the assumption is a convenience rather than a fact about
states.

## 1797

The same year as Venice and the same cause. French troops entered in June 1797, a
Democratic Ligurian Republic was proclaimed, and the constitution of 1528 ended.
Genoa was given to the Kingdom of Sardinia at the Congress of Vienna and has been
Italian since.

`ended.type` is `conquest` with `by` empty, as on the Venetian record, for the
same reason: Revolutionary France is not in this corpus.

Two republics eleven hundred and seven hundred years old, on the same coast,
doing the same thing by opposite methods, ended a month apart by the same
twenty-seven-year-old general. Neither of them appears in the
other's `ended` field, because the relation between them was five hundred years
of competition and one near-fatal war, and none of that is succession.
