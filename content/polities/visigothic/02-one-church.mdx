The Goths were Arians. The population of Spain was Catholic. That is the
structural problem of every one of the post-Roman kingdoms in this corpus's
Latin West — the Vandal and Ostrogothic records have the same one — and this is
the only kingdom that solved it.

In 589, at the Third Council of Toledo, King Reccared renounced Arianism and
brought the Gothic ruling class into the church of the majority. It is this
record's second turning point.

## What it removed and what it created

It removed the line between rulers and ruled at a stroke, and everything that
had followed from it: separate hierarchies, separate churches, a nobility that
could not intermarry with the provincial aristocracy without one side
converting.

Compare the two records beside it. The Vandal kingdom in Africa enforced Arianism
against the Catholic hierarchy with exiles and confiscations, and when Belisarius
landed in 534 the African provinces did not fight for it. Ostrogothic Italy did
not persecute and did not convert either, and Theodoric's execution of Boethius
in 524 is the moment its Roman aristocracy discovered it had somewhere else to
look. This kingdom converted, and survived another hundred and twenty-two years.

What it created was a state run through the church. The councils of Toledo — there
were eighteen of them — legislated, judged, and from 633 claimed the right to
choose the king. The bishops were the kingdom's civil service, its intellectual
class and its constitutional check, and this record's `legitimation` field reads
`election, divine-sanction` because of what happened at Toledo rather than
because of anything a king asserted on his own.

## And the cost

It also produced, within a generation, the most sustained legal campaign against
Jews anywhere in early medieval Europe: forced baptism decreed and then partly
retracted, children removed from parents, property confiscated, and a body of
conciliar and royal legislation on the subject that runs through the seventh
century and gets worse.

The connection is not incidental and the page should not put it in a footnote. A
kingdom that has just abolished the confessional distinction between Goth and
Roman, and defined itself as one Catholic people under one law, has produced a
category of people who do not fit and has given itself an instrument — the
council — for legislating about them. The Liber Iudiciorum on the next chapter's
subject applies to everybody in the kingdom, and the anti-Jewish laws are part
of the same programme of making everybody the same.

## Isidore

The other product of the arrangement is the reason this kingdom appears in
histories of everything else.

Isidore of Seville, archbishop and the presiding figure at the Fourth Council of
Toledo in 633, wrote the *Etymologiae* — twenty books attempting to organise all
secular and religious knowledge by explaining the origins of the words for it.
It is unreliable, enormously influential, and one of the most copied books in
medieval Europe; something like a thousand manuscripts survive, and this site's
Carolingian record's scriptoria produced a good many of them.

He is not an influence count on this record. The three axes measure successor
claims, religions carried and descendant scripts, and a compendium is none of
those things — the same observation this corpus's Carolingian, Nabataean and
Venetian records all end on, from four different directions.
