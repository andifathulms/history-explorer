In 418 the Roman government settled a Gothic army in Aquitaine. The Goths had
been inside the empire since crossing the Danube in 376, had destroyed an
imperial army and killed an emperor at Adrianople in 378 — which is the last
turning point on this site's Roman record — and had sacked Rome in 410. The
settlement gave them land and a treaty, and made them federates: an army in
imperial service, under its own king, holding territory in a province.

This record starts there because that is the earliest point at which there is a
polity rather than an army. What it is not is a migration of a people out of
Scandinavia into Gaul, which is the account the Gothic historian Jordanes gives
and which Heather's book — this record's other source — argues against at length.
Gothic identity on that reading was assembled inside the empire in the fourth
and fifth centuries out of several groups, under pressure from the Huns, and the
Visigoths of 418 are a coalition that had been in existence for about a
generation.

## The edge from Rome

This site records `roman -> visigothic`, type `absorbed remnants of`, dated 418,
and the type is doing careful work.

`conquered by` would be false: the Goths did not conquer Rome and the settlement
was a Roman decision. `seceded from` would be false: they were never part of the
Roman state in the way a province was. What happened is that a group inside the
empire took over the administration of territory as the central government's
ability to pay and to direct it disappeared, and then kept it. `absorbed remnants
of` is the nearest, and this corpus now records the same edge type for the Vandal
and Ostrogothic records on the same grounds.

All three cross out of the Latin West into the region this site calls Rome and
Byzantium, so none of them is drawn by either thread. That is the regional split
working as designed: these are states assembled from a dead empire's provinces,
which is not the same relation as a state succeeding another one.

## Losing the half it started with

Everything the kingdom is remembered for happened after it lost Gaul.

In 507 Clovis destroyed the Visigothic army near Poitiers and killed King Alaric
II, and Aquitaine went to the Franks. The kingdom's centre moved across the
Pyrenees, and Toledo became the capital under Leovigild in the 570s. The
territory this record's `core_region` field describes for its last two centuries
is a peninsula it had held only partially before 507 and did not fully control
until Leovigild finished the Suevic kingdom in the north-west in 585 and the
Byzantines were pushed out of the south-east in 624.

So a record spanning two hundred and ninety-three years spends its first
eighty-nine in a country it lost, and the state the last four chapters are about
is the second one.
