In 418 the Roman government settled a Gothic army in Aquitaine. The Goths had
been inside the empire since crossing the Danube in 376, had destroyed an
imperial army and killed an emperor at Adrianople in 378, and had sacked Rome in
410. The
settlement gave them land and a treaty, and made them federates: an army in
imperial service, under its own king, holding territory in a province.

418 is the earliest point at which there is a polity here rather than an army.
What it is not is a migration of a people out of Scandinavia into Gaul, which is
the account the Gothic historian Jordanes gives and which Heather argues against
at length.
Gothic identity on that reading was assembled inside the empire in the fourth
and fifth centuries out of several groups, under pressure from the Huns, and the
Visigoths of 418 are a coalition that had been in existence for about a
generation.

## What kind of succession this was

It is worth being careful about what kind of succession this is, because none of
the ordinary words fits.

Conquest would be false: the Goths did not conquer Rome and the settlement was a
Roman decision. Secession would be false: they were never part of the Roman
state in the way a province was. What happened is that a group inside the
empire took over the administration of territory as the central government's
ability to pay and to direct it disappeared, and then kept it. The Vandals and
the Ostrogoths acquired their kingdoms the same way.

That is a different relation from one state succeeding another. These are
polities assembled out of a dead empire's provinces by people who had been
inside the empire, in its pay, for a generation before they were on their own.

## Losing the half it started with

Everything the kingdom is remembered for happened after it lost Gaul.

In 507 Clovis destroyed the Visigothic army near Poitiers and killed King Alaric
II, and Aquitaine went to the Franks. The kingdom's centre moved across the
Pyrenees, and Toledo became the capital under Leovigild in the 570s. The country
the kingdom is remembered for holding is a peninsula it had held only partially
before 507 and did not fully control until Leovigild finished the Suevic kingdom in the north-west in 585 and the
Byzantines were pushed out of the south-east in 624.

So a kingdom that lasted two hundred and ninety-three years spent its first
eighty-nine in a country it lost, and the Spain it is remembered for is the
second half of its life.
