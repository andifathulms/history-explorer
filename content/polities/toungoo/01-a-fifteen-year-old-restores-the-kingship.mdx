Two centuries of Shan migration into the Irrawaddy plains had left
Burma without any single overlord by the time Tabinshwehti took the
throne of Toungoo in 1531, at fifteen years old. Harvey reads his
own advantages as largely inherited rather than earned at first: the
same Shan incursions that had displaced so much of the country had
also driven Burmese refugees south into Toungoo, swelling his own
manpower, and his opponents were themselves Shans - a hill people
Harvey judges to have had, in this period, "a congenital incapacity
for combined action." What Tabinshwehti brought personally was
nerve. At his own accession he insisted on having his ears bored at
Pegu's own Shwemawdaw pagoda rather than anywhere else, and did so
surrounded by armed retainers under the nose of the reigning Talaing
king, whose men did not dare object.

## A letter meant to be intercepted

Tabinshwehti's own campaign against Lower Burma, begun in 1535,
combined force with genuine cunning. Unable to take Pegu city by
direct assault, he sent its two ablest commanders home with a letter
- deliberately written so it would be intercepted - promising each of
them a conquered city to rule once "the matter you arranged with me"
was finished. Pegu's own king read the letter as proof of treason and
had both men executed; deprived of their best leaders, the Talaing
defense collapsed, and Tabinshwehti entered Pegu without a fight in
1539. He completed the conquest of Lower Burma two years later at
Martaban, a wealthier prize defended by Portuguese mercenary ships
under a rival adventurer, Paulo Seixas - a siege that held out seven
months before hunger forced its own surrender.

## The comrade who would inherit everything

Harvey singles out one figure from these campaigns as a genuine
turning point in the quality of Burmese leadership: Bayinnaung, son
of Tabinshwehti's own wet nurse and husband to his sister, whose
willingness to destroy his own army's rafts after crossing a river
against a vastly larger enemy force - reasoning that retreat was no
longer an option worth keeping open - Harvey calls "a breath of new
life after three centuries of mannikins." That same nerve would carry
Bayinnaung to the Toungoo throne himself within a decade, and to a
reach across mainland Southeast Asia his own brother-in-law and
patron never approached.
