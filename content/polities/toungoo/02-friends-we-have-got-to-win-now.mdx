Tabinshwehti's own reign did not survive its own success. A
Portuguese favourite at court taught the king's bride foreign cooking
and introduced him to wine and spirits; Harvey records the king's own
decline into drunkenness in blunt terms, quoting his reply to
Bayinnaung's own remonstrance - "I have made friends with drink,
Brother, do thou manage the affairs of state" - as he surrendered
real government to his brother-in-law rather than reform. Bayinnaung
kept his own loyalty intact even as the king's misrule worsened,
refusing repeated appeals from Burmese, Shan and Talaing ministers
alike to simply take the throne himself, and quietly blocking
executions rather than open rebellion. It could not last. Talaing
chamberlains lured Tabinshwehti into a jungle on a false pretext -
a supposed white elephant - and beheaded him there in 1550, at
thirty-five; he was afterward worshipped as a nat, a guardian spirit,
at Rangoon's own Shwedagon.

## An empire built on knowing numbers do not matter

Bayinnaung's own reign, from 1551 to 1581, carried the kingdom he
inherited to a reach no earlier or later Toungoo ruler matched -
suzerainty extending into Siam, Chiang Mai, Manipur and the Shan
states, built on the same conviction that had already defined his
career under Tabinshwehti: that spirit and resolve mattered more than
raw numbers. Facing an enemy force many times the size of his own
early in his career, he had destroyed his own men's escape rafts
rather than leave open the option of retreat, telling his own
alarmed officers simply, "Friends, we have got to win now." That
same temperament, applied now to an entire kingdom rather than a
single river crossing, gave the Toungoo dynasty its widest extent -
a peak his own successors, ruling from a much-reduced Ava after his
death, would spend the next century and a half failing to hold onto.
