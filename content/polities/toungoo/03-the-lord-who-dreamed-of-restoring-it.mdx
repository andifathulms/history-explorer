Bayinnaung's empire did not survive him by more than a generation.
His son Nanda lost Pegu itself to a Talaing rebellion around 1599, and
the kingdom he had spent his own reign assembling broke apart into
sixteen years of petty, mutually hostile states - Ava, Prome, Toungoo,
Syriam, Chiang Mai and others smaller still, several of them held by
Bayinnaung's own sons and grandsons with no loyalty left between them.

## Patient reconquest, one Shan state at a time

One of those grandsons, known to history as the lord of Nyaungyan,
held only Ava and the country immediately around and above it, and
spent the whole of his own working life reducing the Shan states one
by one - Mogaung, Mohnyin, Bhamo, Mone, Yawnghwe - rebuilding upward
from the smallest possible base rather than trying to reclaim
Bayinnaung's old reach at a stroke. He even persuaded China to
extradite a fugitive Shan chief who had taken refuge across the
border, the last real assertion of the old Chinese claim to
overlordship in Upper Burma; after 1628, Harvey notes, Burma sent no
further tribute missions north at all. Nyaungyan died in 1605 on the
march home from a campaign, his embalmed body carried back to Ava
propped upright on its own elephant in full jeweled robes; before the
funeral fire had finished consuming it, his son Anaukpetlun was
already administering the oath of allegiance to everyone gathered
around the pyre.

## A conquest completed within Anaukpetlun's own decade

Anaukpetlun inherited Upper Burma and the northern Shan states
already reunified, and used that base to overrun a Lower Burma his
father had left depopulated and undefended. He defeated his own
brothers and cousins who had carved up the old Toungoo territories
between them, and when Prome's defenders laid down their arms in
1607, one small page boy refused to abandon his own lord even in
defeat - standing over him with a sword in each hand, daring anyone
to approach. Anaukpetlun, rather than have the boy killed, took him
into royal service instead; he went on to a long career and died a
minister of the Hluttaw Council. In 1610 Anaukpetlun took Toungoo
itself, carrying home to Ava the sacred tooth relic, two-thirds of
its cattle, and the deported populations earlier wars had scattered
across three different cities - the last piece of Bayinnaung's old
kingdom still standing apart, except for one Portuguese-held port
that would take him three more years, and a full siege, to bring
under the crown at all.
