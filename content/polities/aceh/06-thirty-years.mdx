The Netherlands declared war on Aceh in March 1873.

The first expedition landed, was fought to a standstill, lost its commander
killed, and re-embarked. That had not happened to a Dutch force in the
archipelago in living memory. The second expedition, later the same year, took
Banda Aceh and the palace, and discovered that it had taken a town.

What followed was the longest and most expensive war the Dutch fought in
Indonesia. The sultanate's field capacity had been the *uleëbalang* levies
since 1641, which meant there was no single army to defeat and no single
authority whose surrender ended anything; when the Dutch bought or broke one
chief, the war continued under the next, and increasingly under the *ulama*
rather than the chiefs at all. Sultan Muhammad Daud Syah surrendered in January
1903, thirty years and some tens of thousands of deaths after the palace fell.

## The last one still fighting

Sultan Muhammad Daud Syah's surrender in 1903 is the latest ending of any
sultanate in this part of the world by well over a century. Patani, the next
most recent, went in 1786.

Only one other polity in the archipelago outlasted it, and the contrast is
worth holding onto: Sulu, which signed its sovereignty away by document in
1915. Two endings in the twentieth century, in the same seas, a dozen years
apart. One is a signature on a contract. The other is thirty years of war, some
tens of thousands of dead, and a sultan who did not stop until there was nobody
left to fight alongside him.
