The Netherlands declared war on Aceh in March 1873.

The first expedition landed, was fought to a standstill, lost its commander
killed, and re-embarked. That had not happened to a Dutch force in the
archipelago in living memory. The second expedition, later the same year, took
Banda Aceh and the palace, and discovered that it had taken a town.

What followed was the longest and most expensive war the Dutch fought in
Indonesia. The sultanate's field capacity had been the *uleëbalang* levies since
1641, which meant there was no single army to defeat and no single authority
whose surrender ended anything; when the Dutch bought or broke one chief, the war
continued under the next, and increasingly under the *ulama* rather than the
chiefs at all. Sultan Muhammad Daud Syah surrendered in January 1903, thirty
years and some tens of thousands of deaths after the palace fell.

## The end fields

`ended.type` is `conquest` and `ended.by` is empty, because the Netherlands is
not in this corpus. Fourteen records across the archipelago have an empty `by`,
and the European-shaped ones are the largest group: Melaka against Portugal in
1511, Ternate against the Dutch company in 1683, Banten against it in 1684,
Brunei piecemeal by 1888, this record against the Dutch state in 1903, and Sulu
against the United States in 1915.

The Kilwa record's last chapter named this pattern and called it a
Portuguese-shaped absence. On this page the shape is Dutch, and the observation
is the same one: a corpus organised around polities cannot express an ending
delivered by a chartered company or a colonial state until somebody writes those
pages, and until then the field says nothing rather than something wrong.

The date matters for one more reason. 1903 is the latest ending in this region by
a hundred and seventeen years — Patani, in 1786, is the next — and one of only
two endings anywhere in the archipelago in the twentieth century. The other is
this corpus's Sulu record, which signed away its sovereignty by document in 1915.
Aceh is the one that was still fighting.
