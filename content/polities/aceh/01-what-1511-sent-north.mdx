title: What 1511 sent north
drafted_from: reid-1988
phase: formation

This corpus's Melaka record ends by saying that Albuquerque took a customs house
rather than a trade, and that the commerce rerouted to Aceh, to Johor, to
Banten, to Brunei and to Patani. Aceh is the only one of the five with a page
here, and it is the one that took most.

The northern tip of Sumatra was a cluster of small ports before 1511 — Pasai was
the important one, and it was Pasai that had converted Melaka's ruler a century
earlier. It has a page here now, and the edge `pasai -> aceh` is the second one
running into this record: Melaka's trade arrived in 1511, and Pasai's coast was
taken from the Portuguese in 1524. Ali Mughayat Syah consolidated them into a single sultanate in the
years around 1496, and then the Portuguese handed him a windfall he had done
nothing to earn: every Muslim merchant who would not trade under a Portuguese
flag needed somewhere else to unload, and Aceh was on the right side of the
strait, upwind of Melaka, and Muslim.

The edge on this site reads `melaka → aceh`, `absorbed remnants of`, 1511. That
type was chosen against `claimed legitimacy of`, which would have been the
easier and wronger call. What moved north was Melaka's business — its merchant
communities, its Malay court culture, its shipping — and not its authority.
Aceh's sultans traced their line to their own coast. Taking a city's trade is
not asserting its inheritance, and coding rule 1 asks for the assertion.

It is the second time on this site's archipelago pages that an edge has turned on
that distinction, and the second time the looser reading would have drawn a
cleaner picture of something that did not happen.
