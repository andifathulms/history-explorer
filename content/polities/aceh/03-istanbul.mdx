Some time around 1562 Aceh sent an embassy to the Ottoman court asking for help
against the Portuguese. It is nine thousand kilometres from Banda Aceh to
Istanbul, the two polities never shared a frontier, and this corpus's Ottoman
record and this one otherwise touch at no point at all.

Something came back. The scale of it is disputed — the Acehnese and the Ottoman
records describe different things, and the fleet that was reportedly ordered was
diverted before it sailed — but cannon arrived, and with them founders who could
cast more, and gunners who could work them. Acehnese artillery is attested in
Portuguese accounts for the rest of the century.

## The value that was not added

This site codes `legitimation` on the Melaka record as `descent` and
`investiture`, the second of those for the Ming reception of Parameswara at
Nanjing in 1411. The value exists in this vocabulary because a set of records
had rulers holding authority granted by an external sovereign and publicly
saying so, and because the older `caliphal-investiture` named a grantor that was
wrong for most of them.

Aceh asked the Ottoman sultan — a caliph, by the claim his own record carries —
for a relationship of exactly that kind. It is the single best-fitting request in
the corpus, and the value is not on this page.

What Aceh received was ordnance. There is no grant of authority in the
transaction that this site can point at, no seal, no investiture, no title
conferred; the sultan of Aceh went on styling himself by his own descent. Coding
rule 8's test is a public assertion of authority derived from someone else, and
guns are not that, however much the polity that supplied them would have liked
them to be.

The turning point is marked contested, which is the honest state of a story
where the Acehnese tradition, the Ottoman archive and the Portuguese reports
describe three different embassies.
