Some time around 1562 Aceh sent an embassy to the Ottoman court, nine thousand
kilometres away, asking for help against the Portuguese.

The two states had never shared a frontier and had no dealings before or after.
The request went the length of the Indian Ocean, across a subcontinent's worth
of other people's politics, to a sultan who had never heard of the petitioner's
enemies in that quarter of the world — and it worked, partially.

## What came back

The scale is disputed. The Acehnese tradition, the Ottoman archive and the
Portuguese reports describe three different embassies and three different
outcomes, and a fleet that was reportedly ordered was diverted before it
sailed.

What is not disputed is the ordnance. Cannon arrived, and with them founders
who could cast more and gunners who could work them. Acehnese artillery is
attested in Portuguese accounts for the rest of the century — which is the
practical test, because the people being shot at had no reason to exaggerate.

For a sultanate not yet seventy years old, on the far edge of the Muslim world,
that is a remarkable piece of diplomacy. Aceh had identified the one power
capable of matching Portuguese gunnery, reached it across an ocean it did not
control, and come back with the technology and the men who understood it.

## What it was asking for

The embassy wanted more than guns. It was asking the Ottoman sultan — who
claimed the caliphate — for protection, and the language of such requests is
the language of a superior taking a client under his authority.

That relationship never formed. There was no seal, no title conferred, no
investiture of any kind; the sultan of Aceh went on styling himself by his own
descent and answering to nobody. What Aceh got was a shipment.

It is worth sitting with the difference, because Aceh's neighbours had done
better. A century and a half earlier Melaka's founder had gone to Nanjing in
person and come back with Ming recognition — an actual grant of standing from
an actual overlord, which shaped how the whole strait treated him. Aceh asked
for the same kind of thing at ten times the distance and received artillery
instead.

Artillery turned out to be the more useful of the two, and it lasted about as
long.
