The `peak` ruler on this record is Iskandar Muda, who reigned from 1607 to 1636
and whose name means the young Alexander — the same Alexander the Melakan
chronicle put at the head of its dynasty's genealogy, which is a small
demonstration of how much of this region's political vocabulary Melaka had set.

He took the pepper coast on both sides of Sumatra, took Pahang and Kedah across
the water, and moved populations wholesale from the ports he conquered to the
land around his own capital. The army he built was centred there: war captives
settled where he could call on them, an elephant corps, and the artillery. None
of it depended on the territorial chiefs.

## Why the military field is null

That is deliberate, and it is why this record's `military_basis` is null rather
than coded.

Aceh has two military systems in its documented life and the second was built to
undo the first. Iskandar Muda's arrangement — a force held at the capital,
recruited from people with no local attachment, precisely so that no *uleëbalang*
could withhold it — worked for one reign. After 1641 the chiefs recovered their
levies, the capital's force decayed, and Acehnese military capacity for the next
two centuries was whatever the territorial lords chose to raise. It was that
second system, not the first, that fought the Dutch for thirty years.

Coding rule 7 asks for the rule rather than the outcome. This record has two
rules in sequence, and a single value in the field would be a claim about which
century counts. Forty records on this site are null in this field, and on most
of them it means nobody has opened a source that says. This one means the
sources agree and describe two different things.
