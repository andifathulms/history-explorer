Iskandar Muda reigned from 1607 to 1636, and his name means the young Alexander
— the same Alexander the Melakan chronicle put at the head of its dynasty's
genealogy, which is a small demonstration of how much of this region's
political vocabulary Melaka had set.

He took the pepper coast on both sides of Sumatra, took Pahang and Kedah across
the water, and moved populations wholesale from the ports he conquered to the
land around his own capital. The army he built was centred there: war captives
settled where he could call on them, an elephant corps, and the artillery. None
of it depended on the territorial chiefs.

## Two armies, and the second undid the first

Aceh has two military systems in its documented life, and that is worth stating
plainly because the second was built specifically to undo the first.

Iskandar Muda's arrangement was a force held at the capital and recruited from
people with no local attachment, precisely so that no *uleebalang* could
withhold it from him. It worked for one reign. After 1641 the chiefs recovered
their levies, the force at the capital decayed, and Acehnese military capacity
for the next two centuries was whatever the territorial lords chose to raise.

It was that second system, not the first, that fought the Dutch for thirty
years — and fought them far more effectively than a single royal army would
have, for the reason that there was no longer anybody whose surrender could end
the war.
