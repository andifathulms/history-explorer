Iskandar Muda's son-in-law and successor died in 1641 without a son. The
*orangkaya* — the merchant aristocracy that Iskandar Muda had spent thirty years
suppressing, executing and taxing — put his widow on the throne.

Safiatuddin reigned thirty-four years. She was followed by three more women, and
Aceh was ruled by queens without interruption from 1641 to 1699.

This paragraph used to call that the longest run of reigning women anywhere in
this corpus by a wide margin. It was written before the Patani record existed,
and it is wrong: Patani had four queens across a hundred and four years, from
1584 to 1688, beginning fifty-seven years before Safiatuddin. That record's first
chapter sets the two side by side, and the comparison makes the argument below
stronger rather than weaker.

It was not a rule and it was not a principle. It was a mechanism. A queen could
not lead an army in person, could not accumulate a personal following among the
war captains, and depended on the merchants for revenue. The orangkaya had
watched what an unrestrained sultan did to them and chose rulers who structurally
could not do it again, four times running, until the ulama produced a fatwa
against female rule and the practice stopped.

## What this record does to `matrilineal`

Earlier in this corpus's life the succession vocabulary gained the value
`matrilineal`, on an audit of five records: Ghana, where al-Bakri states the
rule; Makuria, on the Arabic accounts and the titulature; Asante, on Wilks's
institutional detail; and two more on periods this site does not carry. Mali was
deliberately left null, because what the sources describe there is a pattern
rather than a rule, and that decision is the change's best justification.

Aceh is the other side of the same test. Fifty-eight years of women on the
throne, and nothing matrilineal within a thousand miles of it. Descent at Aceh
ran through men; every one of these queens held the throne as the widow or
daughter of a sultan, and the second, third and fourth were chosen by a council
of merchants. Had the value been defined by the sex of the ruler rather than by
the line of descent, this record would now be carrying it, and it would be
saying something false.

`succession_rule` here is null. The comment in the data says why at length, and
it is the same reason as the military field: the sources are clear, and what
they are clear about is not a rule.
