Le Thanh Tong, the fourth king of the dynasty, was crowned at
eighteen in 1460 and went on to become one of the most illustrious
rulers Vietnam ever had. He steered the kingdom through the
political unrest that had brought him to the throne and quickly
gave every department of government - administration, public
instruction, war, finance, law - his own vigorous attention.

## An army of two hundred and sixty thousand

His defining campaign came against Champa, the kingdom on Dai Viet's
southern border. After a Cham raid into Hoa Chau, Le Thanh Tong
resolved to pursue the Cham forces into the heart of their own
territory. He sent 100,000 men by sea and marched personally at the
head of 160,000 more, studying the geography of Champa as he went
and refining his plan of attack. On the seventh day of the second
month of 1471 he embarked his army - swollen, according to the
annals, to as many as 700,000 men by local levies gathered along the
way - aboard a thousand junks.

## Cha Ban falls

The Cham king, Tra Toan, sued for peace once the scale of the
invasion became clear, but Le Thanh Tong refused to settle for
anything less than the Cham capital itself. On the twenty-seventh day
of the second month his forces seized the citadel of Thi Nai in a
single stroke; by the next day the capital, Cha Ban, was completely
surrounded, and on the first day of the third month it fell by
storm. Forty thousand Cham were killed and thirty thousand taken
prisoner, Tra Toan among them. Le Thanh Tong had ordered his life
spared in the fighting and had him treated with consideration, but
the captive king, weakening steadily in captivity, died before the
army had even returned home. Le Thanh Tong ordered his head cut off
and displayed at the prow of his own royal junk beneath a white
banner reading: "Head of Tra Toan, source of Champa's misfortune."
Champa itself was broken into three small principalities under Cham
chiefs who answered, from then on, to Dai Viet.
