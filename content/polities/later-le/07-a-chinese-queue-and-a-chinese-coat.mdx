By the 1780s the Le kings reigned without ruling, their throne long
overshadowed first by the Trinh lords in the north and then by the
Tay Son rebellion that broke the Trinh in turn. Le Chieu Thong,
the dynasty's twenty-seventh king, turned to the Qing empire for
help against the Tay Son general Nguyen Hue, who had taken Hanoi in
his name but was fast becoming a power the throne could not control.
A Qing army marched south to restore him.

## Destroyed in a single campaign

Nguyen Hue met that army and destroyed it. The Qing commander
escaped only by his own agility, scaling a wall and fleeing across
the river; the rest of his force, cut off when the Vietnamese seized
the bridge linking the citadel to the far bank, was killed almost to
the last man. Le Chieu Thong fled north with his defeated protectors;
the queen mother and the crown prince, leaving too late, found the
bridge already cut and had to seek refuge in the western provinces
instead. The king himself crossed into Chinese territory, gradually
rejoined there by mandarins scattered in the retreat.

## A symbolic abdication

New Qing reinforcements were promised at first, but the general
given command of them, bribed with gold and silver sent by Nguyen
Hue's brother, talked the emperor out of continuing the campaign on
the Le king's behalf. Le Chieu Thong's advisers, reading the
situation clearly, persuaded him to shave his head, take up the
queue, and dress in Chinese clothing - a gesture that amounted to a
moral abdication, and one his own chief adviser used exactly that
way, writing to the Qing emperor that the king of Annam had settled
permanently and safely within the empire's own territory and that
there was no longer any reason to continue the war. The emperor
accepted the advice, recalled his troops, and received the Tay Son's
own envoys instead. Le Chieu Thong, stripped of his throne, was
brought to Beijing, arriving in the second month of 1789. He was
given a minor honorary rank and a small monthly allowance for
himself and his attendants, and was never permitted to leave.
