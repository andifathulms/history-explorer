Three decades after Le Thanh Tong's death, real power at the Le
court had passed into the hands of a general named Mac Dang Dung,
who kept the reigning king as a captive figurehead while he
methodically broke resistance to his own authority across the
provinces. He was not gentle even with his own family: in 1526 he
had his own brother-in-law put to death after his wife, seeking
revenge for his taking ten concubines, falsely accused the man of
conspiracy. King Le Chieu Tong, held prisoner since Mac Dang Dung's
allies had captured him, was murdered on his captor's order that
same year, at the age of twenty-six.

## A boy king kept for show

Mac Dang Dung did not seize the throne outright at once. He kept the
young Le Cung Hoang on it as a legal fig leaf while he finished his
preparations, surrounding the boy with pleasures precisely so that he
would not see clearly what was happening around him - by Mac Dang
Dung's own household's later account, an image the court could point
to from a distance so the people would still see a king seated on the
throne. In the meantime Mac Dang Dung accumulated titles and
ceremonial honors that made him, in substance, a second king standing
beside the first.

## The abdication no honorable minister would write

When the moment came, Mac Dang Dung ordered his minister of the
interior to draft an act of abdication transferring the throne to
him. That minister, Truong Phu Thuyet, refused outright, asking by
what right such a thing could be demanded of him. A more compliant
official, Nguyen Van Thoi, wrote the document instead, and Mac Dang
Dung had the boy king sign it before proclaiming himself ruler under
the name Minh Duc in the sixth month of 1527. He degraded Le Cung
Hoang and the queen mother at once, confined them, and before long
ordered them to take their own lives. Mac Dang Dung then settled
into the royal palace at Thang Long, gave his ancestors the title of
kings, and set about securing the loyalty of officials who, as even
his own court could sense, had little real devotion to him.
