Describe the Ya'ariba from the inside and you get a mountain imamate: an elected
imam, qualified in law before he is anything else, tribal horsemen raised through
kinship, and a revenue built on tithes and dates.

Describe what they actually did and you get a naval power.

They took a fortified harbour from a European empire. They built and operated an
ocean-going armed fleet. They projected force across two thousand miles of open
water, down the East African coast and into the approaches to India. And they
sustained a thirty-three-month siege by sea against relief squadrons sent from
Goa.

## Two descriptions of one polity

The gap between those two accounts is the most interesting thing about this
state, and it is easy to miss because the second half was built so fast.

Nothing in the imamate's constitution anticipates a navy. The office was designed
for the interior — for a scholar-judge chosen by those competent to judge, ruling
a country of mountains and wadis where an outside army could arrive and could not
stay. Its army was the tribes, and the tribes were not sailors. Ships cost money
that tithes on dates do not obviously produce, and a fleet needs permanent
crews, yards, timber and captains who answer to somebody continuously — none of
which a tribal levy supplies.

They acquired all of it inside a generation, and they acquired it by taking
Portuguese infrastructure rather than by growing their own. Muscat came with a
harbour, fortifications and the accumulated apparatus of a maritime empire's
forward base. The imamate captured a going concern and ran it.

## Which description is right

Both, and the tension between them is not resolved by picking one.

The chapter that follows this one is about 1719, when the scholars elected a
child and other scholars held that the election was therefore invalid — an
argument about qualification, conducted in the vocabulary of the interior, by an
institution that at that moment also owned Mombasa. The imamate never stopped
being the thing it was designed to be. It simply acquired, in about fifty years,
an ocean's worth of assets that its constitution had nothing to say about.
