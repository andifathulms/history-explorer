title: Elam, one more time

Elymais is the Greek form of Elam, and the name is three thousand years older
than the kingdom that carried it here.

Elam was one of the oldest states in the world: Susa on the plain and Anshan in
the highlands, writing in its own language in its own script, contemporary with
Sumer and Akkad and periodically at war with both, and finally destroyed by
Ashurbanipal in the 640s BC in a campaign the Assyrian reliefs record with
unusual relish.

Elam did not have one ending. It had several, separated by centuries, and each
time the name came back on a different state: the Elam of Awan and Shimashki
and the sukkalmah, which brought down the Third Dynasty of Ur in 2004; the
Middle Elamite kingdom that sacked Babylon in 1158 and carried Hammurabi's stele
home to Susa; the Neo-Elamite kingdom Ashurbanipal destroyed.

What follows here is the last of them, and it is five hundred years after
Ashurbanipal. The Achaemenids wintered at Susa, and then came Alexander and the
Seleucids, and then, around 147 BC, as the Seleucid empire was losing Iran to
the Parthians, a man called Kamnaskires took the royal title in the highland
country above the Susiana plain and the name Elam was a kingdom again.

The kingdom lasted three hundred and seventy years, most of it as somebody's
client, and produced no narrative whatever. There is no Elymaean history and no
chronicle. What there is, is coins, some rock reliefs on outcrops in the Zagros,
scattered notices in Greek and Latin writers, and a reputation for being worth
robbing.
