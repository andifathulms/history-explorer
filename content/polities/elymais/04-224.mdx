Ardashir took Elymais around 224, on his way to everything else: Fars first,
then Kerman, then Elymais and the Gulf coast, then Ctesiphon and the empire.
Khuzistan became a Sasanian province and one of the most valuable.

It is worth pausing on the six kingdoms that went the same way, because between
them they are the whole structure of the Parthian empire, and each is the same
object seen once more. Persis, Elymais, Characene, Adiabene, Media Atropatene,
Armenia. A hereditary local dynasty. Its own silver, with Greek legends that
gradually stop being Greek. Between one and five centuries of somebody's
vassalage. No surviving narrative of its own. And an ending in the 220s at the
hands of the sub-king from Fars who took the lot.

They are identical in structure and they fall on a very long spectrum of
consequence.

Armenia became a kingdom that outlived every empire around it and is still
there. Persis produced the dynasty that ended the whole arrangement and ruled
Iran for four centuries. Atropatene gave its name to a country. Adiabene's royal
house became Jewish, and its capital became the senior bishopric of a church
that reached China. Characene was where the ships unloaded.

And Elymais had temples somebody robbed.

That range is the reason for looking at all six rather than the two famous ones.
An empire held together by magnates is only legible if you can see the magnates,
and five of these six would not appear in a narrative history of Iran at all —
not because they did not matter but because nobody in them wrote a book.
