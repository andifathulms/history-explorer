Aurelian became emperor in 270, stabilised the Danube, and then came east. He
took Anatolia with little fighting, beat the Palmyrene army twice — at Immae
near Antioch and again at Emesa — and besieged Palmyra itself. The city
surrendered in 272. Zenobia was caught trying to reach the Euphrates, and
Vaballathus disappears from the record.

Aurelian left a garrison and went west. In 273 Palmyra rose again, killed the
garrison, and Aurelian turned round and came back. This time the city was sacked,
its walls broken and its population dispersed or killed, and Palmyra as a power
ended in a single season. Zenobia herself was, by most accounts, displayed in
Aurelian's triumph in Rome and then settled in Italy, which is a considerably
better outcome than a defeated sovereign usually got.
