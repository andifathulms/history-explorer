The Arabs took the country in the 650s and did not abolish it. The Mihranid
princes went on governing under Arab suzerainty for another century and a half,
paying tribute and keeping their houses and their church — the same terms a
dozen frontier principalities from the Caspian to the Atlas were living on.

Two things ended it, at different times and in quite different registers.

In 705 the Albanian catholicosate was subordinated to the Armenian one under
Arab authority. For a country whose distinctness rested on having its own
church, with its own scripture in its own script, that is the decisive act.
Whatever the princes still governed, there was no longer an Albanian
Christianity for a country to be about. The manuscripts stopped.

And then the line ended, in a way its own history records almost in passing.

## Who killed the last one, and who got the country

Movses Dasxuranci gives it in a single sentence dropped into a year's worth of
raids and locusts. The lord Nerseh son of Philippe killed Varaz-Trdat son of
Step'annos, slaughtered his son on his mother's breast, and took everything he
owned. Dasxuranci adds the identification, because by his time it needed one:
this Varaz-Trdat was of the Mihranid family, which had inherited Albania from
father to son, and he was the eighth ruler after Varaz-Grigor, the first prince.

That is the end of the princely line, around 822. It is not the end of the
country's own government, which is the part usually left out.

Within about fifteen years the principality had passed to another Albanian
house. Sahl i Smbatean, of the Eranshahik family, captured Babak — the leader of
the great Khurramite revolt, whom the caliphate had spent twenty years failing
to catch — and delivered him to the caliph. His reward, in Dasxuranci's words,
was sovereignty over Armenia, Georgia and Albania, to rule authoritatively and
regally over all three. His descendants held Khachen, in the highland country
east of Lake Sevan, and the Albanian catholicosate went on existing in that
corner long after the church as a whole had been put under Armenian authority.

So the succession is not nothing. It is a native house receiving its own country
back from Baghdad as payment for a police operation, holding it in a mountain
district, under a name almost nobody outside the region used.

## What continued, and under what name

The lowland went on as Arran: a province with a large Christian population,
which was gradually reclassified as Armenian because after 705 there was no
other category available for it, fought over by whichever dynasty was strongest
— eventually the Shaddadids, who were Kurds and had nothing to do with any of
this.

Armenia and Georgia lost their crowns earlier than Albania did, and both got
them back. Albania kept a princely house in the mountains and lost its church,
and the church was the thing that could not be recovered. A country whose
distinctness had consisted of a liturgy in its own script, with the script gone,
had nothing left to be distinct with.

What it left is a language spoken today in two villages, a body of
Armenian-language historical writing about itself, and fifty-two letters that
had to be recovered from a fire in a monastery on the wrong continent.
