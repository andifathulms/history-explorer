The Sasanians abolished the Albanian monarchy in 510 and governed through a
marzban, as they had done in Armenia in 428 and would do in Iberia in 580. All
three Caucasian crowns were removed by the same empire inside a hundred and
fifty years.

In all three the nobility survived, and in Albania it produced a new dynasty.
The Mihranids were of Sasanian noble descent, established in the country in the
late sixth century, and they took the old Albanian princely title of Arranshah
and governed as princes rather than kings.

Javanshir ruled from about 637 to 680, and he came to power in the exact decade
the world he had been born into ended. He fought for the Sasanians against the
Arabs at Qadisiyya, and watched the empire he had served disappear within five
years. What followed was fifteen years of about as exposed a diplomatic position
as a small country has ever held.

He submitted to Byzantium and took a title from Constans II. He negotiated with
the caliph Mu'awiya and obtained terms — reduced tribute, autonomy, his own
church left alone. He fought the Khazars coming down through the Derbend pass and beat them back
once; when they returned in greater force two years later he could not hold
them a second time, accepted tribute and vassalage, and married a daughter to
the khagan to make the peace durable. In a decade and a half he acknowledged
three empires and was governed by none of them.

He was assassinated by his own nobles in 680, which is the other permanent
feature of a polity built on hereditary houses.

The eulogy composed for him by Davtak the Melodist survives — in Armenian, in
the *History of the Albanians* — and is one of the earliest datable poems in
Caucasian literature. It is an acrostic, and it is about a man who kept a
country by agreeing with everyone.
