The decimal place-value system with a symbol for zero, treated as a number rather
than a blank, is attested in India in this period. Aryabhata's astronomical work
of 499 uses place-value; the Bakhshali manuscript and the inscriptional record
carry the notation; Brahmagupta in the seventh century writes the rules for
operating with zero and with negative numbers.

It travelled. Indian astronomical texts reached Baghdad in the eighth century,
al-Khwarizmi wrote on Indian calculation in the ninth, and his work reached Latin
Europe through translations in the twelfth — which is why the digits are called
Arabic in English and Indian in Arabic, and why *algorithm* is his name.

Every number on this site is written in it. The reach figures, the spans, the
percentiles in the rankings view, the years on the timeline.

This page's descendant-scripts count is null and its influence bars are empty,
because the coding rules count writing systems, religions carried and successor
claims, and a numeral notation is none of those. The rules are being applied
correctly and the result is a page that ranks this empire modestly while the site
that ranks it could not display a single figure without the thing the empire
produced.

That is stated here rather than fixed, because fixing it would mean adding an
axis to make one polity score better, which is exactly the kind of judgement the
measures exist to keep out. The honest arrangement is a rule that is uniform, a
page that says where it falls short, and a reader who has been told.
