The Gupta empire held the Gangetic plain from around 320 and never covered as
much ground as the Mauryas had, governing much of its territory through subject
kings rather than directly. Its reputation rests on what its court sustained
rather than on what it conquered.

The Sanskrit literary canon takes its classical shape here — Kalidasa is placed
in this period — along with the codification of Hindu legal and philosophical
texts, temple architecture in stone, and the mathematics. The decimal place-value
system with a symbol for zero is attested in India in this period, passed to the
Islamic world where al-Khwarizmi wrote on it in ninth-century Baghdad, and
reached Europe from there as Arabic numerals.

<Figure id="gu-deogarh" />

That is not a writing system, a religion or a dynasty, and it is therefore
invisible to almost every way anybody has of comparing empires. It is also, by
some distance, the most widely used thing any state in this list produced.

<Figure id="gu-sarnath" />

The empire itself fragmented in the sixth century under pressure from the Hunas
and from two centuries of granting away its own revenue.
