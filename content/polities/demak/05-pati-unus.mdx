Raden Patah's successor reigned three years, between a founder and a conqueror,
and he is the reason Demak is remembered for something other than taking Java.

He commanded the fleets that attacked Portuguese Melaka in 1512 and 1513 and
failed. Javanese tradition calls him Pangeran Sabrang Lor — the prince who
crossed to the north.

## The ship

Portuguese accounts of the 1513 attack describe the Javanese flagship as a
*jong* of extraordinary size — bigger than anything they had, with multiple
layers of hull planking that their guns could not penetrate, carrying a very
large crew.

The numbers in those accounts are the numbers of frightened men and should not
be taken literally. What they establish, without any figure being trustworthy,
is that in 1513 a Javanese sultanate could build ships a European fleet found
alarming — and that the reason the attack failed was not the ships.

It was that Melaka is eight hundred miles away, the fleet had to be supplied
across that distance, and an assault on a fortified position from the sea is
the hardest operation there is.

## Why it failed

Demak fought with Javanese levies from the rice country and with the fleets of
coastal chiefs who served rather than obeyed, and both were adequate to the
thing it had done everywhere else, which was to take a port on its own island
chain.

Melaka was a different problem. An assault on a fortified position from the sea
is the hardest operation there is, and this one had to be mounted and then fed
across eight hundred miles of open water, twice, against a garrison that could
be resupplied from Goa. The prince who crossed to the north is named for the
crossing, not for what happened at the far end of it.
