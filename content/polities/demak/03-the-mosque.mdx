The great mosque at Demak is a Javanese building. It has a stacked pyramidal
roof of the kind that stood over Javanese shrines before Islam, no dome, no
minaret, and four main pillars — the *soko guru* — one of which tradition says
was assembled from wood chips by one of the *wali songo*, the nine saints.

It is the oldest substantially surviving mosque on Java, and it is the whole
argument of this chapter in one building.

## What Islamisation looked like here

The *wali songo* are the nine figures Javanese tradition credits with
converting the island: Sunan Kalijaga, Sunan Bonang, Sunan Giri, Sunan
Gunungjati and the rest, each with a tomb that is a pilgrimage site and a body
of story attached.

What the stories consistently describe is accommodation. Sunan Kalijaga is
supposed to have used *wayang* — the shadow theatre, whose repertoire is the
Ramayana and the Mahabharata — to teach, and to have introduced gamelan
performance at the mosque as a way of drawing a crowd. The Javanese Islamic
tradition that came out of it kept the shadow plays, the gamelan, the calendar
partly recalculated rather than replaced, and a great deal of the older
vocabulary.

The Hindu-Buddhist tradition of the Javanese courts is usually said to have
moved east to Bali when Majapahit ended, and it did. But that is only half of
where it went. Some of it crossed the strait intact, and some of it stayed
exactly where it was, inside the religion that replaced it.

## Two mechanisms at once

It would be easy to read the mosque and the wali songo as the whole story of
how Java became Muslim. It is about half of it.

The other half was campaigns. Demak carried Islam inland to the rice country
and the interior courts by force through the 1520s and 1540s, and one of those
campaigns killed Trenggana, the sultan who had ordered it.

Both were running at the same time, and the division of labour is fairly clean:
an army took the courts, and a shadow-puppet repertoire took the villages. The
first is why there was a sultanate at all. The second is why Javanese Islam
looks the way it does.
