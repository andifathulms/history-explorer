In 1512 and again in 1513 Demak sent a fleet against the Portuguese at Melaka,
under Pati Unus, and both attempts failed.

Ports in these islands mostly experienced European power the other way round —
a fleet arrives, and you deal with the consequences. Melaka itself had fallen
to one two years earlier. Demak did the unusual thing and crossed eight hundred
miles of sea to attack a fortified city held by the power that had just taken
it. That the attempt failed is less surprising than that it was made.

## The rice again

Demak sat on the north coast with the Javanese granary behind it, and what made
it rich was not growing rice but exporting it — to the ports of the archipelago
that could not feed themselves, including, until 1511, Melaka.

Melaka had no farmland at all and bought most of its food from Java. The two
cities were the two halves of one arrangement. When Melaka fell to the
Portuguese, the polity that had been selling it rice sent ships to try to take
it back — which is a commercial motive as much as a religious one, and the
sources give both.
