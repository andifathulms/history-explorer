This corpus's Majapahit record ends with a chapter arguing that nothing much
happened in 1527: no siege, no treaty, no last king killed, and a traditional
date of 1478 that is wrong twice over. What it describes instead is a slow
inversion — a north coast that became Muslim through trade over a century and a
half, in a state whose economy depended on the Hindu-Buddhist interior's rice
reaching that coast's ships.

Demak is the polity that finished the inversion, and it is the reason the
Majapahit record's `ended.by` field was empty until now.

Raden Patah was installed at Demak around 1475 as a vassal of Majapahit and had
declared himself independent by about 1478. The Javanese chronicles trace his
descent to Majapahit's own royal house, and that genealogy is not decoration: it
is the entire basis on which a port town claimed the right to rule Java rather
than merely to trade from it. This record's `legitimation` carries `descent` for
it, and the Majapahit record's `successor_claims` — null since it was written —
now reads one, and the item is this page.

## What the field can now say

Two changes came out of writing this record, both of them on somebody else's
page.

`majapahit.ended.by` reads `[demak]`. The type stays `gradual absorption`,
because the argument in that chapter is unaffected by naming an absorber — this
corpus's Ghana record is `gradual absorption` with `by: [mali]` for exactly the
same kind of ending, a polity that was taken over rather than defeated.

And the edge `majapahit → demak` is typed `absorbed remnants of` and marked
contested, rather than `conquered by`. The looser reading would have drawn a
cleaner picture of an event that the sources say did not happen as an event.
