---
title: The coast takes the interior
drafted_from: ricklefs-2008
phase: formation
---

Nothing much happened in 1527. No siege, no treaty, no last king killed.

What had been happening for a century and a half before it was a slow
inversion: a north coast that became Muslim through trade, in a state whose
economy depended on the Hindu-Buddhist interior's rice reaching that coast's
ships. Demak is the polity that finished the inversion.

Raden Patah was installed at Demak around 1475 as a vassal of Majapahit and had
declared himself independent by about 1478. The Javanese chronicles trace his
descent to Majapahit's own royal house, and that genealogy is not decoration:
it is the entire basis on which a port town claimed the right to rule Java
rather than merely to trade from it.

It is also worth noticing what kind of claim it is. Demak did not conquer
Majapahit and never said it had. It said it was Majapahit — the same line, the
same right, a different religion and a different coast — and that argument was
still being made a century later, when Pajang's founder claimed descent from
Majapahit's last king and from the Demak sultan he had just overthrown, at the
same time.
