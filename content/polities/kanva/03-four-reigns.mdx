Vasudeva Kanva was succeeded by three of his own descendants. Between the
four of them, the dynasty held the throne for forty-five years — and of
those four reigns, nothing whatever is recorded beyond the bare fact of
succession.

## What the silence implies

Smith reads the brevity of the reigns, taken together with the total
absence of any other detail, as its own kind of evidence: a dynasty this
poorly documented, following two predecessors whose ends were both violent
successions by trusted subordinates, most likely continued the pattern
rather than breaking it. Nothing survives to confirm that any of the four
Kanva kings died peacefully, and nothing survives to prove otherwise
either. The silence is total in both directions.

## The last of them

The fourth king, Susarman, was killed in 27 BC by an Andhra ruler from the
Deccan, ending the shortest-lived and least documented of the four
dynasties to hold Magadha's throne in succession — Nanda, Maurya, Shunga,
Kanva — across the three centuries since Mahapadma Nanda first took it by
force from the Shishunagas.

## What forty-five years of silence actually means

It does not mean nothing happened at Pataliputra between 72 and 27 BC. It
means that whatever happened left no inscription any later scholar has
found, no coinage any numismatist can attribute with confidence, and no
story any chronicler thought worth preserving beyond the two facts that
bracket the whole dynasty: how it began, and how it ended. Between a
minister's murder of his own king and a southern conqueror's murder of his
successor's great-grandson, forty-five years of Magadhan history have
simply vanished — not disputed, not contradicted by rival accounts, just
absent.
