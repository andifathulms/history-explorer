The Wattasid century runs between two pressures and gets thinner throughout.

To the north, Portugal and Spain on the coast. To the south, from about 1510, a
sharifian family in the Sous organising the resistance to exactly that — which
is this site's next page and the thing the Wattasids could not do.

The dynasty's response was the one available to a weak government with a strong
enemy on two sides, and it was fatal to its standing. It made terms. Wattasid
sultans signed truces with Portugal, traded with the fortresses, and at various
points accepted Portuguese support against domestic rivals; one of them,
Muhammad, had been a hostage in Portugal as a boy and is known in the sources
as al-Burtuqali, the Portuguese. In the 1540s, with the Saadis taking the south,
Fez sought Ottoman help from Algiers, and Ottoman troops installed a Wattasid
in Fez in 1554 for a few months before the Saadis threw them both out.

Both moves are intelligible and both confirmed the charge. A dynasty accused of
being unable to defend Muslim territory answered by allying first with the
Christian power holding the ports and then with a foreign Muslim power holding
Algiers.

Muhammad al-Shaykh of the Saadis took Fez in 1549 and again decisively in 1554,
and the Wattasid line ended. This record types that `dynastic replacement`
rather than conquest: the state, its administration and its capital continued
uninterrupted, and what changed was the family and the argument for it.

Two things worth keeping. This is the last dynasty to rule Morocco on a purely
political claim; every Moroccan dynasty since — the Saadis on the next page and
the Alawis who followed them and reign today — has been sharifian, claiming
descent from the Prophet. The Wattasids are the point at which that became the
requirement.

And the page is thin because the century is. Four chapters would be padding.
What this polity did was hold a country it could not defend for eighty years
while better-organised neighbours took its coast and a better-credentialed
rival took its interior, and the record shows a null reach, a null population,
three empty influence counts and one turning point that is somebody else's
conquest of a port.
