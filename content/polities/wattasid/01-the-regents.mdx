---
title: The regents who stopped pretending
drafted_from: abun-nasr-1987
phase: formation
---

The Marinid dynasty spent its last century with children and nonentities on the
throne and its government in the hands of the Wattasids — Zenata kinsmen of the
Marinids who became hereditary regents from 1420 and refused to give the
regency back. In 1465 a rising in Fez killed the last Marinid, and in 1472 the
Wattasids stopped ruling in anybody's name and took the throne.

That is the oldest mechanism in politics: the instrument of a state becoming
heavier than the state. Here it produced one of the weakest governments the
region ever had.

The problem was legitimacy, and it is worth setting out, because everything
that happened to the dynasty afterwards followed from it. The Almoravids had a legal programme; the Almohads had a
mahdi; the Marinids had no doctrine but did have two centuries of possession
and a madrasa-building record. The Wattasids had a family relationship to the
dynasty they had displaced, and possession. That is all, and it is a thin thing
to rule Morocco on.

What made it worse is what was happening on the coast. Portugal had taken Ceuta
in 1415 — the Marinid page records it — and did not stop: Arzila and Tangier in
1471, then Safi, Azemmour, Agadir, Mazagan, Mogador and a string of smaller
posts down the Atlantic, until by the 1520s the Moroccan coastline was a chain
of European fortresses with a Moroccan interior behind it. Spain was doing the
same on the Mediterranean side, at Oran, Bougie and eventually Tunis.

A government whose only claim is that it holds the country, and which visibly
cannot hold the coast, is in trouble with anyone who can make a better claim.
In the south, in the Draa and the Sous, somebody could.
