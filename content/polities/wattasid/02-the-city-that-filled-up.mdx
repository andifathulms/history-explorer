title: The city that filled up
drafted_from: abun-nasr-1987

The Wattasids governed Morocco during the two decades in which al-Andalus
stopped existing, and the largest thing that happened to their country was
demographic rather than political.

Granada surrendered in January 1492 — the ending on this site's Nasrid page —
and the Capitulations that guaranteed the Muslim population its religion and
its law were void within seven years. Conversion became compulsory in Castile
in 1502. Perhaps two hundred thousand people left for North Africa at or soon
after the conquest, and the flow continued in waves for more than a century,
through the Alpujarras rising of 1568 and the final expulsions of 1609 to 1614.

A large part of them came to Fez, which had absorbed Andalusi refugees before —
this site's Idrisid page has a whole quarter of the city settled by people
expelled from Cordoba in 818 — and which now received a population of urban,
literate, skilled exiles from a country with a higher material culture than the
one taking them in.

What they brought is visible in Morocco still. Andalusi craftsmen carried the
silk weaving, the tilework, the leather trades and the building techniques of
Granada; Andalusi musicians brought the *al-ala* repertoire that is still the
classical music of Morocco and Algeria; Andalusi families supplied secretaries
and jurists to the chancery. The dialect of Fez, the architecture of its
merchant houses and the food are all marked by it.

It was not a comfortable absorption. The refugees were resented as foreigners
in places, competed for trade and office, and some were suspected of the
lukewarm Islam of people who had spent a generation being baptised under
compulsion. But the receiving of them is the largest single thing the Wattasid
state did, and it did it while losing a war on the coast and its authority in
the interior.

That is worth saying plainly because the previous chapter and the next are
about a dynasty failing. This is the one column in which it did not. Morocco
absorbed the population of a destroyed country and turned it into part of
itself, and the government that presided over that is the one this site's own
record codes with the weakest legitimation in the region.
