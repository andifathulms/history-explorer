title: Power over a passage

Srivijaya is the polity that most tests this site's measurements, which is why it
is here.

Its power was not territorial. It sat on the Melaka and Sunda straits — the only
sea route between the Indian Ocean and the South China Sea — and it made its
living by making that passage safe for those who called at its ports and unsafe
for those who did not. It commanded the loyalty of coastal chiefs and the crews
who could otherwise have been pirates, through a mixture of redistributed trade
wealth, ritual authority and oath-taking recorded in stone inscriptions that
threaten spectacular curses against the disloyal. Chinese sources describe it as
the place the southern trade came through for six centuries.

There is no meaningful figure for its peak extent, and this page's reach axis is
empty as a result. That is not a gap in scholarship so much as a mismatch between
the measure and the thing: square kilometres of land under state control does not
describe a polity whose asset was a strait. Taagepera does not tabulate it, and
computing an area from the map polygon is forbidden here — hard rule 5 — because
the polygon would be a drawing of an idea rather than a measurement.

Srivijaya is also the case named in this site's own design notes as the reason
continuity is a section rather than a spine. It has no succession relationship to
anything else here and never will, and it is a first-class polity on this site
regardless.
