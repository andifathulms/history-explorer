Yijing, a Chinese Buddhist monk, sailed for India in 671 and stopped at
Srivijaya on the way. He stayed about six months to learn Sanskrit grammar,
went on to Nalanda for a decade, came back, and stayed at Palembang again — for
years this time — writing and translating.

His advice to Chinese monks heading for India was to stop here first and do a
year or two of preparation, because the Sanskrit was as good and the discipline
the same. He describes more than a thousand monks in residence.

## Traffic in both directions

Srivijaya did not invent Buddhism and converted nobody at spearpoint. What it
did was be the place between China and India where the texts, the language and
the discipline were available, for six hundred years, on the route everybody
had to take anyway.

And the traffic ran both ways. Yijing came south to prepare for India. Atisha —
the eleventh-century master whose teaching reshaped Tibetan Buddhism, and whose
lineage the Tibetan schools were still arguing over centuries later — is
recorded as having studied for twelve years under a teacher at Srivijaya before
going north.

A Bengali monk trains in Sumatra and reforms Buddhism in the Himalayas. That is
the kind of thing a polity on a trade route does that has nothing to do with
trade.

## What the monasteries were for

The connection between the monks and the tolls is not incidental.

A state with no land tax and no army has exactly two things to spend its
customs revenue on. One is keeping the crews loyal. The other is building the
reputation that makes ships want to call in the first place — and a ruler who
endowed monasteries, hosted foreign scholars and funded a monastery at Nalanda
in Bengal was not being pious at the expense of policy.

He was making Palembang a place worth stopping at. A shipmaster choosing
between harbours is choosing on safety, on price, and on whether the place is
consequential enough to be worth the detour. Srivijaya spent six centuries
ensuring the answer to the third question was yes, and the monks were a
substantial part of how.
