The Fulani jihad of Usman dan Fodio took Ngazargamu in 1808 and the mai fled. The
state was recovered not by him but by a scholar from the north — Muhammad al-Amin
al-Kanemi — who raised a force, drove the Fulani back, and then governed the
country for thirty-eight years while the mai continued to reign.

This corpus has a name for that arrangement. The Ethiopian record's fourth
turning point is the Zemene Mesafint, the era of the princes, when the emperors
at Gondar reigned without ruling while regional lords fought over who would
control them. Bornu's version was tidier and had only one lord, and it ended the
same way: in 1846 al-Kanemi's son killed the last mai and took the throne
himself.

Eight hundred years of one dynasty ended in a room.

## What the vocabulary could and could not say about that

This record's fifth turning point is tagged `succession-crisis`, and the comment
above it in the data says the build rejected the type that fits.

`dynastic replacement` is a value on this site — it is one of six ways a polity
can end — and it is not one of the nine ways an event inside a polity's life can
be classified. So the eight-hundred-year dynasty being killed and replaced,
which is unambiguously a dynastic replacement, is filed under the nearest of the
nine, which understates it.

That is the ordinary condition of a closed vocabulary and this site's practice is
to note it and move on rather than to grow a value for one case. What is worth
recording is that the two lists overlap in subject and not in membership, which
had not come up before.

## The end

The state continued under the Kanemi shehus for another forty-seven years, moved
its capital to Kukawa, and was still functioning when Europeans began arriving
overland to describe it — Denham and Clapperton in the 1820s, Barth in the 1850s,
whose account is the fullest outside description this record has.

In 1893 Rabih az-Zubayr, a Sudanese warlord with a firearms-equipped army built
out of the wreckage of the Egyptian slaving administration in the south, took the
country and killed its ruler. The French killed Rabih in 1900, and the territory
was partitioned between France, Britain and Germany.

This record's `ended.by` is empty because none of those four powers is in this
corpus.

## Six of nine

That is the sixth African record on this site to end with an empty `by`, and the
region now has nine.

Aksum ends in a `gradual absorption` with no conqueror named at all. Kush ends
with nobody, the traditional candidate having been dismissed. Mali ends in a
`fragmentation` with an empty `by`. Kilwa ends against Portugal, which has no
page. Ethiopia ends against a committee of its own officers, which is not a
polity this site can name. And this one ends against a warlord and three colonial
empires, none of which is here.

Three of the nine end against a power the site can name. Ghana's is the record
next to it on the same river system — `ghana -> mali`, which is one of this
region's three internal edges. The other two, Songhai's Saadi sultanate and
Makuria's Mamluks, are both outside the region.

That is not a fact about African states being hard to end. It is a fact about
which polities this corpus has written, and the region's blurb says so. What the
pattern shows is where the boundary of the project currently runs: at the
Sahara, at the Red Sea, and at the point where European maritime empires arrive.
