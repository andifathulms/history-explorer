The last century of it runs: the Fulani jihad taking Ngazargamu in 1808, al-Kanemi recovering the state and then governing it for thirty-eight years
while the mai reigned, and the killing of the last mai in 1846 by al-Kanemi's
son.

## The end

The state continued under the Kanemi shehus for another forty-seven years, moved
its capital to Kukawa, and was still functioning when Europeans began arriving
overland to describe it — Denham and Clapperton in the 1820s, Barth in the 1850s,
whose account is the fullest description of the kingdom by any outsider.

In 1893 Rabih az-Zubayr, a Sudanese warlord with a firearms-equipped army built
out of the wreckage of the Egyptian slaving administration in the south, took the
country and killed its ruler. The French killed Rabih in 1900, and the territory
was partitioned between France, Britain and Germany.

## What actually ended it

Not Europe, in the first instance. A slave raider.

Rabih az-Zubayr had been an officer of Zubayr Pasha's private empire in the
Bahr al-Ghazal, the enormous slaving concern that the Egyptian administration in
the Sudan had licensed and then tried to absorb. When that collapsed, Rabih went
west with a core of trained riflemen and spent twenty years assembling a state
out of raiding. By 1893 he had enough men and enough guns to take Bornu outright,
and he burned Kukawa and killed its ruler.

Eight hundred years of dynasty, and then forty-seven of the family that
replaced it, ended at the hands of a man who had begun as somebody else's
enforcer in a business Bornu itself had been running for a millennium.

He held it for seven years. A French column killed him in 1900, and the territory
was divided between France, Britain and Germany — which is why Kanem is in Chad,
Bornu is in Nigeria, and the lake the kingdom was named for now has four
countries on it.
