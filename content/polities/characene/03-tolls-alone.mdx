---
title: Three polities on a single value
drafted_from: wiesehofer-1996
phase: aside
---

This record's `revenue_basis` field reads `trade-toll` and nothing else. Two
other polities in this corpus are coded the same way, and putting the three
side by side says something the individual pages cannot.

Palmyra taxed a road across a desert. An oasis with water in a place with none,
midway between Damascus and the Euphrates, whose merchant families financed the
caravans and raised the archers that escorted them, and whose surviving tariff
inscription of AD 137 lists the duty on slaves, purple wool, olive oil and camel
loads.

Derbend taxed a pass three kilometres wide between the mountains and the
Caspian — the only practicable land route between the steppe and Iran, walled by
a Sasanian emperor and held for two centuries by an emirate that lived on what
went through.

Characene taxed a transfer: the point where sea cargo had to become river cargo
because ships could not go further.

The three are separated by eight hundred years and three thousand kilometres and
they are the same object. A small permanent settlement on a route with no
practical alternative, producing little itself, whose entire political history is
other people's need to pass — and in all three cases a polity that outlasted much
larger neighbours precisely because it was worth more to everyone functioning
than conquered.

The differences are instructive too. Palmyra used the position for something
else for thirteen years and this site's record of it is mostly about them.
Derbend never did, and its `turning_points` list is empty. Characene is between:
it never attempted anything, and it has one turning point, which is about a Roman
emperor arriving and leaving.

There is a fourth member of the type that this corpus codes differently, and the
difference is worth noting. The Sharifate of Mecca on the Arabian pages carries
`trade-toll` and `tribute` together, because the thing it controlled was not a
route but a destination, and destinations are paid for rather than taxed.
