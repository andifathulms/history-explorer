title: Ardashir takes the delta

Ardashir of Persis took Characene around 222, two years before he killed
Artabanus IV and took the Parthian empire. This corpus's Persis record describes
the sequence — Fars first, then Kerman, then Elymais and the Gulf coast, then
the empire — and this kingdom is one of the pieces taken on the way.

It is a small ending and it is characteristic. Characene had been a vassal for
three hundred and fifty years, of Seleucids and then Parthians, and had survived
a Roman emperor arriving in person. What finished it was not a great power but
the neighbouring vassal, moving early, while the empire both of them belonged to
was fighting itself.

The Sasanians renamed the province Meshan and kept the port working; the delta
went on being where the Gulf traffic transferred, through the Sasanian centuries
and then under the caliphate, when Basra was founded a few dozen kilometres away
and did the same job on a larger scale. This corpus's Abbasid and Qarmatian
records are about that later traffic, and its Rasulid record is about what
happened when the volume moved to the Red Sea instead.

What Characene leaves in this corpus is a name in somebody else's inscription.
The Palmyrene record's first chapter has men honoured for bringing a caravan
safely from Spasinou Charax, set up by the merchants who travelled with them.
That was the only trace of this kingdom on the site until now, and it is a fair
summary of what a transit polity is: known, in the end, from a receipt somebody
else put up.
