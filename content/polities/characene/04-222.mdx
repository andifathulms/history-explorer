Ardashir of Persis took Characene around 222, two years before he killed
Artabanus IV and took the Parthian empire itself. The sequence was Fars first,
then Kerman, then Elymais and the Gulf coast, and this kingdom is one of the
pieces picked up on the way.

It is a small ending and it is characteristic. Characene had been somebody's
vassal for three hundred and fifty years, first the Seleucids' and then the
Parthians', and had survived a Roman emperor turning up in person. What finished
it was not a great power at all. It was the neighbouring vassal, moving early,
while the empire both of them belonged to was busy fighting itself.

The Sasanians renamed the province Meshan and kept the port working, because
there was nothing else to do with a delta at the head of a gulf. Cargo went on
being transferred there through the Sasanian centuries and then under the
caliphate, on a larger scale each time.

What Characene leaves behind is a name in somebody else's inscription. At
Palmyra the merchants who had travelled with a caravan put up monuments to the
men who brought it safely back from Spasinou Charax, and that — a mention in a
receipt, filed at the other end of the route — is a fair summary of what a
transit kingdom is.
