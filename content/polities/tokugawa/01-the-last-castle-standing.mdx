Ieyasu's victory at Sekigahara in 1600 made him Japan's actual master
without yet giving him the formal title to match. He waited three
years, consolidating his own authority, before receiving investiture as
Sei-i Tai Shogun in 1603 - the office Yoritomo had first created four
centuries earlier, now passing to a house that would hold it longer
than any before it.

<Figure id="tk-ieyasu" />

## An heir who would not simply fade away

Hideyoshi's son Hideyori remained alive at Osaka Castle throughout
these years, a nominal rival whose mere existence gave any future
opposition to Tokugawa rule a name to rally around. Ieyasu, increasingly
suspicious of Jesuit priests Hideyori continued to entertain at his own
court, eventually resolved the danger the only way that would settle it
permanently.

<Figure id="tk-hideyori" />

## A single day, a hundred thousand dead

In 1615 Ieyasu called out the combined levies of Kyushu and the Kantō
and laid siege to Osaka Castle. The fighting on 9 June was ferocious
even by the standards of a country accustomed to civil war: by the end
of a single day the citadel burned, Hideyori and thousands of his
followers were dead, and Jesuit accounts of the campaign put the total
casualties, in a war that lasted barely longer than the siege itself,
at a hundred thousand men. Catholicism in Japan, one historian later
observed, received its death blow in the very same year Puritan
pilgrims were landing at Plymouth to plant the seeds of a different
religious settlement an ocean away.

<Figure id="tk-osaka" />

## A dynasty founded on the last rival's grave

With Osaka's fall, no remaining claimant carried enough prestige or
following to challenge Tokugawa authority openly. The house Ieyasu had
built through decades of patient alliance and calculated warfare now
held the country without a serious rival left standing - a foundation
his own successors would spend the following two and a half centuries
defending less through conquest than through an increasingly rigid
system of control over every domain that might otherwise have
threatened it.
