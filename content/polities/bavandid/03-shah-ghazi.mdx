---
title: Shah Ghazi and the neighbours in the next valley
drafted_from: chi-4
phase: peak
---

The second Bavand line reached its height under Shah Ghazi Rustam, who ruled
from about 1142 to 1165 and is the one member of this dynasty the wider sources
treat as a figure in his own right rather than as a name in a list of vassals.

He held the Caspian provinces against Seljuk claimants at a point when the
Great Seljuk sultanate — whose page on this site ends with an empire held as a
family possession and administered by atabegs — was coming apart, and he
extended Bavand authority over Gilan and into Rayy and Qumis. He struck his own
coin. The Seljuk sultan Mas'ud campaigned against him and did not finish him.

What he is remembered for is the Isma'ilis.

The Nizari state on this site's Iranian Intermezzo page held Alamut and a
network of fortresses in the Alborz — the same mountains, a few valleys away.
The two were neighbours in the most literal sense available, and the
relationship was murderous. Nizari agents killed Shah Ghazi's son. He responded
with a campaign of extermination against the Isma'ili settlements he could
reach, and the sources report him building a tower of their heads, which is the
kind of detail medieval chroniclers report about a ruler they approve of.

That episode is the reason this chapter exists, because it is one of the few
places where two polities in this corpus are recorded fighting each other at
village scale. The Nizari page describes a state of scattered castles that no
imperial army could finish. This is what that looked like to the people living
in the next valley: not an unassailable fortress network but a neighbour whose
method was to kill your son, against whom a local ruler with local knowledge
could do considerably more damage than a sultan arriving from the plateau with
an army.

Shah Ghazi died around 1165. His line lasted another forty-five years and ended
in 1210 with a murder, and the province passed to the Khwarazmshahs — briefly,
because the Mongols were nine years away.
