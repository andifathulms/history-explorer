Ibn Isfandiyar stopped writing in the early thirteenth century. Somebody else
picked the chronicle up and carried it to 1349, and the last pages of that
continuation are the only close account of how the dynasty ended.

## A haircut

Start with the detail that has nothing to do with the ending and explains the
period better than anything that does.

In 1343 the Sarbadar amir Mas'ud came out of Khurasan to take Mazandaran. The
country united against him — the chronicler has an old noble, Kiya Jamal al-Din
Ahmad Jal, sending word to his kinsmen to defend the honour of Mazandaran and
not to trouble themselves about what happened to him, since he was an old man
who had lived his life. Mas'ud was driven out and killed, and his head was cut
off, stuffed with straw, and displayed on the spot where he had lately camped
with a great army.

Then this. The people of Tabaristan, except the learned and the religious, wore
their hair long and loose, sometimes a yard in length. A few days after the
execution, Malik Jalal al-Dawla noticed that his dead enemy had shaved his head,
and adopted the practice himself, along with the turban. His kinsmen copied him;
then everybody else did. The long locks called *kalalak* and the head-dress
called *kala-band* were abandoned, and that was the end of them.

A province gave up its national hairstyle because a king liked the look of a man
whose head was on a pole.

## The bath

The end of the dynasty is six years later and is a small, domestic murder.

Mazandaran had come apart into factions — the kiyas of Chalav on one side, the
kiyas of Jalal on the other, and the *ustandar* of Rustamdar holding the
mountains behind them. Into that, the chronicler says, came the dervish orders,
the pirs and the murids, which had already appeared in Khurasan. Kiya Afrasiyab
of Chalav and his kinsmen became enthusiasts for the new devotion and tried to
bring the king to it. Their saint and spiritual director, Amir Sayyid Qiwam
al-Din, disliked Malik Fakhr al-Dawla for what the text calls his real or
supposed heretical tendencies.

On 17 April 1349 Fakhr al-Dawla was murdered in the bath at Amul by Ali Kiya
son of Afrasiyab and his brother Muhammad Kiya.

Seven centuries of holding the Elburz against Arabs, Samanids, Ziyarids, Buyids,
Ghaznavids, Seljuks, Khwarazmshahs and Mongols, and the thing that finished the
house was a religious quarrel among its own nobles, conducted with a knife, in a
bathhouse, in its own capital.

## The last attempt

He left four young sons, the eldest ten years old, and they had nowhere to go
except to Malik Jalal al-Dawla Iskandar, the ustandar of Rustamdar — the same
man who had taken up the shaved head. He sheltered them, brought them up like
princes of his own house, and when they were grown undertook several campaigns
to put them back.

On one of them he attacked Kiya Afrasiyab and the sayyid at Maran-Dih near Amul
and killed Afrasiyab. Then he was outnumbered by the Mazandaranis and driven
back, and three hundred and thirty of his men of Rustamdar were killed.

That, the chronicle says, was the last attempt of the kings of Bavand to assert
their power.

## Seven hundred and five years

The continuator does the arithmetic himself and gets a different answer from the
one usually given. He dates Baw's independence to AH 45, which is AD 665 or 666,
and reckons the dynasty at seven hundred and five years in Mazandaran — because
he is counting from a slightly later founding and to the murder rather than to
anything else.

Six hundred and ninety-eight, seven hundred and five: the disagreement is the
kind that arises whenever anyone tries to put a single number on something that
lasted this long and stopped twice on the way. Neither figure is wrong. Both are
answers to slightly different questions, and a house that could be reconstituted
after sixty-seven years of not existing is not really the sort of thing that has
a length.

What is not in doubt is the last line of the chronicle, which is that the house
of Bavand had endured in Mazandaran since a Sasanian noble made himself
independent there, and that it ended with a man being killed in a bath.
