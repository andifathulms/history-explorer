title: A province writes itself down
drafted_from: chi-4

Around 1210 a man named Ibn Isfandiyar, whose father had been a senior official
of this dynasty and who had been at the Bavand court himself under Ardashir I,
started collecting material for a history of Tabaristan. He finished it a few
years later, in Persian, and it survives.

The *Tarikh-i Tabaristan* is a local history in the strict sense: not a
dynasty's chronicle and not a universal history with a province in it, but an
account of one region from its legendary origins through its Sasanian
princes, its resistance to the Arab conquest, its conversion, its Alid and
Zaydi imams and its ruling families, to the writer's own time. It preserves
material found nowhere else, including a Persian translation of a letter
attributed to a Sasanian-era Tabaristani ruler, and it is the principal source
for most of what the previous chapters on this page assert.

That is worth stating directly. Almost everything a reader can know about the
Bavands comes through a book written by a courtier of the Bavands, at the end
of their second line, about his own province.

This corpus has met that shape twice before and drawn opposite conclusions each
time. The *Tarikh-i Sistan* on the Saffarid page is treated as an asset: a
local Persian voice about a dynasty every other source describes as bandits,
and the site says so gratefully. The Nizari page has the opposite — an archive
destroyed and the account left to enemies. Ibn Isfandiyar is a third position
and the most common one in practice: not hostile, not independent, and the only
witness there is.

The site's answer is the same as everywhere else, which is to say what the
evidence is rather than to correct for it silently. The Bavand record here
gives a span of six hundred and ninety-eight years, three ruler slots of which
one is empty, and no cited extent or population. The chapter after this one is
about what the span figure means. This one is about where all of it comes from:
one man, one province, one book, written at a court that was about to stop
existing, by somebody who had grown up in it.
