---
title: A Fatimid client in the holy cities
drafted_from: daftary-2007

Ali al-Sulayhi rose in the Haraz mountains in 1047 with a small following and
within fifteen years held Yemen: Sana'a, the highlands, the Tihama coast, Aden,
and the country east towards Hadramawt. He took Zabid from the Najahids, killed
their ruler, and installed his own governor. It is a rapid conquest by any
measure, and it was done by a man whose authority came from a missionary
network rather than from a tribe or an army he had inherited.

Then he went further than Yemen. Sulayhid influence extended over the sharifate
of Mecca — Ali is recorded as having intervened in the appointment of the amir
of the holy city and as having had the khutba there read for the Fatimid caliph
al-Mustansir.

That is the high-water mark of the Fatimid claim and it should be given its
proper weight. The Fatimids in Cairo were a Shi'i caliphate asserting that the
Abbasid in Baghdad was a usurper. Having the Friday sermon at Mecca — the one
place in the Muslim world where the question of whose name is preached is
visible to pilgrims from everywhere — read in their name was worth more than a
province. And it was achieved not by a Fatimid army, which never came within a
thousand miles of it, but by a mission that had converted a judge's son in the
Yemeni mountains twenty years earlier.

This site's Fatimid page argues that the da'wa was the empire's principal
instrument and that it worked best where Fatimid troops never went. Mecca in the
1060s is the demonstration.

It did not last. In 1067 Ali set out on the pilgrimage with his wife Asma and a
substantial escort, and was ambushed on the road by the Najahids of Zabid, whose
ruler's son had waited fourteen years to do it. He was killed and his head set
on a spear outside Zabid; Asma was taken and held in a house overlooking it. His
son al-Mukarram raised the highlands, stormed Zabid and got her out.

The state survived and the reach did not. After 1067 the Sulayhids held the
southern highlands and lost, progressively, the coast, Sana'a, and any influence
in Mecca at all.
