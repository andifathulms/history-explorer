This site's Fatimid page has a chapter on the *da'wa* — the hierarchy of
missionaries directed from Cairo, operating overwhelmingly outside Fatimid
territory, in the business of persuading a rival caliph's subjects that their
caliph was a usurper. It notes that the mission worked well enough to produce
states, and names Yemen. This is that state.

Ali al-Sulayhi was the son of a Sunni judge in the Haraz mountains who
converted after a long association with a Fatimid missionary, and in 1047 he
rose in the mountains with a following. Within fifteen years he held most of
Yemen, and for a period he controlled the appointment of the amirs of Mecca,
which meant a Fatimid client was administering the holy cities on behalf of a
caliph in Cairo whose name was preached there. That is the high-water mark of
the Fatimid claim, and it was reached through a mission rather than an army.

The Sulayhids ruled as Fatimid vassals and did not pretend otherwise. The
`legitimation` field on this page reads *caliphal-investiture*, which is the
same value carried by the Samanids, Ghaznavids and Tulunids on this site — with
the difference that those polities held their diplomas from a caliph they
regarded as legitimate and largely ignored, while the Sulayhids held theirs
from a caliph whose mission had made them and who remained their religious
superior. The word is the same and the relationship is not.

Ali was ambushed and killed on the road to Mecca in the 1060s and his wife was
taken captive; his son al-Mukarram recovered the position and rescued her, and
then, in 1086, handed the government to his own wife. What follows is the
reason this page is here.
