When al-Mukarram died, the Sulayhid prince Saba ibn Ahmad asked his widow to
marry him. She refused. He raised troops and marched on Jibla to compel her, and
she raised a larger army than his, and the two hosts fought for several days.

Then her half-brother said to Saba: by God, she will not agree to what you want
except on the command of the Imam al-Mustansir, Prince of the Faithful.

So Saba stopped fighting, went back to his fortress, and wrote to Cairo.

## The delivery

The caliph's answer came in the form of two envoys and one of his own eunuchs,
who bore the honorific title Right Hand of the Mission and was empowered to
enter the queen's presence. The scene is described by a man who travelled with
them.

They came into the palace at Jibla. Her ministers, her secretaries and the
officers of state were standing, and the eunuch stood among them and delivered
the caliph's greeting with her full titles — the Favoured, the Pure, the
Unparagoned of her time, Sovereign Lady of the Kings of Yemen, Pillar of Islam,
Treasure of the Faith, Refuge of the truly directed, Friend of the Prince of the
Faithful — and then the command, with the verse that made it one: *Unto no
believer, male nor female, belongeth liberty of choice when God and His apostle
have decreed a command.* The caliph gave her in marriage to Saba, with a dowry
of a hundred thousand dinars in money and fifty thousand in rarities, perfumes
and robes.

## The reply

She answered in Qur'an, and she chose her passage with enormous care. It is the
speech of the queen of Sheba, receiving Solomon's letter.

*Verily a gracious letter hath been conveyed unto me. It is from Solomon, and
behold it saith: In the name of God, the Merciful, the Gracious. Resist me not
but come unto me with submission.*

Then she cut the quotation off at exactly the point where it would have gone
against her. In the Qur'an the queen's next words are to her counsellors —
*advise me, and nothing will I determine until you have spoken.* Arwa said: *I
do not say, touching the command of our lord, O counsellors advise me, and
nothing will I determine until you shall have spoken.*

She had cast the caliph as Solomon and herself as Bilkis, a queen of Yemen
receiving a summons from a greater king, and then declined the one part of the
role that involved being talked into it.

And she named the men who had done this to her. As for you, Ibn al-Isfahani —
by God, you have not carried a true message to our lord from Saba. You have
wrested words from their sense, and your souls have prompted you to an act of
wickedness. My recourse is patience, and God is He whose help is to be implored
against what you have set forth.

## What actually happened

Her vizier and her officers then talked to her in pacifying terms until she
consented, which is a sentence worth sitting with: the queen who had just
refused to consult her counsellors was brought round by her counsellors.

A contract was drawn up. Saba came to Jibla with a great retinue and stayed a
month, feasting his army and spending on it as much again as the dowry had cost
him. And in the course of that month, Umara says, the contemplation of her aims
and her actions made him feel small. He saw that his own reputation was dimmed
beside hers, and that nobody could fairly be compared with her. Her people, he
noticed, all called their Lady their Mistress.

In the end he sent her a private request: receive me in your palace, so that
people will believe the marriage was consummated. She agreed.

What happened that night has two versions at Jibla. Some said she received him
in her own apartments for one night and he left at first light. Others said she
sent him a slave girl who looked very like her, that he was warned of it, and
that the girl stood at the head of his couch all night while he sat without once
raising his eyes to her. At dawn he said his prayers, ordered the drums beaten
for departure, and told the girl to take a message to her mistress: *she is a
precious pearl, to be worn only by whoever is worthy of her.*

They never met again.
