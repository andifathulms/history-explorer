---
title: Fifty years, from Jibla
drafted_from: daftary-2007

Arwa al-Sulayhi governed Yemen for about half a century, in her own name, and
is the first woman to appear in a ruler field anywhere on this site.

That is worth stating plainly rather than as a curiosity, because the corpus
has been consistent up to now: sixty-odd polities, three ruler slots each, and
every one of them a man. Some of that is the period and some of it is the
sources, which record women as mothers and regents where they record them at
all. Arwa is not an exception the sources had to be squeezed to produce. She
took the government in 1086, moved the capital in 1087 from Sana'a — exposed,
and contested by the Zaydi imams to the north and the Najahids on the coast —
to Jibla in the southern highlands, and ruled from there until she died in
1138.

She also held religious office, which is the rarer thing. The Fatimids
recognised her as *hujja*, the senior rank of the mission in Yemen, which no
other woman held in the Isma'ili hierarchy. Political authority delegated to a
queen is unusual in this corpus; religious authority is close to unique.

It mattered when Cairo broke. This site's Fatimid page records the disputed
succession of 1094, in which a vizier put his own candidate on the throne over
the elder son, and the mission split; the eastern network went to Alamut under
Hasan-i Sabbah and became the Nizari state on this site's Iranian Intermezzo
page. Yemen went the other way, with al-Musta'li. When the Musta'li line in
turn produced a disputed succession in 1130, Arwa recognised the infant Tayyib
as imam, declared him in concealment, and reconstituted the mission in Yemen
under her own authority as a hierarchy that no longer answered to Cairo at all.

The consequence outlived her state by a long way. The mission she directed sent
missionaries east to Gujarat, and the Tayyibi communities of western India
descend from that. The Sulayhid state fragmented at her death in 1138 with no
successor, having lasted ninety-one years; the religious organisation she
detached from Cairo has lasted nine hundred.
