Egypt had spent the last years of Assyria propping it up. Necho II marched north
in 609 to help Ashur-uballit hold Harran — the campaign on which, according to
the biblical account, he killed Josiah of Judah at Megiddo on the way — and when
Assyria failed he simply stayed, holding the Levant as far as the Euphrates for
four years. The question of who had inherited Assyria's western provinces was
therefore open, and it was settled in one afternoon.

In the summer of 605 the Babylonian crown prince Nebuchadnezzar attacked the
Egyptian army in its position at Carchemish on the upper Euphrates, broke it
inside the city, and pursued the survivors to Hamath, where he destroyed what
was left. The Babylonian Chronicle's language is unusually direct: not a single
man returned to his own country.

## Three weeks

What happened next is the reason this is a turning point on the record rather
than a battle in a chapter. Nebuchadnezzar was still campaigning in Syria when
word reached him that his father had died in Babylon on the eighth of Ab. He
handed the army and the prisoners to his officers, took a small escort, and
crossed the desert direct — the Chronicle gives twenty-three days from
Nabopolassar's death to his own accession.

That is a detail about roads and horses, and it is also the whole of the
succession problem in the ancient Near East compressed into three weeks. A
victorious crown prince eight hundred kilometres from the capital was, in most
of this corpus's other records, the beginning of a civil war. Here it was an
administrative event. He was in Babylon before anyone could organise an
alternative, and he reigned forty-three years.

## What it settled

Egypt did not hold territory in Asia again. Necho tried once more in 601 and
fought Nebuchadnezzar to a bloody draw on the Egyptian frontier — the Chronicle
admits heavy Babylonian losses, which is not the kind of thing it usually admits
— and after that the border was the border. Judah, Tyre, Sidon, Damascus and the
Philistine cities changed from Egyptian clients to Babylonian ones.

For this site's purposes the significance is narrower and worth stating plainly:
the Levant that the Assyrian record spent two hundred years and this corpus's
most elaborate deportation machinery acquiring was transferred entire, in a
single campaigning season, to a state that had existed for twenty-one years.
