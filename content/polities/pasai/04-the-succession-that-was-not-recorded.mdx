This record's `last` ruler is null and its `succession_rule` is null, and the two
nulls are related.

Pasai's fifteenth century was violent. The sultanate changed hands repeatedly,
factions of the port aristocracy backed rival claimants, and by the time the
Portuguese arrived in 1509 they found a place that had been through several
disputed accessions and gave conflicting reports about who was in charge.

That is roughly what this corpus's Melaka record says about its own last decades,
and this corpus's Aceh record says about the sultanate that eventually took
Pasai's coast. It is the ordinary condition of a Malay port sultanate: the
merchant aristocracy is the thing that persists and the dynasty is the thing that
is contested.

## What the Portuguese did with it

They took Pasai in 1521 by intervening in one of those disputes on one side,
which is how European powers took most of what they took in the archipelago for
the next century.

This corpus's Sunda record's fourth chapter has the same shape from the other
direction — a kingdom inviting the Portuguese in as protection against its
neighbours in 1522 and losing its port to those neighbours five years later. This
corpus's Banten record ends with a son taking the throne from his father with
Dutch company troops. This corpus's Makassar record ends with a company fighting
alongside a Bugis prince whose people Makassar had conquered.

Four records across the archipelago, four European interventions, and in every
one of them the European was invited by a party to a local quarrel.

That is not an argument that the outcomes were anybody's fault but the people who
delivered them. It is an argument about what a chronology organised around
European arrival gets wrong: on these four records, the arrival is not the event.
The quarrel is, and the arrival is what one side did about it.

## The empty slot

`rulers.last` on this record is null. Six records across this site's three
archipelago regions carry a null in one of the three ruler slots, and this one falls in a specific place: on the
ruler of a polity that was taken by a European power in a documented year.

This site's position on a null in a ruler slot is that a name entered with an
invented reign reads on the page exactly like a sourced one. It holds here. But
it is worth saying plainly that the reason this particular name is missing is not
that Pasai was obscure in 1521 — it is that the people who wrote about Pasai in
1521 were more interested in the harbour than in the man.
