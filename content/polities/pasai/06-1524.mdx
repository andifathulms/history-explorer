The Portuguese took Pasai in 1521, ten years after they took Melaka. Ali
Mughayat Syah took it from them in 1524 and absorbed its territory into the
sultanate he was assembling forty miles up the same coast.

<Figure id="ali-mughayat" />

The oldest Muslim throne in Southeast Asia became a district of a state that
was not yet three decades old.

## What Aceh was made of

Aceh was assembled out of two things, and Pasai is one of them.

The first arrived in 1511, when Albuquerque took Melaka and the merchants,
shipping and Malay court culture that would not trade under a Portuguese flag
went north. That gave the new sultanate a commercial position and a class of
people who knew how to work it.

The second was this coast: the harbours, the pepper hinterland behind them, and
the two and a half centuries of standing that Pasai had accumulated as the
place where Islam had first held a throne. Aceh took the lot in a single
campaign against a European garrison that had been there three years.

Within a century Aceh was calling itself the veranda of Mecca and keeping a
court full of scholars. A good deal of what made that credible had been built
at Pasai before Aceh existed.

## What it did not take

It never claimed to be Pasai.

Aceh's sultans traced their own line and built their own legitimacy out of
their own court — the scholars, the pilgrimage traffic, the embassy to
Istanbul. They had Pasai's coast, Pasai's harbours and Pasai's prestige, and
they never once said they were its continuation.

That is worth noticing because it was available to them and it is what most
dynasties in that position do. Melaka's kings had routed their genealogy back
through Palembang to Srivijaya on far thinner grounds. Aceh had a real
inheritance to claim and did not bother, which tells you something about how
confident the new sultanate was: it did not need a predecessor. It had pepper,
a fleet, and the strait.
