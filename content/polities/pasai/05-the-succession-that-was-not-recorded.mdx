title: The succession that was not recorded

Nobody knows who the last sultan of Pasai was.

Pasai's fifteenth century was violent. The sultanate changed hands repeatedly,
factions of the port aristocracy backed rival claimants, and by the time the
Portuguese arrived in 1509 they found a place that had been through several
disputed accessions and that gave them conflicting reports about who was in
charge.

That is the ordinary condition of a Malay port sultanate, and Melaka and Aceh
both describe their own last decades in much the same terms. The merchant
aristocracy is the thing that persists; the dynasty is the thing that is
contested.

## What the Portuguese did with it

They took Pasai in 1521 by intervening in one of those disputes on one side,
which is how European powers took most of what they took in the archipelago for
the next century.

That is not an argument that the outcome was anybody's fault but the people who
delivered it. It is an argument about what a chronology organised around
European arrival gets wrong at Pasai specifically. The Portuguese did not
arrive to a stable throne and break it; they arrived to a throne several
factions were already fighting over, and backed the side that let them keep
the harbour. The quarrel was the event. The arrival was what one side did about
it.

## The name nobody wrote down

It is worth being plain about why the last sultan's name is missing. It is not
that Pasai was obscure in 1521. Portuguese officials were standing in the
harbour writing reports.

It is that the people who wrote about Pasai in 1521 were far more interested in
the harbour than in the man who was supposed to be ruling it — and a name
entered now, from inference, would look on the page exactly like a name
somebody had actually recorded.
