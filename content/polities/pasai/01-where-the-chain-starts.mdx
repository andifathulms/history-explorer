Seven records in this region carry `religions_carried: 1` for Sunni Islam, and
this one is upstream of the other six.

Melaka's is for the conversion of the eastern archipelago — the ports of Java's
north coast, Brunei, Sulu, the Moluccas, the southern Philippines — spread by
merchants, marriages and the prestige of the region's dominant court. Ternate's
is for the far eastern reach of that, out along Halmahera and the Sula islands;
Tidore's for its own dependencies beyond them, the Raja Ampat and the coasts of
western New Guinea. Aceh's is for the Sumatran highlands and for being the
archipelago's point of embarkation for the pilgrimage. Demak's is for taking
Islam inland across Java by force after 1527, and Makassar's for the campaigns
that carried it through South Sulawesi between 1608 and 1611.

And Melaka's own conversion, around 1414, came from a marriage to a princess of
Pasai.

Pasai is where Islam first held a throne in Southeast Asia.

Merah Silu, who took the name Malik al-Salih, is placed here around 1267. His
gravestone is dated 1297 and is the earliest dated Muslim royal gravestone in the
region — a piece of imported Gujarati marble on the north Sumatran coast, with a
sultan's name on it in Arabic. Marco Polo, passing in 1292, noted that the towns
of this coast had converted and the interior had not.

## What "first" means and what it does not

Islam had been in these waters for centuries before 1267. Arab and Persian
shipping used this route from the eighth century; there were Muslim merchant
communities in the ports long before any of them had a Muslim ruler.

What changed at Pasai is that a state adopted it. Coding rule 2 does not count
adoption — this record's count of one is for propagation past a boundary, and the
boundary it crossed was into Melaka in 1414. But the adoption is what made the
propagation possible, because what a port's merchants do is trade and what a
court can do is marry its daughters into other courts.

The seven records and their seven counts are one movement seen in seven places: a
throne on this coast in 1267, a marriage across the strait in 1414, ports
converting in Melaka's wake across the eastern archipelago, the two clove islands
carrying it to their own separate dependencies, an army going inland from the
Javanese north coast after 1527, campaigns through South Sulawesi from 1608, and
the Sumatran highlands behind Aceh.

Each crossed a different boundary, which is why each counts separately and none
of them double-counts the others — a claim the Tidore record has to defend
explicitly, because its boundary and Ternate's are two kilometres apart.

It is the densest run of one religion in one region anywhere on this site, and it
was assembled backwards: this page, the head of it, was among the last written.
