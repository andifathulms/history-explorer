The Portuguese took Pasai in 1521, ten years after Melaka. Ali Mughayat Syah took
it from them in 1524 and absorbed its territory into the sultanate he was
assembling forty miles up the same coast.

`ended.by` names Aceh, and the edge `pasai → aceh` is drawn.

That is the third `ended.by` filled on this site's archipelago pages in two
passes, and the only one of the three filled by a polity that already had a page
here. Singhasari's
waited for the Kediri records to be written; Majapahit's waited for Demak. This
one was empty for a different reason — the record it needed was the one that
did not exist, and the polity was on the site all along.

## Aceh has two incoming edges now

They are the two things it was assembled out of.

`melaka → aceh`, `absorbed remnants of`, 1511: the merchants, the shipping and
the Malay court culture that would not trade under a Portuguese flag, which went
north after Albuquerque took the city.

`pasai → aceh`, `conquered by`, 1524: the coast itself, and the sultanate that
had held it for two and a half centuries.

The Aceh record's first chapter says the northern tip of Sumatra was a cluster of
small ports before 1511 and that Pasai was the important one. Both halves of that
sentence now have pages, and the sequence they describe is legible on the
succession graph rather than only in prose.

## The count that stays at zero

`successor_claims` on this record is zero, and the distinction is the same one
this site drew when it typed the Melaka edge.

Aceh took Pasai's territory, its position, its coast and its pepper hinterland,
and did not claim to be it. Aceh's sultans traced their own line and built their
own legitimacy — the scholars, the pilgrimage, the veranda of Mecca — out of
their own court rather than out of this one's. Taking what a polity had is not
asserting that you are its continuation, and rule 1 asks for the assertion.

It is the third time on this site's archipelago pages that an edge has turned on
that distinction,
and the third time the looser reading would have drawn a tidier picture of
something that did not happen.
