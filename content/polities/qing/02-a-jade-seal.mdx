Nurhaci's one real defeat came at the very end of his life, when a Chinese
general named Yuan Chonghuan held the fortress of Ningyuan against him with
European cannon the Manchus had no answer for. It was the first and only
time in his career that Nurhaci had to call off an assault and raise a
siege, and he retired to his capital sick with disappointed pride and died
there in September 1626, not quite sixty-eight. His son Hong Taiji inherited
a confederacy still without a single Chinese province to its name.

## Organising for the long war

Hong Taiji's first contribution was structural rather than territorial: he
divided the Manchu population into the Eight Banners, hereditary military
corps each with its own leader, flag and trumpeter, converting a coalition
of clan followings into something closer to a standing national army. Chinese
deserters who came over to his side were organised the same way, under their
own banner, though Boulger is careful to note the arrangement was one of
military convenience rather than any claim to equal national standing.

Campaign followed campaign through the 1630s — into Chahar Mongol territory,
into Shanxi, wherever Ming defences proved weakest — and each one Hong Taiji
fought less for outright conquest than for plunder, prestige and the steady
attrition of Ming authority beyond the fortress line at Ningyuan and
Shanhaiguan, which continued to hold against him as it had against his
father.

## An accident with a seal

It was an accident of recovered property, more than any battle, that gave
Hong Taiji his opening to claim the Chinese throne outright. The jade seal
of the fallen Yuan dynasty, lost in the Mongolian wilds when the Mongols were
expelled from China two and a half centuries earlier, had been found by a
shepherd, passed from hand to hand among Mongol chiefs, and eventually
reached Hong Taiji. Its arrival was treated, among the Mongol clans, as
something close to a sign: forty-nine separate chiefs hastened to pay him
allegiance once it became known he held it, and — as though ratifying their
own submission — they demanded that the King of Korea do the same. Korea's
king tried refusing even to open the letters informing him of the new
arrangement; a Manchu army settled the question of his independence at the
point of the sword.

## Emperor of China, on paper

At the close of the 1635 campaign, encouraged by this cascade of submissions
he had done comparatively little fighting to earn, Hong Taiji assumed for
the first time the style of Emperor of China. It was a claim with almost
nothing behind it yet in Chinese territory — Ningyuan and Shanhaiguan still
barred the road to Beijing, and would go on barring it for another nine
years — but it converted the House Nurhaci had founded from a frontier
confederacy into a rival court, waiting only for the Ming to hand it the
opening its own arms had not yet been able to force.
