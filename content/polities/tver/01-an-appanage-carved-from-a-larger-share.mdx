Tver did not begin as anyone's deliberate creation. It emerged from the
same process that had already broken Vsevolod III's own undivided
territory into five shares under his sons and then into a dozen smaller
fragments under his grandsons - in Tver's own case, cut from the larger
appanage of Pereiaslavl, itself one of the five original divisions. No
source dates the moment precisely. What can be dated is the point at
which a named Prince of Tver first appears acting as an independent
power in his own right, negotiating on equal terms with one of the most
formidable free cities in the whole of Rus.

## A treaty that names Tver among the powers that mattered

In 1270, Novgorod concluded a treaty with Prince Yaroslav of Tver that
placed real, specific limits on how their two states would treat each
other's dependent populations - among other things, guaranteeing that a
master's own kholop, a bonded laborer, could not have his word against
that master credited without corroboration. A further treaty in 1308,
this time with Prince Michael of Tver, went still further, arranging for
the extradition of runaway laborers who fled across the border between
the two states in either direction. Treaties of this specificity were not
the kind of document one power extends to an appanage it can safely
ignore. Within two generations of its own likely founding, Tver was
already negotiating with Novgorod as an equal, on questions detailed
enough to matter to both sides' own economies.

## A rival built on the same stock as its future conqueror

Tver's princes descended, as Moscow's own eventually would, from
Vsevolod III through his son Yaroslav - making the two lines, whatever
their later rivalry, close cousins within the same broken-up inheritance.
What set Tver apart in its own early generations was not any special
claim of blood, since Moscow could make an equally valid one, but a
head start: an appanage established and negotiating as a serious regional
power while Moscow's own share of the family inheritance was still, by
most accounts, one of the least significant in all of Northern Rus.
