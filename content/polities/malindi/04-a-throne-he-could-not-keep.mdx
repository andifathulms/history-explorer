Yusuf, Ahmad's son, was sent to Goa to be raised after his father's
murder and baptised there as Dom Geronimo Chingulia. Around 1630 the
Portuguese made him Sultan of Mombasa in his father's place - a
Christian ruler, educated and shaped in the colonial capital itself,
who should by every intention behind his upbringing have been the
most reliable client Portugal had ever installed on the coast.

He was not. Subject, like his father, to the routine indignities of
the Portuguese captain stationed over him, Yusuf secretly kept
Muhammadan rites at his murdered father's grave - and when a
Portuguese observer saw him at it and reported him to the governor as
a relapsed Muslim, the governor's threat to have him deported to Goa
reached Yusuf's own ears before it could be carried out.

## The massacre and the fire

Yusuf did not wait to be deported. Taking three hundred followers on
the pretence of a courtesy visit, he entered the fort, killed the
governor Pedro Leytam de Gamboa with his own hand, and watched his men
massacre the rest of the Portuguese garrison. A handful who reached
the sanctuary of St. Augustine's convent held out seven days before
Yusuf promised them their lives if they surrendered - and shot them
down, men, women and children, the moment the gates opened. He then
publicly renounced Christianity.

Portugal answered with everything it could spare: a flotilla of five
hundred men from Goa, reinforced at Mombasa to eight hundred, besieged
the fort for three months in 1632 without retaking it. But Yusuf knew
what the siege's failure had bought him was time, not safety. He
loaded his cannon and goods onto two captured Portuguese ships, cut
down the town's fruit trees, destroyed what he could not carry, and
sailed for Arabia rather than wait for the fleet Portugal would
certainly send next. A Portuguese captain returning from Zanzibar
found Mombasa deserted and simply occupied it. Malindi's century of
loyalty, and the throne it had won at Mombasa, ended in the same fire
the family that had inherited both had set with its own hands.
