title: The frontier that held

Everything on this site to Delhi's west was destroyed by the Mongols. Khwarazm
went in 1220–1231, Baghdad in 1258. Delhi did not.

Mongol forces raided the Punjab repeatedly from the 1220s and mounted serious
invasions in the 1290s and early 1300s, several reaching Delhi itself. Ala al-Din
Khalji beat them. The reasons were partly structural — the Indus was a real
barrier, the Punjab was hard country to cross with large cavalry armies, and the
Chagatai khanate that faced India was the poorest and most divided of the
successor states — and partly that Ala al-Din built a large standing army and
paid for it by fixing market prices in Delhi and taxing agrarian produce at
punitive rates.

The consequence was that northern India became the refuge of the Persianate
world. Scholars, administrators, poets and Sufi shaykhs from Khurasan and
Transoxiana arrived as refugees across three generations, and the Persian
literary and administrative culture that the Mongols had disrupted at its source
continued at Delhi without a break. The polity that ends this site's Iranian
thread by inheriting it is not in Iran.
