---
title: The slave kings
drafted_from: jackson-1999
phase: formation
---

Qutb al-Din Aibak was a Turkic slave bought in Nishapur, sold on, and risen to
command the Indian campaigns of Muhammad of Ghur. When Muhammad was murdered in
1206 without a son, Aibak took the Indian provinces and ruled them from Lahore
and then Delhi as an independent sultan.

The pattern was two and a half centuries old by then. Alptigin was a Samanid
slave-general who took Ghazna and founded the line that became the Ghurids'
overlords; here a Ghurid slave does to the Ghurids exactly what a Samanid slave
had done to the Samanids. The institution of military
slavery was designed to give rulers soldiers with no local loyalties, and it
worked so well that the soldiers repeatedly became the rulers.

Aibak's successor Iltutmish, also a slave and also Aibak's son-in-law,
consolidated the sultanate, took an investiture diploma from the Abbasid caliph
in 1229 — an act with no practical content and considerable value, since the
caliph could not have withheld anything he wanted — and left a state rather than
a conquest.
