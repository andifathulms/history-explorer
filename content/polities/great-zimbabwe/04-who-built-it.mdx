For most of a century the question of who built Great Zimbabwe was answered by
people who had decided the answer in advance.

## The sequence

Karl Mauch reached the site in 1871 and concluded it was built by Phoenicians —
the Queen of Sheba's palace, on the reasoning that a beam of cedar he took to be
Lebanese could not be local. It was local sandalwood.

Cecil Rhodes's British South Africa Company funded excavation by Richard Hall in
1902. Hall cleared the deposits down to bedrock across large areas of the site,
searching for the ancient foreign layer and destroying the stratigraphy that
would have dated what was actually there. He published his conclusion that it was
Phoenician or Sabaean.

David Randall-MacIver excavated in 1905 and said it was African and medieval.
Gertrude Caton-Thompson excavated in 1929, using a stratigraphic method Hall had
not used and which could no longer be used in the areas he had cleared, and said
the same: African, medieval, built by the ancestors of the people living there.

She was right, and the professional argument was substantially over from that
point. The political one was not.

## What was done with it afterwards

The Rhodesian government of the 1960s and 1970s required that official guides,
museum displays and publications present the site's origins as unproven or
foreign. Archaeologists working in the country were placed under pressure not to
publish the African attribution. Peter Garlake, the country's senior inspector of
monuments, left the post and the country over it.

The state that did this took its name — Rhodesia — from Rhodes, whose company had
funded the excavation that destroyed the evidence. The state that replaced it in
1980 took its name from the site.

## A familiar problem with an unusual motive

Plenty of these kingdoms are known mainly through accounts written by people with
a stake in them. Songhai is known largely from two chronicles written by the
scholarly families its founder had persecuted. Almost everything about the first
Bulgarian empire's opening two centuries was written by Byzantines. Ghana's
destruction by the Almoravids, a conquest that probably never happened, survived
for a century on the strength of being a tidy story.

In each of those the interested party was a rival or a successor, and the
distortion was old. Here it was a modern colonial state that needed the ruins on
its territory not to have been built by the people it was governing, and it had
the archaeology, the museum labels and the civil service to say so with.

## What it cost

Hall's clearance removed the stratified deposits from large parts of the site, so
the dating is coarser and the sequence thinner than it should be. Whatever those
layers held about how the place was organised, fed and governed is simply gone.

We know Great Zimbabwe lived on gold and on the trade that carried it, because
the workings and the imports survive being dug out badly. How it raised its
authority, how it fed itself, and how power passed from one holder to the next
are blank — and some part of them might not have been, if the first serious
excavation had been looking for what was there rather than for who else could
have put it there.
