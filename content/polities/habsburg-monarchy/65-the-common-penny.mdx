Maximilian expected the states' own gratitude for his concessions to
translate into the generous war funding he actually needed, enough to
let him reassert imperial power in Italy the way earlier emperors
once had. Instead the diet, after long hesitation and repeated
delays, voted a standing army grandly described as "sufficient to
check the progress of the French" - and funded it with just a
hundred fifty thousand florins, a third from the Habsburgs' own
hereditary lands and the rest structured as a loan, to be raised over
four years through a new levy called the common penny, a small tax on
the value of land and personal property. Even that modest sum came
wrapped in conditions: Maximilian had to give his royal word not to
extend the tax past its stated term, and to let seven treasurers based
at Frankfurt handle its collection and distribution, only one of them
his own appointee, with an account submitted to the diet every year.
An equal sum was voted, under the same restrictive terms, to fund the
new public peace and Imperial Chamber Court.

## Money that never came

The tax was structured with real precision - one florin per thousand
on land and moveable property, proportionally more from princes,
prelates and towns, a flat capitation on Jewish subjects regardless of
wealth - but precision in the law did not translate into money in the
treasury. Suabia and Franconia's own states were among many who simply
withheld or delayed payment, and after repeated new ordinances from
successive diets trying to force compliance, barely half the promised
sum was ever actually collected - and even that arrived so slowly that
Charles VIII could have conquered the whole of Italy before German
gold reached anyone capable of raising troops with it. Rather than
leading nine thousand men into Italy as he had promised, Maximilian
could scrape together only three thousand to send - a fraction of
what the diet had voted, and nowhere near enough to let him take
personal command of the war he had spent so much political capital
convening the diet to fight.
