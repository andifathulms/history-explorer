The Treaty of Senlis had left one piece of business deliberately
unresolved: France was to hold Burgundy itself until its rival claims
were settled by negotiation or law, while returning three named towns
once Philip, Maximilian's own son, turned twenty. Philip, reaching
that age, demanded not just the towns but every territory France had
carved from the old Burgundian inheritance - and Charles VIII simply
stalled, offering excuse after excuse, until the whole dispute was
still unresolved at his own death in April 1498. Charles left only a
daughter, so the throne passed to the Duke of Orléans, who took the
name Louis XII and, divorcing his own wife, married the same Anne of
Brittany who had once been engaged to Maximilian himself.

## A war that settled nothing by force

Philip's renewed appeals to the new king went no better than before,
and Maximilian, backed by a diet willing to send a diplomatic
deputation but not real troops, dispatched a mixed force of Austrian
soldiers and Swiss mercenaries into Burgundy under William de Vergy.
Early gains evaporated once the Swiss contingent either mutinied over
unpaid wages or was simply bought off by the enemy, and the Austrians
were pushed back into Franche-Comté. Louis XII, whose own ambitions
had already turned toward conquering Milan and had no interest in a
prolonged Burgundian distraction, offered peace on terms Philip
accepted readily: the three disputed towns were restored, and any
further claim to the duchy of Burgundy itself was effectively
abandoned by referring the whole question to the Parliament of Paris
- a body under French control that was never going to rule against its
own king. Philip, as Count of Flanders, even formally invested Louis
with Boulogne in exchange for his own investiture over Artois,
Charolais and Flanders. Maximilian, deeply unhappy with a settlement
he had no real power to block once the empire withheld its own
support, had no choice but to accept it and turn his attention
elsewhere - toward troubles in Guelderland, Friesland and Switzerland
that France was quietly stoking against him.
