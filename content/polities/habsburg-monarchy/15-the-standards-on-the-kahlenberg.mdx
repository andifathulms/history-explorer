Leopold I's own reign settled into a long, grinding contest over
Hungary rather than the set-piece wars that had defined his
predecessors. A Protestant and noble rebellion under Imre Thököly,
fed by Leopold's own broken promises of religious toleration and
backed by Ottoman support, controlled Upper Hungary by 1682 and drew
the sultan directly into the conflict the following year: Grand Vizier
Kara Mustafa marched on Vienna with an army Coxe puts at two hundred
thousand men, while Leopold's own court could barely muster forty
thousand to meet it. When the Duke of Lorraine's outnumbered forces
fell back on the capital, they found a city already emptying -
Leopold and his court had fled the night before amid what Coxe records
as open curses against the ministers and Jesuits blamed for provoking
a war the population had been left undefended against.

## A city that emptied and a city that held

What saved Vienna was not the emperor's own presence but Lorraine's
preparations and the defense mounted by the city's governor, Rüdiger
von Starhemberg: suburbs demolished for clear fields of fire, walls
hastily repaired, citizens and students drilled alongside the garrison.
The Ottoman siege opened on 14 July 1683 and ground on for two months
while Vienna's own defenders, reduced by disease and constant assault,
watched the outer works fall one by one with no relief in sight -
German reinforcements slow to muster, the Polish army still gathering
on the Silesian frontier. Leopold himself, reduced to pleading rather
than commanding, wrote directly to King John Sobieski of Poland to
march ahead of his own main force: "however inferior in number, your
name alone, so terrible to the enemy, will ensure a victory."

## The twelfth of September

Sobieski answered by force-marching a vanguard of three thousand
cavalry across Silesia and Moravia before his own full army had
assembled, and on 12 September the relief force appeared on the
heights of the Kahlenberg above the city - the sight of Christian
standards on the hillside reviving a garrison that had all but given
up hope of rescue. The combined imperial and Polish attack broke the
already-exhausted besiegers within hours; the grand vizier abandoned
his own camp in the night rather than risk annihilation, leaving
behind artillery, provisions and a standard believed at the time to be
the Prophet's own banner. Sobieski, writing to his own queen with the
plunder still being catalogued, joked that "the grand vizier has left
me his heir, and I inherit millions of ducats."

## Two very different entrances

Vienna's own gratitude fell entirely on the Polish king. He entered
the city the next morning to crowds that hailed him as father and
deliverer, struggling to touch his horse or his garment; Leopold's own
return three days later, by contrast, drew no cheers at all - he
walked to the cathedral on foot, carrying a candle, in what Coxe reads
as genuine humility rather than performance, aware the city's
gratitude belonged to someone else. The military campaign that
followed the relief pushed the Ottomans out of most of Hungary within
a decade and a half: Leopold's own diet at Pressburg declared the
Hungarian crown hereditary in the House of Austria in 1687, ending the
elective kingship that had shaped two centuries of contested Habsburg
succession there, and Prince Eugene of Savoy's decisive victory at
Zenta in 1697 forced the sultan to the negotiating table. The Peace of
Karlowitz, signed in 1699, confirmed nearly all of Hungary and
Transylvania under Habsburg rule for the first time since the disaster
at Mohács a century and three-quarters before - closing, in Leopold's
own reign, a contest that had outlasted seven Habsburg rulers before
him.
