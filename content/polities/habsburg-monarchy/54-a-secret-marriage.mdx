While Maximilian fought for Austria and Hungary, a second inheritance
dispute was unfolding in Brittany that would test his family's
fortunes just as severely. Duke Francis II died in September 1488
leaving only two daughters, with thirteen-year-old Anne inheriting a
duchy immediately torn between pro-French and pro-English factions and
surrounded by counselors of doubtful loyalty. Like Mary of Burgundy
before her, Anne became the object of intense competition: Alain
d'Albret, rough-mannered and too old for her taste despite his real
service to her late father; the imprisoned Duke of Orléans, whom she
genuinely favored but who was already married to the French king's own
sister and in no position to seek a divorce; and Maximilian himself,
whose interest in the match was as much about checking French power as
securing the duchy outright.

## A wedding announced too late

Maximilian had opened negotiations even before Francis's death, working
through the same Prince of Orange who had once brokered his own match
with Mary, and pursued the courtship in careful secrecy to avoid
provoking French intervention before the deal was sealed. Anne, won
over as much by Maximilian's own reputation as by any of his rivals'
qualities, agreed to set aside her feelings for Orléans and accept a
prince whose rank and alliances seemed capable of actually protecting
her duchy. Historians have never agreed on the exact date of the
marriage - it was solemnized by proxy, without Maximilian himself
present, sometime around the start of 1490 - but the secrecy that
protected the negotiation could not protect what came next: France
had wanted Brittany for generations, and the regency government of
Anne of Beaujeu had already spent years working to isolate the duchy
from outside help before this marriage gave her the perfect pretext
to finish the job.
