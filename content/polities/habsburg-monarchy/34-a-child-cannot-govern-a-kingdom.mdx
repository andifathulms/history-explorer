Bohemia handled the same problem differently, and with a Habsburg
twist of its own. Elizabeth had claimed the Bohemian crown for her
infant son immediately after his birth, but the Calixtin party balked:
their leader argued plainly that "a child cannot govern a kingdom,"
and proposed electing an adult ruler in the meantime, promising to
reconsider Ladislaus's own claim once he turned twenty-four. A
sixteen-member committee first offered the crown to Albert, Duke of
Bavaria, who declined it outright as belonging to Ladislaus by right;
the estates then turned to Frederick himself, who answered with the
same principle in almost the same words - "I will not rob my relation
of his right" - and refused even the regency, recommending instead
that the Bohemians govern themselves under a native regent while he
personally supervised the boy's education.

## Two regents, one civil war

The Bohemians took that advice literally, naming Ladislaus king in
absentia and appointing two co-regents - one Calixtin, one Catholic -
who agreed on almost nothing and dragged the kingdom back toward the
factional violence of the Hussite wars. The Calixtins even offered
the regency to the same Empress Barbara who had once schemed against
Albert's own succession, only to have the diet reject her demands as
too extravagant; when the Catholic regent was finally driven out, sole
power passed to the Calixtin faction and, after further infighting
among the Calixtins themselves, to a rising figure named George of
Poděbrady. Poděbrady tried in 1447 to have the whole succession
reopened, asking a diet whether Bohemia might not be better served by
"a monarch acquainted with our language and customs" than by an
absent infant - a proposal the Catholic party defeated, though the
attempt cost Bohemia several more years of internal conflict before
Poděbrady finally crushed his Catholic rival outright in 1450 and
governed, in practice, as king in everything but name.

## An unwelcome guardian

Hungary, meanwhile, was handed back to Ladislaus by circumstance
rather than negotiation: his Polish rival died fighting the Ottomans
at the Battle of Varna in 1444, and the Hungarian diet promptly
recognized the infant king again, demanding his person and the sacred
crown just as the Bohemians had. Frederick refused both requests,
leaving Hungary to be governed instead by Hunyadi as regent - a
soldier who went on to survive a Turkish rout, capture, and escape by
his own hands (he felled two captors squabbling over his gold cross
before they could decide who owned him) while continuing to hold the
Ottoman frontier alone. All three of Ladislaus's kingdoms, by the late
1440s, wanted the same thing Frederick refused to give them: the
actual presence of their own king, still held at Wiener Neustadt under
a guardian increasingly unwilling to let him go.
