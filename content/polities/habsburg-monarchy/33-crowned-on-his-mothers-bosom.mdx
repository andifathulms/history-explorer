Albert V died leaving a pregnant widow and three thrones with three
different rules for succession: Austria passed only through male
heirs, while Hungary and Bohemia were both elective kingdoms whose own
estates could, in principle, choose anyone. Elizabeth asked all three
to wait for her delivery before settling anything, and Austria and
Bohemia agreed - Austria naming Frederick, Duke of Styria, as
guardian should a son be born. Hungary's nobles, dreading a long
regency with the Ottomans pressing their southern frontier, pushed
Elizabeth instead toward marrying the King of Poland and handing him
the crown outright, with only a promise that her own unborn child,
if male, would still be helped to Austria and Bohemia later. The
ambassadors carrying that offer had barely left Hungary when Elizabeth
gave birth to a son, baptized Ladislaus and known ever after as
Ladislaus Posthumus - and immediately tried to recall the embassy. She
was too late: the Polish king had already accepted, and marched into
Hungary at the head of an army before her infant son was four months
old.

## A four-month-old king

Elizabeth's own answer was audacious: in May 1440 she carried the
infant Ladislaus to Székesfehérvár, where the Archbishop of Esztergom
crowned him king of Hungary on his own mother's chest. Hungary split
into two camps - one behind the adult Polish claimant and his ablest
general, John Hunyadi, who argued a grown warrior-king was what a
country facing Ottoman invasion actually needed; the other loyal to
the infant and his widowed mother, strong enough to hold Upper Hungary
under a defiant commander named Jan Jiskra but too weak to dislodge
Hunyadi's party from the rest of the kingdom. Elizabeth retreated to
Vienna, placed her son under Frederick's formal guardianship, and
mortgaged both a border city and the sacred Crown of St. Stephen
itself - an object Hungarians considered essential to any king's own
legitimacy - to secure his support.

## A queen's suspicious death

A peace brokered by the Pope nearly resolved the standoff: the Polish
king would rule as regent during Ladislaus's minority and marry
Albert's elder daughter, succeeding outright only if the boy died
without heirs. The agreement never took effect. Elizabeth died at
Győr on 24 December 1442, "not without suspicions of poison" in Coxe's
own words, and the Polish king simply assumed full royal title and
authority in the vacuum her death left behind. Frederick, now
guardian to an infant king with no mother to protect his claim any
longer, took up arms on Ladislaus's behalf and kept the civil war
burning for years before exhaustion on both sides - and a renewed
Ottoman threat - finally forced a three-year truce that let the rival
king turn his own attention to the Turks instead.
