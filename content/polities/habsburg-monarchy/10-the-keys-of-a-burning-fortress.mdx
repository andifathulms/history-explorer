John Sigismund's continued raiding gave Maximilian his opening in
Hungary: an Austrian force under General Swendy overran Transylvanian
positions through the winter of 1565, and when Maximilian offered to
resume his father's own tribute in exchange for the existing truce,
an aging Süleyman chose provocation instead, mustering for one final
campaign. He reached Belgrade in the spring of 1566, received John
Sigismund with the same regal honours he had shown him before, and
declared that despite his own failing health he had taken the field
again to "chastise the house of Austria, or perish under the walls of
Vienna."

## Count Zrínyi's sally

Süleyman's march up the Danube turned aside when the garrison of a
small marsh fortress called Szigetvár killed one of his own favourite
pashas in a raid. Count Miklós Zrínyi held the town with barely
fifteen hundred men, and for thirty-four days matched the endurance
Güns had shown a generation before - the Turks forced across the
surrounding marsh on causeways of their own construction, raising
earthworks for their batteries, launching assault after assault.
Zrínyi's garrison, reduced at last to six hundred men and an inner
fort already burning around them, chose a final sortie over surrender:
Zrínyi took the keys of the fortress, armed himself with his own
ancestral sabre, and led what remained of his men out to die fighting
rather than be overrun. Almost none survived it. The Turks took the
ruin at a cost Coxe puts at twenty thousand men - and Süleyman himself
did not live to see the victory, dying on 4 September 1566 of fatigue
and the marsh's own foul air, his death concealed by the grand vizier
until the new sultan Selim could reach the army and order it home.

## A peace that cost one fortress and gained a province

Maximilian, who had kept his own main force on the defensive near
Győr rather than risk a battle that could have opened Hungary outright
to John Sigismund, used Süleyman's death to negotiate from strength:
an armistice concluded in May 1567 left each side in possession of
what it actually held, costing Maximilian only Szigetvár and Gyula
while extending his own territory some two hundred miles beyond the
Tisza. John Sigismund refused to be bound by a peace he had no part
in making and kept intriguing among Hungary's own magnates, but died
in 1571 without ever reopening a war Maximilian's own truce with the
sultan had made unwinnable for him.

## A crown he hesitated too long to take

Poland's own elective throne fell vacant the same decade, and
Maximilian's own hesitation - weighing whether to take the crown
himself or transfer it to his son Ernest, wary of the war with the
Turks a Polish throne might invite, unwilling to accept the onerous
terms Poland's own nobility demanded - let a faster rival, Stephen
Báthory, marry the Polish princess Anne and take the crown outright in
May 1575. Maximilian signed the same terms only after his rival
already held them, and was still trying to rally Sweden and Denmark
against Báthory when his own health, already failing, gave out. He
died at Regensburg on 12 October 1576, fifty years old, in the middle
of a diet whose business closed the same day - a reign contemporaries
in every one of his kingdoms, Catholic and Protestant alike, praised
with a rare unanimity Coxe records at length: a Bohemian embassy's own
tribute called his subjects "as happy under his government as if he
were our father," and Germany revived for him the old Roman title
Delight of Mankind.
