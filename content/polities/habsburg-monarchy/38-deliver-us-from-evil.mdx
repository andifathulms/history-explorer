Ladislaus's own reign ended before the consequences of Hunyadi's
execution could fully play out. He traveled to Prague to marry
Madeleine, daughter of King Charles VII of France, but fell suddenly
and mortally ill in the midst of the wedding preparations, and died
within thirty-six hours. George of Poděbrady rushed to his side the
moment the first symptoms appeared; the dying king took his regent's
hand, entrusted the kingdom to his care, and asked him to see his
Hungarian and Austrian attendants safely home. After an affectionate
farewell and the rites of the church, he asked, as a final renunciation
of worldly vanity, that his own prized golden hair be cut off - and,
fixing his eyes on a crucifix and reciting the Lord's Prayer, died at
the words "deliver us from evil," seventeen years old, on the eve of
his own wedding.

## A verdict on a short life

Europe mourned a prince who might have united Hungary, Bohemia and
Austria into the single strongest barrier against Ottoman expansion
anywhere on the continent, and his own subjects remembered him as
more than he had actually been. Coxe's own assessment is far less
generous: a "feeble and irresolute character," bigoted and
intolerant, who showed none of the courage or initiative his father
had already displayed at the same age, drifted from one faction's
influence to the next, and - amid two regents as capable as Hunyadi
and Poděbrady, and an active Ottoman threat on his own southern
border - never once showed the slightest desire to lead his own
armies. Had he lived, Coxe judges, his religious intolerance would
likely have reignited the Hussite wars, and his betrayal of the
Hunyadi family would have kept Hungary in open revolt.

## Plague, not poison

The suddenness of his death and the uncertain nature of his illness
fed immediate rumors of poison, with German accounts pointing directly
at Poděbrady - suspicions that gained currency mainly because
Poděbrady himself went on to claim the Bohemian throne once it fell
vacant. Coxe sides firmly with the Bohemian historians who investigated
the charge afterward and concluded the actual cause was plague, not
murder - though the accusation would outlive any proof for it, and the
fight over who would inherit what Ladislaus left behind was only just
beginning.
