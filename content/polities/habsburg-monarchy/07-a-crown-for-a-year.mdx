Albert's ambitions soon reached past his own three duchies into
Bohemia itself, whose king Wenceslas II had grown dangerously powerful
in his eyes - ruler of Bohemia and Poland both, and briefly of Hungary
as well through a rival succession dispute the Pope himself had tried
and failed to settle by force. When Wenceslas refused Albert's own
demands to surrender both foreign crowns and cede territory and silver
mines besides, Albert put him under the imperial ban and invaded, only
to be forced into a disorderly retreat from the siege of Kutná Hora
when the miners resisted harder than expected and Wenceslas raced back
from Hungary to relieve his own kingdom. Wenceslas died in June 1305
before the war could resume, and his son and successor, seventeen and
inexperienced, bought peace instead by ceding territory and doing
homage for both crowns - a settlement that lasted barely a year before
the young king was himself assassinated in August 1306, the last male
of his ancient line.

## Rudolph of Austria

With the Bohemian throne suddenly open, Albert moved to claim it for
his own family, citing both his authority as feudal overlord and an
old compact with the previous dynasty that entailed Bohemia on the
House of Austria in default of a male heir. He put forward his eldest
son Rudolph, who strengthened his own claim by promising to marry the
widowed queen, against a rival candidate backed by the dead king's own
grieving sisters - who came barefoot into the assembly at Prague to
beg the nobles for their preferred candidate, and were overruled
anyway. Rudolph was elected in April 1306 and entered Prague in
triumph, but the mild and amiable young king, pressed by his own
father's example, taxed the Bohemians heavily, stripped churches of
their ornaments, and persecuted a bishop who protested - provoking the
same kind of revolt his father had spent a career provoking elsewhere.
Rudolph was still in the field putting down that rebellion when
dysentery killed him in July 1307, twenty-two years old and not yet a
full year into a reign the Bohemians had never really accepted.

## "We will have no Austrian king!"

Albert proposed his second son, Frederick, as Rudolph's replacement,
but when a nobleman rose in the assembly at Prague to name him, the
hall erupted: "We will have no Austrian king!" The nobleman pressed
on anyway, taunting the crowd that they would perhaps choose some
peasant from the village their own ancient line was supposed to have
sprung from and marry him to the widowed queen instead - and in the
brawl that followed he and two other Austrian partisans were killed
outright, while the estates turned instead to Henry of Carinthia by
unanimous acclamation. Albert, stung as much by his son's public
rejection as by the loss of Bohemia itself, marched into the kingdom
with Frederick at his side and laid siege to two towns, but winter,
determined garrisons and harassing Bohemian raids forced a retreat
before he could make good on his claim - and he was still planning a
larger campaign for the following spring when his own life was cut
short by an entirely different quarrel, one much closer to home.
