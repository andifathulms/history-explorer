Even while losing ground in Flanders, Frederick secured something more
durable for his family: he maneuvered to have Maximilian elected King
of the Romans, guaranteeing the imperial succession would stay within
the House of Austria after his own death. He could count on all three
ecclesiastical electors, either indebted to him personally or
favorable to Maximilian, and three of the secular electors besides
through family ties - but wary of opposition from Bohemia's own king,
he had the whole election conducted with such secrecy and speed that
Ladislaus learned Maximilian had already been chosen before he even
knew the matter was under discussion.

## A right ignored, and reclaimed

Ladislaus took the omission as a personal insult, and his own
subjects shared his anger enough that Bohemia's estates briefly
floated separating from the empire altogether and building a
counter-alliance with Hungary and Poland to invalidate the election
outright. Backed by that threat, Ladislaus refused to recognize
Maximilian, prepared for war, and formally cited both an old imperial
decree and the Golden Bull itself confirming Bohemia's right to
participate in any election of a king of the Romans - demanding
recognition of that right going forward, and further concessions
exempting him from traveling in person to receive investiture or
accompany the emperor to Rome. The electors, indignant at the scope of
those last two demands, nearly let the dispute reopen the whole
succession; only Frederick's own direct intervention produced a
compromise, with the electors apologizing for the irregular election
and promising never to bypass Bohemia's rights again, while Ladislaus
accepted Maximilian as king and dropped his demand for a formal
penalty payment.
