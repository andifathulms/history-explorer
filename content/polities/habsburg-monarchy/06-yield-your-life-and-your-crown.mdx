Adolf of Nassau's own reign gave Albert his opening. The new king had
alienated the electors through arbitrary attempts to enrich his own
family, offending even the Archbishop of Mainz who had made him king
in the first place, and Albert spent 1297 quietly assembling a
counter-coalition - the Archbishop himself, the King of Bohemia, and
the electors of Cologne, Saxony and Brandenburg - while working to
pacify his own restive duchies so no rebellion could stab him in the
back a second time. A diet at Mainz drew up formal charges against
Adolf, and when he refused to answer them, declared him deposed for
contumacy and raised Albert to the throne on 23 June 1298. Adolf did
not accept the verdict quietly, gathering his own supporters among the
Rhineland princes, and the two rival kings met in battle at Göllheim,
between Speyer and Worms, on 2 July.

## The lance and the reply

Albert had armed a picked body of infantry with a poniard of his own
design, made specifically to strike at horses and open a path to
Adolf's own person, and the tactic worked: Adolf's cavalry broke, and
Adolf himself, dismounted and struck hard enough in the head to lose
his helmet, remounted anyway and cut his way through the melee toward
the spot where Albert was directing his own troops. "Yield your life
and your crown!" Adolf shouted as he closed the distance. "The event
is in the hand of Providence!" Albert replied, and drove his lance
into his rival's face; Adolf's horse went down under him in the same
moment, and he died there, overpowered by the men around him. Albert,
wary of holding a crown people might call a battlefield prize rather
than a lawful election, had the electors of Trier and the Palatinate
formally renounce his earlier deposition of Adolf and re-elect him
unanimously instead - a legal fiction that let him claim the throne
free of any taint. He was crowned at Aachen, held a diet at Nuremberg
grander than any before it, and at that same gathering invested his
own three sons, Rudolph, Frederick and Leopold, with Austria, Styria
and Carniola - securing, this time with an emperor's own authority
behind it, the family possession his father had first carved out a
generation earlier.
