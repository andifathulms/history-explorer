Rudolf's death left the family thinner than it had been in decades:
his next brother Frederick had already died in a hunting accident,
leaving only Albert, seventeen, and Leopold, fifteen, to carry the
line their father's own family compact had built. Albert, as the
elder, took sole administration under that compact's own terms, and
the brothers' first shared task was defending the Tyrol itself - Duke
Stephen of Bavaria renewed his old claim the moment Margaret Maultasch
died in 1366, seizing several towns before three years of negotiation,
mediated by Emperor Charles IV, produced a settlement at Scharding in
1369: Bavaria renounced Tyrol for good in exchange for a large cash
payment and a handful of towns, and Austria kept a province rich in
silver mines and mountain passes into Italy, held by a people the
source calls remarkable for their military loyalty.

## A compact broken from within

The peace did not last between the brothers themselves. Albert was
placid, devout and bookish; Leopold impetuous, prodigal and as
militarily gifted as any Habsburg before him, and increasingly
unwilling to share power on his elder brother's terms. Given charge of
the family's Swabian lands and a joint hand in governing Tyrol,
Leopold pressed for more until he extorted an entirely new family
compact: Albert kept Austria alone, while Leopold took everything
else - Alsace, Swabia, Styria, Carinthia and the whole of Tyrol,
along with Wiener Neustadt and its surrounding district. It directly
violated both the old family compact and the imperial investiture,
both of which required the territories to stay undivided under the
eldest brother, but Charles IV approved the new arrangement anyway,
with a comment that captured decades of Habsburg-Luxembourg rivalry in
a single line: "We have long laboured in vain to humble the house of
Austria, and now the dukes of Austria have humbled themselves."
Leopold, now ruling the greater share of the family's possessions in
his own right, wasted no time turning that inheritance toward further
conquest - purchasing towns, absorbing counties, and extending Austrian
authority across Swabia and the upper Rhine faster than any Habsburg
duke before him.
