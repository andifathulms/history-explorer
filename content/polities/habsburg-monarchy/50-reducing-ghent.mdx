The Treaty of Arras did nothing to settle Flanders's own suspicion of
Maximilian - if anything, his continued presence nearby, his
preference for foreign advisors, and his real influence in the
Netherlands' other provinces deepened it, while Maximilian himself
resented a settlement that stripped him of both his own son's
upbringing and any share in governing Flanders. The resulting war saw
Flemish rebels backed openly by France, but Maximilian pressed his own
military advantage hard: he took the principal towns around Ghent,
surprised Dendermonde, and beat back a rebel force reinforced by
several thousand French troops. By July 1485 Ghent itself submitted,
its burghers forced to recognize him as guardian of his son and
governor of Flanders in exchange for his promise not to remove Philip
from the Netherlands and to honor every act the estates had passed
since Mary's own death.

## A war for Burgundy's lost provinces

With Flanders reasserted, Maximilian turned toward France itself,
partly to punish Louis's own government for backing the Flemish
rebellion and partly to try to recover Burgundian territory Louis XI
had seized outright. A succession dispute in France - between the
regent, Anne of Beaujeu, and the Duke of Orléans as first prince of
the blood - gave him an opening: he allied with Orléans's own faction
and with Francis II, Duke of Brittany, who was fighting a similar
battle against French interference in his own duchy and had already
sheltered several of Orléans's exiled supporters. In 1486 Maximilian
sent a herald to Paris urging reform and personally led twelve
thousand men into Picardy, taking Thérouanne and Mortagne - only to be
checked by Anne of Beaujeu's own vigorous countermeasures. The young
King Charles VIII took the field in person, recovered Thérouanne,
overran Brittany, and crushed the rebel coalition outright at the
Battle of Saint-Aubin; Orléans and the Prince of Orange were both
captured, and on 28 August 1488 the Duke of Brittany signed a peace
that reduced his own duchy to French vassalage - a total collapse of
the coalition Maximilian had spent two years building.
