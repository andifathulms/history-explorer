Ferdinand of Austria had two separate grounds to claim Louis's own
vacant crowns - old family compacts entailing the succession on the
House of Austria in default of a male heir, and his own marriage to
Anne, the dead king's sister - but he understood, as his predecessors
often had not, that Bohemians and Hungarians valued their right of
election too highly to simply accept a hereditary claim asserted over
their heads. He offered himself as a candidate through the normal
electoral process in both kingdoms rather than resting on inheritance
alone. Bohemia gave him little real contest: opposed only by the Duke
of Bavaria, he was chosen by a twenty-member electoral committee on 26
October 1526, promised in writing to respect Bohemia's own rights,
privileges and customs, and was crowned at Prague with his wife Anne
the following February.

## A rival crowned the same year

Hungary proved far harder to settle. John Zápolya, Voivode of
Transylvania, had led forty thousand men toward Mohács too late to
join the battle, and used that intact army to put himself forward as
a native alternative to a foreign candidate - convening the estates at
Tokaj, where a faction of nobles averse to Habsburg rule crowned him
with Hungary's own sacred Crown of St. Stephen that November. Ferdinand's
own sister Mary, Louis's widow, countered by summoning a rival diet at
Bratislava, where nobles who judged Zápolya's own assembly improperly
convened declared his election invalid and raised Ferdinand to the
throne instead. Ferdinand backed the claim with real force, marching
into Hungary with a combined German and Bohemian army; town after
town surrendered without resistance, Zápolya abandoned Buda without a
fight, and a fresh diet confirmed Ferdinand's own kingship, giving
Zápolya's remaining supporters a three-and-a-half-week ultimatum to
submit or be declared enemies of the country. Zápolya's own forces
lost engagement after engagement, and he was eventually driven into
exile in Poland, sheltered by a brother-in-law king.

## A sultan's threat

Ferdinand, secure for the moment in both kingdoms, overreached almost
immediately: confident in the strength of his own legal claim, he sent
an embassy to Constantinople demanding the return of Belgrade and
other fortresses the Ottomans had already taken from Hungary - a
demand delivered, by contemporary account, with enough arrogance to
provoke Süleyman himself into a furious reply. "Belgrade! Go tell your
master that I am collecting troops and preparing for my expedition. I
will suspend at my neck the keys of my Hungarian fortresses, and will
bring them to that plain of Mohács where Louis, by the aid of
Providence, found a defeat and a grave. Let Ferdinand meet and conquer
me, and take them after severing my head from my body! But, if I find
him not there, I will seek him at Buda, or follow him to Vienna."
Zápolya, still fighting for his own restoration, allied openly with
the sultan against Ferdinand, and within three years Süleyman himself
was marching on Vienna at the head of an army numbering, by report,
three hundred thousand men - a threat the newly composite Habsburg
state would have to answer at the very gates of its own capital
before its first decade was out.
