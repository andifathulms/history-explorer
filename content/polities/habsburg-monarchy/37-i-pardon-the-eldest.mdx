The mutual suspicion between Cilli and the Hunyadi brothers finally
broke into open violence in March 1457, when the two men met by
chance and traded bitter accusations until Cilli seized a sword from
a bystander and wounded Ladislaus Hunyadi in the head; Ladislaus's own
followers rushed to his defense, and in the resulting brawl Cilli was
mortally wounded. The killers went straight to the king to justify
themselves, insisting Cilli had struck first and deserved exactly what
he got - and Ladislaus Posthumus, grieving but outwardly composed,
had the body sent home for burial and continued on toward Timișoara
without any visible sign of retribution to come.

## A mother's plea, and a broken promise

Hunyadi's widow met the king's party there, dressed in mourning with
her younger son Matthias at her side, and threw herself at his feet
begging forgiveness for her elder son's act and asking the king not to
forget her late husband's service to the crown. Ladislaus raised her
up himself, promised to treat her as a mother and her sons as
brothers, urged her to set aside her grief since a man who had lived
as her husband had "doubtless lives in Christ," pardoned the elder
Hunyadi outright, and sent the family away laden with gifts after a
day of shared festivity. Reassured by these promises, both brothers
followed the king back to Buda - where, once they had let their guard
down completely, German troops sealed the palace gates, arrested both
young men along with their accomplices in Cilli's death, and beheaded
Ladislaus Hunyadi on the spot. The betrayal of a solemn pardon, on top
of the execution of a beloved commander's own son, turned Hungary
against its king outright: Hunyadi's widow rallied her husband's own
old supporters and threatened to hand Hungary's border fortresses to
the Ottomans unless Matthias was freed, forcing Ladislaus to flee the
kingdom under public curses, taking the surviving brother with him to
Vienna as a hostage rather than release him.
