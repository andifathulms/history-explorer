Morgarten checked Leopold but did not end the wider civil war, which
dragged on for years as both sides gathered new allies - Louis winning
over John of Bohemia with the promise of the vacant margraviate of
Brandenburg, Frederick and Leopold assembling a combined force of
Austrian, Hungarian and Swiss-frontier troops for one final reckoning.
By 1322 Frederick had massed some eighteen thousand infantry, four
thousand archers and seven thousand horse in Bavaria, and dug in near
Mühldorf to wait for Leopold's own army to arrive from the west before
committing to battle - a sound plan he abandoned in a rush of youthful
impatience, giving battle to Louis alone rather than wait. He rode out
in armor blazoned with the Austrian eagle and a helmet crowned in gold,
determined, in Coxe's phrase, to be as pre-eminent in danger as he was
in rank; Louis, by contrast, wore a plain coat of mail marked only by
a white cross and handed field command to an experienced general,
Seyfried Schweppermann, while he himself stood by the imperial
standard.

## A borrowed banner

The battle ran from dawn to dusk with the outcome genuinely in doubt,
tilting if anything toward Frederick, until the Burgrave of Nuremberg
broke the deadlock with a ruse: he led four hundred horsemen around to
Frederick's own flank flying Austrian colors and sounding Austrian
trumpets, and Frederick's exhausted troops, mistaking them for
Leopold's long-awaited reinforcement, broke into premature celebration
- a celebration that turned to panic the instant the "reinforcement"
attacked instead. Frederick's army collapsed; his brother Henry was
captured, and Frederick himself fought on nearly alone until his horse
was killed under him and he was overpowered and forced to surrender
his sword. Louis received his defeated rival with unexpected warmth:
"The battle is not lost by your fault," he told him. "The Bavarians
have experienced, to their cost, that you are a most valiant prince,
and you ought to reflect that victory is in the disposal of
Providence." Gracious words did not mean freedom, though - Louis
confined Frederick in the fortress of Trausnitz, and with his chief
rival now a prisoner, turned to consolidating the victory Mühldorf had
finally handed him outright.
