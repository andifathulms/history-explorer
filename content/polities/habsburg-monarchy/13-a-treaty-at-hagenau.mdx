Frederick's and Leopold's deaths, paradoxically, steadied the House of
Austria more than either brother's life ever had: with no more
Habsburg pretender to the imperial throne, the family's chief source
of friction with Louis of Bavaria disappeared, and the two surviving
brothers, Albert and Otho, governed so closely in step that Coxe finds
no evidence they ever administered their inheritance separately.
Albert, tall and commanding in his youth, had likely been intended for
the Church before marrying the heiress of a valuable Alsatian county
and adding it to the family's holdings; a paralytic illness at
thirty-two left him unable to walk unaided for the rest of his life,
though it never dulled his mind, and he governed from horseback or a
litter for nearly three decades afterward. Otho, cheerful enough to be
nicknamed "the Jovial," had learned soldiering under Leopold and briefly
considered his own bid for the empire at the pope's urging - a papal
scheme to keep Germany's civil war alive that Otho and Albert were
wise enough to refuse.

## Making peace with old wars

Instead the brothers negotiated: mediated by John of Bohemia, they
concluded a peace with Louis at Hagenau in August 1330 that ended,
after sixteen years, the war their own brothers had started over the
double election. Both sides revoked their old hostile acts, Louis
confirmed the family's full possessions, and in a mark of genuine
reconciliation mortgaged four imperial cities to them as compensation
for the war's expenses, with a standing promise to name Otho his own
vicar in Germany whenever Louis himself traveled beyond the Alps. It
was the first durable peace between the houses of Austria and Bavaria
in a generation, and it freed Albert to turn his attention toward a
rival more dangerous, in the long run, than Louis had ever been: John
of Bohemia, the restless, ambitious head of the House of Luxembourg,
who treated the emperor less as his sovereign than as a convenient
partner and had already begun carving out a personal dominion of his
own in Lombardy.
