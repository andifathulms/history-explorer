To rally German support for the league against France, Maximilian
broke with tradition by summoning the empire's diet not to Nuremberg,
the customary site for the first assembly of a new reign, but to
Worms, closer to both the Netherlands and the Italian theater of war.
The gathering that opened on 26 May 1496 drew more princes and nobles
than any diet in memory, and Maximilian presided over it himself,
pressing two demands with everything his own eloquence could muster:
a nominal request for help against renewed Ottoman raids into Croatia,
and his real object, an appeal for troops to drive the French out of
Italy altogether.

## Every argument he had

Maximilian threw every available pressure behind the second demand. He
warned the assembled princes that French control of both Brittany and
Naples would let Charles VIII crush the church's and the empire's own
liberties outright; he paraded ambassadors from Naples's own exiled
king as living proof of what French conquest meant; he formally
invested Ludovico Sforza with Milan before the assembly specifically
so Sforza could then claim the diet's own support as a fellow member
of the empire; and he let a papal legate describe, in vivid detail,
the Pope's own flight from Rome and the atrocities French troops had
committed there. Coxe judges this the one moment when direct imperial
intervention might genuinely have driven France from Italy and
recovered its old imperial fiefs for good - a rare alignment of
motive, moral case and opportunity all at once.

## An empire grown deaf to its own emperor

None of it worked. The German states had grown numb to complaints
about Turkish incursions after decades of hearing them, and proved no
more moved by abstract arguments about the European balance of power
than they had been by any of Frederick's own earlier appeals for help.
Maximilian's own eloquence, address and personal influence - genuinely
formidable, by every account - simply could not overcome an empire
that had learned, across generations of unanswered requests, to treat
its own emperor's foreign wars as somebody else's problem.
