The 1458 partition of Austria - Lower Austria to Frederick, Upper
Austria to his brother Albert, a slice of Carinthia to their cousin
Sigismund, with all three sharing Vienna as a joint residence - solved
nothing. Frederick's own indolence made him unpopular with the
capital's citizens, and Albert, never satisfied with his own share of
the inheritance, fanned that discontent into open war. Vienna itself
split into factions: the senate and the more moderate burghers backed
the emperor, while the populace rallied behind a demagogue named
Wolfgang Holzer in Albert's own cause. Frederick rode to the city's
defense with a body of Styrian cavalry to protect his wife and infant
son Maximilian, sheltering in the citadel, and talked his way past
three days of hostile argument to gain entry - but when he refused to
meet every demand of the rebellious faction, the mob rose again,
plundered his supporters' homes, and drove him back into the citadel
under open siege, with Albert himself arriving to take command of the
blockade.

## Three weeks of provisions

Frederick held the fortress with barely two hundred men, resolved, in
his own words, to be buried under its ruins before he would surrender
to his own rebellious subjects. He appealed to the empire's assembled
states at Nuremberg, and when a messenger reached them warning that
the garrison had only three weeks of food left, the princes voted to
send help - too slowly, as always, to matter on their own. What
actually saved Frederick was faster and closer to home: George of
Poděbrady, King of Bohemia, sent his own son ahead with five thousand
men and followed in person with eight thousand more, breaking the
siege before the promised imperial relief could even set out. Both
brothers submitted their dispute to Poděbrady's own arbitration, and
the resulting settlement gave Albert Lower Austria for eight years in
exchange for an annual payment, while Frederick - escorted home by a
thousand Bohemian horsemen - rewarded his rescuer generously, raising
Poděbrady's own two sons to princes of the empire and granting Prague
toll exemptions across every imperial territory.
