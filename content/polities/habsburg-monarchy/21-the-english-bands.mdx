Leopold's rapid expansion drew an unexpected invader: his own cousin,
the celebrated soldier Enguerrand de Coucy, who had married into the
English royal family as a hostage after the Battle of Poitiers and
spent the years since fighting popes' wars against the Visconti in
Italy rather than serve against either his French king or his English
father-in-law. When peace between England and France in 1375 left
thousands of mercenaries without employment, Coucy gathered them into
an army forty thousand strong - six thousand of them English, splendid
enough in appearance that Swiss chroniclers simply called the whole
force "the English bands" - and marched into Alsace to claim
territory he said belonged to him through his own mother's marriage
portion. Leopold rejected the claim as contrary to the family's own
succession rules and, unable to meet so large a force in open battle,
shut himself up in Brisach and laid waste the surrounding country to
starve the invaders out.

## Rescued by his own enemies

Only Bern and Zurich answered Leopold's appeal for Swiss help; the
rest of the confederacy stayed home. It hardly mattered - Coucy's army
found the local population ready to fight on its own account. The
hardy peasants of Entlibuch, joined by volunteers from the Forest
Cantons, routed three thousand of Coucy's men near the lake of
Sempach; villagers around Lake Biel and Lake Neuchâtel destroyed
another detachment; and the citizens of Bern struck the heaviest blow
of all, marching through a winter night to surprise and scatter eight
hundred troops under a Welsh commander. Battered by repeated defeats
and by cold and hunger - "the best allies of the duke of Austria," as
Coxe puts it - Coucy's mercenaries grew mutinous, and he retreated
into Alsace, ravaged what he could, and abandoned his claim once
Leopold ceded him two minor towns as a face-saving settlement. His own
soldiers scattered as such armies always did; Coucy himself went on to
a crusade against the Ottomans, was captured at Nicopolis, and died in
captivity - the invasion that Leopold's own diplomacy had failed to
prevent was, in the end, broken by exactly the Swiss cantons he
considered his family's oldest enemy.

## Venice's war, and a port on the Adriatic

Barely free of Coucy, Leopold was drawn into a war between Venice, the
King of Hungary and the lords of Padua over control of the Venetian
mainland - a conflict that cost him more than it gained. He sold off
territory he had won in the Trevigiano for sixty thousand ducats once
the fighting turned against him and his own Swabian and Alsatian lands
grew restless in his absence, but the same war left him one lasting
prize: the city of Trieste, whose citizens, unhappy under the
Patriarch of Aquileia's weak protection during the fighting, offered
their own submission to Leopold instead. He accepted, promised to
respect their self-government, and secured for the House of Austria
its first port on the Adriatic - a foothold on the sea that would
outlast every other territory won or lost in that same war.
