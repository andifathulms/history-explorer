title: The insignia from Baghdad
drafted_from: peacock-yildiz-2013

Emir Gazi ruled from about 1104 to 1134 and spent it holding off two empires at
once, which is a thing this corpus does not otherwise record a Turkmen
principality managing.

To the west was the Sultanate of Rum, contesting the same plateau. To the north
and west was Byzantium under John II Komnenos, who campaigned in Anatolia
repeatedly in the 1130s with the serious intention of recovering it and who was
a considerably better soldier than his reputation suggests. To the south were
the crusader principalities of Antioch and Edessa, whose whole northern
frontier ran against Danishmendid country.

Emir Gazi fought all three and lost none of them decisively. He took Malatya in
1103, took Ankara and Kastamonu from the Seljuks, beat a crusading army, held
the Byzantine campaigns to sieges of individual towns, and at his death the
Danishmendids controlled more of Anatolia than Konya did.

The recognition followed. The sources report the caliph in Baghdad sending him
the insignia of a sultan — a diploma, a standard, a robe — which is the
transaction this site's Iranian Intermezzo pages record over and over, arriving
at the far western end of the Islamic world. A Turkmen emir in Sivas was
worth investing, and being invested was worth having.

He is also the last Danishmendid who held everything at once. His son Melik
Mehmed held it, and after Mehmed's death in 1142 the realm divided and stayed
divided, which is the subject two chapters on. The peak of this polity is one
long reign, and everything after it is the same territory administered by
people who were not speaking to each other.
