Melik Mehmed died in 1142 and the realm divided between two branches, one at
Sivas and one at Malatya. That is the whole of the contraction; everything else
follows from it.

They did not reunite, and the reason they did not is that the division suited
each of them better than a reunion would have. A Danishmendid at Malatya facing
Nur al-Din of Aleppo had a different set of problems from a Danishmendid at
Sivas facing Byzantium and Konya, and each could get better terms from his own
neighbours by dealing separately than either could by subordinating himself to
a cousin.

So each dealt separately. With the Seljuks. With Manuel Komnenos, who campaigned
in Anatolia through the 1140s and 1150s with the serious intention of recovering
it. With Nur al-Din, who was assembling northern Syria in exactly these decades
and had an interest in a weak neighbour on his northern flank.

Every one of those relationships was rational for the branch that entered it and
fatal for the dynasty as a whole. Three larger powers were each able to buy one
Danishmendid against another, and none of the three had to fight both at once.

## The arithmetic

It is worth stating what was lost, because the state that split in 1142 was not
a small one.

Emir Gazi at his death held Sivas, Niksar, Tokat, Amasya, Kayseri, Ankara,
Kastamonu and Malatya — more of Anatolia than Konya did, with the caliph's
diploma to say it was lawful. Thirty-six years later there was nothing.

Kilij Arslan II took the Sivas branch by 1175 and Malatya in 1178. He did it in
pieces, over years, without a decisive battle, because there was no longer a
Danishmendid army to fight — only a succession of towns each held by somebody
with his own arrangement to protect, most of whom eventually judged that Seljuk
service was a better arrangement than the one they had.

They were not wrong about that either. The surviving Danishmendids went into the
Seljuk administration as commanders and provincial governors and their
descendants are visible there for generations. The family did well out of the
end of its own state, which is the ordinary result of a dynasty being absorbed
rather than destroyed and is the reason so few of them fight to the last.
