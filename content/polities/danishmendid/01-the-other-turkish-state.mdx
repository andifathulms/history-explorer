---
title: The other Turkish state
drafted_from: peacock-yildiz-2013
phase: formation
---

Anatolia after Manzikert had two Turkish states, and for most of the first
century the one nobody remembers was the stronger.

Manzikert did not hand Anatolia to anybody. It removed the Byzantine frontier
system, and what moved into the country behind it was not an army under a
command but a number of Turkmen groups under their own leaders, each taking
what it could hold. Two of them became states. The house of Seljuk took the
west and Konya. A commander remembered as Danishmend Gazi — whose origins the
sources cannot agree on, making him variously a Turkmen emir, an Armenian, a
kinsman of the Great Seljuk sultan and a veteran of Manzikert — took the
centre and north-east, with Sivas, Niksar, Tokat and eventually Malatya and
Kayseri.

For most of the twelfth century the Danishmendids were the stronger. They
captured Bohemond of Antioch in 1100 and held him three years — the sources
differ over whether the captor was the founder or the son who succeeded him,
which is of a piece with everything else about this dynasty's early
generations; a Danishmendid
and Seljuk alliance destroyed the crusading army of 1101; and at their height
they held more of Anatolia than Konya did, with the caliph reportedly sending
Emir Gazi the insignia of a sultan.

What that meant on the ground is best shown by the money. Danishmendid coins
carry inscriptions in Greek,
and some of them carry a figure on horseback spearing a dragon, which is Saint
George. A Turkish Muslim dynasty in central Anatolia was minting in the language
of its subjects with the imagery of their religion within sixty years of
arriving.

That is what the Turkish settlement of Anatolia actually looked like at the
start. Not a replacement of one population by another, but a small armed elite
governing Greeks and Armenians and using whatever vocabulary of authority was
already lying about.
