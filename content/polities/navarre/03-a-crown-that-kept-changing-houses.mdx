title: A crown that kept changing houses

Navarre never regained anything like Sancho the Great's own reach.
Wedged between Castile, Aragon and France, its crown passed by
marriage and inheritance through one foreign house after another - by
1285 it had become, outright, a province of the French crown, and
stayed one until 1328, when the death of Charles IV of France without
a male heir let it recover its own separate existence under the house
of Evreux.

## A prince, a poisoned sister, and a throne enthroned by force

That same pattern of a crown passing through foreign hands repeated
inside Navarre's own royal family when it produced its own succession
crisis. Charles of Viana,
heir to both Navarre and, through his father Juan II, to Aragon
itself, died before he could inherit either - a death Aragon's own
nobility blamed on Juan's second wife. The throne of Navarre should
then have passed to Charles's sister Blanche; instead her own father
had her imprisoned, and she died in captivity soon after, by one
account poisoned on her sister's own orders. That younger sister,
Leonor, was enthroned in Blanche's place, and her marriage to the
count of Foix installed a new dynasty on a throne two of its own
predecessors had just died fighting over.

## A dynasty too short-lived to matter

The house of Foix did not hold Navarre for long. Wedged as always
between larger neighbors and now further weakened by the manner of
its own accession, the kingdom entered the sixteenth century without
the strength to withstand what came next - a conquest that would end,
within a generation of Leonor's own coronation, the independence
Navarre had kept, in one reduced form or another, since the wars that
first produced it.
