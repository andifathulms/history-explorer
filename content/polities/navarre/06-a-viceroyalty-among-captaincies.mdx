---
title: A viceroyalty among captaincies

Ferdinand's 1512 conquest of Spanish Navarre was not simply an act of
arms. The pope sanctioned the taking of the portion of the kingdom lying
south of the Pyrenees specifically, and on that sanction Navarre was
formally and permanently added to the Spanish domain - a conquest with a
paper title behind it, not only a battlefield one.

## A kingdom governed, not simply annexed

What Ferdinand actually absorbed was a country whose own kings had never
ruled by unchecked will. Chapman notes that in Navarre the administration
of justice belonged to the king, but that no king of Navarre could hold
court, make war, make peace or agree a truce without first consulting his
own nobles - the same constitutional habit of mind that had made Navarre's
throne itself an elected, treaty-bound office more than once in its own
history. Spain inherited that habit along with the territory, and did not
try to erase it.

## The one region that kept a viceroy

The clearest evidence of what Navarre kept is administrative rather than
military. When the Bourbon kings reorganized Spain's regions under
captain-generals during the eighteenth century - Aragon, Catalonia,
Valencia, Galicia, Andalusia and the rest all reduced to that same
standard colonial-style command - Navarre alone was left as a viceroyalty,
governed under the older and more prestigious title that the rest of
Spain's own provinces had long since lost. More than two hundred years
after Ferdinand's conquest, a kingdom that no longer had a king of its own
still had a form of government no other Spanish region was allowed to
keep.

## The half across the mountains

French Navarre, spared the 1512 conquest, followed a quieter path to the
same end. It remained for a time under the same house of Foix that had
lost the Spanish kingdom, then passed into the kingdom of France outright
- absorbed rather than conquered, the last piece of a realm that had once
stretched, under Sancho the Great, across nearly the whole of Christian
Spain, ending finally as an ordinary province of France.
