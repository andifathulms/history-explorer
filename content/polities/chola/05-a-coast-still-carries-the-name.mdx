No Chola prince followed Rajendra III. Where the Pandya kingdom had
spent two centuries as a subordinate the Chola throne fought again and
again to keep in line, the Chola homeland itself was simply absorbed
into the victorious Pandya empire outright after 1279 - more
completely, the record shows, than the Pandya country had ever been
absorbed into the Chola empire even at the height of Chola power. The
dynasty's own name did not disappear with its throne, though. The
Tamil term for the Chola country, Cholamandalam, went on being used
long after there was any Chola king left to rule it, passing eventually
into European mouths as the name of the whole eastern seaboard those
kings had once governed - Coromandel.

## Chiefs who kept borrowing a fallen name

The title outlived the dynasty in smaller ways too. Local chieftains
across the old Chola lands went on claiming descent from it for
centuries after 1279, whether through branches of the Telugu-Coda
family or more direct lines back to the Cholas of the Tamil country
proper. A "Vira Chola Raja" heavy with honorific titles is recorded
ruling in the Bangalore district in 1301; a "Vira Choda" and his own
son appear in North Arcot around the same years; inscriptions from the
island of Srirangam dated as late as 1481 and 1530 still record gifts
from men carrying a Telugu-Coda title descended from the old royal
name. None of them ruled anything resembling the Chola empire itself -
they were local grandees borrowing a name still worth invoking two and
a half centuries after the throne it named had gone silent.

## A name still worth invoking under someone else's empire

The clearest sign of how long the memory held is a grant recorded at
Kumbakonam in 1554, made under the Vijayanagara emperor Achyuta Deva
Raya's own reign: a local Mahamandalesvara styling himself Gopa Raja
Rudradeva-Chola endowed worship at the Adi Kumbheswarar temple, still
carrying the old dynastic name as part of his own titulature nearly
three centuries after the last Chola king vanished from the record. By
then the name governed nothing - it survived only in a coastline's
own geography and in the vanity of men who found it still worth
claiming.
