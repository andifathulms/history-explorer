The American Revolution asked the League a question its own law of
unanimity could not answer twice. Both Continental Congress and the
British sought Iroquois support, and when the council of sachems and
chiefs took up whether to declare for the English, the Oneida alone
opposed the measure as unwise - opposition that, under the League's own
requirement of unanimous consent, was enough by ancient law to defeat
the war measure as an act of the confederacy itself. But Morgan records
that "the course of events had, at this time, greatly impaired and
weakened the confederacy," and the ancient principle could not hold:
it was finally agreed that each nation could enter the war on its own
responsibility. Mohawk, Onondaga, Cayuga and Seneca warriors took up
arms for the English; the Oneida did not.

## Sullivan's fire

The border war that followed brought an American reprisal the
confederacy had never previously faced on its own ground. In 1779,
General Sullivan led four thousand men into Seneca territory as far as
the Genesee, burning towns, fruit orchards and stores of grain before a
detachment turned north to ravage Cayuga settlements as well. The
Mohawk, judging their own country no longer defensible, abandoned it
during the war and resettled in Canada, on the Grand River and near
Kingston, where their descendants live on reserved land still.

## An ally who signed without them

The peace of 1783 between Britain and the United States made no
provision for the Iroquois at all, leaving a League that had fought as
Britain's ally to negotiate its own survival with the victorious
republic. Morgan calls what followed, in effect, the end of the
confederacy's political existence: United States and New York State
jurisdiction was extended over Iroquois territory in the years after the
war, land was ceded in a long series of negotiations that Morgan judges
to have been conducted justly by New York's own standard even as they
narrowed Oneida holdings to a single small reservation, and the Six
Nations became, from that point forward, dependent nations within a
republic that had not existed when their council fire was first
kindled on the shore of Onondaga lake.
