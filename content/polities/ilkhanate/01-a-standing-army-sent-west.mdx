The Ilkhanate is the only one of the four Mongol successor states that was
created on purpose. The other three are inheritances that hardened into
kingdoms. This one was a commission.

## The assignment

In 1251 the assembly that made Möngke Great Khan resolved on two great
campaigns: one against the Song in the south, under Kublai, and one against the
west, under their brother Hülegü.

The western brief was specific. Reduce the Ismaili strongholds in the Elburz.
Bring the caliph in Baghdad to obedience. Then go on to Syria and Egypt. Hülegü
was given, by the accounts, two men in ten from the whole empire's forces, and a
train that included Chinese siege engineers — the empire's most valuable
technical asset, moved four thousand miles because a campaign required it.

He left in 1253 and travelled for two years.

## Alamut

The Nizari Ismailis had held a chain of mountain fortresses in northern Iran for
a hundred and sixty years, and no army had ever taken them. Their method was to
be impossible to besiege and dangerous to leave alone: castles on crags with
their own water and granaries, and a practice of political assassination that
had made every Seljuk and Khwarazmian ruler negotiate rather than campaign.

Hülegü campaigned. The fortresses fell one at a time through 1256, and in
November the Imam Rukn al-Din surrendered Alamut itself and was sent east to
the Great Khan — and killed on the way.

The library at Alamut was catalogued before it was burned by Juvaini, who was
Hülegü's own secretary and who preserved in his history what he chose to
preserve of a doctrinal tradition he had just helped destroy. Almost everything
known about the Nizaris of Alamut comes from the men who took it.

## What kind of state this was going to be

Two things about the founding shaped everything after.

The first is that Hülegü was not conquering for himself. He was an imperial
army commander with a mandate, and the territory he took was the Great Khan's;
the title *il-khan* means subordinate khan, and the coinage struck in Iran
carried the Great Khan's name for decades. It is the only one of the four
successor states that formally acknowledged a superior, and it went on doing so
after the superior was three thousand miles away and out of touch.

The second is that he arrived with the whole apparatus of empire behind him
rather than as a prince taking a share. That is why the Ilkhanate looks
different from the Golden Horde: it inherited a functioning Persian
administrative tradition, kept it, staffed it with Persian bureaucrats, and
produced within two generations the most sophisticated state in the Mongol
world.

What it did first, though, was destroy the institution the Islamic world had
been organised around for five hundred years.
