---
title: A client among clients
drafted_from: chi-4
phase: peak
---

After 935 the Ziyarids held Tabaristan and Gurgan — the strip of dense,
rain-fed forest and rice country between the Elburz mountains and the Caspian,
which is unlike anywhere else in Iran and hard to enter from the plateau. They
held it for another hundred and fifty years, and for nearly all of that they
held it from somebody.

The list of overlords is a summary of this site's Iranian Intermezzo. Vushmgir
manoeuvred between the Samanids at Bukhara and the Buyids to his south, taking
Samanid support against his brother's former officers. His successors
acknowledged the Buyids. When Ghaznavid power reached the Caspian the Ziyarids
acknowledged Mahmud, and later Mas'ud. When the Seljuks came they acknowledged
the Seljuks. The dynasty's method was to be too troublesome to occupy and too
small to be worth the trouble.

Qabus ibn Vushmgir is the one the sources are interested in, and his career is
the method's limit. He took the throne in 977, backed the wrong side against
the Buyids within four years, and spent seventeen years in exile in Khurasan.
He came back in 998 when Buyid power was failing, and ruled for fourteen more.

He was famous for his Arabic prose style — his letters were collected and
studied — and for his patronage: al-Biruni, who appears on this site's
Ghaznavid page as the author of the book about India, spent years at Gurgan
under Qabus, dedicated his *Chronology of Ancient Nations* to him around the
year 1000, and observed lunar eclipses from the city. He was also, on the
consistent report of the same sources that admired him, frightening. In 1012
his commanders deposed him and he died in confinement.

That is the second time in this dynasty's history that the Ziyarid army
disposed of a Ziyarid, and the pattern the corpus keeps recording is the same
one that killed the founder. What is unusual here is not the mechanism but the
recovery: after 1012 the dynasty simply went on, smaller, in the mountains, for
another eighty years.
