---
title: Vushmgir, between two powers
drafted_from: chi-4
phase: peak
---

Mardavij's brother inherited the wreck of a project and held the Caspian for
thirty years, which is the part of this dynasty's history that explains why
there was a dynasty at all.

Vushmgir — the name means "quail-catcher", which is what he had been doing when
the news of his brother's murder reached him — could not hold what Mardavij had
taken. Isfahan, Hamadan and the Jibal went to the officers who had killed or
outlived his brother, and among them, as the previous chapter says, were the
sons of Buya. What he kept was Tabaristan and Gurgan: the Caspian provinces the
family had come from.

Then he spent three decades being squeezed. The Buyids came at him from the
south and west, taking Rayy and pressing into the mountains; the Samanids, whose
page on this site describes them as the great power of the north-east, came at
him from the east — and the useful thing about his career is that he survived by
using each against the other. When the Buyids took his territory he acknowledged
the Samanids and campaigned back with their troops. When the Samanids were
distracted he governed on his own account. He lost Gurgan and recovered it more
than once.

That is the posture the Ziyarids kept for the next hundred and fifty years and
it is worth naming, because this corpus records so many polities that could not
manage it. The Saffarids ran the sequence backwards and were destroyed. The
Ma'munids were asked for one sentence in the Friday sermon and their commanders
killed their ruler rather than concede it. Vushmgir conceded whatever was
asked, by whoever was strongest that decade, and kept the mountains.

He died in 967, reportedly in a hunting accident, while preparing to campaign
alongside a Samanid army. The dynasty he left held the Caspian for another
hundred and twenty years without ever again threatening anybody outside it,
which was the correct assessment of what it could do.
