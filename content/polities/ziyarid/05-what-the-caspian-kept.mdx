title: What the Caspian kept

The Ziyarids end around 1090, when the Isma'ilis of this site's Nizari page
took their territory in the Alborz and Tabaristan. The last of them, Gilanshah,
is the son the *Qabus-nama* was written for.

It is worth ending this page on the region rather than the family, because the
Caspian is the one part of the Iranian world this corpus can now describe as a
system.

Four polities on this site come out of it or hold it. The Ziyarids and the
Buyids are both Daylamite houses from the same mountains, one of which stayed
and one of which took Baghdad. The Bavands held the Elburz through all of it and
outlasted everyone. The Nizaris arrived at the end and took the ground from
whoever still had it. Between them they cover 651 to 1273, and the pattern is
consistent in a way the plateau's is not.

The pattern is this. The Caspian provinces are separated from the Iranian
plateau by a mountain wall and from everything else by sea, forest and rain.
They were the last part of Iran to become Muslim — two centuries after the
plains — and they went on producing infantry when the rest of the region had
gone over to cavalry, which is why Daylamite soldiers are in every army in this
corpus for a century. Nobody on the plateau could hold the mountains, and
nobody in the mountains could hold the plateau for long: Mardavij tried and was
killed, the Buyids succeeded and stopped being a Caspian power the moment they
did.

So the Caspian dynasties survive by paying and outlasting, and the durable ones
are the ones that understood that early. The Bavands, who were ended three
times and came back twice, are the extreme case. The Ziyarids, after Mardavij,
are the ordinary one.

This record's `ended.by` names the Nizaris, which is accurate and slightly
misleading. What ended in 1090 was a family holding a province. The province
kept doing what it had been doing — resisting the plateau, producing soldiers,
housing whoever was currently unable to be dislodged — for another two hundred
years, under different names, on the same terms.
