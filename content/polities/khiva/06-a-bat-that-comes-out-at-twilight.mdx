Iltazar drowned trying to conquer Bukhara less than two years after
seizing the throne he had just invented for himself, and his brother
turned the same family's second attempt into the state that actually
lasted.

## Dismissing a puppet

Iltazar became Inak on his own brother's death and moved within days:
he raised ten thousand armoured Uzbeks, summoned the ulema and the
tribal notables, and told them plainly he was ruler now and they no
longer needed a ceremonial Kazakh khan to rubber-stamp it. Only one man
in the room objected - the Atalik Bek Pulad, who urged him to follow
his ancestors' example rather than seize the title outright - and
Iltazar remembered the objection long after everyone else had
acclaimed him. He crushed the Yomut Turkomans who refused his new
tribute terms, forced a marriage on a reluctant seyid's daughter to
found his own line, and eventually had Bek Pulad cut down at the palace
door as he left an audience, provoking a short revolt from the man's own
Uyghur tribe that ended, in a contemporary Bukharan envoy's phrase, in
"the kind of peace imposed by the wolf."

Confidence turned to overreach in 1805, when Iltazar raided the
outskirts of Bukhara itself and made off with fifty thousand sheep. The
Bukharan amir finally moved against him in force the following year;
Iltazar's own crossing of the Oxus by boat became a rout when too many
of his own men crowded aboard and sank it, drowning him along with two
of his brothers. A third brother, Kutlugh Murad, was captured and sent
to Bukhara as a hostage-governor. A fourth, Muhammad Rahim, got away.

## The brother who consolidated

Muhammad Rahim was installed as khan by the Khwarazmians before his
captured brother could even return from Bukhara to claim the succession
himself - and Kutlugh Murad, to his credit, simply accepted the
demotion to inak rather than contest it. The two brothers spent the
next two decades doing methodically what Iltazar had tried to do by
raid: Muhammad Rahim executed sixty tribal grandees he had lured to a
banquet under pretence of rewarding them, crushed his own rebellious
uncle, and conquered the long-independent Aral Uzbeks after a fugitive
in the rival khan's own service assassinated him in his sleep and
delivered the head to Khiva in a sack. Muhammad Rahim married the dead
man's daughter, binding his own line to the old Khwarazmian royal stock
by marriage rather than by conquest alone, and spent the rest of his
reign extracting tribute from the Kazakh clans along the frontier
instead of merely raiding them.

## What the two reigns actually built

Iltazar had proven a Qungrat inak could simply stop pretending to serve
a khan; Muhammad Rahim proved one could actually govern as one, for
decades rather than months, without losing the throne to the next
ambitious cousin. By the time his own son Allah Quli succeeded him, the
dynasty Iltazar had declared into existence over one afternoon's
unanimous applause was no longer an improvisation - it was the only
government Khwarazm had left.
