Khwarazm is an irrigated island. The Oxus comes down out of the mountains, spreads
into a delta before the Aral Sea, and around that green triangle in every direction
there is sand — the Kara Kum to the south, the Kizil Kum to the east, the Ust-Urt
plateau to the north-west, the Caspian beyond that.

Everything about the khanate that grew there follows from the geography.

## Why it existed

It was one of the oldest settled countries in Central Asia and one of the richest
per acre, because the delta silt and the canals grew wheat, melons and cotton in a
place that has almost no rain. It was also, by the sixteenth century, politically
separate from Transoxiana in a way it had not always been, because the desert
between them was two weeks wide.

When the Uzbek conquest swept south out of the Kipchak steppe, the Shaybanid
princes who took Khwarazm were a different branch from the ones who took Bukhara,
and the two lines stayed distinct and hostile for three hundred years. Ilbars took
the country in 1511 — Abu'l-Ghazi dates it 911 of the Hijra, and Howorth points out
that this is clearly a mistake — and his descendants held it.

## Why nobody could take it

Because to reach it you had to cross the desert, and armies that crossed the desert
arrived in no condition to fight.

That single fact protected the khanate against Persia, against Bukhara, and for a
century and a half against Russia. Peter the Great sent an expedition under Prince
Bekovich-Cherkassky in 1717 that reached the country and was destroyed there. A
second Russian attempt turned back. The desert did the work.

It also meant that everyone who did make the journey — merchants, pilgrims, envoys,
Persian peasants caught in a raid — arrived exhausted, and that the roads across it
were the khanate's hunting ground.

## The other consequence

A small, wealthy, unreachable state on the edge of a desert full of Turkmen
horsemen has one obvious export, and Khiva made it an institution.

The slave trade was not incidental. Persians taken on the Khorasan
frontier and Russians taken on the Caspian shore were brought to the Khivan markets
in numbers large enough to constitute an economy, and the khanate's relations with
Persia, with Russia and with the Turkmen tribes on whom it depended for both
cavalry and captives were all organised around it. Vámbéry, travelling with a
Turkmen caravan in 1863, met Persian slaves at every stage, and a Russian sailor
among them.

Abolishing it was the first thing the Russians wrote into the treaty in 1873.

## What kind of state

Vámbéry's summary of the constitution is that it was of Mongol origin: a khan or
padishah chosen from the midst of a victorious race, with a fixed set of named
officers beside him.

In practice the khan disposed despotically of the property and lives of his
subjects, with no check on him except the ulema, and then only when the ulema were
led by men whose learning and irreproachable lives had made them objects of
affection to the people and of dread to the khan.

That was the theory in 1863. By then the khans had for two generations been
whatever their prime ministers said they were.
