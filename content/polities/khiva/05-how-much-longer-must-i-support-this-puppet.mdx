By the middle of the eighteenth century the khan of Khiva was a Kazakh prince
imported for the purpose, kept in seclusion and shown to the people three times a
year.

## The inaqs

The real government belonged to the *inaq* — the prime minister, the senior bi of
the Uzbek tribes. The office had become hereditary in the Qungrat family, who were
also governors of Hazarasp, and by the 1750s they had made the khanship ornamental.

The daily routine of the arrangement survives. The khan sat in brocade; every day
the inaq and the grandees had an audience with him; the courtiers went to the palace
and the inaq sat beside him. Ishmed became inaq in 1755 and held the post for
seventeen years, and the office passed from father to son after him.

Why keep a khan at all? For the same reason the Manghits kept the Janids at
Bukhara and Edigü kept khans at Sarai: because the title required Chinggisid descent
and the Qungrats did not have it. So they sent to the Kazakh steppe for a Jochid,
installed him, fed him, and did not let him be seen.

## Iltazar

Ivaz Inaq died in 1804. The eldest son declined the succession in favour of his
brother Iltazar, who was proclaimed inaq and for six months paid his daily respects
to the Kazakh khan in the citadel.

Then he called his brother in one night and made the case.

> Timurlenk, Nadir Shah, and Muhammed Rahim of Bukhara were none of them khan's
> sons, but men like themselves who had earned their own fortunes.

*Thanks to God, I have judgment, courage, and soldiers. How much longer must I
support this puppet?*

His brother approved and recited the *fatiha* over it. The next day Iltazar had the
Kazakh khan brought out of the fortress and sent home, telling him that another
would be sent for to fill his place. Instead he raised ten thousand Uzbeks in
armour, summoned the ulema, the ataliqs and the inaqs, and told them he had become
their ruler himself and they had no further need of a Kazakh khan.

Everyone assented and called down blessings on him except Bek Pulad, the ataliq of
the Uyghur tribe, who said it was not worthy of him, that he should imitate his
father and his ancestors, and that it might not be God's pleasure that he should
bring so grave a matter to a happy conclusion. Since everyone else was unanimous he
eventually acquiesced. Iltazar remembered it.

## The same year, two khanates

It is worth putting the two events side by side, because they are the same event.

At Bukhara in 1785 the Manghit ataliq set aside the last Janid and took power, and
solved the descent problem by calling himself amir instead of khan. At Khiva in
1804 the Qungrat inaq set aside an imported Kazakh and took the title of khan
anyway, and dared anybody to say otherwise.

Both dynasties were Uzbek tribal families that had been running the administration
for generations. Both ruled until 1920. Neither had a drop of Chinggisid blood, and
by the nineteenth century it no longer mattered — which is the real end of the
Mongol political inheritance in Central Asia, two centuries after the last khan who
could actually claim it stopped mattering.
